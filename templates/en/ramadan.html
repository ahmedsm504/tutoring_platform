Ramadan is the month of the Quran, and the best Ramadan is the one we prepare for early. Here you will find a daily khatam plan that fits your time, a memorization plan for children, a 30-day Juz Amma challenge, and simple activities that make the month special at home.

How many pages a day to finish the Quran in Ramadan?

The standard Madani mushaf has 604 pages. Divide them over the days of the month and spread your reading across the five daily prayers, and the daily portion becomes surprisingly light:

One khatam

20 pages

a day = 4 pages after each prayer (about one juz a day)

Two khatams

40 pages

a day = 8 pages after each prayer

Three khatams

If your time is limited, one calm khatam with reflection is far better than several rushed ones without understanding. The Prophet ﷺ was at his most generous in Ramadan, and Jibril would meet him every night of Ramadan to study the Quran with him (al-Bukhari and Muslim). Ramadan is not only a month of reading more: it is a month of reviewing, understanding and living with the Quran.

Reading when you are not fluent yet

Many adults, especially those who grew up outside the Arab world, feel shy about reading the Quran in Arabic. Do not let that keep you from it. Read a smaller amount slowly and correctly, listen to the same pages from a good reciter, and read a translation alongside so you understand what you recite. The Prophet ﷺ said that the one who reads the Quran with difficulty, stumbling over it, receives a double reward (al-Bukhari and Muslim). Ramadan is a beautiful time to start lessons and fix your recitation, so that next Ramadan you can read with ease.

A simple daily rhythm

  • After Fajr: the quietest time of the day. Read your largest portion here, even ten minutes makes a difference.
  • During the day: listen to the day's juz on your commute or while cooking, so the words stay with you.
  • Before iftar: a short portion with the children, followed by du'a, which is a time when du'a is hoped to be answered.
  • After Tarawih: read a short tafsir of what you heard in prayer, so the recitation in salah becomes meaningful.

One juz a day: read the summary first

If you are reading one juz a day, spend five minutes on its summary before you start: the main themes, the key verses and the stories it contains. Reading with that map in mind makes the Arabic far easier to follow, even if you rely on a translation, and turns the khatam into a journey rather than a page count.

The last ten nights and Laylat al-Qadr

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever stands in prayer in Ramadan out of faith and seeking reward will have his past sins forgiven" (al-Bukhari and Muslim). 'A'ishah asked him what she should say if she knew which night was Laylat al-Qadr, and he said: "Say: O Allah, You are Pardoning and You love to pardon, so pardon me" (at-Tirmidhi, who graded it hasan sahih).

  • Give the last ten nights more reading time, even if it means cutting back elsewhere.
  • Teach your children this du'a and say it together on the odd nights.
  • Give charity in the last ten nights, however small, and let the children hand it over themselves.
  • Read Surah Al-Qadr with its meaning, so the family knows why this night is better than a thousand months.

Start preparing now

What spoils Ramadan for many people is that reading feels heavy in the first week, because the jump from no daily reading to a juz a day is too big. So:

  • Start a small daily portion now, two pages a day, and increase it gradually.
  • Correct your recitation and tajweed before Ramadan, so you can read accurately and comfortably.
  • Review the surahs you have memorized, and plan revision with our memorization planner.
  • Decide your family's Ramadan goals together: one khatam for the adults, ten surahs for the children, one good habit for everyone.

Common questions about Ramadan and the Quran

How many pages a day do I need to read to finish the Quran in Ramadan?

About 20 pages a day for one khatam in 30 days, which is 4 pages after each prayer. If the month turns out to be 29 days, add a page on a few days.

When does Ramadan 2027 start?

Astronomical calculations expect it on or around Monday 8 February 2027. The official start depends on the moon sighting announced in each country, so it may differ by a day.

What should my child memorize in Ramadan?

The last ten surahs of Juz Amma suit most children. If they already know them, they can continue with the rest of Juz Amma in the 30-day challenge.

When should I start preparing for Ramadan?

Now, or from Sha'ban: a small daily reading habit, correcting your recitation, and reviewing what you have memorized.
