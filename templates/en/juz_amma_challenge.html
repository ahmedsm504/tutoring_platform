How to do the challenge with your child

  1. Listen first: play the day's portion three times while your child follows in the mushaf. Hearing a skilled reciter before memorizing protects the child from learning mistakes that are hard to fix later.
  2. Repeat verse by verse: your child repeats each verse until they can say it without looking, then links it to the verse before it. Linking is what turns separate verses into a surah they can recite in one go.
  3. Recite it in prayer: ask your child to recite yesterday's portion in today's prayer. Using a surah in salah is the strongest way to keep it.
  4. Tick the day: when the portion is done, tick it here and let your child watch the bar fill up. Children love seeing their own progress.
  5. Celebrate each surah: make a certificate with their name and hang it in their room.

Why start with the short surahs?

Juz Amma is printed from An-Naba to An-Nas, but most children learn it in reverse. The last surahs are only three to six verses long, so a child finishes several in the first days and feels successful straight away. That early confidence carries them through the longer surahs later in the month, such as Al-Fajr, Al-Mutaffifin and An-Naba, which the plan splits over two days.

Doing the challenge in Ramadan 🌙

Ramadan has 29 or 30 days and so does the challenge, which makes it a natural fit. Tap "Start on 1 Ramadan" above and every day will show its date and its day of Ramadan. Many families find the best time for the new portion is the half hour before iftar, when the house is calm, with the recitation check after Tarawih or before bed. The last ten nights then become review and celebration. Our Ramadan Quran plan has a khatam schedule for adults and daily activities for children.

Share your child's progress

Type your child's name and tap "Progress picture to share". You will get a picture with the stars they have earned so far. Send it to the family group: encouragement from grandparents, aunts and uncles means a lot to a child and keeps them going to the end. You can also print the whole chart and stick it on the fridge so your child can tick each day by hand.

What if a day is too much for my child?

That is completely normal. Young children, around five to seven, can take two days over one portion. Consistency matters far more than speed, and the Prophet ﷺ said that the most beloved deeds to Allah are the most regular, even if small (al-Bukhari and Muslim). If your child already knows some of the short surahs, the first week will go faster.
