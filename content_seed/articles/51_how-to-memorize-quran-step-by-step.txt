"How do I memorize the Quran and not forget it?" is the question we hear more than any other. The honest answer: there's no magic trick, but there is a **clear method**, and students who follow it make steady progress. Here it is, exactly as we use it with our students.

## Why start today: the virtue of memorizing
Before the plan, remember why you are starting. It is your fuel on difficult days:
- The Prophet ﷺ said: "The best of you are those who learn the Quran and teach it" (Bukhari).
- He ﷺ said: "It will be said to the companion of the Quran: recite and rise, and recite slowly as you used to recite in the world, for your rank will be at the last verse you recite" (Abu Dawud and At-Tirmidhi, who graded it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ).
- He ﷺ also said that the one skilled in the Quran is with the noble, righteous scribes, and the one who recites it with difficulty, stumbling over it, has a double reward (Muslim).

Every verse you memorize is a step up, and every effort is rewarded. You do not have to finish to gain the virtue; you are in worship from the first verse.

## Before you start: three things that decide your success
1. **Sincere intention and patience.** Hifz is a long journey. People who chase speed usually stop after a few weeks.
2. **One fixed Mushaf.** Always memorize from the same print, ideally the 15-line Madinah Mushaf where each page ends at the end of a verse. Your visual memory learns where each verse sits on the page.
3. **A fixed daily time.** For many people the best time is right after Fajr. Thirty minutes every day beats three hours once a week.

## Where should you start?
There is no single required order, and each approach has advantages:
- **From the end of the Mushaf**: start with Juz 'Amma, then Tabarak, then Qad Sami', and work backwards. This suits most beginners and children: the surahs are short, progress is quick and motivating, and these are the surahs recited most in prayer.
- **From the beginning**: start with Al-Baqarah and go in order. This suits people with strong motivation and a fixed schedule, because long surahs need stamina.
- **With favourite surahs**: start with surahs you love or recite often, such as [Al-Mulk](/blog/memorize-surah-al-mulk-english/), [Al-Kahf](/blog/memorize-surah-al-kahf-english/) and [Ya-Sin](/blog/memorize-surah-yasin-english/), then continue with a system. Fine as a start, but don't make it your permanent method, or your memorization will be scattered.

Whatever you choose, choose one way and stick to it.

## How much should I memorize per day?
| Level | Suggested daily portion |
|---|---|
| Beginner or young child | 3–5 short verses |
| Intermediate | half a page (about 7 lines) |
| Confident, with time | one page |
| Full-time student | two pages or more, with strong revision |

## The 7 steps to memorize your portion
### 1. Correct your recitation first
The worst thing is to memorize a verse with a mistake; fixing it later is far harder. Read the portion to your teacher first, or listen to it several times.

### 2. Listen to a reciter at least three times
Listen to the portion recited by a master such as Sheikh Al-Husary or Al-Minshawi, with your eyes on the Mushaf. Our [Quran pages](/quran/) let you repeat each verse up to ten times.

### 3. Break it into small chunks
One or two verses, or two to three lines, at a time.

### 4. Focused repetition
Read the first chunk from the Mushaf 5 times, then recite it **from memory** 5 times. Do the same with the next chunk, then **join the two** and recite them together 3 times. Continue until the portion is complete.

### 5. Link the end of each verse to the next
The most common stumble is the jump from one verse to the next. When memorizing each verse, read the first two words of the following verse with it.

### 6. Recite the whole portion three times in a row without a mistake
If you slip even once, restart the count. This is the real test that it has settled.

### 7. Pray with it
Recite your new portion in your Sunnah or night prayers the same day. Reciting in prayer is one of the strongest ways to lock it in.

## Understand before you memorize
A verse you understand is memorized faster and forgotten more slowly. Before each portion:
- **Read a short explanation** of the verses in a reliable translation or brief tafsir, two or three minutes.
- **Know the topic of the passage**: is it a story, a ruling, or a description of Paradise or Hell? The topic ties the verses together in your mind.
- **Notice the key words**: the word that changes between two similar verses is exactly where you will slip, so mark it in your mind.

For a big-picture view of the juz you are memorizing, read its summary in [The 30 Juz of the Quran: A Complete Guide](/blog/30-juz-of-the-quran-guide-english/). Knowing the themes and sections of the juz lets you memorize knowing where you are in the surah.

## Handling similar verses
**Mutashābihāt** are verses that repeat with small differences, and they are where memorizers slip most. To handle them:
1. **Don't avoid them.** When a verse resembles one you know, stop and compare.
2. **Write the difference.** In a small notebook, write the two verses side by side and mark the word that differs.
3. **Link the difference to meaning or position.** Why is the word different here? Which of the two comes first in the Mushaf?
4. **Review the notebook weekly.** Similar verses need their own revision.

## Ways to strengthen memorization
The seven steps are the foundation. Add whichever of these suit you:
- **Writing**: write the portion from memory, then compare with the Mushaf. Writing exposes hidden mistakes, especially in similar verses.
- **Repeated listening**: play the portion while commuting or doing light work. Your ear memorizes without you noticing, but don't let this replace repetition with your own tongue.
- **Picturing the page**: close your eyes and see where the verse sits: top, middle, left or right. This visual memory saves you when you get stuck.
- **Reciting aloud**: memorizing in a whisper is weaker than memorizing in a voice you can hear.
- **Reciting to someone else**: stronger than reciting to yourself, because you can't skip past a mistake.

## Revision: half of Hifz
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Keep refreshing your knowledge of the Quran, for by the One in whose hand is Muhammad's soul, it escapes faster than camels from their tethers" (Bukhari and Muslim).

So no memorization method works without daily revision:
- **Revise yesterday's portion** before starting a new one.
- **Revise the last 5–10 pages** daily.
- **Revise part of your older memorization** daily, in rotation.

Full details in [our revision system](/blog/quran-revision-system-english/).

### Near and far revision
Divide your revision in two:
- **Near revision**: your most recent memorization, from yesterday's portion back to roughly the last twenty pages. Revise this **every day**, because it slips fastest.
- **Far revision**: everything memorized before that, divided across the days of the week so you pass over all of it in one or two weeks.

The more you memorize, the bigger the far revision becomes, until it is larger than your new portion. That is normal: someone who has memorized twenty juz spends most of their time revising, and that is exactly right.

**Golden rule:** if new memorization and revision compete for time, **revision wins**. A day without a new portion does no harm; a week without revision can cost you a month of work.

## A daily schedule for half a page
| Time | Activity | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| After Fajr | memorize the new portion with the seven steps | 30 min |
| Mid-morning or Dhuhr | recite the new portion once | 5 min |
| After Asr | near revision | 20 min |
| After Maghrib or Isha | far revision | 20 min |
| In Sunnah and night prayers | recite the new portion and some revision | within prayer |

That's about an hour and a quarter a day. If you can't manage it, cut the new portion, not the revision. Using your night prayer for revision is especially powerful: see [revising in night prayer](/blog/revision-in-night-prayer-english/).

## A weekly schedule
- **Saturday to Wednesday**: new memorization plus near and far revision.
- **Thursday**: no new portion; recite everything from this week to your teacher or a partner.
- **Friday**: longer far revision, and recite Surah Al-Kahf.

A weekly consolidation day with no new memorization stops weaknesses piling up.

## How long does it take to memorize the whole Quran?
The Madinah Mushaf has about 604 pages. This table shows the number of **actual memorization days**, not counting revision days and breaks:

| Daily portion | Approximate memorization days |
|---|---|
| 2 pages | 302 days |
| 1 page | 604 days |
| half a page | 1,208 days |
| a third of a page (5 lines) | 1,812 days |

Memorize half a page five days a week, and you can finish in under five years, with steady revision. Finishing with strong memorization matters far more than finishing fast.

## Mistakes that make memorization slip
1. Memorizing from different Mushaf prints.
2. Moving on before the previous portion is solid.
3. Skipping revision for a week or more.
4. Memorizing without correcting recitation, so you memorize your mistakes.
5. Comparing yourself to others. Consistency beats speed.

6. **Memorizing by eye only or by ear only.** Combine looking, listening, reciting from memory and reciting in prayer.
7. **Neglecting page beginnings.** Memorizers often stumble moving from one page to the next.
8. **Memorizing when exhausted.** Memorizing after a long workday wastes your time. Memorize at your freshest time and revise at other times.

For more pitfalls, read [10 mistakes beginner memorizers make](/blog/beginner-memorizer-mistakes-english/).

## A worked example: one page of Surah Al-Mulk
Suppose today's portion is the first page of Surah Al-Mulk, about twelve verses:
1. **Read a short explanation**: the verses speak of Allah's dominion, death and life as a test, the seven heavens in layers, the state of the disbelievers in the Fire, and the reward of those who fear their Lord unseen.
2. **Listen three times** to a skilled reciter with your eyes on the Mushaf.
3. **Divide it into four chunks by meaning**: verses 1-2 on dominion and the test, 3-5 on the heavens, 6-11 on the Fire, and 12 on those who fear their Lord.
4. **Memorize each chunk by repetition**: five times looking, five times from memory, then join it to the previous chunk.
5. **Recite the whole page** three times in a row without a mistake.
6. **Pray with it** in the Sunnah of Maghrib or in Witr.

Dividing by meaning turns twelve separate verses into four connected pieces. Tomorrow, revise this page before starting the next one.

## Signs your memorization is solid
- You can **start from any verse** on the page and continue without hesitation.
- You **move between pages** without stopping at the top of each one.
- You **recite it in prayer** without fear of getting stuck.
- You **recite it a week later** without looking.
- You **know its similar verses**: if someone recites a similar verse, you know the difference.

## When motivation dips
Every memorizer goes through dry spells. What helps:
- **Don't drop the portion completely**: memorize one verse instead of a page, but keep the thread unbroken.
- **Switch to revision**: low days are better for revision than for new material.
- **Change the place or time**: sometimes the boredom is with the routine, not the Quran.
- **Find a circle or a partner**: good company carries you when you are weak.
- **Make dua**: memorization is a gift from Allah before it is an effort.

## Frequently asked questions
### Can I memorize the Quran after forty?
Yes, and many people have done it in their forties and fifties. Choose a small, steady portion, give revision its full share, and use understanding to help your memory. Adults with attention difficulties can read [hifz for adults with ADHD](/blog/hifz-for-adults-with-adhd-english/).

### What if I forget what I memorized?
Don't despair and don't start over. Re-memorize what you forgot; the second time is much easier than the first. Then increase your revision.

### Should I memorize alone or with a teacher?
Alone is possible, but harder to sustain and harder to correct. A teacher or partner keeps you committed and catches mistakes before they settle.

### Should I memorize from an app or a printed Mushaf?
What matters most is **one fixed layout**. If you use an app, choose a page view that matches the Madinah Mushaf and don't change it. Choosing a reciter to follow matters too: see [how to choose a reciter for memorization](/blog/choosing-reciter-for-memorization-english/).
