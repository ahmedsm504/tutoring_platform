"How do I memorize the Quran and not forget it?" is the question we hear more than any other. The honest answer: there's no magic trick, but there is a **clear method**, and students who follow it make steady progress. Here it is, exactly as we use it with our students.

## Before you start: three things that decide your success
1. **Sincere intention and patience.** Hifz is a long journey. People who chase speed usually stop after a few weeks.
2. **One fixed Mushaf.** Always memorize from the same print, ideally the 15-line Madinah Mushaf where each page ends at the end of a verse. Your visual memory learns where each verse sits on the page.
3. **A fixed daily time.** For many people the best time is right after Fajr. Thirty minutes every day beats three hours once a week.

## How much should I memorize per day?
| Level | Suggested daily portion |
|---|---|
| Beginner or young child | 3–5 short verses |
| Intermediate | half a page (about 7 lines) |
| Confident, with time | one page |
| Full-time student | two pages or more, with strong revision |

## The 7 steps to memorize your portion
### 1. Correct your recitation first
The worst thing is to memorize a verse with a mistake; fixing it later is far harder. Read the portion to your teacher first, or listen to it several times.

### 2. Listen to a reciter at least three times
Listen to the portion recited by a master such as Sheikh Al-Husary or Al-Minshawi, with your eyes on the Mushaf. Our [Quran pages](/quran/) let you repeat each verse up to ten times.

### 3. Break it into small chunks
One or two verses, or two to three lines, at a time.

### 4. Focused repetition
Read the first chunk from the Mushaf 5 times, then recite it **from memory** 5 times. Do the same with the next chunk, then **join the two** and recite them together 3 times. Continue until the portion is complete.

### 5. Link the end of each verse to the next
The most common stumble is the jump from one verse to the next. When memorizing each verse, read the first two words of the following verse with it.

### 6. Recite the whole portion three times in a row without a mistake
If you slip even once, restart the count. This is the real test that it has settled.

### 7. Pray with it
Recite your new portion in your Sunnah or night prayers the same day. Reciting in prayer is one of the strongest ways to lock it in.

## Revision: half of Hifz
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Keep refreshing your knowledge of the Quran, for by the One in whose hand is Muhammad's soul, it escapes faster than camels from their tethers" (Bukhari and Muslim).

So no memorization method works without daily revision:
- **Revise yesterday's portion** before starting a new one.
- **Revise the last 5–10 pages** daily.
- **Revise part of your older memorization** daily, in rotation.

## How long does it take to memorize the whole Quran?
The Madinah Mushaf has about 604 pages. This table shows the number of **actual memorization days**, not counting revision days and breaks:

| Daily portion | Approximate memorization days |
|---|---|
| 2 pages | 302 days |
| 1 page | 604 days |
| half a page | 1,208 days |
| a third of a page (5 lines) | 1,812 days |

Memorize half a page five days a week, and you can finish in under five years, with steady revision. Finishing with strong memorization matters far more than finishing fast.

## Mistakes that make memorization slip
1. Memorizing from different Mushaf prints.
2. Moving on before the previous portion is solid.
3. Skipping revision for a week or more.
4. Memorizing without correcting recitation, so you memorize your mistakes.
5. Comparing yourself to others. Consistency beats speed.
