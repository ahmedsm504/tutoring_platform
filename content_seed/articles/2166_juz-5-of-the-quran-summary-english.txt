Juz 5 begins with ﴿۞وَٱلۡمُحۡصَنَٰتُ مِنَ ٱلنِّسَآءِ إِلَّا مَا مَلَكَتۡ أَيۡمَٰنُكُمۡۖ﴾, "And forbidden are married women, except those your right hands possess", so it is called **Juz Wal-Muhsanat**. All of it lies in **Surah An-Nisa**, from verse **24** to verse **147**: ﴿مَّا يَفۡعَلُ ٱللَّهُ بِعَذَابِكُمۡ إِن شَكَرۡتُمۡ وَءَامَنتُمۡۚ وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ شَاكِرًا عَلِيمٗا﴾, "What would Allah do with your punishment if you are grateful and believe? Allah is ever Appreciative, All-Knowing." In the Madinah Mushaf it fills **pages 82 to 101**.

If the opening of An-Nisa in Juz 4 set out the rulings on orphans and inheritance, Juz 5 builds **a just society** at every level: the family, neighbours, rulers and the ruled, the community in peace and war, and then it exposes the hidden enemy: hypocrisy.

## The big idea: justice in every relationship

The juz can be summed up as **justice in every relationship**: between husband and wife, between a person and relatives and neighbours, between rulers and the ruled, between Muslims and others, and even between a person and himself: ﴿كُونُواْ قَوَّـٰمِينَ بِٱلۡقِسۡطِ شُهَدَآءَ لِلَّهِ وَلَوۡ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِكُمۡ﴾, "Be persistently standing firm in justice, witnesses for Allah, even if it be against yourselves." The theme of **obeying Allah and His Messenger** as the measure of faith recurs, and so does **ease and mercy**: ﴿يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ أَن يُخَفِّفَ عَنكُمۡۚ وَخُلِقَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ ضَعِيفٗا﴾, "Allah wants to lighten your burden, for mankind was created weak."

## Section 1: Completing the marriage rulings (24-28)

The juz completes the list of prohibited marriages from the end of Juz 4, forbids married women, and permits others through dowry with the aim of chaste marriage, not fornication. It mentions marrying slave women for those unable to do otherwise. Then come three merciful divine "wills":
- ﴿يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ لِيُبَيِّنَ لَكُمۡ وَيَهۡدِيَكُمۡ سُنَنَ ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبۡلِكُمۡ وَيَتُوبَ عَلَيۡكُمۡۗ﴾, "Allah wants to make things clear to you, guide you to the ways of those before you, and accept your repentance."
- ﴿وَٱللَّهُ يُرِيدُ أَن يَتُوبَ عَلَيۡكُمۡ﴾, "Allah wants to accept your repentance", in contrast to those who follow desires and want you to deviate greatly.
- ﴿يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ أَن يُخَفِّفَ عَنكُمۡۚ وَخُلِقَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ ضَعِيفٗا﴾.

**Lesson**: family law is not a set of chains; it is clarification, guidance, repentance and relief.

## Section 2: Wealth, major sins and envy (29-33)

- ﴿لَا تَأۡكُلُوٓاْ أَمۡوَٰلَكُم بَيۡنَكُم بِٱلۡبَٰطِلِ إِلَّآ أَن تَكُونَ تِجَٰرَةً عَن تَرَاضٖ مِّنكُمۡۚ﴾, "Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly, but only through trade by mutual consent", and ﴿وَلَا تَقۡتُلُوٓاْ أَنفُسَكُمۡۚ﴾, "and do not kill yourselves."
- **A great promise for avoiding major sins**: ﴿إِن تَجۡتَنِبُواْ كَبَآئِرَ مَا تُنۡهَوۡنَ عَنۡهُ نُكَفِّرۡ عَنكُمۡ سَيِّـَٔاتِكُمۡ وَنُدۡخِلۡكُم مُّدۡخَلٗا كَرِيمٗا﴾, "If you avoid the major sins you are forbidden, We will remove your lesser misdeeds and admit you to a noble entrance." In Al-Bukhari and Muslim: "Avoid the seven destructive sins", and he ﷺ listed associating partners with Allah, sorcery, killing a soul unlawfully, consuming riba, consuming an orphan's wealth, fleeing the battlefield, and slandering chaste believing women.
- **The cure for envy**: ﴿وَلَا تَتَمَنَّوۡاْ مَا فَضَّلَ ٱللَّهُ بِهِۦ بَعۡضَكُمۡ عَلَىٰ بَعۡضٖۚ﴾, "Do not wish for what Allah has given some of you over others", then the alternative: ﴿وَسۡـَٔلُواْ ٱللَّهَ مِن فَضۡلِهِۦٓۚ﴾, "and ask Allah of His bounty."

## Section 3: The family in conflict (34-35)

﴿ٱلرِّجَالُ قَوَّـٰمُونَ عَلَى ٱلنِّسَآءِ بِمَا فَضَّلَ ٱللَّهُ بَعۡضَهُمۡ عَلَىٰ بَعۡضٖ وَبِمَآ أَنفَقُواْ مِنۡ أَمۡوَٰلِهِمۡۚ﴾, "Men are caretakers of women, by what Allah has given one over the other and by what they spend from their wealth." In context, *qiwamah* is a responsibility of care and provision. The verse sets out gradual steps for marital breakdown and ends with a warning: ﴿فَإِنۡ أَطَعۡنَكُمۡ فَلَا تَبۡغُواْ عَلَيۡهِنَّ سَبِيلًاۗ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيّٗا كَبِيرٗا﴾, "If they obey you, seek no means against them; Allah is Most High, Most Great", reminding the husband that Allah is above him.

Then **family arbitration**: ﴿فَٱبۡعَثُواْ حَكَمٗا مِّنۡ أَهۡلِهِۦ وَحَكَمٗا مِّنۡ أَهۡلِهَآ إِن يُرِيدَآ إِصۡلَٰحٗا يُوَفِّقِ ٱللَّهُ بَيۡنَهُمَآۗ﴾, "Appoint an arbitrator from his family and one from hers; if they both want reconciliation, Allah will bring it about between them." This is a foundation of family reconciliation: two arbitrators from the families, and the intention to reconcile is the condition for success. Read [I Only Want Reconciliation](/blog/in-uridu-illa-al-islah-meaning-english/).

## Section 4: The verse of ten rights (36-42)

﴿۞وَٱعۡبُدُواْ ٱللَّهَ وَلَا تُشۡرِكُواْ بِهِۦ شَيۡـٔٗاۖ وَبِٱلۡوَٰلِدَيۡنِ إِحۡسَٰنٗا وَبِذِي ٱلۡقُرۡبَىٰ وَٱلۡيَتَٰمَىٰ وَٱلۡمَسَٰكِينِ وَٱلۡجَارِ ذِي ٱلۡقُرۡبَىٰ وَٱلۡجَارِ ٱلۡجُنُبِ﴾, "Worship Allah and associate nothing with Him, and be good to parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbour, the distant neighbour", then the companion at your side, the traveller, and those your right hands possess. Scholars call it **the verse of ten rights**: the right of Allah, followed by nine rights of people moving from the closest to the furthest.

It then condemns those who are miserly and urge others to be miserly, and those who spend to be seen. Then a scene of the Day of Judgement: ﴿فَكَيۡفَ إِذَا جِئۡنَا مِن كُلِّ أُمَّةِۭ بِشَهِيدٖ وَجِئۡنَا بِكَ عَلَىٰ هَـٰٓؤُلَآءِ شَهِيدٗا﴾, "How will it be when We bring from every nation a witness, and bring you as a witness over these?" In Al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn Mas'ud recited An-Nisa to the Prophet ﷺ until he reached this verse, and he said, "Enough for now", and his eyes were streaming with tears.

## Section 5: Prayer and purification (43)

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ لَا تَقۡرَبُواْ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَأَنتُمۡ سُكَٰرَىٰ حَتَّىٰ تَعۡلَمُواْ مَا تَقُولُونَ﴾, "O you who believe, do not approach prayer while intoxicated, until you know what you are saying." It was a stage before the final prohibition of wine in Al-Ma'idah. The same verse gives **tayammum** for those who find no water: ﴿فَتَيَمَّمُواْ صَعِيدٗا طَيِّبٗا﴾, "then seek clean earth", a mercy and relief from Allah.

**Benefit**: "until you know what you are saying" shows that understanding what you say is one of the aims of prayer, a doorway to khushu'.

## Section 6: The People of the Book and shirk (44-57)

It speaks of those given a portion of scripture who buy error and distort words from their places. Then the great rule: ﴿إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَغۡفِرُ أَن يُشۡرَكَ بِهِۦ وَيَغۡفِرُ مَا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ لِمَن يَشَآءُۚ﴾, "Allah does not forgive that partners be associated with Him, but forgives what is less than that for whom He wills." It criticises those who praise their own purity, believe in false objects of worship, and envy people for Allah's bounty. Then the scene of skins replaced in the Fire, and in contrast, gardens for the believers.

## Section 7: Trust, obedience and judgement (58-70)

One of the Quran's most important passages on **governance and justice**:
- ﴿۞إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَأۡمُرُكُمۡ أَن تُؤَدُّواْ ٱلۡأَمَٰنَٰتِ إِلَىٰٓ أَهۡلِهَا وَإِذَا حَكَمۡتُم بَيۡنَ ٱلنَّاسِ أَن تَحۡكُمُواْ بِٱلۡعَدۡلِۚ﴾, "Allah commands you to render trusts to those to whom they are due, and when you judge between people, to judge with justice." This addresses those in authority first. Read [Render Trusts to Their Owners: 4:58](/blog/tuaddu-al-amanat-ila-ahliha-meaning-english/).
- ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓاْ أَطِيعُواْ ٱللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُواْ ٱلرَّسُولَ وَأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَمۡرِ مِنكُمۡۖ﴾, "O you who believe, obey Allah, obey the Messenger and those in authority among you." This addresses the people. Notice that "obey" is repeated for Allah and the Messenger but not for those in authority: obeying them follows obedience to Allah and His Messenger. Then the reference point in disputes: ﴿فَإِن تَنَٰزَعۡتُمۡ فِي شَيۡءٖ فَرُدُّوهُ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَٱلرَّسُولِ﴾, "If you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger." Read [Obey Allah and Obey the Messenger](/blog/atiullah-wa-atiu-ar-rasul-meaning-english/).
- Criticism of those who seek judgement from false authorities, and of the hypocrites who turn people away from the Messenger.
- The divine oath: ﴿فَلَا وَرَبِّكَ لَا يُؤۡمِنُونَ حَتَّىٰ يُحَكِّمُوكَ فِيمَا شَجَرَ بَيۡنَهُمۡ ثُمَّ لَا يَجِدُواْ فِيٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ حَرَجٗا مِّمَّا قَضَيۡتَ وَيُسَلِّمُواْ تَسۡلِيمٗا﴾, "By your Lord, they will not truly believe until they make you judge in their disputes, then find no discomfort in what you decide, and submit fully." In Sahih Al-Bukhari it is mentioned in connection with a dispute between Az-Zubayr ibn al-'Awwam and a man of the Ansar over irrigation.
- The reward: ﴿وَمَن يُطِعِ ٱللَّهَ وَٱلرَّسُولَ فَأُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ مَعَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنۡعَمَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيۡهِم مِّنَ ٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَ وَٱلصِّدِّيقِينَ وَٱلشُّهَدَآءِ وَٱلصَّـٰلِحِينَۚ﴾, "Whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger will be with those Allah has blessed: the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous." These are "those You have blessed" whose path we ask for in Al-Fatiha.

## Section 8: Fighting and defending the oppressed (71-87)

- Take precautions and go out when called.
- ﴿وَمَا لَكُمۡ لَا تُقَٰتِلُونَ فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡمُسۡتَضۡعَفِينَ مِنَ ٱلرِّجَالِ وَٱلنِّسَآءِ وَٱلۡوِلۡدَٰنِ﴾, "Why should you not fight in the cause of Allah and for the oppressed men, women and children?" Lawful fighting aims to lift oppression from the weak.
- ﴿إِنَّ كَيۡدَ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنِ كَانَ ضَعِيفًا﴾, "Indeed the plot of Shaytan is ever weak."
- **No escape from death**: ﴿أَيۡنَمَا تَكُونُواْ يُدۡرِككُّمُ ٱلۡمَوۡتُ وَلَوۡ كُنتُمۡ فِي بُرُوجٖ مُّشَيَّدَةٖۗ﴾, "Wherever you are, death will overtake you, even if you are in lofty towers."
- **Good and bad**: all is from Allah by decree, and the harm that reaches you comes from yourself as its cause.
- ﴿مَّن يُطِعِ ٱلرَّسُولَ فَقَدۡ أَطَاعَ ٱللَّهَۖ﴾, "Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah."
- **The verse of reflection**: ﴿أَفَلَا يَتَدَبَّرُونَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَۚ وَلَوۡ كَانَ مِنۡ عِندِ غَيۡرِ ٱللَّهِ لَوَجَدُواْ فِيهِ ٱخۡتِلَٰفٗا كَثِيرٗا﴾, "Do they not reflect on the Quran? Had it been from other than Allah, they would have found much contradiction in it." Read [Do They Not Reflect on the Quran?](/blog/afala-yatadabbaruna-al-quran-meaning-english/).
- **Spreading news**: the ban on broadcasting reports of safety or fear before referring them to the Messenger and those in authority.
- **Returning greetings**: ﴿وَإِذَا حُيِّيتُم بِتَحِيَّةٖ فَحَيُّواْ بِأَحۡسَنَ مِنۡهَآ أَوۡ رُدُّوهَآۗ﴾, "When you are greeted, greet with something better or return it." Read [Return a Greeting with a Better One](/blog/return-a-greeting-with-a-better-one-english/).

**Notice**: the verse on greetings sits in the middle of verses on fighting. Even in war, Islam preserves the etiquette of peace.

## Section 9: Hypocrites and the sanctity of blood (88-94)

Hizb 10 begins: ﴿۞فَمَا لَكُمۡ فِي ٱلۡمُنَٰفِقِينَ فِئَتَيۡنِ﴾, "Why are you divided into two groups about the hypocrites?" Rulings follow for those who fight and those who withdraw. Then the **sanctity of a believer's life**: compensation and expiation for accidental killing, and the severe warning for deliberate killing in verse 93. Then verification: ﴿إِذَا ضَرَبۡتُمۡ فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ فَتَبَيَّنُواْ وَلَا تَقُولُواْ لِمَنۡ أَلۡقَىٰٓ إِلَيۡكُمُ ٱلسَّلَٰمَ لَسۡتَ مُؤۡمِنٗا﴾, "When you go out in the cause of Allah, verify, and do not say to one who offers you peace, 'You are not a believer.'" No one is to be declared a disbeliever out of desire for worldly gain.

## Section 10: Strivers, migration and the fear prayer (95-104)

- ﴿لَّا يَسۡتَوِي ٱلۡقَٰعِدُونَ مِنَ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ غَيۡرُ أُوْلِي ٱلضَّرَرِ وَٱلۡمُجَٰهِدُونَ﴾, "Not equal are those believers who sit back, other than those with disabilities, and those who strive." The exception "other than those with disabilities" was revealed after Ibn Umm Maktum, who was blind, mentioned his condition, as in Sahih Al-Bukhari.
- **Migration**: the angels ask those who died while able to migrate, ﴿فِيمَ كُنتُمۡۖ﴾, "What condition were you in?", while the truly oppressed are excused. Then a promise to the migrant: ﴿وَمَن يُهَاجِرۡ فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ يَجِدۡ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ مُرَٰغَمٗا كَثِيرٗا وَسَعَةٗۚ﴾, "Whoever migrates for Allah will find on earth many places of refuge and abundance."
- **Shortening prayer while travelling**, and the **fear prayer** in congregation, showing that prayer is not dropped even in battle.
- Then the rule: ﴿إِنَّ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ كَانَتۡ عَلَى ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ كِتَٰبٗا مَّوۡقُوتٗا﴾, "Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers at fixed times."

## Section 11: Justice in judgement (105-115)

﴿إِنَّآ أَنزَلۡنَآ إِلَيۡكَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ بِٱلۡحَقِّ لِتَحۡكُمَ بَيۡنَ ٱلنَّاسِ بِمَآ أَرَىٰكَ ٱللَّهُۚ وَلَا تَكُن لِّلۡخَآئِنِينَ خَصِيمٗا﴾, "We have sent down to you the Book in truth so that you judge between people by what Allah has shown you; do not be an advocate for the deceitful." Commentators mention that these verses relate to a Muslim who stole and then blamed an innocent man; the Quran cleared the innocent man and did not favour the insider.

Then a door of hope: ﴿وَمَن يَعۡمَلۡ سُوٓءًا أَوۡ يَظۡلِمۡ نَفۡسَهُۥ ثُمَّ يَسۡتَغۡفِرِ ٱللَّهَ يَجِدِ ٱللَّهَ غَفُورٗا رَّحِيمٗا﴾, "Whoever does wrong or wrongs himself and then seeks Allah's forgiveness will find Allah Forgiving and Merciful."

Then the measure of private conversation: ﴿۞لَّا خَيۡرَ فِي كَثِيرٖ مِّن نَّجۡوَىٰهُمۡ إِلَّا مَنۡ أَمَرَ بِصَدَقَةٍ أَوۡ مَعۡرُوفٍ أَوۡ إِصۡلَٰحِۭ بَيۡنَ ٱلنَّاسِۚ﴾, "No good is there in much of their private talk, except for one who enjoins charity, kindness or reconciliation between people."

## Section 12: Shirk, Shaytan's promises and the way of Ibrahim (116-126)

The rule is repeated: ﴿إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَغۡفِرُ أَن يُشۡرَكَ بِهِۦ﴾. Shaytan's promises are exposed: ﴿يَعِدُهُمۡ وَيُمَنِّيهِمۡۖ وَمَا يَعِدُهُمُ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنُ إِلَّا غُرُورًا﴾, "He promises them and arouses desires in them, but Shaytan promises them nothing except delusion." Then the measure of reward: ﴿لَّيۡسَ بِأَمَانِيِّكُمۡ وَلَآ أَمَانِيِّ أَهۡلِ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِۗ مَن يَعۡمَلۡ سُوٓءࣰا يُجۡزَ بِهِۦ﴾, "It is not by your wishful thinking nor that of the People of the Book; whoever does wrong will be recompensed for it." Then: ﴿وَمَنۡ أَحۡسَنُ دِينٗا مِّمَّنۡ أَسۡلَمَ وَجۡهَهُۥ لِلَّهِ وَهُوَ مُحۡسِنࣱ وَٱتَّبَعَ مِلَّةَ إِبۡرَٰهِيمَ حَنِيفٗاۗ وَٱتَّخَذَ ٱللَّهُ إِبۡرَٰهِيمَ خَلِيلٗا﴾, "Who is better in religion than one who submits his face to Allah while doing good and follows the way of Ibrahim, the upright? And Allah took Ibrahim as an intimate friend."

## Section 13: Back to women, and reconciliation (127-134)

The surah returns to its name: they ask you for a ruling about women. Then the case of a wife who fears her husband's aversion: ﴿فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيۡهِمَآ أَن يُصۡلِحَا بَيۡنَهُمَا صُلۡحٗاۚ وَٱلصُّلۡحُ خَيۡرࣱۗ﴾, "There is no blame on them if they make peace between themselves, and peace is best." Then realism: ﴿وَلَن تَسۡتَطِيعُوٓاْ أَن تَعۡدِلُواْ بَيۡنَ ٱلنِّسَآءِ وَلَوۡ حَرَصۡتُمۡۖ فَلَا تَمِيلُواْ كُلَّ ٱلۡمَيۡلِ﴾, "You will never be able to be perfectly equal between wives, even if you try, so do not incline completely to one." Perfect equality of feeling is impossible; open injustice is forbidden. Then: ﴿وَإِن يَتَفَرَّقَا يُغۡنِ ٱللَّهُ كُلࣰّا مِّن سَعَتِهِۦۚ﴾, "If they separate, Allah will enrich each from His abundance."

**Reflect**: ﴿وَٱلصُّلۡحُ خَيۡرࣱۗ﴾, "and peace is best", is three words that could serve as a rule for every home.

## Section 14: Justice and the hypocrites (135-147)

﴿۞يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ كُونُواْ قَوَّـٰمِينَ بِٱلۡقِسۡطِ شُهَدَآءَ لِلَّهِ وَلَوۡ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِكُمۡ أَوِ ٱلۡوَٰلِدَيۡنِ وَٱلۡأَقۡرَبِينَۚ﴾, "O you who believe, stand firm for justice, witnesses for Allah, even against yourselves, your parents or your relatives." True testimony favours no self, parent or relative, and no rich person for wealth or poor person for poverty. Read [Stand Firm for Justice: 4:135](/blog/kunu-qawwamina-bil-qist-meaning-english/).

The juz then closes with the surah's fullest description of the hypocrites:
- They take disbelievers as allies and seek honour with them, ﴿فَإِنَّ ٱلۡعِزَّةَ لِلَّهِ جَمِيعٗا﴾, "but all honour belongs to Allah."
- They wait to see how things turn out: if the believers win, they say "Were we not with you?"
- ﴿إِنَّ ٱلۡمُنَٰفِقِينَ يُخَٰدِعُونَ ٱللَّهَ وَهُوَ خَٰدِعُهُمۡ وَإِذَا قَامُوٓاْ إِلَى ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ قَامُواْ كُسَالَىٰ يُرَآءُونَ ٱلنَّاسَ وَلَا يَذۡكُرُونَ ٱللَّهَ إِلَّا قَلِيلٗا﴾, "The hypocrites try to deceive Allah, but He is deceiving them; when they stand for prayer they stand lazily, showing off to people, and remember Allah only a little."
- Wavering between the two sides, belonging to neither.
- ﴿إِنَّ ٱلۡمُنَٰفِقِينَ فِي ٱلدَّرۡكِ ٱلۡأَسۡفَلِ مِنَ ٱلنَّارِ﴾, "The hypocrites will be in the lowest depth of the Fire."
- Yet repentance is open: except those who repent, reform, hold fast to Allah and make their religion sincerely for Allah.
- The close of the juz: ﴿مَّا يَفۡعَلُ ٱللَّهُ بِعَذَابِكُمۡ إِن شَكَرۡتُمۡ وَءَامَنتُمۡۚ﴾, "What would Allah do with your punishment if you are grateful and believe?"

For the hypocrites' full portrait across the Quran see [Surah Al-Munafiqun Meaning in English](/blog/surah-al-munafiqun-meaning-english/).

## Tajweed points in Juz 5

### Idgham with ghunnah in ﴿مِّن نَّجۡوَىٰهُمۡ﴾ and ﴿مِن وَرَآئِكُمۡ﴾
Sakin noon before noon or waw merges with a two-count ghunnah.

### Iqlab at verse endings such as ﴿سَمِيعَۢا بَصِيرٗا﴾
An-Nisa often ends verses with Allah's names in tanween; when ba follows, the tanween becomes a hidden meem.

### ﴿يُدۡرِككُّمُ﴾: merging identical letters
The first kaf is sakin and the second voweled, so they merge into one doubled kaf.

### Stopping on tanween fath
Many verse endings in An-Nisa end in tanween fath, such as ﴿حَكِيمٗا﴾ and ﴿رَّحِيمٗا﴾; when stopping, read it as a two-count alif (madd 'iwad).

### Connecting and cutting hamzahs in ﴿وَٱتَّخَذَ ٱللَّهُ إِبۡرَٰهِيمَ﴾
The connecting hamzah drops in continuous reading, while the hamzah of "Ibrahim" is a cutting hamzah and is always pronounced. For the rules of madd see [Madd Rules in Tajweed](/blog/madd-rules-tajweed-english/).

## Similar verses to watch

### 1. The two verses on shirk: 48 and 116
- 48: ﴿وَمَن يُشۡرِكۡ بِٱللَّهِ فَقَدِ ٱفۡتَرَىٰٓ إِثۡمًا عَظِيمًا﴾, "has invented a tremendous sin."
- 116: ﴿وَمَن يُشۡرِكۡ بِٱللَّهِ فَقَدۡ ضَلَّ ضَلَٰلَۢا بَعِيدًا﴾, "has gone far astray."
**Key**: the first is in the context of the People of the Book, who know and invent lies, so "invented"; the second concerns idolaters who strayed without scripture, so "gone astray".

### 2. The promise of Paradise: 57 and 122
- 57: after ﴿أَبَدٗاۖ﴾ comes ﴿لَّهُمۡ فِيهَآ أَزۡوَٰجࣱ مُّطَهَّرَةࣱۖ﴾, "for them therein are purified spouses."
- 122: after ﴿أَبَدٗاۖ﴾ comes ﴿وَعۡدَ ٱللَّهِ حَقࣰّاۚ وَمَنۡ أَصۡدَقُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ قِيلࣰا﴾, "the promise of Allah, in truth; and who is truer than Allah in speech?"

### 3. Justice in An-Nisa and Al-Ma'idah
- An-Nisa 135: ﴿كُونُواْ قَوَّـٰمِينَ بِٱلۡقِسۡطِ شُهَدَآءَ لِلَّهِ﴾, justice first.
- Al-Ma'idah 8: ﴿كُونُواْ قَوَّـٰمِينَ لِلَّهِ شُهَدَآءَ بِٱلۡقِسۡطِۖ﴾, "for Allah" first.
**Key**: An-Nisa puts justice first because the context is testimony against oneself and relatives; Al-Ma'idah puts Allah first because the context is fairness to enemies. See ["Let Not Hatred of a People Lead You to Injustice": Quran 5:8 Explained](/blog/justice-even-with-enemies-5-8-meaning-english/).

### 4. Tayammum in An-Nisa and Al-Ma'idah
An-Nisa 43 reads ﴿فَٱمۡسَحُواْ بِوُجُوهِكُمۡ وَأَيۡدِيكُمۡۗ﴾ without *minhu* ("from it"), while Al-Ma'idah 6 adds *minhu*.

## How to memorize Juz 5 with understanding

1. **The whole juz is one surah**, so rely on the map to know where you are.
2. **An-Nisa's verse endings are similar** (*hakima*, *'alima*, *rahima*, *khabira*). Link each ending to its verse: legal verses tend to end with knowledge and wisdom, verses on repentance with forgiveness and mercy.
3. **Learn the verse of ten rights in order** and count them on your fingers.
4. **For the fear-prayer verses**, read how the prayer is performed in a short fiqh book first.
5. **Memorize the hypocrites' traits at the end as one unit**, and link them to their traits at the start of Al-Baqarah.
6. **Revise with Juz 4**, since it is the same surah.

For technique see [How to Memorize the Quran Step by Step](/blog/how-to-memorize-quran-step-by-step/).

## Practical lessons from Juz 5

- **Do not wish for what others have**; ask Allah of His bounty.
- **Avoid the major sins**, and Allah will remove the lesser ones.
- **In marital conflict, look for two arbitrators** and intend reconciliation.
- **Fulfil the ten rights**, from parents to those under your care.
- **Render trusts**: at work, with money and with secrets.
- **Refer disputes to Allah and His Messenger.**
- **Reflect on the Quran**; there is no contradiction in it.
- **Return greetings with better ones.**
- **Verify before judging people.**
- **Testify to the truth even against yourself.**
- **Beware laziness in prayer**: it is a trait of the hypocrites.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is Juz 5 called "Wal-Muhsanat"?
Because it begins with ﴿۞وَٱلۡمُحۡصَنَٰتُ مِنَ ٱلنِّسَآءِ﴾.

### What is the verse of ten rights?
An-Nisa 4:36. It begins with Allah's right to be worshipped alone, then parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbour, the distant neighbour, the companion at one's side, the traveller, and those under one's authority.

### Where does Juz 5 end?
At An-Nisa 4:147. Juz 6 begins with ﴿۞لَّا يُحِبُّ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡجَهۡرَ بِٱلسُّوٓءِ مِنَ ٱلۡقَوۡلِ﴾, "Allah does not like the public mention of evil."

### At which verse did the Prophet ﷺ weep?
At ﴿فَكَيۡفَ إِذَا جِئۡنَا مِن كُلِّ أُمَّةِۭ بِشَهِيدٖ وَجِئۡنَا بِكَ عَلَىٰ هَـٰٓؤُلَآءِ شَهِيدٗا﴾ (4:41), as reported in Al-Bukhari and Muslim from Ibn Mas'ud.

### Does Juz 5 contain rulings on prayer?
Yes: not approaching prayer while intoxicated, tayammum, shortening prayer while travelling, the fear prayer, and the principle that prayer has fixed times.

## Conclusion

Juz 5 is a charter of complete justice: at home between spouses, in society with neighbours and the weak, in government between rulers and ruled, in court between insiders and outsiders, in war while protecting life, greetings and prayer, and finally within the self through honest testimony. Read with this measure, it shows why An-Nisa is one of the great surahs of law.
