Surah Al-Fil tells, in five verses, a story the Arabs all knew. Some of the people of Mecca had witnessed it themselves when the surah was revealed: a great army came with an elephant to destroy the Ka'bah, and Allah sent birds in flocks that pelted it with stones of baked clay, leaving it like straw eaten by cattle. On the well-known view of the historians, the Prophet ﷺ was born in that very year.

This guide explains the surah verse by verse in English, tells the story as the commentators and historians relate it, and draws out its lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Fil
- **Number of verses**: 5.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 105, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: after "the companions of the elephant" in its first verse.

## The Full Text of Surah Al-Fil
﴿أَلَمۡ تَرَ كَيۡفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِأَصۡحَٰبِ ٱلۡفِيلِ﴾ ﴿أَلَمۡ يَجۡعَلۡ كَيۡدَهُمۡ فِي تَضۡلِيلٖ﴾ ﴿وَأَرۡسَلَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ طَيۡرًا أَبَابِيلَ﴾ ﴿تَرۡمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٖ مِّن سِجِّيلٖ﴾ ﴿فَجَعَلَهُمۡ كَعَصۡفٖ مَّأۡكُولِۭ﴾ (Quran 105:1-5)

**Meaning:** Have you not seen how your Lord dealt with the companions of the elephant? Did He not make their plan go astray? And He sent against them birds in flocks, striking them with stones of baked clay, and He made them like eaten straw.

## The Story as Told by the Commentators and Historians
Ibn Kathir and other commentators, together with historians such as Ibn Ishaq, give this summary:
1. **The church in San'a'.** Abraha al-Ashram, the governor of Yemen on behalf of the king of Abyssinia, built a great church in San'a' and wanted to divert the Arabs' pilgrimage away from the Ka'bah to it.
2. **The Arabs' anger.** The Arabs were angered, and it is reported that one of them defiled the church. Abraha swore to destroy the Ka'bah.
3. **The march.** He set out with a large army and one or more elephants, an animal the Arabs had never used in war.
4. **'Abd al-Muttalib and his camels.** Near Mecca the army seized camels belonging to 'Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet's grandfather. He went to Abraha to ask for them back. Abraha was surprised that he asked about his camels and not about the Ka'bah, and 'Abd al-Muttalib gave his famous answer: "I am the owner of the camels, and the House has a Lord who will protect it."
5. **Mecca empties.** The people of Mecca withdrew to the mountain passes, unable to resist such an army.
6. **The elephant kneels.** When the elephant was turned towards Mecca, it knelt and refused to move; when turned in another direction, it went quickly.
7. **The birds.** Allah sent flocks of birds, each carrying small stones, which they dropped on the army and destroyed it. Abraha himself died afterwards.

### What is confirmed in authentic hadith
In Sahih al-Bukhari, in the account of al-Hudaybiyah, the Prophet's camel knelt and people said she had become stubborn. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Al-Qaswa' has not become stubborn, nor is that her nature; rather, the One who held back the elephant held her back." And in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that on the day of the conquest of Mecca the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah held back the elephant from Mecca, and gave authority over it to His Messenger and the believers." These two hadiths confirm the core of the story: Allah held back the elephant from Mecca.

## Verse 1: Have You Not Seen?
﴿أَلَمۡ تَرَ كَيۡفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِأَصۡحَٰبِ ٱلۡفِيلِ﴾ (Quran 105:1)

**"Have you not seen"**: have you not come to know, with knowledge as certain as sight? The event was so famous among the Arabs, and some of them had seen it, that it was like something witnessed. **"How your Lord dealt"**: not "what" He did but "how", because the wonder is in the manner: small birds destroying a mighty army. **"The companions of the elephant"**: named after the elephant, as if it were the most prominent thing about them and what they relied on most; yet it was the first to fail them.

## Verse 2: Their Plan Went Astray
﴿أَلَمۡ يَجۡعَلۡ كَيۡدَهُمۡ فِي تَضۡلِيلٖ﴾ (Quran 105:2)

Did He not make their scheme to destroy the Ka'bah end in loss and failure? They did not achieve what they wanted; their effort was wasted and they were destroyed. As Surah Fatir puts it: "Evil plotting encompasses only its own people."

## Verses 3–4: Birds in Flocks, Stones of Baked Clay
﴿وَأَرۡسَلَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ طَيۡرًا أَبَابِيلَ﴾ ﴿تَرۡمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٖ مِّن سِجِّيلٖ﴾ (Quran 105:3-4)

**"Birds in flocks"** (*tayran ababil*): groups of birds following one another. *Ababil* is a plural with no singular, meaning scattered flocks. **"Stones of sijjil"**: stones of hardened, baked clay, as Allah explains in the story of the people of Lut in Surah Adh-Dhariyat: "stones of clay".

## Verse 5: Like Eaten Straw
﴿فَجَعَلَهُمۡ كَعَصۡفٖ مَّأۡكُولِۭ﴾ (Quran 105:5)

He made them like dry leaves of crops that cattle have eaten and trampled, broken and scattered. *'Asf* is the leaves or straw of grain. The image shows complete destruction and humiliation: an army that came in pride with its elephant ended like the leftovers of fodder.

## Word Meanings
- **Alam tara**: have you not seen, come to know with certainty.
- **Ashab al-fil**: the companions of the elephant, Abraha's army.
- **Kaydahum**: their plot, their scheme.
- **Tadlil**: going astray, failure.
- **Ababil**: flocks, groups following one another.
- **Sijjil**: baked clay.
- **'Asf**: straw, dry leaves of crops.
- **Ma'kul**: eaten by cattle.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **A rhetorical question, "Have you not seen?"**, affirming something the listeners knew.
2. **"Your Lord"**: linking the Lord to the Prophet ﷺ honours him, and hints that protecting the House prepared the way for his mission.
3. **"Birds" left indefinite**: small in size, great in effect.
4. **The contrast**: the largest land animal defeated by some of the smallest flying creatures.
5. **The closing simile** paints total destruction.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Izhar shafawi** in *alam tara* and *alam yaj'al*: the meem is clear before *ta* and *ya*; and in *kaydahum fi*, with care to keep the meem clear before *fa*.
- **Ikhfa shafawi** in *tarmihim bi-hijarah*: the meem is hidden before *ba* with ghunnah.
- **Ikhfa** in *min sijjil*: the nun sakinah hidden before *sin*.
- **Izhar** in *tayran ababil*: the tanween clear before hamza.
- **Idgham with ghunnah** in *ka-'asfin ma'kul*: tanween merges into meem.
- **Qalqalah sughra** in *yaj'al*: the *jim* with sukun.
- **Madd 'arid** when stopping on *al-fil*, *tadlil*, *ababil*, *sijjil* and *ma'kul*.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before Al-Fil comes Al-Humazah, about the one who "gathered wealth and counted it, thinking his wealth would make him immortal". Al-Fil shows another man deceived by his power and army, destroyed by Allah. After it comes Quraysh, which begins "For the security of Quraysh", and many commentators link the two: Allah destroyed the people of the elephant so that Quraysh would remain safe in Mecca and their journeys.

## The Year of the Elephant and the Birth of the Prophet ﷺ
The well-known view of the historians is that the Prophet ﷺ was born in the Year of the Elephant. Scholars see wisdom in this: Allah protected His House shortly before the birth of the Prophet ﷺ who would one day cleanse it of idols, and after the event the Arabs held Quraysh in greater honour, calling them "the people of Allah, for whom Allah fought". The event prepared the ground for the mission.

## Lessons from the Story
1. **Allah protects His House.** A Muslim should not fear for Allah's religion; he should fear for himself, whether he will be among its supporters.
2. **Material power is not everything.** An army with an elephant was defeated by birds.
3. **Reliance after taking the means.** 'Abd al-Muttalib said "the House has a Lord who will protect it". In Islam, reliance on Allah does not mean abandoning the means; the Prophet ﷺ prepared at Badr and the Trench, then relied on Allah. The Year of the Elephant was a special case in which Allah showed His power as a sign before the mission.
4. **Plots return on their makers.**
5. **Blessings call for gratitude.** That is why Surah Quraysh follows, commanding worship of the Lord of the House.

## Believing in Miracles
Some people try to explain the story in purely material terms, suggesting the birds were microbes or an epidemic. The sounder approach is to accept the apparent meaning of the Quran: real birds, real stones. The One who created the laws of nature can suspend them. It is telling that Quraysh never denied this surah when it was revealed, though they were eager to deny the Prophet ﷺ, because they knew the event for certain.

## The Ka'bah: A Protected House
Allah protected the Ka'bah from the people of the elephant and made it "a standing for the people" (5:97). Among its virtues: it is the first House established for mankind (3:96); it is the qiblah of Muslims everywhere; it is the destination of Hajj and 'Umrah, and in al-Bukhari and Muslim the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever performs Hajj for Allah without obscenity or wrongdoing returns like the day his mother bore him"; and it is a sanctuary where trees are not cut and game is not disturbed.

## Practical Lessons
1. Trust Allah's support for His religion.
2. Do not be deceived by your strength or wealth.
3. Beware of plotting against people.
4. Take the means, then rely on Allah.
5. Thank Allah for the blessing of security.
6. Honour the House of Allah and long to visit it.

## Teaching Children
- Tell the story with pictures of the elephant and the birds.
- Focus on the message: Allah is stronger than every power.
- Teach them 'Abd al-Muttalib's words, explaining that complete tawhid came with Islam.

## The Soldiers of Allah in the Quran
The birds of Surah Al-Fil are one example of the soldiers of Allah that no one knows but Him: "None knows the soldiers of your Lord except Him" (74:31). The Quran mentions others: the wind that destroyed 'Ad and scattered the Confederates; the water that drowned the people of Nuh and Pharaoh's army; the blast that destroyed Thamud; the angels who supported the believers at Badr. A believer never measures Allah's power by human standards, and never despairs of His help however strong falsehood appears.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Who were the people of the elephant?
The army of Abraha al-Ashram, the Abyssinian governor of Yemen, who came to destroy the Ka'bah.

### What does "tayran ababil" mean?
Birds in flocks, groups following one another.

### What does "stones of sijjil" mean?
Stones of hardened, baked clay.

### Is the story confirmed in authentic hadith?
Yes. Al-Bukhari reports "the One who held back the elephant", and al-Bukhari and Muslim report "Allah held back the elephant from Mecca".

### When did it happen?
In the year the Prophet ﷺ was born, according to the well-known view of the historians, called the Year of the Elephant.

### What is the link between Al-Fil and Quraysh?
Many commentators connect them: the people of the elephant were destroyed so that Quraysh would remain secure.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Fil?
5 verses.

### How many elephants were in Abraha's army?
Reports differ: one great elephant or several. The Quran uses the singular "the elephant", and the lesson is in the event, not the number.

### Why did Allah protect the Ka'bah when the people of Mecca were idolaters?
Because it is His House, and protecting it prepared the way for the mission of the Prophet ﷺ who would cleanse it of idols.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Fil reminds you that however great the power of the oppressors, Allah has soldiers no one sees. An army with an elephant became like eaten straw through small birds. Do not fear for Allah's religion; fear instead being far from it. And put your trust in Allah, as 'Abd al-Muttalib said: the House has a Lord who will protect it.
