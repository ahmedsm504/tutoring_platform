Have you ever asked a friend: "Can I borrow your pencil?" and they said "No!" even though they had five? Or seen someone push away a child who just wanted to play? These seem like small things, but Allah talks about them in a whole surah: Surah Al-Ma'un. It describes a person whose belief in the Day of Judgement is not real, and how it shows: he is unkind to orphans, doesn't care about hungry people, is careless about prayer, shows off, and refuses even small kindnesses.

Al-Ma'un means "small kindnesses" or "everyday things people lend each other", like a pot, a bucket, salt or a needle. The surah teaches children that real faith shows in how we treat people, especially the weak, and in sincere prayer. In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Al-Ma'un to children in simple English, with practical ways to act on it, activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Ma'un

- **Position**: surah 107, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: seven.
- **Type**: scholars differ; many say the first part is Meccan and the second Madinan.
- **Name**: al-Ma'un means small, everyday items or kindnesses that people share.

## The Whole Surah

﴿أَرَءَيۡتَ ٱلَّذِي يُكَذِّبُ بِٱلدِّينِ﴾ ﴿فَذَٰلِكَ ٱلَّذِي يَدُعُّ ٱلۡيَتِيمَ﴾ ﴿وَلَا يَحُضُّ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ ٱلۡمِسۡكِينِ﴾ ﴿فَوَيۡلࣱ لِّلۡمُصَلِّينَ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ هُمۡ عَن صَلَاتِهِمۡ سَاهُونَ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ هُمۡ يُرَآءُونَ﴾ ﴿وَيَمۡنَعُونَ ٱلۡمَاعُونَ﴾ (Quran 107:1-7)

## Verse 1: Have You Seen the One Who Denies the Judgement?

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah asks: Have you seen the person who denies ad-Din, the Day of Judgement and the reward for good and bad deeds? Allah is about to show us what such a person looks like, not by what he says, but by what he does."

### Lesson for kids: faith shows in actions
"Anyone can say 'I believe'. But real belief shows in how we behave. If someone truly believes they will meet Allah, they will be kind, honest and caring."

## Verse 2: He Pushes Away the Orphan

### Explaining it to your child
"An orphan is a child whose father has died. Yadu'u means to push away roughly. The person in this surah is harsh with orphans: he pushes them away, ignores them and doesn't give them their rights."

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said: "I and the one who looks after an orphan will be in Paradise like this," and he held up his index and middle fingers, slightly apart (al-Bukhari). The Prophet ﷺ himself was an orphan: his father died before he was born, and his mother died when he was six.

### Activity: kindness to orphans
- If there is an orphan in your child's class, encourage them to be a good friend, include them in games and share with them.
- As a family, consider sponsoring an orphan through a trustworthy charity, and let your child contribute part of their pocket money.
- Teach your child: never tease anyone about their family situation.

## Verse 3: He Doesn't Encourage Feeding the Poor

### Explaining it to your child
"This person not only doesn't feed hungry people himself, he doesn't even encourage others to do it. He just doesn't care. A believer cares about hungry people and encourages everyone to help."

### Activity: the family food drive
Organise a mini food drive with your child: they make a poster, ask family members and neighbours to donate tins and dry food, and then deliver the box together to a food bank or mosque. They are "encouraging others to feed the poor", the opposite of verse 3.

## Verses 4–5: Woe to Those Who Are Careless About Prayer

### Explaining it to your child
"Woe to those who pray but are careless about their prayer: they keep delaying it until its time almost runs out, or rush through it without paying attention. Notice: Allah didn't say 'those who forget something in prayer', because everyone forgets sometimes. He said those who are careless about it, who don't value it."

### Tip for parents
Explain gently that this is not about a child who sometimes forgets a rak'ah. It's about not caring. Encourage your child to pray on time and calmly.

### Activity: on-time prayer chart
Make a chart with the five prayers for each day of the week. Every prayer prayed on time gets a green tick. At the end of the week, celebrate the green ticks, without shaming any gaps.

## Verse 6: Those Who Show Off

### Explaining it to your child
"Some people do good things only so others will see and praise them. That's called riya', showing off. They pray nicely when people are watching and carelessly when alone. Allah wants us to do good for Him, not to impress people."

### Lesson for kids: sincerity
"Do good deeds even when nobody sees, like tidying your room when no one asked, or giving charity quietly. Allah sees, and that's what matters."

### Activity: the secret good deed
Challenge your child to do one secret good deed every day this week, something nobody knows about except Allah. At the end of the week, they can tell you what they did, or keep it secret between them and Allah!

## Verse 7: They Refuse Small Kindnesses

### Explaining it to your child
"Al-ma'un are small, everyday things people lend each other: a pot, a bucket, salt, sugar, a pencil, an eraser. The person in this surah refuses to share even these tiny things. If someone won't share a pencil, how will they share bigger things?"

### Lesson for kids: small kindnesses matter
"Lending your eraser, sharing your crayons, letting someone have a turn on the swing, helping a classmate find their page: these are all ma'un. They are small, but Allah mentions them in the Quran!"

### Activity: the kindness jar
Keep a jar at home. Every time a family member does a small kindness, like sharing, lending or helping, they put a pebble in the jar. When the jar is full, the family has a special treat together.

## The Two Halves of the Surah

Scholars noticed that the surah has two parts:
- **Verses 1–3**: how we treat **people**: orphans and the poor.
- **Verses 4–7**: how we treat **Allah**: prayer and sincerity, then back to people with small kindnesses.

### Explaining it to your child
"A good Muslim is good with Allah and good with people. Prayer without kindness is not enough, and kindness without prayer is not enough. We need both!"

## How the Companions Lived This Surah

The companions understood that small kindnesses were part of faith. Ibn Mas'ud explained al-ma'un as the everyday things people lend each other, like a bucket or a cooking pot. In their neighbourhoods, people borrowed salt, fire and water from each other, and refusing such small things was considered very poor character.

### Explaining it to your child
"In the time of the Prophet ﷺ, neighbours were always lending each other things: a pot to cook in, a bucket to get water, a little salt. Today it might be a phone charger, a pencil, an umbrella or a cup of sugar. Being generous with small things makes a whole neighbourhood feel like a family."

### Activity: the neighbour list
Make a list with your child of small things you could offer neighbours: help carrying shopping, lending tools, sharing extra food, watering plants when they travel. Then choose one to do this week, together.

### Activity: classroom ma'un
Before school, help your child pack one "spare" item to share, like an extra pencil or eraser, so they're ready if a classmate needs one. They become the classroom's helper, living verse 7 every day.

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Pencil Case"

Yusuf had the best pencil case in the class: twenty coloured pencils, three erasers, a fancy sharpener and gel pens in every colour. When his classmate Daniel, a quiet boy who had recently lost his father, asked: "Can I borrow a blue pencil?", Yusuf said quickly: "No, you'll break it."

Daniel turned away, looking sad, and coloured his sky in grey pencil.

That evening, Yusuf's mother was teaching him Surah Al-Ma'un. When she reached "wa yamna'unal-ma'un", she explained: "This means they refuse even small things, like lending a pot or a pencil." Yusuf went quiet. Then she read "fa dhalikal-ladhi yadu'ul-yatim" and explained that being harsh with orphans is something Allah dislikes very much.

Yusuf's face turned red. "Mum, today Daniel asked me for a pencil and I said no. And his dad died last month."

His mother hugged him. "It's good that you noticed. What can you do about it?"

The next morning, Yusuf put his pencil case on Daniel's desk. "Sorry about yesterday. You can use any colour you like." Daniel smiled shyly and picked the blue pencil. At break, Yusuf asked Daniel to play football with him and his friends. By the end of the week, they were sitting together every day.

Ask your child: "Why did Yusuf feel bad? Which verses made him change? What small kindness can you do tomorrow?"

## What We Learn From Surah Al-Ma'un

- **Real faith** shows in our actions.
- **Be kind to orphans**; the Prophet ﷺ was an orphan too.
- **Care about hungry people** and encourage others to help.
- **Pray on time** and with attention.
- **Do good for Allah**, not to show off.
- **Share small things**: pencils, toys and turns.
- **Be good with Allah** and good with people.

## One-Week Plan: Memorize and Act

- **Day 1**: memorize verses 1–3 and talk about orphans.
- **Day 2**: memorize verses 4–5 and start the prayer chart.
- **Day 3**: memorize verses 6–7.
- **Day 4**: recite the whole surah and begin the secret good deed challenge.
- **Day 5**: start the kindness jar.
- **Day 6**: organise the mini food drive.
- **Day 7**: recite Al-Ma'un in prayer and celebrate!

## Questions to Ask Your Child

1. What does "al-Ma'un" mean?
2. How does the person in the surah treat orphans?
3. What does the Prophet ﷺ say about those who care for orphans?
4. What does "careless about prayer" mean?
5. What is showing off, and why is it bad?
6. Give three examples of small kindnesses.
7. What are the two halves of the surah about?

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### My child sometimes forgets a rak'ah. Is that what the surah warns about?
No. Scholars explain that the warning is about being careless and not valuing prayer, delaying it without reason or not caring about it, not about honest forgetfulness, which everyone experiences.

### How do I teach sincerity without making my child anxious about showing off?
Encourage secret good deeds and praise their intentions. Explain that feeling happy when someone notices your good deed is natural; what matters is that you did it for Allah.

### Is it okay to praise my child publicly for good deeds?
Yes, encouragement is good. Just balance it with helping them enjoy good deeds nobody sees, so their motivation comes from Allah.


### Which verse should I start with?
Start with verse 7 about small kindnesses, because children can practise it the same day.
