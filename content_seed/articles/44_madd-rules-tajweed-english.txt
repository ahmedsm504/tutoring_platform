**Madd** means elongation: stretching a vowel sound for a set length. Getting Madd right is one of the biggest differences between ordinary reading and beautiful, correct recitation. In this guide we follow the recitation of **Hafs from ʿAsim**, via the Shatibiyyah route, which is the most widespread recitation in the world today.

## What Madd is, and what causes it
Linguistically, *madd* means stretching or increasing. In Tajweed it means **lengthening the sound of a Madd letter**. Its opposite is *qaṣr*: keeping the Madd letter at its natural length with no extra stretch.

All Madd falls into two big groups:
- **Original Madd** (*aṣlī*): needs no cause. This is the natural Madd of two counts.
- **Secondary Madd** (*farʿī*): longer than natural because of a cause. There are only two causes: a **hamza** or a **sukoon** coming after the Madd letter.

This one idea answers most beginners' questions. At every Madd letter, ask: is a hamza after it? Is a sukoon after it? If neither, it is natural: two counts. If a hamza comes *before* it, it is Badal: also two counts.

## What is a "count" (ḥaraka)?
Madd is measured in **counts** (*harakāt*). One count is roughly the time it takes to fold or unfold one finger at a normal pace. What matters most is **consistency**: if a Madd is two counts, it should always be two counts.

## The three Madd letters
- **Alif** (ا) after a fatha: ﴿قَالَ﴾ (*qāla*)
- **Waw** (و) with sukoon after a damma: ﴿يَقُولُ﴾ (*yaqūlu*)
- **Yaa** (ي) with sukoon after a kasra: ﴿قِيلَ﴾ (*qīla*)

## 1. Natural Madd (Madd Ṭabīʿī): 2 counts
The basic elongation, with no hamza or sukoon after the Madd letter. Always **2 counts**: ﴿نُوحِيهَآ﴾, ﴿قَالُوا﴾.

Also 2 counts:
- **Madd Badal:** hamza comes *before* the Madd letter: ﴿ءَامَنُوا﴾, ﴿أُوتُوا﴾.
- **Madd ʿIwaḍ:** stopping on a word ending with tanween fatha: stop on ﴿عَلِيمًا﴾ as *ʿalīmā*.
- **Small Silah:** the pronoun "hu/hi" between two vowelled letters: ﴿إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ﴾ (*innahū kāna*).

### A Madd letter before a silent letter in the next word
If a Madd letter ends a word and the next word starts with a silent letter, **the Madd letter is dropped in pronunciation** when you join them, and there is no Madd at all. For example ﴿قَالُواْ ٱلۡحَمۡدُ﴾ is read *qālul-ḥamd*, and ﴿فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ﴾ is read *fis-samāwāt*. If you stop on the first word, its natural Madd returns. Beginners often stretch the yaa of *fī* here, which is a mistake.

## 2. Madd because of hamza
### Madd Muttaṣil (connected): 4 or 5 counts
The Madd letter and the hamza are **in the same word**: ﴿جَآءَ﴾, ﴿ٱلسَّمَآءِ﴾, ﴿سُوٓءَ﴾. It's obligatory to lengthen it: **4 or 5 counts**, and up to 6 when you stop on a word ending with hamza.

### Madd Munfaṣil (separated): 4 or 5 counts
The Madd letter ends one word and the hamza starts the next: ﴿إِنَّآ أَعۡطَيۡنَٰكَ﴾, ﴿قُوٓا أَنفُسَكُمۡ﴾. For Hafs via Shatibiyyah: **4 or 5 counts**.

### Big Silah: 4 or 5 counts
The pronoun "hu/hi" between two vowelled letters, followed by a hamza: ﴿مَالَهُۥٓ أَخۡلَدَهُۥ﴾.

### Exceptions in the pronoun "hu/hi" for Hafs
The rule is that the pronoun haa between two voweled letters is extended into a waw or yaa sound. Hafs has a few well-known exceptions worth memorising:
- ﴿وَيَخۡلُدۡ فِيهِۦ مُهَانًا﴾ in Al-Furqan: the haa **is** extended, even though the letter before it is silent.
- ﴿يَرۡضَهُ لَكُمۡ﴾ in Az-Zumar: the haa is **not** extended, only a short damma, even though it sits between voweled letters.
- ﴿أَرۡجِهۡ﴾ in Al-A'raf and Ash-Shu'ara', and ﴿فَأَلۡقِهۡ﴾ in An-Naml: the haa is read **silent**.

**Rule of thumb:** pick one length (say 4) and stick with it throughout your recitation. Never make Munfasil longer than Muttasil.

## 3. Madd because of sukoon
### Madd ʿĀriḍ lis-sukūn (due to stopping): 2, 4 or 6 counts
When you **stop** on a word whose last letter comes after a Madd letter, that letter becomes silent and you may elongate **2, 4 or 6 counts**: stopping on ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾, ﴿نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾.

### Madd Līn: 2, 4 or 6 counts (when stopping)
Waw or Yaa with sukoon after a **fatha** (the "soft" letters), when you stop on the next letter: ﴿خَوۡفٍۭ﴾, ﴿ٱلۡبَيۡتِ﴾. No elongation when you continue reading.

### Madd Lāzim (compulsory): 6 counts
A Madd letter followed by a **permanent sukoon** (present whether you stop or continue). Always **6 counts**:
- In a word, with a shadda: ﴿ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾, ﴿ٱلۡحَآقَّةُ﴾
- In the opening letters of surahs: the Laam and Meem in ﴿الٓمٓ﴾, and ﴿قٓ﴾, ﴿صٓ﴾, ﴿نٓ﴾. The letter ʿAyn (in ﴿كٓهيعٓصٓ﴾) may be 4 or 6.

### Madd Farq: 6 counts
This occurs where the question hamza enters a word starting with the definite article. The connecting hamza is turned into a long alif stretched **6 counts**, like Madd Lazim, to distinguish a question from a statement. It appears in three words in six places: ﴿ءَآللَّهُ﴾ (Yunus and An-Naml), ﴿ءَآلذَّكَرَيۡنِ﴾ (twice in Al-An'am) and ﴿ءَآلۡـَٰٔنَ﴾ (twice in Yunus). Hafs also has a second option here: **easing** (*tas-hīl*) the second hamza between a hamza and an alif, with no elongation.

### Madd Tamkin
Two yaas, the first doubled with kasra and the second a silent Madd yaa, as in ﴿حُيِّيتُم﴾ and ﴿ٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَ﴾. It is really a **natural Madd** of two counts. The name means "firming": the reciter gives the doubled yaa its full weight, then stretches the second, so neither is swallowed.

### The opening letters of surahs
The fourteen separated letters at the start of surahs fall into three groups:
| Group | Letters | Madd | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| No Madd | alif | none | the alif in ﴿الٓمٓ﴾ is just *alif* |
| Natural Madd | the five letters of *ḥayy ṭahur*: ح ي ط ه ر | 2 counts | *hā* and *yā* in ﴿كٓهيعٓصٓ﴾ |
| Madd Lazim | the eight letters of *naqaṣa ʿasalukum*: ن ق ص ع س ل ك م | 6 counts (ʿAyn 4 or 6) | ﴿قٓ﴾, ﴿نٓ﴾, the *sīn* in ﴿طسٓمٓ﴾ |

The reason: the names of *ḥayy ṭahur* letters have two sounds ending in a Madd (*ḥā*, *yā*), so nothing silent follows the Madd. The names of *naqaṣa ʿasalukum* letters have three sounds with a Madd in the middle and a silent letter at the end (*nūn*, *qāf*), so a permanent sukoon follows the Madd, making it Lazim. ʿAyn has a soft letter in the middle (*ʿayn*), not a Madd letter, which is why 4 counts is also allowed.

## When two causes meet: the stronger one wins
Sometimes one Madd letter has two causes. You apply **the stronger** and drop the weaker. From strongest to weakest:
1. **Lazim**
2. **Muttasil**
3. **ʿĀriḍ lis-sukūn**
4. **Munfasil**
5. **Badal**

Examples:
- Stopping on ﴿ٱلسَّمَآءِ﴾: it has Muttasil and ʿĀriḍ, so you stretch **4, 5 or 6 counts**, never 2, because Muttasil is stronger than ʿĀriḍ.
- ﴿وَلَآ ءَآمِّينَ ٱلۡبَيۡتَ﴾: ﴿ءَآمِّينَ﴾ has Badal and Lazim, so it is stretched **6 counts**.

## Short, medium and full
Teachers describe lengths with three words you will hear often:
- **Qaṣr** (short): 2 counts.
- **Tawassuṭ** (medium): 4 counts.
- **Fuwayq at-tawassuṭ** (a little above medium): 5 counts.
- **Ishbāʿ** or **ṭūl** (full): 6 counts.

So "read the ʿĀriḍ with tawassuṭ" means 4 counts, and "give the Lazim ishbāʿ" means 6.

## Worked examples
### Surah Al-Fatiha
- ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ﴾: a small alif after the meem, **natural**, 2 counts.
- ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ when stopping: a Madd yaa before a meem made silent by the stop, **ʿĀriḍ**, 2, 4 or 6.
- ﴿مَٰلِكِ يَوۡمِ ٱلدِّينِ﴾: the alif of ﴿مَٰلِكِ﴾ is **natural**; the waw of ﴿يَوۡمِ﴾ is a soft (Līn) letter, not stretched when joining; the yaa of ﴿ٱلدِّينِ﴾ is **ʿĀriḍ** when you stop.
- ﴿نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾ when stopping: **ʿĀriḍ**.
- ﴿وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾: an alif followed by a doubled laam in one word, **Lazim (heavy, in a word)**, 6 counts; then the final yaa is **ʿĀriḍ** when stopping.

**Consistency in Al-Fatiha:** if you stop at the end of each verse, choose one length for ʿĀriḍ for the whole surah: 2 everywhere, or 4 everywhere, or 6 everywhere. Stretching ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ to 6 and ﴿ٱلدِّينِ﴾ to 2 is a mistake.

### Ayat al-Kursi
- ﴿ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ﴾: in ﴿لَآ إِلَٰهَ﴾ an alif ends one word and a hamza starts the next, **Munfasil**, 4 or 5.
- ﴿لَا تَأۡخُذُهُۥ سِنَةࣱ﴾: the pronoun haa between voweled letters, **small Silah**, 2 counts.
- ﴿مَن ذَا ٱلَّذِي﴾: the alif of ﴿ذَا﴾ drops when joining, because a silent letter follows.
- ﴿وَهُوَ ٱلۡعَلِيُّ ٱلۡعَظِيمُ﴾ when stopping: **ʿĀriḍ** in ﴿ٱلۡعَظِيمُ﴾.

### Surah Al-Kawthar and Surah Al-Humazah
- ﴿إِنَّآ أَعۡطَيۡنَٰكَ﴾: **Munfasil**, 4 or 5; then the small alif in ﴿أَعۡطَيۡنَٰكَ﴾ is **natural**.
- ﴿يَحۡسَبُ أَنَّ مَالَهُۥٓ أَخۡلَدَهُۥ﴾: the alif in ﴿مَالَهُۥٓ﴾ is **natural**, then the pronoun haa is extended before a hamza, **big Silah**, 4 or 5 like Munfasil.

## Common mistakes
1. Stretching a natural Madd beyond 2 counts.
2. Inconsistent lengths in the same recitation.
3. Shortening Madd Lazim, for example a short *ad-dāllīn*.
4. Elongating Līn letters while continuing (not stopping).

5. Stretching a Madd letter before a silent letter when joining, as in ﴿فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ﴾.
6. Shortening Muttasil to 2 when stopping on a word like ﴿ٱلسَّمَآءِ﴾. It should be at least 4.
7. Neglecting the ʿAyn in ﴿كٓهيعٓصٓ﴾ and ﴿عٓسٓقٓ﴾, or giving it only 2 counts.
8. Extending the pronoun haa where Hafs does not, as in ﴿يَرۡضَهُ لَكُمۡ﴾.

## A two-week practice plan
- **Days 1-2**: natural Madd only. Recite Al-Ikhlas and count two on your finger for every Madd, no more.
- **Days 3-4**: Muttasil and Munfasil. Choose one length, say 4, and recite An-Nasr and Al-Kawthar with it everywhere.
- **Days 5-6**: ʿĀriḍ. Recite Al-Fatiha three times: once with 2 at every verse end, once with 4, once with 6.
- **Day 7**: Līn. In Surah Quraysh, stop on ﴿ٱلۡبَيۡتِ﴾ and ﴿خَوۡفِۭ﴾ with each of the three lengths.
- **Days 8-9**: Lazim. Recite the openings of Al-Baqarah, Maryam, Ash-Shura and Al-Qalam, giving the *naqaṣa ʿasalukum* letters 6 counts.
- **Days 10-11**: Badal and Silah. Recite Al-Humazah, watching the big Silah in ﴿مَالَهُۥٓ أَخۡلَدَهُۥ﴾.
- **Days 12-14**: take one full page of Juz Amma, label every Madd with its type and length before reciting, then record yourself and compare with a skilled reciter.

## Frequently asked questions
### How long is one count in seconds?
Counts are not measured in seconds, because they depend on your pace: slow, careful recitation is slower than fast recitation. What matters is proportion: a 6-count Madd is three times a 2-count Madd at the same pace.

### Can Hafs shorten Munfasil to 2 counts?
Shortening Munfasil is reported for Hafs via the Ṭayyibah route. Via Ash-Shāṭibiyyah, the route of most printed Mushafs, it is read with 4 or 5 counts. If you recite by the Shatibiyyah, do not shorten it.

### Why must Muttasil never be shorter than Munfasil?
Because in Muttasil the hamza is in the same word, so the cause is firmer and stronger. That is why, in a single recitation, Muttasil may equal Munfasil or exceed it, but never be shorter.

### What is the difference between Līn and a natural Madd?
A Madd letter is silent and preceded by its matching vowel: damma before waw, kasra before yaa. A Līn letter is a silent waw or yaa preceded by a **fatha**, as in ﴿خَوۡفٍۭ﴾ and ﴿ٱلۡبَيۡتِ﴾, and it is stretched only when you stop.

### Can I stretch ʿĀriḍ to 6 and Munfasil to 4?
Yes. They are different categories, and each has its own length. What matters is consistency within each category: every Munfasil the same length, and every ʿĀriḍ the same length in one sitting.
