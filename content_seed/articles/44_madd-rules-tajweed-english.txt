**Madd** means elongation: stretching a vowel sound for a set length. Getting Madd right is one of the biggest differences between ordinary reading and beautiful, correct recitation. In this guide we follow the recitation of **Hafs from ʿAsim**, via the Shatibiyyah route, which is the most widespread recitation in the world today.

## What is a "count" (ḥaraka)?
Madd is measured in **counts** (*harakāt*). One count is roughly the time it takes to fold or unfold one finger at a normal pace. What matters most is **consistency**: if a Madd is two counts, it should always be two counts.

## The three Madd letters
- **Alif** (ا) after a fatha: ﴿قَالَ﴾ (*qāla*)
- **Waw** (و) with sukoon after a damma: ﴿يَقُولُ﴾ (*yaqūlu*)
- **Yaa** (ي) with sukoon after a kasra: ﴿قِيلَ﴾ (*qīla*)

## 1. Natural Madd (Madd Ṭabīʿī): 2 counts
The basic elongation, with no hamza or sukoon after the Madd letter. Always **2 counts**: ﴿نُوحِيهَآ﴾, ﴿قَالُوا﴾.

Also 2 counts:
- **Madd Badal:** hamza comes *before* the Madd letter: ﴿ءَامَنُوا﴾, ﴿أُوتُوا﴾.
- **Madd ʿIwaḍ:** stopping on a word ending with tanween fatha: stop on ﴿عَلِيمًا﴾ as *ʿalīmā*.
- **Small Silah:** the pronoun "hu/hi" between two vowelled letters: ﴿إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ﴾ (*innahū kāna*).

## 2. Madd because of hamza
### Madd Muttaṣil (connected): 4 or 5 counts
The Madd letter and the hamza are **in the same word**: ﴿جَآءَ﴾, ﴿ٱلسَّمَآءِ﴾, ﴿سُوٓءَ﴾. It's obligatory to lengthen it: **4 or 5 counts**, and up to 6 when you stop on a word ending with hamza.

### Madd Munfaṣil (separated): 4 or 5 counts
The Madd letter ends one word and the hamza starts the next: ﴿إِنَّآ أَعۡطَيۡنَٰكَ﴾, ﴿قُوٓا أَنفُسَكُمۡ﴾. For Hafs via Shatibiyyah: **4 or 5 counts**.

### Big Silah: 4 or 5 counts
The pronoun "hu/hi" between two vowelled letters, followed by a hamza: ﴿مَالَهُۥٓ أَخۡلَدَهُۥ﴾.

**Rule of thumb:** pick one length (say 4) and stick with it throughout your recitation. Never make Munfasil longer than Muttasil.

## 3. Madd because of sukoon
### Madd ʿĀriḍ lis-sukūn (due to stopping): 2, 4 or 6 counts
When you **stop** on a word whose last letter comes after a Madd letter, that letter becomes silent and you may elongate **2, 4 or 6 counts**: stopping on ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾, ﴿نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾.

### Madd Līn: 2, 4 or 6 counts (when stopping)
Waw or Yaa with sukoon after a **fatha** (the "soft" letters), when you stop on the next letter: ﴿خَوۡفٍۭ﴾, ﴿ٱلۡبَيۡتِ﴾. No elongation when you continue reading.

### Madd Lāzim (compulsory): 6 counts
A Madd letter followed by a **permanent sukoon** (present whether you stop or continue). Always **6 counts**:
- In a word, with a shadda: ﴿ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾, ﴿ٱلۡحَآقَّةُ﴾
- In the opening letters of surahs: the Laam and Meem in ﴿الٓمٓ﴾, and ﴿قٓ﴾, ﴿صٓ﴾, ﴿نٓ﴾. The letter ʿAyn (in ﴿كٓهيعٓصٓ﴾) may be 4 or 6.

## Summary table
| Madd | Counts (Hafs, Shatibiyyah) |
|---|---|
| Natural, Badal, ʿIwaḍ, small Silah | 2 |
| Muttaṣil (connected) | 4 or 5 (6 when stopping on hamza) |
| Munfaṣil (separated), big Silah | 4 or 5 |
| ʿĀriḍ lis-sukūn, Līn (stopping) | 2, 4 or 6 |
| Lāzim | 6 |

## Common mistakes
1. Stretching a natural Madd beyond 2 counts.
2. Inconsistent lengths in the same recitation.
3. Shortening Madd Lazim, for example a short *ad-dāllīn*.
4. Elongating Līn letters while continuing (not stopping).
