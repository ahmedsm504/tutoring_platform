Surah An-Nazi'at opens with a scene most people never think about: the angels who come to draw out souls at the moment of death. From there it moves to the shaking of the earth on the Last Day, the hearts that tremble and the eyes that are lowered, and then to a story that shows what arrogance leads to: Pharaoh standing before his people and declaring, "I am your lord, the most high." The surah closes by dividing all people into two groups, and it gives the key to Paradise in one sentence: the one who feared standing before his Lord and restrained his soul from desire.

This guide explains Surah An-Nazi'at verse by verse in English, with authentic hadith from al-Bukhari and Muslim and practical lessons for daily life.

## Quick Facts About Surah An-Nazi'at
- **Number of verses**: 46.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 79, in the thirtieth juz (Juz 'Amma).
- **Name**: An-Nazi'at means "those who pull out", taken from its first verse.
- **Main theme**: the reality of the resurrection, shown through the moment of death, the Last Day, the story of Pharaoh and the signs of creation.

## The Central Message of the Surah
The surah answers people who doubted the resurrection. It does not argue in abstract terms; it shows them scenes. The angels taking souls, the earth quaking, a tyrant destroyed, the heavens raised and the earth spread out. Each scene says the same thing: the One who did all this can bring you back. And the conclusion is practical: since the Hour is coming, the only question that matters is which of the two groups you will be in.

## Verses 1–5: The Oaths
﴿وَٱلنَّـٰزِعَٰتِ غَرۡقٗا﴾ ﴿وَٱلنَّـٰشِطَٰتِ نَشۡطٗا﴾ ﴿وَٱلسَّـٰبِحَٰتِ سَبۡحٗا﴾ ﴿فَٱلسَّـٰبِقَٰتِ سَبۡقٗا﴾ ﴿فَٱلۡمُدَبِّرَٰتِ أَمۡرٗا﴾ (Quran 79:1-5)

Allah swears by five groups. Most commentators, including Ibn Kathir, explain them as angels:
1. **"Those who pull out violently"**: the angels who tear out the souls of the disbelievers with force.
2. **"Those who draw out gently"**: the angels who draw out the souls of the believers with ease, as water flows out of a jug.
3. **"Those who glide swiftly"**: the angels who descend and ascend between heaven and earth.
4. **"Those who race ahead"**: the angels who hurry to carry out Allah's commands.
5. **"Those who arrange each matter"**: the angels who manage what Allah has entrusted to them.

**Why begin with death?** Because death is the first resurrection a person will witness. Everyone doubts the Last Day until the angel of death arrives. The contrast between "violently" and "gently" is also a warning: the way your soul leaves your body depends on how you lived.

## Verses 6–14: The Two Blasts
﴿يَوۡمَ تَرۡجُفُ ٱلرَّاجِفَةُ﴾ ﴿تَتۡبَعُهَا ٱلرَّادِفَةُ﴾ ﴿قُلُوبࣱ يَوۡمَئِذٖ وَاجِفَةٌ﴾ ﴿أَبۡصَٰرُهَا خَٰشِعَةࣱ﴾ (Quran 79:6-9)

**"The quaking one"** (*ar-rajifah*) is the first blast of the Trumpet, at which everything dies. **"The one that follows"** (*ar-radifah*) is the second blast, at which everyone is raised. Between them, hearts are **"pounding"** with fear and eyes are **"humbled"**, unable to look up.

﴿يَقُولُونَ أَءِنَّا لَمَرۡدُودُونَ فِي ٱلۡحَافِرَةِ﴾ ﴿أَءِذَا كُنَّا عِظَٰمٗا نَّخِرَةٗ﴾ ﴿قَالُواْ تِلۡكَ إِذٗا كَرَّةٌ خَاسِرَةࣱ﴾ ﴿فَإِنَّمَا هِيَ زَجۡرَةࣱ وَٰحِدَةࣱ﴾ ﴿فَإِذَا هُم بِٱلسَّاهِرَةِ﴾ (Quran 79:10-14)

The disbelievers used to mock: "Will we really be returned to our former state, even after we are decayed bones? That would be a losing return!" Allah answers that it will take only **"a single shout"**, and suddenly they will be on **"the surface of the earth"** (*as-sahirah*), the open, flat land of the gathering. What they thought impossible will take a single moment.

## Verses 15–26: Musa and Pharaoh
﴿هَلۡ أَتَىٰكَ حَدِيثُ مُوسَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿إِذۡ نَادَىٰهُ رَبُّهُۥ بِٱلۡوَادِ ٱلۡمُقَدَّسِ طُوًى﴾ ﴿ٱذۡهَبۡ إِلَىٰ فِرۡعَوۡنَ إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَقُلۡ هَل لَّكَ إِلَىٰٓ أَن تَزَكَّىٰ﴾ ﴿وَأَهۡدِيَكَ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَتَخۡشَىٰ﴾ (Quran 79:15-19)

The story of Musa ﷺ is told more often in the Quran than any other, and here it is told briefly to comfort the Prophet ﷺ and warn those who rejected him. Allah called Musa in **"the sacred valley of Tuwa"** and sent him to Pharaoh **"who had transgressed"**.

**Notice the gentle invitation**: Musa was told to say, "Would you like to purify yourself, and let me guide you to your Lord so that you may fear Him?" This was addressed to the worst tyrant of his age, and still the words were soft and inviting. In Surah Taha Allah told Musa and Harun: "Speak to him gently, so that perhaps he may take heed or fear." If this was the manner for speaking to Pharaoh, how much more gentle should we be with ordinary people?

﴿فَأَرَىٰهُ ٱلۡأٓيَةَ ٱلۡكُبۡرَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَكَذَّبَ وَعَصَىٰ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ أَدۡبَرَ يَسۡعَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَحَشَرَ فَنَادَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَقَالَ أَنَا۠ رَبُّكُمُ ٱلۡأَعۡلَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَأَخَذَهُ ٱللَّهُ نَكَالَ ٱلۡأٓخِرَةِ وَٱلۡأُولَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَعِبۡرَةٗ لِّمَن يَخۡشَىٰٓ﴾ (Quran 79:20-26)

Musa showed him **"the greatest sign"**, the staff turning into a serpent and his hand shining white. Pharaoh **denied and disobeyed**, then turned away plotting, gathered his people and proclaimed: **"I am your lord, the most high."** So Allah seized him with **"the punishment of the hereafter and of the first life"**: drowning in this world and the Fire in the next.

**"Indeed in that is a lesson for whoever fears"**: the story is not history for its own sake. It is a lesson that only those who fear Allah will take. Arrogance can make a person claim what is not his, but Allah's seizure comes when He wills.

## Verses 27–33: Which Is Harder to Create?
﴿ءَأَنتُمۡ أَشَدُّ خَلۡقًا أَمِ ٱلسَّمَآءُۚ بَنَىٰهَا﴾ ﴿رَفَعَ سَمۡكَهَا فَسَوَّىٰهَا﴾ ﴿وَأَغۡطَشَ لَيۡلَهَا وَأَخۡرَجَ ضُحَىٰهَا﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡأَرۡضَ بَعۡدَ ذَٰلِكَ دَحَىٰهَآ﴾ ﴿أَخۡرَجَ مِنۡهَا مَآءَهَا وَمَرۡعَىٰهَا﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡجِبَالَ أَرۡسَىٰهَا﴾ ﴿مَتَٰعٗا لَّكُمۡ وَلِأَنۡعَٰمِكُمۡ﴾ (Quran 79:27-33)

After the story comes an argument from creation: **"Are you harder to create, or the heaven that He built?"** He raised its ceiling and proportioned it, darkened its night and brought out its daylight. **"And after that He spread the earth"**, brought out its water and pasture, and set the mountains firmly, all **"as provision for you and your livestock"**.

**The logic is simple**: the One who created the vast heavens and spread out the earth will find it easy to recreate a human being. And the purpose of all this creation is mentioned at the end: provision for you. The universe was prepared for you, so how can you be ungrateful to the One who prepared it?

## Verses 34–41: The Great Overwhelming Event
﴿فَإِذَا جَآءَتِ ٱلطَّآمَّةُ ٱلۡكُبۡرَىٰ﴾ ﴿يَوۡمَ يَتَذَكَّرُ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ مَا سَعَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَبُرِّزَتِ ٱلۡجَحِيمُ لِمَن يَرَىٰ﴾ (Quran 79:34-36)

**"The Great Overwhelming Event"** (*at-Tammah al-Kubra*) is the Day of Resurrection, called so because it overwhelms everything before it. On that day **"man will remember what he strove for"**: every deed comes back to memory, and Hell will be displayed for all to see.

﴿فَأَمَّا مَن طَغَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَءَاثَرَ ٱلۡحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنۡيَا﴾ ﴿فَإِنَّ ٱلۡجَحِيمَ هِيَ ٱلۡمَأۡوَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَأَمَّا مَنۡ خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِۦ وَنَهَى ٱلنَّفۡسَ عَنِ ٱلۡهَوَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَإِنَّ ٱلۡجَنَّةَ هِيَ ٱلۡمَأۡوَىٰ﴾ (Quran 79:37-41)

**The two groups**:
- **The first group** has two qualities: he **"transgressed"** and he **"preferred the life of this world"**. His destination is Hell.
- **The second group** also has two qualities: he **"feared standing before his Lord"** and he **"restrained the soul from desire"**. His destination is Paradise.

**This is one of the most important verses in the Quran for self-discipline.** Fearing the standing before Allah means remembering that you will stand before Him and be asked. Restraining the soul from desire (*nahy an-nafs 'an al-hawa*) means refusing to follow whatever the self craves when it conflicts with what Allah loves. Notice that the cure for following desire is not willpower alone; it is the fear of standing before Allah. When that fear is alive in the heart, saying no to desire becomes possible.

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The Fire is surrounded by desires, and Paradise is surrounded by things that are disliked." This is exactly the choice the surah describes.

## Verses 42–46: When Is the Hour?
﴿يَسۡـَٔلُونَكَ عَنِ ٱلسَّاعَةِ أَيَّانَ مُرۡسَىٰهَا﴾ ﴿فِيمَ أَنتَ مِن ذِكۡرَىٰهَآ﴾ ﴿إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ مُنتَهَىٰهَآ﴾ ﴿إِنَّمَآ أَنتَ مُنذِرُ مَن يَخۡشَىٰهَا﴾ ﴿كَأَنَّهُمۡ يَوۡمَ يَرَوۡنَهَا لَمۡ يَلۡبَثُوٓاْ إِلَّا عَشِيَّةً أَوۡ ضُحَىٰهَا﴾ (Quran 79:42-46)

The disbelievers kept asking, "When will it arrive?" The answer: **"What have you to do with mentioning it? To your Lord is its finality."** Knowledge of the Hour belongs to Allah alone. In the famous hadith of Jibril in Sahih Muslim, when Jibril asked the Prophet ﷺ about the Hour, he answered: "The one asked about it knows no more than the one asking."

The Prophet's ﷺ role was only to warn: **"You are only a warner for whoever fears it."** And in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Sahl ibn Sa'd reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "I and the Hour have been sent like these two," and he joined his index and middle fingers.

**"On the day they see it, it will be as if they had not remained except for an evening or its morning"**: the whole life of this world, however long it felt, will seem like part of a single day.

## Word Meanings
- **An-Nazi'at**: those who pull out, the angels who take souls with force.
- **An-Nashitat**: those who draw out gently.
- **Ar-Rajifah**: the first blast of the Trumpet.
- **Ar-Radifah**: the second blast that follows it.
- **Wajifah**: pounding, trembling with fear.
- **Nakhirah**: decayed, crumbling.
- **As-Sahirah**: the open surface of the earth where people are gathered.
- **Tuwa**: the name of the sacred valley where Allah spoke to Musa.
- **At-Tammah al-Kubra**: the Great Overwhelming Event, the Day of Resurrection.
- **Al-Hawa**: desire, the inclination of the self.
- **Mursaha**: its arrival, its fixed time.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **Opening with five oaths**: the rhythm is fast and gripping, matching the subject of death and resurrection.
2. **"Violently" and "gently"**: a contrast that shows two very different deaths.
3. **The soft invitation to Pharaoh**: "Would you like to purify yourself?" A question, not a command.
4. **"I am your lord, the most high"**: the height of arrogance, followed directly by its punishment.
5. **The balanced description of the two groups**: two qualities for each, and one destination for each.
6. **"An evening or its morning"**: a whole life compressed into a few hours.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd muttasil** (connected prolongation): in *as-sama'* in verse 27.
- **Idgham with ghunnah**: in *qulubun yawma'idhin* (verse 8), the tanween merges into the ya' with nasalisation.
- **Izhar halqi**: in *man khafa* (verse 40), the noon sakinah is pronounced clearly because kha' is a throat letter.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *adbara* (verse 22), the dal sakinah bounces lightly.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah An-Naba' before it ends with the disbeliever wishing he were dust, and An-Nazi'at begins with the taking of souls and the resurrection, continuing the same theme. Surah 'Abasa after it opens with the Prophet's ﷺ encounter with a blind man who came "fearing", which connects with An-Nazi'at's statement that the Prophet ﷺ is "only a warner for whoever fears".

## Restraining the Soul: A Practical Programme
Verse 40 deserves to be turned into daily practice. Here is how:
1. **Remember the standing before Allah**: before acting on a desire, picture yourself being asked about it.
2. **Delay the first impulse**: most desires weaken if you simply wait and leave the place.
3. **Lower the gaze**: much desire begins with the eyes.
4. **Fill your time**: an empty schedule is an open door for desires.
5. **Choose your company**: friends either strengthen your restraint or weaken it.
6. **Fast**: the Prophet ﷺ recommended fasting for young people who cannot marry, as in al-Bukhari and Muslim, because it trains the soul.
7. **Make du'a**: ask Allah to purify your soul and protect you from its evil.

## Lessons from Pharaoh's Story
- **Arrogance grows step by step**: Pharaoh denied, then disobeyed, then turned away, then plotted, then gathered people, then claimed lordship.
- **Power does not last**: the man who said "I am your lord, the most high" ended up drowned.
- **Gentleness is the method of da'wah**: even with the worst of people.
- **The lesson is only for those who fear**: a hard heart can hear the story and learn nothing.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Remember how your soul will be taken**: and live in a way that makes it gentle.
2. **Speak gently when calling others to good**: as Musa was commanded.
3. **Reflect on the heavens and the earth**: they were prepared for you.
4. **Do not prefer this world over the hereafter**: it is one of the two qualities of the people of Hell.
5. **Train yourself to say no to desire**: this is the path to Paradise.
6. **Do not waste time asking when the Hour will come**: ask what you have prepared for it.

## How to Memorise Surah An-Nazi'at
- **Day 1**: verses 1–14.
- **Day 2**: verses 15–26 (the story of Musa and Pharaoh).
- **Day 3**: verses 27–33.
- **Day 4**: verses 34–46, then revise the whole surah.

## Teaching Children
- **Tell them the story of Musa and Pharaoh** simply, focusing on how Musa spoke gently.
- **Explain "restraining the soul"** with examples: not taking a sweet that belongs to someone else, or switching off a game when it is time to pray.
- **Look at the sky together** and read verses 27–29.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does "An-Nazi'at" mean?
It means "those who pull out", and most commentators explain it as the angels who take the souls of the disbelievers with force.

### Who are "those who draw out gently"?
The angels who take the souls of the believers easily and gently.

### What are ar-rajifah and ar-radifah?
The first and second blasts of the Trumpet on the Last Day.

### What did Pharaoh say in Surah An-Nazi'at?
He gathered his people and proclaimed: "I am your lord, the most high."

### What is at-Tammah al-Kubra?
The Great Overwhelming Event, a name for the Day of Resurrection.

### What is the key to Paradise mentioned in this surah?
Fearing the standing before one's Lord and restraining the soul from desire (verses 40–41).

### Does anyone know when the Hour will come?
No. Its knowledge is with Allah alone, as the surah says and as the Prophet ﷺ told Jibril.

### How many verses are in Surah An-Nazi'at?
46 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

## Conclusion
Surah An-Nazi'at takes you from the moment your soul leaves your body to the moment you stand before Allah, and on the way shows you a tyrant who thought he was the highest lord. Its final word is simple and demanding: fear the standing before your Lord, restrain your soul from desire, and Paradise will be your home.
