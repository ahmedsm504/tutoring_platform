Surah An-Nasr is three verses carrying two pieces of news at once: joyful news of victory, the conquest of Mecca and people entering Islam in crowds, and moving news that the Prophet's mission was complete and his meeting with his Lord was near. That is how Ibn 'Abbas understood it, and 'Umar agreed. It is also, according to Ibn 'Abbas in Sahih Muslim, the last surah to be revealed in full.

This guide explains the surah in English and reflects on the etiquette it teaches: meeting success with glorification and seeking forgiveness, not with pride.

## Quick Facts About Surah An-Nasr
- **Number of verses**: 3.
- **Type**: Medinan.
- **Position**: Surah 110, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Names**: An-Nasr (Victory), *Idha ja'a nasrullah*, and the surah of farewell (*at-tawdi'*).
- **In the order of revelation**: the last surah revealed complete, according to Ibn 'Abbas.

## The Full Text of Surah An-Nasr
﴿إِذَا جَآءَ نَصۡرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡفَتۡحُ﴾ ﴿وَرَأَيۡتَ ٱلنَّاسَ يَدۡخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ ٱللَّهِ أَفۡوَاجٗا﴾ ﴿فَسَبِّحۡ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسۡتَغۡفِرۡهُۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابَۢا﴾ (Quran 110:1-3)

**Meaning:** When the victory of Allah has come, and the conquest, and you see the people entering the religion of Allah in crowds, then glorify the praise of your Lord and ask His forgiveness. Indeed, He is ever accepting of repentance.

## Ibn 'Abbas and 'Umar: Reading Between the Lines
In Sahih al-Bukhari, Ibn 'Abbas narrated: "'Umar used to admit me with the elders of Badr. Some of them felt uneasy and said: Why do you bring this young man in with us when we have sons like him? 'Umar said: He is one whose standing you know. One day he called me in with them, and I knew he had called me only to show them. He asked: What do you say about the words of Allah: *When the victory of Allah has come, and the conquest*? Some said: We were commanded to praise Allah and seek His forgiveness when He gives us victory and conquest. Others were silent. He said to me: Is that what you say, Ibn 'Abbas? I said: No. He said: Then what do you say? I said: It is the appointed time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, which He informed him of. *When the victory of Allah has come, and the conquest*: that is the sign of your appointed time; *so glorify the praise of your Lord and ask His forgiveness; indeed He is ever accepting of repentance*. 'Umar said: I know nothing of it except what you say."

**Lessons from the story:**
1. Ibn 'Abbas's deep understanding despite his youth; the Prophet ﷺ had prayed, "O Allah, teach him the Book," as reported in al-Bukhari.
2. 'Umar's habit of honouring people of knowledge regardless of age.
3. The Quran has layers of meaning that Allah opens to whom He wills.
4. Both meanings stand together: the etiquette of victory, and the sign of the Prophet's approaching death.

## Verse 1: When the Victory of Allah Comes
﴿إِذَا جَآءَ نَصۡرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡفَتۡحُ﴾ (Quran 110:1)

**"When"** (*idha*) is used for something certain to happen. Victory is not a possibility; it is a promise.

**"The victory of Allah"**: victory is attributed to Allah, not to the strength of the Muslims. "Victory comes only from Allah" (3:126).

**"And the conquest"**: according to most commentators, the conquest of Mecca in the eighth year after the Hijrah, when the Prophet ﷺ entered the city that had expelled him, and cleansed the Ka'bah of idols.

## Verse 2: People Entering in Crowds
﴿وَرَأَيۡتَ ٱلنَّاسَ يَدۡخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ ٱللَّهِ أَفۡوَاجٗا﴾ (Quran 110:2)

**"And you see"**: the Prophet ﷺ would witness it with his own eyes.

**"Entering the religion of Allah in crowds"**: whole tribes and delegations, rather than individuals one at a time.

**Why did they come in crowds after the conquest?** In Sahih al-Bukhari, 'Amr ibn Salimah explained: "The Arabs were waiting on the conquest to accept Islam. They said: Leave him and his people; if he prevails over them, he is a true prophet. When the conquest happened, every tribe hurried to accept Islam." The following year became known as the Year of Delegations.

## Verse 3: Glorify, Praise and Seek Forgiveness
﴿فَسَبِّحۡ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسۡتَغۡفِرۡهُۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابَۢا﴾ (Quran 110:3)

**"Glorify the praise of your Lord"**: declare Him free of every imperfection, together with praising Him for the blessing of victory.

**"And ask His forgiveness"**.

**"Indeed, He is ever accepting of repentance"** (*tawwab*): He has always accepted the repentance of His servants and always will.

### Why seek forgiveness at the moment of victory?
Scholars mention several reasons:
1. **Good deeds are sealed with istighfar.** Prayer, Hajj and gatherings all end with seeking forgiveness, and so does a lifetime of mission.
2. **Humility.** So that no pride or self-admiration enters the heart at success.
3. **Making up for shortcomings.** However much a person does, he falls short of what Allah deserves.
4. **Preparation to meet Allah**, according to Ibn 'Abbas's understanding.

### How the Prophet ﷺ lived it
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah said: "The Prophet ﷺ used to say often in his bowing and prostration: *Subhanaka Allahumma rabbana wa bi-hamdika, Allahumma-ghfir li*, interpreting the Quran." That is, applying this surah. And in another wording in al-Bukhari and Muslim: "After *Idha ja'a nasrullahi wal-fath* was revealed, he did not pray a prayer without saying in it: *Subhanaka rabbana wa bi-hamdika, Allahumma-ghfir li*."

## The Last Surah Revealed Complete
In Sahih Muslim, 'Ubaydullah ibn 'Abdullah ibn 'Utbah said that Ibn 'Abbas asked him: "Do you know the last surah of the Quran revealed all at once?" He said: "Yes: *Idha ja'a nasrullahi wal-fath*." Ibn 'Abbas said: "You are right." Individual verses were revealed after it, but it was the last complete surah.

## Word Meanings
- **Nasr Allah**: the help and victory of Allah.
- **Al-Fath**: the conquest, of Mecca.
- **Afwaja**: in crowds, groups after groups.
- **Fa-sabbih**: so glorify, declare Him free of imperfection.
- **Bi-hamdi rabbika**: with the praise of your Lord.
- **Istaghfirhu**: ask His forgiveness.
- **Tawwab**: ever accepting of repentance.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"Idha" for certainty.**
2. **Victory attributed to Allah**, removing any claim of human power.
3. **"The religion of Allah"**: attributing the religion to Allah honours it.
4. **Glorification, praise and forgiveness together**: exalting Allah, thanking Him, and humbling oneself.
5. **"Kana tawwaba"**: the verb *kana* conveys permanence: He has always been accepting of repentance.
6. **A surah of joy and farewell at once.**

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd muttasil** in *ja'a*: four or five counts.
- **Heavy lam in "Allah"** in *nasrullah*, because it follows a *damma*.
- **Light lam in "Allah"** in *dinillah*, because it follows a *kasra*.
- **Qalqalah sughra** in *yadkhulun*: the *dal* with sukun.
- **Ghunnah** on the doubled nun in *innahu*.
- **Madd 'iwad** when stopping on *afwaja* and *tawwaba*: the tanween becomes a two-count alif.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before An-Nasr comes Al-Kafirun, declaring "for you is your religion and for me is mine". An-Nasr then promises that this uncompromising religion will triumph, and people will enter it in crowds. After it comes Al-Masad, the end of Abu Lahab, one of Islam's fiercest enemies: victory for the truth, ruin for falsehood.

## The Etiquette of Success in Islam
The Prophet ﷺ lived this surah on the day of the conquest of Mecca:
1. **Humility.** The biographers describe him entering Mecca with his head lowered in humility before Allah.
2. **Forgiveness.** He pardoned the people of Mecca who had persecuted and expelled him.
3. **Cleansing the House.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn Mas'ud reported that he entered Mecca on the day of conquest with three hundred and sixty idols around the Ka'bah, and he struck them with a stick in his hand, saying: "The truth has come and falsehood has vanished."
4. **Glorification and seeking forgiveness**, as the surah commanded.

**For everyone who succeeds**: when you pass an exam, win a contract or finish a project, do not attribute it to yourself. Say: this is from the favour of my Lord, and glorify Him and seek His forgiveness.

## Seeking Forgiveness at the End of Good Deeds
The Sunnah teaches us to end acts of worship with istighfar:
- **After prayer**: in Sahih Muslim, Thawban reported that the Prophet ﷺ said *astaghfirullah* three times on finishing his prayer.
- **After Hajj**: "Then depart from where the people depart and seek forgiveness of Allah" (2:199).
- **At dawn after night prayer**: "and in the hours before dawn they seek forgiveness" (51:18).
- **At the end of gatherings**, with the supplication of expiation.
- **At the end of a life**, as in this surah.

## Practical Lessons
1. Attribute success to Allah.
2. Meet blessings with glorification and praise.
3. Seek forgiveness often, especially after finishing good deeds.
4. Stay humble in victory.
5. Say in bowing and prostration: *Subhanaka Allahumma rabbana wa bi-hamdika, Allahumma-ghfir li*.
6. Remember that everything has an end, and prepare to meet Allah.
7. Call people to Allah: they enter His religion when they see the truthfulness of its people.

## Teaching Children
- Tell them the story of the conquest of Mecca and how the Prophet ﷺ forgave his enemies.
- Teach them to say *alhamdulillah* when they succeed, not "I'm the best".
- Teach them the dhikr of bowing from this surah.

## Victory in the Quran: Laws and Conditions
Victory in the Quran follows patterns that Allah has set:
1. **Supporting Allah's cause**: "If you support Allah, He will support you and plant your feet firmly" (47:7).
2. **Patience**: "If there are twenty patient among you, they will overcome two hundred" (8:65).
3. **Unity**: "Do not dispute, or you will lose courage and your strength will depart" (8:46).
4. **Reliance on Allah**: "Victory comes only from Allah, the Almighty, the Wise."
5. **Taking the means**: "Prepare against them whatever strength you can" (8:60).
Victory is not an accident; it is the fruit of these laws.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What is "the conquest" in Surah An-Nasr?
The conquest of Mecca in 8 AH, according to most commentators.

### What does "entering the religion of Allah in crowds" mean?
Whole tribes and groups accepting Islam rather than individuals.

### Why was the Prophet ﷺ told to seek forgiveness at the time of victory?
For humility, to seal his mission with istighfar, and, as Ibn 'Abbas understood, as a sign that his meeting with Allah was near.

### How did Ibn 'Abbas interpret Surah An-Nasr?
As a notification of the Prophet's approaching death, and 'Umar agreed, as in Sahih al-Bukhari.

### What was the last surah revealed?
Surah An-Nasr was the last surah revealed complete, according to Ibn 'Abbas in Sahih Muslim.

### What did the Prophet ﷺ say after it was revealed?
*Subhanaka Allahumma rabbana wa bi-hamdika, Allahumma-ghfir li*, in his bowing and prostration, as 'A'ishah reported in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### How many verses are in Surah An-Nasr?
3 verses.

### Is the victory in the surah only the conquest of Mecca?
Primarily the conquest of Mecca, but its meaning extends to every victory and success Allah grants the believers, which they should meet with glorification and seeking forgiveness.

### Did the Prophet ﷺ seek forgiveness even though he was forgiven?
Yes. In Sahih al-Bukhari, Abu Hurayrah reported that he said: "By Allah, I seek Allah's forgiveness and repent to Him more than seventy times a day." He did so out of worship, gratitude and to teach his Ummah.

### What is the main lesson of Surah An-Nasr for us?
Meet every blessing and success with glorification, praise and seeking forgiveness, and remember that every journey ends with meeting Allah.

## Conclusion
Surah An-Nasr teaches you how to stand at the top: not with pride, but with glorification, praise and seeking forgiveness. Every success you reach is a victory from Allah, every deed you complete needs istighfar, and every journey has an end. Keep your tongue moist with the words the Prophet ﷺ repeated after this surah: Glory be to You, O Allah, our Lord, and praise; O Allah, forgive me.
