Every month, Muslim women who love the Quran face the same question: "I am on my period. Can I continue my daily recitation? Can I revise my memorisation? I teach Quran classes; can I correct my students? I have a memorisation test this week; should I postpone it?" These are real questions affecting up to a quarter of each month, and the postnatal period (nifas) which can last up to forty days.

This is an area of **legitimate scholarly disagreement**, with evidence on each side. This article presents the views fairly, explains the strength of the evidence, and then offers practical solutions for women who memorise and teach, whichever view they follow.

## Clarifying the Question
Four different issues are often mixed together:
1. **Reciting the Quran** from memory or by looking at it without touching a Mushaf. This is the main subject of this article.
2. **Touching the Mushaf**: a separate issue with different evidence, where the majority view is stricter.
3. **Remembering Allah (dhikr) and making du'a**: permitted for a menstruating woman by consensus, including Quranic phrases said as dhikr rather than as recitation, according to many scholars.
4. **Listening to the Quran and reading tafsir**: permitted by agreement.

So the real disagreement concerns the first issue: may a menstruating woman recite the Quran with her tongue?

## View One: Not Permitted (the Majority)
The **Hanafi, Shafi'i and Hanbali** schools, in their main positions, hold that a menstruating woman, a woman in nifas and a person in janabah should **not recite the Quran**. They allow certain exceptions, which vary by school:
- **Quranic phrases intended as dhikr**, such as saying *Bismillah* before eating or *Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un* at a calamity.
- **Less than a verse**, according to some.
- **Teaching word by word**, according to some Hanafi scholars: the teacher says a word, pauses, then the next.

### Their evidence
1. **The hadith of Ibn 'Umar**: "The menstruating woman and the person in janabah should not recite anything of the Quran." Reported by at-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah.
2. **The hadith of 'Ali**: "Nothing kept the Messenger of Allah ﷺ from the Quran except janabah." Reported by Abu Dawud, at-Tirmidhi, an-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah; at-Tirmidhi graded it hasan sahih.
3. **Analogy with janabah**: if the person in janabah, who can remove the state with a bath in minutes, is prevented from reciting, then menstruation, a more serious state, is included.
4. **Respect for the Quran**: purity is part of the etiquette of recitation.

## View Two: Permitted (the Malikis and Ibn Taymiyyah)
The **Maliki** school permits a menstruating woman to recite the Quran without touching the Mushaf, reasoning that menstruation lasts for days, that she may forget her memorisation, and that unlike the person in janabah she cannot end her state whenever she wishes. This was also al-Shafi'i's earlier opinion, and something similar is narrated from Imam Ahmad.

**Ibn Taymiyyah** argued strongly for permissibility. His reasoning, in summary: women menstruated in the time of the Prophet ﷺ, and if reciting the Quran were forbidden to them like prayer is, the Prophet ﷺ would have made it clear to his community, and the Mothers of the Believers would have known it and conveyed it. Since no authentic prohibition was transmitted, it is not forbidden. His student Ibn al-Qayyim agreed, and many contemporary scholars take this view.

### Their evidence
1. **The original ruling is permissibility**, and no authentic, explicit text forbids it.
2. **The hadith of 'A'ishah in Hajj**: when she began menstruating, the Prophet ﷺ told her, "Do everything the pilgrim does, except do not circumambulate the House until you are pure." Reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim. Pilgrims remember Allah and recite the Quran, and he excluded only tawaf.
3. **Statements of the early generations**: al-Bukhari mentions, in his chapter on menstruation, that Ibrahim an-Nakha'i said, "There is no harm in her reciting a verse," and that Ibn 'Abbas saw no harm in recitation for the person in janabah.
4. **The difference from janabah**: the person in janabah can bathe immediately; the menstruating woman cannot end her state for days, and prohibiting her causes real hardship, especially for those who memorise and teach.

## How Strong Is the Prohibiting Hadith?
This is the heart of the issue:
- **The hadith of Ibn 'Umar** ("should not recite anything") is considered **weak** by most hadith scholars, because it is narrated by Isma'il ibn 'Ayyash from narrators of the Hijaz, and his narrations from them are weak by agreement. Al-Bukhari, al-Bayhaqi, Ibn Taymiyyah and al-Albani, among others, judged it weak.
- **The hadith of 'Ali** was graded hasan by at-Tirmidhi and weakened by others because of a narrator, 'Abdullah ibn Salimah. In any case it concerns **janabah**, not menstruation, and describes the Prophet's practice rather than a prohibition.

The conclusion of many specialists is that **no authentic, explicit hadith forbids a menstruating woman from reciting**. The majority relied on the combined evidence and analogy; those who permit relied on the original permissibility and the absence of a sound prohibition.

## Which View Should You Follow?
This is a matter of legitimate ijtihad, and neither view should be condemned:
- **The more cautious approach** is to limit recitation to what you need, such as revision, teaching and ruqyah, while increasing dhikr and listening.
- **If you follow the majority view**, you have wide room in the exceptions: dhikr, listening, looking at the text without reciting, and word-by-word teaching according to some.
- **If you follow the Maliki view and Ibn Taymiyyah**, you may recite from memory or from a phone without touching a printed Mushaf, especially if you fear forgetting or need to teach.
- **If you follow a particular school or scholar**, keep to their position.

What matters most is not to let these days become a reason to abandon the Quran for weeks at a time.

## Practical Questions
### Can I read from my phone during my period?
Many contemporary scholars hold that a phone does not take the ruling of a Mushaf. So those who permit recitation permit reading from a phone without difficulty. Those who prohibit recitation still permit **looking** at the screen and reflecting without reciting aloud.

### Can I revise my memorisation?
According to those who permit: yes, from memory or from a phone. According to the majority: listen daily to the portion you are revising, follow along on the screen, repeat it in your heart, and read its tafsir. Some scholars who hold the majority view permitted recitation for a woman who fears forgetting, which is a recorded opinion among the Shafi'is.

### Can a Quran teacher teach during her period?
According to those who permit: yes, without difficulty. According to some Hanafi scholars: she may teach word by word. In all cases she may listen to her students and correct them, since listening and correcting is not recitation.

### What about memorisation tests?
If you follow the permissive view, take the test. If you follow the majority view, ask to postpone it, or take the concession for need mentioned by some scholars. Quran schools should plan for this and offer alternative dates.

### Can I recite ruqyah and the morning and evening adhkar?
The prophetic supplications are permitted without disagreement. Verses such as Ayat al-Kursi and the three Quls may be said with the intention of dhikr and protection, which many scholars who prohibit recitation still allow.

### Is nifas the same?
Yes. The postnatal period takes the same ruling as menstruation here, and since it can last longer, the need is often greater.

## What Is Permitted by Agreement
So that no woman feels cut off from the Quran, here is what is permitted without disagreement:
1. **Listening to the Quran**, recorded or live.
2. **Dhikr**: tasbih, tahmid, tahlil, takbir and istighfar.
3. **Du'a** in all its forms.
4. **Reading tafsir** and the meanings of verses.
5. **Reflecting** on verses you have memorised.
6. **Attending lessons** and listening to them.
7. **Sending blessings on the Prophet** ﷺ.
8. **Teaching children** adhkar and du'as.

## Menstruation Is Not Impurity of the Person
It is important to understand that menstruation is something Allah decreed for the daughters of Adam, and it is not a defect in a woman's faith or a sign of distance from Allah. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah said: "The Prophet ﷺ would recline in my lap while I was menstruating and recite the Quran." And in Sahih Muslim, when she hesitated to pass him a mat because she was menstruating, he said: "Your menstruation is not in your hand." A menstruating woman's body is pure apart from the blood itself. These days are a concession from Allah, not a separation from His Book.

## A Practical Plan for Women Who Memorise
- **If you follow the permissive view**: continue your usual revision from memory or from your phone, without touching a printed Mushaf. If you feel tired, focus on revision rather than new memorisation.
- **If you follow the majority view**: listen daily to your revision portion from a skilled reciter, follow on the screen, repeat it silently in your heart, and read the tafsir of what you have memorised.
- **In both cases**: use these days to deepen understanding. Many women memorise without knowing the meaning of what they memorise; these days are an opportunity to change that.

## Istihadah Is Different
Some women experience bleeding outside their regular cycle, or bleeding that continues beyond the normal length of their period. This is called **istihadah** (irregular, non-menstrual bleeding), and its ruling is completely different. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Fatimah bint Abi Hubaysh told the Prophet ﷺ that her bleeding did not stop, and he said: "That is a vein, not menstruation. When your menstruation comes, leave the prayer, and when it goes, wash the blood from yourself and pray." A woman with istihadah prays, fasts, and recites the Quran like any pure woman, according to the scholars. She performs wudu for each prayer according to the majority, and may touch the Mushaf with wudu. Knowing the difference spares many women from abandoning the Quran unnecessarily for weeks.

## Advice for Quran Schools and Families
- **Plan the schedule**: build flexible test dates so that no student is pressured to choose between her test and her view.
- **Respect the student's school**: a teacher should not impose her own view on a student who follows another.
- **Provide listening tasks**: on these days, assign tafsir reading, listening and reflection homework.
- **Speak openly**: many girls are too shy to ask about this; a short, respectful explanation from a female teacher removes confusion.
- **Fathers and husbands**: encourage the women of your home to keep their link with the Quran through listening and dhikr, and do not treat these days as distance from worship.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can I read the Quran on my period?
There is a legitimate difference of opinion: the majority prohibit reciting, while the Malikis and Ibn Taymiyyah permit it.

### Is the hadith "should not recite anything of the Quran" authentic?
Most hadith scholars consider it weak, because it is narrated by Isma'il ibn 'Ayyash from narrators of the Hijaz.

### Can I touch the Mushaf on my period?
The majority hold that she should not touch a printed Mushaf; this is a separate issue from reciting.

### Can I listen to the Quran on my period?
Yes, by agreement.

### Can I make dhikr that includes Quranic verses?
Yes, with the intention of dhikr; many scholars who prohibit recitation still permit this.

### Is a woman in nifas the same?
Yes, the ruling is the same.

### What if my period begins in the middle of reciting?
Stop reciting if you follow the majority view, and continue with dhikr and listening; those who permit recitation may continue without touching a printed Mushaf.

### Is istihadah the same as menstruation?
No. A woman with istihadah prays and recites like any pure woman, according to the scholars.

## Conclusion
Reciting the Quran during menstruation is an issue of legitimate scholarly difference: the majority prohibit it with exceptions, while the Malikis and Ibn Taymiyyah permit it, and no authentic, explicit hadith forbids it. Whichever view you follow, do not cut yourself off from the Quran: listen, reflect, remember Allah, and revise in the way your chosen view allows. These days are a mercy and a concession from Allah, not a break from His Book.
