What was the very first word of the Quran that Allah sent down? Not "pray", not "fast", not "fight", but "Iqra'": "Read!" The first command in Islam was to read and learn. It came to the Prophet ﷺ in a quiet cave on a mountain near Makkah, brought by the angel Jibril, and it changed the whole world.

Surah Al-'Alaq contains these first five verses. It goes on to talk about how Allah taught people by the pen, how some people become arrogant when they think they don't need Allah, and how one man tried to stop the Prophet ﷺ from praying. It ends with a beautiful command: "Prostrate and draw near." In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Al-'Alaq to children in simple English, with the story of the first revelation, activities that celebrate reading and learning, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-'Alaq

- **Position**: surah 96, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: nineteen.
- **Type**: Meccan; its first five verses were the very first revelation.
- **Name**: al-'Alaq means "a clinging clot", describing an early stage of a baby's development in the womb. It is also called "Iqra'".
- **It contains a sajdah**: at the end of verse 19, there is a prostration of recitation.

## The Whole Surah

﴿ٱقۡرَأۡ بِٱسۡمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ﴾ ﴿خَلَقَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ مِنۡ عَلَقٍ﴾ ﴿ٱقۡرَأۡ وَرَبُّكَ ٱلۡأَكۡرَمُ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي عَلَّمَ بِٱلۡقَلَمِ﴾ ﴿عَلَّمَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمۡ يَعۡلَمۡ﴾ ﴿كَلَّآ إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لَيَطۡغَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَن رَّءَاهُ ٱسۡتَغۡنَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلرُّجۡعَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَرَءَيۡتَ ٱلَّذِي يَنۡهَىٰ﴾ ﴿عَبۡدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَرَءَيۡتَ إِن كَانَ عَلَى ٱلۡهُدَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَوۡ أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقۡوَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَرَءَيۡتَ إِن كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَلَمۡ يَعۡلَم بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ﴾ ﴿كَلَّا لَئِن لَّمۡ يَنتَهِ لَنَسۡفَعَۢا بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ﴾ ﴿نَاصِيَةٖ كَٰذِبَةٍ خَاطِئَةٖ﴾ ﴿فَلۡيَدۡعُ نَادِيَهُۥ﴾ ﴿سَنَدۡعُ ٱلزَّبَانِيَةَ﴾ ﴿كَلَّا لَا تُطِعۡهُ وَٱسۡجُدۡۤ وَٱقۡتَرِب۩﴾ (Quran 96:1-19)

## The Story of the First Revelation

### Scene 1: The cave of Hira
"Before he became a prophet, Muhammad ﷺ loved to go to a small cave called Hira, high on a mountain near Makkah, now called Jabal an-Nur, the Mountain of Light. He would stay there for days, thinking about Allah and worshipping Him, away from the noise and idols of Makkah. His wife Khadijah would prepare food for him."

### Scene 2: Jibril comes
"One night in Ramadan, when he was forty years old, the angel Jibril suddenly appeared to him and said: Iqra'! Read! The Prophet ﷺ replied: I am not one who reads, because he had never learned to read. Jibril held him tightly and said again: Read! He said again: I am not one who reads. A third time, Jibril held him and then recited: Iqra' bismi Rabbikal-ladhi khalaq, Read in the name of your Lord who created."

### Scene 3: Rushing home to Khadijah
"The Prophet ﷺ rushed home, trembling, and said to Khadijah: Cover me, cover me! She covered him until he calmed down, and he told her what had happened. She comforted him with beautiful words: Never! By Allah, Allah will never disgrace you. You keep family ties, you help the weak, you give to the poor, you honour guests and you support people in hardship."

The full story is reported in Sahih al-Bukhari from 'A'ishah.

### Lesson for kids: a kind heart is a strong heart
"Khadijah knew the Prophet ﷺ was a good man because of how he treated people. She listed his kindness: helping the weak, giving to the poor, honouring guests. Our good character is a sign of a good heart."

## Verses 1–2: Read in the Name of Your Lord

### Explaining it to your child
"Read in the name of your Lord who created, who created the human being from a clinging clot. Notice: read 'in the name of your Lord'. Our learning should be with Allah, for Allah, starting with Bismillah. And Allah reminds us how He created us from something tiny in our mother's womb."

### Activity: Bismillah before books
Teach your child to say "Bismillah" before opening any book or starting homework, and "Rabbi zidni 'ilma", "My Lord, increase me in knowledge", afterwards.

## Verses 3–5: Taught by the Pen

### Explaining it to your child
"Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous, who taught by the pen, taught the human being what he did not know. Think about it: everything you know, you learned from Allah, through parents, teachers, books and the pen. Writing lets knowledge travel across the world and through hundreds of years!"

### Activity: the pen of knowledge
Give your child a special pen and a notebook titled "What Allah Taught Me Today". Every evening, they write or draw one new thing they learned. At the end of the month, flip through it together and say: "Allamal-insana ma lam ya'lam!"

### Activity: Muslim scientists
Share short stories of Muslim scholars who loved learning, like Ibn Sina in medicine, al-Khwarizmi in mathematics, and al-Idrisi in map-making. Explain that the first word of the Quran, "Read!", inspired many Muslims to become great learners.

## Verses 6–8: When People Feel They Don't Need Allah

### Explaining it to your child
"No! The human being becomes arrogant when he thinks he is self-sufficient, when he has money and power and thinks he doesn't need Allah. But to your Lord is the return. Everyone goes back to Allah in the end."

### Lesson for kids
"Even if we have lots of things, we always need Allah: for our breath, our health and our food. Remembering that keeps us humble."

## Verses 9–14: The Man Who Tried to Stop the Prayer

### Explaining it to your child
"Abu Jahl, one of the leaders of Makkah, hated Islam. He saw the Prophet ﷺ praying at the Kaaba and threatened to step on his neck! Allah describes him: Have you seen the one who forbids a servant when he prays? Does he not know that Allah sees?"

### From the Sunnah
Muslim reported that Abu Jahl came to stop the Prophet ﷺ while he was praying, but suddenly stepped back, shielding himself with his hands. When asked what happened, he said he saw a trench of fire, terror and wings between himself and the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ said that if he had come closer, the angels would have snatched him.

### Lesson for kids: Allah sees
"Alam ya'lam bi-annallaha yara, does he not know that Allah sees? This little verse is a big reminder for us too. Allah sees everything we do, good and bad."

### Activity: Allah sees
Write "Allah sees" on a small card and place it on your child's desk. It's a gentle reminder to be honest, kind and to do their best, even when no one else is watching.

## Verses 15–18: The Warning

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah warns that if this man doesn't stop, he will be dragged by the forelock, the front of his head, the lying, sinful forelock. Let him call his friends; Allah has His angels. Nobody can stop the prayer of someone who prays to Allah."

## Verse 19: Prostrate and Draw Near

### Explaining it to your child
"The surah ends: No! Don't obey him. Prostrate and draw near to Allah. When you're in sujud, with your forehead on the ground, that's when you are closest to Allah."

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said: "The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is in prostration, so make plenty of du'a then" (Muslim).

### Activity: the sujud du'a
Teach your child to make a personal du'a in their sujud, in any language, about anything: their family, their friends, their exams. Tell them: "This is when you're closest to Allah."

### The sajdah of recitation
At the end of this verse, the reciter and listener make a prostration of recitation. Abu Hurayrah reported that he prostrated with the Prophet ﷺ in "Iqra' bismi Rabbik" (Muslim).

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Library Card"

Yusra didn't like reading. Books felt long and boring, and she preferred watching videos. One Saturday, her father took her to the city library. "Why are we here?" she grumbled.

Her father sat down with her in a quiet corner. "Do you know the first word Allah sent down in the Quran?" Yusra shook her head. "Iqra'. Read!" He told her the story of the cave of Hira, Jibril, and the Prophet ﷺ rushing home to Khadijah. "The very first command of Islam was to read. That's how important it is."

Yusra was surprised. "Not pray? Not fast?" "Those came later," said her father. "First: read, in the name of your Lord, who taught by the pen."

He helped her get her very own library card, and she chose three books: one about animals, one about space, and one story. That night, she read the animal book in bed and discovered that a blue whale's heart is as big as a small car. "SubhanAllah!" she said. "Allah taught me something I didn't know!"

From then on, every Saturday was library day. She wrote one new fact each night in her notebook, "What Allah Taught Me Today". By the end of the year, she had read more than fifty books.

Ask your child: "Why did Yusra's father take her to the library? What was the first word of the Quran?"

## What We Learn From Surah Al-'Alaq

- **The first word** of the Quran was "Read!"
- **Learning** should start with Allah's name.
- **Allah taught us by the pen**: reading and writing are gifts.
- **Good character** is a sign of a good heart, as Khadijah said.
- **We always need Allah**, even when we have a lot.
- **Allah sees** everything.
- **No one can stop** someone who prays to Allah.
- **In sujud**, we are closest to Allah.

## Two-Week Memorization Plan

- **Days 1–2**: verses 1–5, the first revelation.
- **Days 3–4**: verses 6–8.
- **Days 5–6**: verses 9–12.
- **Days 7–8**: verses 13–14.
- **Days 9–10**: verses 15–16.
- **Days 11–12**: verses 17–19, with the sajdah.
- **Days 13–14**: recite the whole surah and celebrate!

## Questions to Ask Your Child

1. What was the first word of the Quran?
2. Where was the Prophet ﷺ when Jibril came to him?
3. What did Khadijah say to comfort him?
4. What did Allah teach by?
5. What happens when people think they don't need Allah?
6. Who tried to stop the Prophet ﷺ from praying?
7. When are we closest to Allah?

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Why did Jibril hold the Prophet ﷺ tightly?
Scholars mention that it prepared him for the great weight of revelation and made him fully attentive. For children, it's enough to say it was a special moment to help him receive the message.

### Should my child prostrate at the end of the surah?
It is a sunnah to make a prostration of recitation at the end of verse 19 when reciting or listening outside of obligatory prayer, and in prayer the imam does it too.

### How can I encourage a reluctant reader?
Link reading to "Iqra'", let them choose topics they love, read together, visit libraries, and celebrate every new fact they learn.
