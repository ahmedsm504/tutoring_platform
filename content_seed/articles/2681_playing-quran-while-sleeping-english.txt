In many Muslim homes the Quran plays all day: a Quran radio station in the kitchen, Surah Al-Baqarah played every morning "to keep the devils out", and a calm recitation to fall asleep to every night. These are beautiful habits that show love for the Quran, but they raise important questions. Does playing a recording give the same virtue as reciting? Is there reward in a recitation that plays while nobody is listening? Is it disrespectful to fall asleep while the Quran is playing?

This article answers these questions based on the texts and the views of scholars, and offers practical advice so that the Quran in your home is a source of real blessing, not just background sound.

## Listening vs Hearing: An Essential Difference
Scholars distinguish between two things:
- **Listening** (*istima'*): paying attention to the recitation with the intention of listening, reflecting on it and being silent for it.
- **Hearing** (*sama'*): sound reaching the ears without attention or intention, as happens when the Quran plays in the background while people talk or work.

Allah says:

﴿وَإِذَا قُرِئَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانُ فَٱسۡتَمِعُواْ لَهُۥ وَأَنصِتُواْ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تُرۡحَمُونَ﴾ (Quran 7:204)

The full reward and the promised mercy are tied to **listening attentively and being silent**, not merely to sound reaching the ears. Whoever listens to the Quran with a present heart is in a great act of worship, even if they are listening to a recording.

## Is There Reward in Listening to a Recording?
Many scholars hold that someone who listens attentively to a recorded recitation, intending to listen and reflect, is rewarded, by Allah's permission, because they are listening to the words of Allah. A recording conveys the Quran exactly as it was recited. What matters is the listener's attention and intention.

But the reward of **reciting** yourself, letter by letter, as described in the hadith of at-Tirmidhi about every letter bringing a good deed multiplied tenfold, is connected to the act of recitation. Listening is good; reciting yourself is better and more complete. The Prophet ﷺ himself both recited and loved to listen to the Quran from others, as when he asked Ibn Mas'ud to recite to him.

## Surah Al-Baqarah: Does a Recording Keep Shaytan Away?
It is authentically reported in Sahih Muslim that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Do not make your houses into graves. Shaytan flees from the house in which Surah Al-Baqarah is recited."

The question is whether this virtue is achieved by playing a recording of Surah Al-Baqarah. Contemporary scholars have two views:

### View one: the virtue is for reciting, not for playing a recording
Some scholars hold that the hadith mentions a house in which Surah Al-Baqarah **is recited**, and a recording is a reproduction of a recitation that happened elsewhere. The virtue is connected to the people of the house reciting it themselves. So a recording does not take its place, though listening to it is still good.

### View two: the virtue is hoped for when it is played
Other scholars hold that a home in which the Quran is heard and listened to can hope for good and blessing, and that Allah's bounty is vast, especially when the family is listening attentively.

### The practical position
The more cautious and complete course is **for the family to recite Surah Al-Baqarah themselves**, even if it is divided across the week or among the members of the family, so that they achieve the virtue with certainty. There is no harm in playing it hoping for blessing, provided that playing it does not replace reciting it, and that one does not believe something about it that has not been established.

**A practical idea**: divide Surah Al-Baqarah among the family members, with each person reading five pages or fewer each day. The surah is then completed in the home every two or three days, in the voices of the family.

## Playing the Quran While Ignoring It
- **If it plays in a gathering of chatter and laughter** where nobody pays attention: this is disliked, because it goes against the command to listen and be silent, and makes the Quran mere background where it is not respected.
- **If it plays in markets and shops** where it mixes with bargaining and noise, or in unsuitable places: it is better to avoid this, and many scholars disliked it because it exposes the Quran to inattention.
- **If it plays while you work quietly**, cooking or driving, with part of your attention on it: there is no harm in this, and you are rewarded for whatever attention you give.

The rule: **respect the Quran when it is recited**. If you cannot listen, turn it off or lower it, and do not let it be recited amid idle talk.

## Falling Asleep with the Quran Playing
Many people like to fall asleep to the Quran. Is this disrespectful?

Most scholars see **no harm** in it, especially if the intention is to listen until sleep comes. A person who falls asleep is not accountable for what happens after they sleep. But some practical points:
- **Use a sleep timer** so the recording stops after a while, rather than playing all night with nobody listening.
- **Keep the volume low**, so it does not disturb others in the house.
- **Do not let it replace the remembrances of sleep**. Listening to Ayat al-Kursi is good, but the Sunnah is to **recite** it yourself before sleeping.

## What Is Authentically Recited Before Sleep
The Prophet ﷺ taught specific parts of the Quran to recite before sleep. These are far more valuable than any recording, because they are what he did.

### Ayat al-Kursi
﴿ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ٱلۡحَيُّ ٱلۡقَيُّومُۚ لَا تَأۡخُذُهُۥ سِنَةࣱ وَلَا نَوۡمࣱۚ لَّهُۥ مَا فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِۗ مَن ذَا ٱلَّذِي يَشۡفَعُ عِندَهُۥٓ إِلَّا بِإِذۡنِهِۦۚ يَعۡلَمُ مَا بَيۡنَ أَيۡدِيهِمۡ وَمَا خَلۡفَهُمۡۖ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيۡءٖ مِّنۡ عِلۡمِهِۦٓ إِلَّا بِمَا شَآءَۚ وَسِعَ كُرۡسِيُّهُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضَۖ وَلَا يَـُٔودُهُۥ حِفۡظُهُمَاۚ وَهُوَ ٱلۡعَلِيُّ ٱلۡعَظِيمُ﴾ (Quran 2:255)

In Sahih al-Bukhari, in the story of Abu Hurayrah and the devil who came to steal from the charity, the devil told him: "When you go to your bed, recite Ayat al-Kursi; a guardian from Allah will remain with you, and no devil will come near you until morning." The Prophet ﷺ said: "He told you the truth, although he is a liar."

### The last two verses of Surah Al-Baqarah
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever recites the last two verses of Surah Al-Baqarah at night, they will suffice him."

﴿ءَامَنَ ٱلرَّسُولُ بِمَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيۡهِ مِن رَّبِّهِۦ وَٱلۡمُؤۡمِنُونَۚ كُلٌّ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ وَمَلَـٰٓئِكَتِهِۦ وَكُتُبِهِۦ وَرُسُلِهِۦ لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيۡنَ أَحَدٖ مِّن رُّسُلِهِۦۚ وَقَالُواْ سَمِعۡنَا وَأَطَعۡنَاۖ غُفۡرَانَكَ رَبَّنَا وَإِلَيۡكَ ٱلۡمَصِيرُ﴾ ﴿لَا يُكَلِّفُ ٱللَّهُ نَفۡسًا إِلَّا وُسۡعَهَاۚ لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتۡ وَعَلَيۡهَا مَا ٱكۡتَسَبَتۡۗ رَبَّنَا لَا تُؤَاخِذۡنَآ إِن نَّسِينَآ أَوۡ أَخۡطَأۡنَاۚ رَبَّنَا وَلَا تَحۡمِلۡ عَلَيۡنَآ إِصۡرٗا كَمَا حَمَلۡتَهُۥ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبۡلِنَاۚ رَبَّنَا وَلَا تُحَمِّلۡنَا مَا لَا طَاقَةَ لَنَا بِهِۦۖ وَٱعۡفُ عَنَّا وَٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَٱرۡحَمۡنَآۚ أَنتَ مَوۡلَىٰنَا فَٱنصُرۡنَا عَلَى ٱلۡقَوۡمِ ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ﴾ (Quran 2:285-286)

### The three Quls
In Sahih al-Bukhari, 'A'ishah reported that every night when the Prophet ﷺ went to bed, he would put his palms together, blow into them, and recite Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, then wipe as much of his body as he could, starting with his head, face and the front of his body. He did this three times.

### Surah Al-Mulk and Surah As-Sajdah
At-Tirmidhi reported from Jabir that the Prophet ﷺ would not sleep until he had recited Alif Lam Mim Tanzil (As-Sajdah) and Tabarak (Al-Mulk). Al-Albani graded it authentic.

### Surah Al-Kafirun
Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi reported that the Prophet ﷺ told Nawfal al-Ashja'i to recite Surah Al-Kafirun and then sleep at its end, because it is a declaration of freedom from associating partners with Allah. It is graded hasan.

## Playing the Quran for Children
- **Bedtime**: playing a short surah for children as they fall asleep is a beautiful habit that helps them memorise. But also **recite the bedtime adhkar with them** yourself, so they learn the Sunnah.
- **In the car**: one of the best times to repeat short surahs.
- **During play**: background recitation during noisy play is less beneficial; it is better to choose short, focused listening times.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Relying on a recording instead of reciting**: playing Surah Al-Baqarah daily while nobody in the house ever recites it.
2. **Playing it amid chatter**: talking loudly over a recitation without paying attention.
3. **Playing it loudly enough to disturb neighbours** or people who are sleeping or sick.
4. **Leaving it playing in unsuitable places**, such as bathrooms.
5. **Believing in a "protective" effect that is not established**, treating the recording like an amulet.

## A Suggested Plan for the Family
- **After Fajr**: one family member recites a portion of Surah Al-Baqarah aloud at home.
- **During the day**: play a clear recitation at times when you can listen, such as in the car or while cooking.
- **Evening**: a short family gathering to read a page together, with its meaning.
- **Before sleep**: each family member recites Ayat al-Kursi, the last two verses of Al-Baqarah and the three Quls, and parents recite them with young children.

## How to Make Listening Real Worship
1. **Make an intention** before you press play: "I am listening to the words of Allah to reflect on them."
2. **Choose a time** when part of your mind is free.
3. **Follow along** with a Mushaf or translation sometimes.
4. **Respond** to verses: glorify Allah, ask for His mercy, seek refuge from the Fire.
5. **Stop when you cannot listen**, rather than letting it play unheard.

## Respecting Neighbours
Playing the Quran loudly so the whole building hears it may disturb people who are sleeping, sick, studying, or not Muslim. The Prophet ﷺ told his Companions not to raise their voices in recitation over one another in the mosque, because it disturbed others, as reported by Abu Dawud with an authentic chain. If this applies in the mosque, it applies more so to neighbours.

## Listening in the Car and on the Commute
For many people, the car is where they hear the most Quran. A few tips make this time count:
- **Choose the portion beforehand**: your memorisation or revision portion, or a surah you want to understand.
- **Prefer clear, measured recitation** so you can follow the words even while driving.
- **Respond inwardly**: you can glorify Allah and ask for His mercy in your heart while keeping your attention on the road.
- **Repeat what you know**: recite along softly with surahs you have memorised; this turns listening into recitation.
- **Safety first**: never read from a phone while driving; listening is enough.

## For Those Who Cannot Read
Elderly parents, people with weak eyesight, and new Muslims who cannot yet read Arabic can draw great benefit from listening. For them, attentive listening is a precious door to the Quran. Help them by setting up a simple device with a daily portion, and, if possible, a translation in their language, so they can understand what they hear.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Is there reward in listening to recorded Quran?
Yes, for attentive listening with the intention of reflecting, according to many scholars; reciting yourself is more complete.

### Does playing Surah Al-Baqarah keep Shaytan away?
Scholars differ; the hadith mentions recitation, so the more complete course is for the family to recite it themselves, and there is no harm in playing it as well.

### Is it okay to sleep with the Quran playing?
Most scholars see no harm; use a timer and low volume, and recite the bedtime Sunnah yourself.

### Is it okay to play the Quran in a shop?
It is better to avoid it where it will be mixed with bargaining and noise, so that it is not ignored.

### What should I recite before sleep?
Ayat al-Kursi, the last two verses of Al-Baqarah, the three Quls, and if possible Al-Mulk, As-Sajdah and Al-Kafirun.

### Is listening better than reciting?
Reciting yourself carries the reward of every letter; listening attentively is also worship.

### Should I set a timer to stop the Quran after I fall asleep?
Yes, that is better, so it does not play all night unheard, and it avoids disturbing others.

### Can I listen to the Quran while lying down?
Yes. Allah praises those who remember Him standing, sitting and lying on their sides, and listening while lying down is permitted.

## Conclusion
The Quran playing in your home is a blessing when it is **listened to** and respected, and a missed opportunity when it is mere background. A recording does not replace your own recitation, especially of Surah Al-Baqarah and the bedtime surahs the Prophet ﷺ taught. Recite them yourselves, let the family share them, play the Quran when you can listen, turn it off when you cannot, and fill your home with the voices of its own people reciting the Book of Allah.
