Surah Al-Ghashiyah begins with a question that catches the listener's attention: "Has there come to you the report of the Overwhelming Event?" Then it shows two sets of faces on the Day of Resurrection. Some are humbled and exhausted, having worked hard in this world for nothing that lasts. Others are joyful and content in a lofty garden. Between these two scenes and the close of the surah comes a simple invitation: look at the camel, the sky, the mountains and the earth. Everything you need to know about your Lord is in front of your eyes. The Prophet ﷺ recited this surah every Friday and every Eid.

This guide explains Surah Al-Ghashiyah verse by verse in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Ghashiyah
- **Number of verses**: 26.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 88, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: Al-Ghashiyah means "the Overwhelming", a name for the Day of Resurrection because it overwhelms people with its terrors.
- **Recited in Friday and Eid prayers**: in Sahih Muslim, an-Nu'man ibn Bashir said the Prophet ﷺ used to recite Al-A'la and Al-Ghashiyah in the two Eids and in Friday prayer.

## Verses 1–7: The Humbled Faces
﴿هَلۡ أَتَىٰكَ حَدِيثُ ٱلۡغَٰشِيَةِ﴾ ﴿وُجُوهࣱ يَوۡمَئِذٍ خَٰشِعَةٌ﴾ ﴿عَامِلَةࣱ نَّاصِبَةࣱ﴾ ﴿تَصۡلَىٰ نَارًا حَامِيَةٗ﴾ ﴿تُسۡقَىٰ مِنۡ عَيۡنٍ ءَانِيَةٖ﴾ ﴿لَّيۡسَ لَهُمۡ طَعَامٌ إِلَّا مِن ضَرِيعٖ﴾ ﴿لَّا يُسۡمِنُ وَلَا يُغۡنِي مِن جُوعٖ﴾ (Quran 88:1-7)

**"Has there come to you the report of the Overwhelming Event?"** The question is not asked to get information; it is asked to make the listener pay attention and feel the weight of what is coming.

**The first group of faces, "on that Day, humbled"**:
- **"Working hard and exhausted"** (*'amilatun nasibah*): commentators gave two meanings. Either they toiled in this world on falsehood and misguided worship, and their effort was wasted; or they will toil in the Fire, dragging chains and climbing its heights. Both meanings carry a warning: effort without correct faith and sincerity leads nowhere.
- **"Burning in a blazing Fire."**
- **"Given drink from a boiling spring."**
- **"No food for them except from a bitter thorny plant (*dari'*), which neither nourishes nor satisfies hunger."**

**How hot is that Fire?** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "This fire of yours that the son of Adam kindles is one part of seventy parts of the heat of Hell."

**A lesson about effort**: the faces are "working hard and exhausted", yet they are in the Fire. Hard work alone does not save anyone. What matters is whether the work is built on correct belief and done for Allah.

## Verses 8–16: The Joyful Faces
﴿وُجُوهࣱ يَوۡمَئِذٖ نَّاعِمَةࣱ﴾ ﴿لِّسَعۡيِهَا رَاضِيَةࣱ﴾ ﴿فِي جَنَّةٍ عَالِيَةٖ﴾ ﴿لَّا تَسۡمَعُ فِيهَا لَٰغِيَةٗ﴾ ﴿فِيهَا عَيۡنࣱ جَارِيَةࣱ﴾ ﴿فِيهَا سُرُرࣱ مَّرۡفُوعَةࣱ﴾ ﴿وَأَكۡوَابࣱ مَّوۡضُوعَةࣱ﴾ ﴿وَنَمَارِقُ مَصۡفُوفَةࣱ﴾ ﴿وَزَرَابِيُّ مَبۡثُوثَةٌ﴾ (Quran 88:8-16)

**The second group of faces, "on that Day, joyful"** (*na'imah*): soft and radiant with bliss.
- **"Pleased with their effort"**: they see the reward of their work and are satisfied. Notice the contrast: the first group's effort exhausted them; this group's effort pleased them.
- **"In a lofty garden"**: high in position and in honour.
- **"Where they hear no idle talk"**: no foul words, no gossip, no arguments. Peace in hearing as well as in sight.
- **"In it is a flowing spring."**
- **"In it are raised couches"**, **"cups set in place"**, **"cushions lined up"**, **"and carpets spread out"**.

The description moves through every detail of a perfect sitting place, so the reader can almost see it.

## Verses 17–20: Look Around You
﴿أَفَلَا يَنظُرُونَ إِلَى ٱلۡإِبِلِ كَيۡفَ خُلِقَتۡ﴾ ﴿وَإِلَى ٱلسَّمَآءِ كَيۡفَ رُفِعَتۡ﴾ ﴿وَإِلَى ٱلۡجِبَالِ كَيۡفَ نُصِبَتۡ﴾ ﴿وَإِلَى ٱلۡأَرۡضِ كَيۡفَ سُطِحَتۡ﴾ (Quran 88:17-20)

**"Do they not look at the camels, how they were created?"** Why the camel? Because it was in front of the Arabs every day. It carries heavy loads across the desert, survives long without water, kneels so it can be loaded, and gives milk, meat, wool and transport. A child can lead it. Yet nobody stops to think about how it was made.

**Then the eyes are lifted**: **"And at the sky, how it was raised?"** without pillars. **Then they are lowered to the horizon**: **"And at the mountains, how they were set firmly?"** **Then down to the ground beneath their feet**: **"And at the earth, how it was spread out?"**

**Notice the movement**: from the animal close to you, up to the sky, across to the mountains, down to the earth. A complete view of the world a traveller in the desert would see. The message: the One who made all this can bring you back, and deserves to be worshipped alone.

## Verses 21–26: You Are Only a Reminder
﴿فَذَكِّرۡ إِنَّمَآ أَنتَ مُذَكِّرࣱ﴾ ﴿لَّسۡتَ عَلَيۡهِم بِمُصَيۡطِرٍ﴾ ﴿إِلَّا مَن تَوَلَّىٰ وَكَفَرَ﴾ ﴿فَيُعَذِّبُهُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡعَذَابَ ٱلۡأَكۡبَرَ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ إِلَيۡنَآ إِيَابَهُمۡ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ إِنَّ عَلَيۡنَا حِسَابَهُم﴾ (Quran 88:21-26)

**"So remind, for you are only a reminder. You are not a controller over them."** The Prophet's ﷺ task was to remind; he was not responsible for forcing anyone's heart. This brings relief to everyone who calls people to good: your duty is to convey with wisdom, not to control results.

**"Except whoever turns away and disbelieves, then Allah will punish him with the greatest punishment."**

**"Indeed, to Us is their return. Then indeed, upon Us is their reckoning."** The surah closes with two certainties: everyone will return to Allah, and Allah will hold everyone to account.

## Word Meanings
- **Al-Ghashiyah**: the Overwhelming, the Day of Resurrection.
- **Khashi'ah**: humbled, lowered in disgrace.
- **Nasibah**: exhausted, worn out.
- **Aniyah**: boiling, extremely hot.
- **Dari'**: a bitter, thorny plant.
- **Na'imah**: soft and joyful with bliss.
- **Laghiyah**: idle or foul talk.
- **Namariq**: cushions.
- **Zarabiyy**: fine carpets.
- **Mabthuthah**: spread out.
- **Sutihat**: spread flat.
- **Musaytir**: one who controls or compels.
- **Iyabahum**: their return.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **Opening with a question**: "Has there come to you...?" draws the listener in.
2. **Two kinds of faces**: the face reflects the state of the whole person.
3. **Effort that exhausts and effort that pleases**: a powerful contrast.
4. **The details of Paradise**: couches, cups, cushions, carpets, painted one after another.
5. **The four signs**: camel, sky, mountains, earth, moving the eyes in every direction.
6. **"Indeed to Us... then indeed upon Us"**: two emphatic statements that close the surah firmly.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Idgham with ghunnah**: in *wujuhun yawma'idhin* (verses 2 and 8), the tanween merges into the ya'.
- **Izhar halqi**: in *naran hamiyah* (verse 4), the tanween before the throat letter ha' is pronounced clearly.
- **Ikhfa'**: in *min ju'* (verse 7), the noon sakinah is hidden before the jim.
- **Madd muttasil**: in *as-sama'i* (verse 18).
- **Ghunnah**: in *inna ilayna* (verse 25), on the noon with shaddah.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Al-A'la before it mentioned "the greatest Fire" and "the hereafter is better and more lasting", and Al-Ghashiyah describes both the Fire and the lofty garden in detail. Al-A'la said "So remind", and Al-Ghashiyah says "So remind, for you are only a reminder". Surah Al-Fajr after it gives examples of nations who were destroyed, and ends with the soul at peace entering Paradise.

## Why the Prophet ﷺ Recited It Every Friday
Every week, the Muslims gathered for Friday prayer would hear Al-A'la and Al-Ghashiyah. Think about the effect: a weekly reminder of the two kinds of faces, a weekly invitation to look at creation, and a weekly reminder that "to Us is their return". The week begins with the hereafter in front of you. If you lead a Friday prayer, reviving this Sunnah is a great good.

## Effort That Pleases You
The contrast between "working hard and exhausted" and "pleased with their effort" is worth reflecting on. Everybody works. The question is whether your work will please you on the Day of Resurrection. Effort pleases its owner when:
1. **It is sincere**: done for Allah.
2. **It is correct**: in line with the Sunnah.
3. **It is halal**: earned and spent lawfully.
4. **It is consistent**: in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "The most beloved deeds to Allah are those done regularly, even if small."

## Practical Lessons
1. **Recite Al-A'la and Al-Ghashiyah in Friday prayer** if you lead it.
2. **Look at creation with reflection**: the camel, the sky, the mountains and the earth.
3. **Check your effort**: is it the kind that exhausts or the kind that pleases?
4. **Avoid idle talk**: train yourself for a Paradise where there is none.
5. **Remind with wisdom and leave the results to Allah.**
6. **Remember the return and the reckoning.**

## How to Memorise Surah Al-Ghashiyah
- **Day 1**: verses 1–7.
- **Day 2**: verses 8–16.
- **Day 3**: verses 17–26, then revise the whole surah.

## Teaching Children
- **Show them pictures of camels** and tell them how amazing they are.
- **Go outside and look at the sky, the mountains and the ground** while reading verses 17–20.
- **Describe Paradise from the surah**: couches, cups, cushions and carpets.

## Reflecting on Creation: A Method
The four signs at the end of the surah are not just a list; they teach a method of looking at the world. Here is how to practise it:
1. **Start with what is close to you**: an animal, a plant, your own hand. Ask: who made this, and how?
2. **Lift your eyes**: look at the sky, the clouds, the stars. Ask: what holds this up?
3. **Look to the horizon**: mountains, seas, the line where the land meets the sky. Ask: who fixed these in place?
4. **Look down**: the ground you walk on, spread out and ready for you. Ask: who prepared this for me?
5. **Then turn to your heart**: if all this was created with such care, what was I created for?

The Quran repeatedly blames people who pass by the signs without thinking: "And how many a sign in the heavens and the earth do they pass by while they turn away from it." Reflection (*tafakkur*) is an act of worship, and this surah gives you a simple daily exercise for it.

## The Lofty Garden and Idle Talk
Among all the delights of Paradise, the surah singles out one that is easy to miss: "where they hear no idle talk". In this world, much of our pain comes through our ears: insults, gossip, arguments, empty noise. Paradise is free of all of it. A believer who wants that Paradise can start practising now: leave gatherings full of gossip, avoid arguments, and fill his ears with the Quran and good speech. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him speak good or remain silent."

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What is al-Ghashiyah?
A name for the Day of Resurrection, meaning the Overwhelming, because it overwhelms people with its terrors.

### Who are the "working hard and exhausted" faces?
Those whose effort was built on falsehood and misguidance, so it brought them only exhaustion and the Fire; some commentators also explain it as their toil in Hell.

### What is dari'?
A bitter, thorny plant that is the food of the people of the Fire, which neither nourishes nor satisfies hunger.

### Why does the surah mention the camel?
Because it was in front of the Arabs every day and is a remarkable creation that shows Allah's power.

### What does "You are not a controller over them" mean?
The Prophet's ﷺ duty was to remind and convey, not to force people into faith.

### When is Surah Al-Ghashiyah recited?
The Prophet ﷺ recited it with Al-A'la in Friday and Eid prayers, as reported in Sahih Muslim.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Ghashiyah?
26 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

### What does "pleased with their effort" mean?
The people of Paradise see the reward of their deeds and are content and happy with what they did in this world.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Ghashiyah shows you two faces and asks you to choose. One face worked hard and is exhausted; the other worked and is pleased. To help you choose, it opens your eyes to the camel, the sky, the mountains and the earth. And it ends with the two facts every heart should carry into the week: to Allah is our return, and upon Him is our reckoning.
