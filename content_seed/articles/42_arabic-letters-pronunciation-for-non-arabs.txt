If English (or French, German, Urdu...) is your first language, some Arabic sounds will feel completely new. That's normal. The good news: every one of them can be learned with the right explanation and a bit of daily practice. Here are the ten letters our non-Arabic-speaking students struggle with most.

## A simple test for any letter
Scholars of Tajweed give a neat trick: put a vowelled *hamza* (a) before the letter and make the letter silent, like **"ab"**, **"aq"**, **"aḥ"**. Where the sound stops in your mouth or throat is that letter's point of articulation (*makhraj*).

## 1. ح — Ḥaa (from the middle of the throat)
- **Typical mistake:** pronouncing it like an English "h" (ه), so ﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ﴾ sounds like *al-hamd*.
- **Fix:** tighten the middle of your throat, as if you're breathing on glasses to clean them, but stronger and with friction. It's a breathy, "squeezed" h.

## 2. ع — ʿAyn (from the middle of the throat)
- **Typical mistake:** dropping it or turning it into a glottal stop (ء), so ﴿نَعۡبُدُ﴾ sounds like *na'bud*.
- **Fix:** start from the Ḥaa position, then add voice. Many learners find it helps to say "aaa" while gently tightening the throat, as if you were about to gag slightly.

## 3. ق — Qaaf (from the very back of the tongue)
- **Typical mistake:** saying it like an English "k".
- **Fix:** raise the very back of your tongue to touch the soft palate, further back than for "k". It produces a deeper, "heavier" sound. Try ﴿قُلۡ﴾ (*qul*) versus "cool".

## 4. خ — Khaa and 5. غ — Ghayn (from the top of the throat)
- **Khaa** is like the "ch" in Scottish *loch* or German *Bach*: voiceless and raspy.
- **Ghayn** is its voiced partner, similar to a French gargled "r".
- **Typical mistake:** turning خ into "k" and غ into "g".

## 6. ض — Ḍaad (from the side of the tongue)
The letter Arabic is famous for, often called *lughat aḍ-ḍād*, "the language of Ḍaad".
- **Typical mistake:** saying it like a heavy "d" or like ظ.
- **Fix:** press **the side of your tongue** (usually the left) against your upper back teeth, and let the sound stretch along the tongue's edge. It is heavy (emphatic) and longer than a "d". Practise with ﴿وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾.

## 7. ص — Ṣaad, 8. ط — Ṭaa, 9. ظ — Ẓaa (the emphatic letters)
These are "heavy" versions of س, ت and ذ.
- **Typical mistake:** pronouncing them light, so ﴿ٱلصِّرَٰطَ﴾ sounds like *as-sirāt* with a thin "s" and "t".
- **Fix:** raise the back of your tongue towards the roof of your mouth while pronouncing them. Your mouth should feel "full". Compare *sīn* and *ṣād* back to back.

## 10. ث — Thaa and ذ — Dhaal
- **Thaa** is the "th" in *think*; **Dhaal** is the "th" in *this*.
- **Typical mistake:** speakers of some languages (and many Arabic dialects!) turn them into "s/t" and "z/d".
- **Fix:** let the tip of your tongue touch the edges of your upper front teeth, slightly sticking out.

## Quick reference table
| Letter | Closest sound | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| ح | breathy, squeezed "h" | middle of throat |
| ع | voiced version of ح | middle of throat |
| ق | deep "k" | back of tongue + soft palate |
| خ | "ch" in *loch* | top of throat |
| غ | gargled French "r" | top of throat |
| ض | heavy, stretched "d" | side of tongue + molars |
| ص ط ظ | heavy s / t / dh | tongue tip, with raised back of tongue |
| ث ذ | "th" in *think* / *this* | tongue tip + upper teeth edges |

## A 10-minute daily routine
1. Pick **two letters** per week.
2. Say each one with the "a + silent letter" test, ten times.
3. Read three Quranic words containing each letter.
4. Record yourself and compare with a reciter such as Sheikh Al-Husary on our [Quran pages](/quran/).
