Surah 'Abasa begins in a way that surprises many readers: with Allah gently reproaching His Prophet ﷺ. While he was busy speaking to the leaders of Quraysh, hoping they would accept Islam, a blind man came to him asking to be taught. The Prophet ﷺ frowned and turned away, and Allah revealed verses that made this moment a lesson for the whole Ummah: the value of a person is not in his status but in his desire for guidance. The surah then turns to man himself: his ingratitude, his creation, the food on his plate, and finally the terrifying Day when he will run away from the people he loves most.

This guide explains Surah 'Abasa verse by verse in English, with the authentic account of its revelation and practical lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah 'Abasa
- **Number of verses**: 42.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 80, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: 'Abasa means "he frowned", from its first word.
- **Main themes**: the value of sincere seekers, the honour of the Quran, man's ingratitude, and the Day of Resurrection.

## The Reason for Revelation
At-Tirmidhi reported from 'A'ishah that the verses "He frowned and turned away" were revealed about Ibn Umm Maktum, the blind man. He came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and said, "O Messenger of Allah, guide me," while one of the leaders of the idolaters was with him. The Prophet ﷺ turned away from him and turned towards the other man, saying, "Do you see anything wrong in what I say?" and he said, "No." So this was revealed. At-Tirmidhi graded it *hasan gharib*, and al-Albani graded it authentic.

**Who was Ibn Umm Maktum?** 'Abdullah (or 'Amr) ibn Umm Maktum was one of the early Muslims and a muezzin of the Prophet ﷺ. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Bilal calls the adhan at night, so eat and drink until Ibn Umm Maktum calls the adhan." The Prophet ﷺ also left him in charge of Madinah more than once when he went out on expeditions. The man who was turned away became a man of honour.

## Verses 1–10: The Gentle Reproach
﴿عَبَسَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَن جَآءَهُ ٱلۡأَعۡمَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَمَا يُدۡرِيكَ لَعَلَّهُۥ يَزَّكَّىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَوۡ يَذَّكَّرُ فَتَنفَعَهُ ٱلذِّكۡرَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَمَّا مَنِ ٱسۡتَغۡنَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَأَنتَ لَهُۥ تَصَدَّىٰ﴾ ﴿وَمَا عَلَيۡكَ أَلَّا يَزَّكَّىٰ﴾ ﴿وَأَمَّا مَن جَآءَكَ يَسۡعَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَهُوَ يَخۡشَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَأَنتَ عَنۡهُ تَلَهَّىٰ﴾ (Quran 80:1-10)

**Notice the gentleness of the reproach**: Allah did not say "You frowned"; He said **"He frowned and turned away"**, in the third person, as a mark of respect for the Prophet ﷺ. Then the address turns directly to him: **"And what would make you know? Perhaps he might be purified, or be reminded and the reminder benefit him."**

**The comparison**:
- **The one who considers himself free of need** (the rich leader): **"to him you give attention"**, though **"it is not on you if he is not purified"**.
- **The one who came hurrying to you, fearing Allah** (the blind man): **"from him you are distracted"**.

**The Prophet's ﷺ intention was good**: he hoped that if the leaders accepted Islam, many would follow them. But Allah taught him and us a principle: the sincere seeker comes first, whatever his status. Guidance is in Allah's hands, and our job is to give our attention to those who want it.

## Verses 11–16: The Honoured Scrolls
﴿كَلَّآ إِنَّهَا تَذۡكِرَةࣱ﴾ ﴿فَمَن شَآءَ ذَكَرَهُۥ﴾ ﴿فِي صُحُفٖ مُّكَرَّمَةٖ﴾ ﴿مَّرۡفُوعَةٖ مُّطَهَّرَةِۭ﴾ ﴿بِأَيۡدِي سَفَرَةٖ﴾ ﴿كِرَامِۭ بَرَرَةٖ﴾ (Quran 80:11-16)

**"No! Indeed, it is a reminder"**: the Quran is a reminder, and whoever wills will remember it. It is written in **"honoured scrolls, exalted and purified"**, **"in the hands of scribes, noble and dutiful"**, the angels.

**A beautiful connection**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The one who is skilled in the Quran will be with the noble and dutiful scribes (*as-safarah al-kiram al-bararah*), and the one who recites the Quran and stumbles over it, finding it difficult, will have two rewards." The same words used in this surah for the angels are used for the skilled reciter. If you struggle with your recitation, keep going: you have a double reward.

## Verses 17–23: Woe to Man, How Ungrateful He Is
﴿قُتِلَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ مَآ أَكۡفَرَهُۥ﴾ ﴿مِنۡ أَيِّ شَيۡءٍ خَلَقَهُۥ﴾ ﴿مِن نُّطۡفَةٍ خَلَقَهُۥ فَقَدَّرَهُۥ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ ٱلسَّبِيلَ يَسَّرَهُۥ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ أَمَاتَهُۥ فَأَقۡبَرَهُۥ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ إِذَا شَآءَ أَنشَرَهُۥ﴾ ﴿كَلَّا لَمَّا يَقۡضِ مَآ أَمَرَهُۥ﴾ (Quran 80:17-23)

**"Perish man! How ungrateful he is!"** The surah now addresses the human being who rejects his Lord. It asks him to look at his own beginning: **"From what thing did He create him? From a drop of fluid He created him and proportioned him."** Then **"He eased the way for him"**, out of his mother's womb and along the path of life. Then **"He caused him to die and gave him a grave"**, honouring him with burial rather than leaving him exposed. Then **"when He wills, He will resurrect him"**.

**"No! He has not yet fulfilled what He commanded him"**: despite all of this, man has not done what was asked of him. Every one of us falls short; the surah invites us to admit it.

## Verses 24–32: Look at Your Food
﴿فَلۡيَنظُرِ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ إِلَىٰ طَعَامِهِۦٓ﴾ ﴿أَنَّا صَبَبۡنَا ٱلۡمَآءَ صَبّٗا﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ شَقَقۡنَا ٱلۡأَرۡضَ شَقّٗا﴾ ﴿فَأَنۢبَتۡنَا فِيهَا حَبّٗا﴾ ﴿وَعِنَبٗا وَقَضۡبٗا﴾ ﴿وَزَيۡتُونٗا وَنَخۡلٗا﴾ ﴿وَحَدَآئِقَ غُلۡبٗا﴾ ﴿وَفَٰكِهَةٗ وَأَبّٗا﴾ ﴿مَّتَٰعٗا لَّكُمۡ وَلِأَنۡعَٰمِكُمۡ﴾ (Quran 80:24-32)

**"Then let man look at his food"**: an invitation to stop and think about the plate in front of you. Allah poured down water abundantly, split the earth, and caused to grow in it grain, grapes, herbs, olives, date palms, dense gardens, fruit and pasture, **"as provision for you and your livestock"**.

**Try this**: next time you eat, trace the food back. The bread came from wheat, the wheat from a seed, the seed split the soil because water came down from the sky. You did none of this. The surah wants every meal to become an act of remembrance and gratitude.

## Verses 33–37: The Day a Man Flees
﴿فَإِذَا جَآءَتِ ٱلصَّآخَّةُ﴾ ﴿يَوۡمَ يَفِرُّ ٱلۡمَرۡءُ مِنۡ أَخِيهِ﴾ ﴿وَأُمِّهِۦ وَأَبِيهِ﴾ ﴿وَصَٰحِبَتِهِۦ وَبَنِيهِ﴾ ﴿لِكُلِّ ٱمۡرِيٕٖ مِّنۡهُمۡ يَوۡمَئِذٖ شَأۡنࣱ يُغۡنِيهِ﴾ (Quran 80:33-37)

**"The Deafening Blast"** (*as-Sakhkhah*) is a name for the Day of Resurrection, from a word meaning a cry so loud it almost deafens. On that day a man **"will flee from his brother, his mother and his father, his wife and his children"**. **"For every person that day will be a matter enough to occupy him."**

**Notice the order**: the list moves from the less close to the closest. A brother, then parents, then wife and children, the people a man would normally die to protect. On that Day he runs from them, because his own account is too heavy.

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "People will be gathered barefoot, naked and uncircumcised." She said: "O Messenger of Allah, men and women together, looking at one another?" He said: "The matter will be too severe for them to be concerned with that."

## Verses 38–42: Two Kinds of Faces
﴿وُجُوهࣱ يَوۡمَئِذٖ مُّسۡفِرَةࣱ﴾ ﴿ضَاحِكَةࣱ مُّسۡتَبۡشِرَةࣱ﴾ ﴿وَوُجُوهࣱ يَوۡمَئِذٍ عَلَيۡهَا غَبَرَةࣱ﴾ ﴿تَرۡهَقُهَا قَتَرَةٌ﴾ ﴿أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡكَفَرَةُ ٱلۡفَجَرَةُ﴾ (Quran 80:38-42)

**The faces of the believers**: **"bright, laughing, rejoicing"** at the good news they receive. **The faces of the disbelievers**: **"covered with dust, overcome by darkness"**. **"Those are the disbelievers, the wicked."**

## Word Meanings
- **'Abasa**: he frowned.
- **Tawalla**: he turned away.
- **Yazzakka**: he might be purified.
- **Istaghna**: considered himself free of need.
- **Talahha**: you are distracted, you give no attention.
- **Safarah**: scribes, the angels who write the scrolls.
- **Qadb**: herbs or fresh fodder.
- **Abb**: pasture or grass for animals.
- **As-Sakhkhah**: the deafening blast, the Day of Resurrection.
- **Qatarah**: darkness, gloom.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **The third person in "He frowned"**: a reproach that preserves the Prophet's ﷺ dignity.
2. **"Came to you hurrying" versus "considers himself free of need"**: a sharp contrast between seeking and arrogance.
3. **"Perish man! How ungrateful he is!"**: an exclamation of amazement at human ingratitude.
4. **The list of foods**: a feast of words that fills the reader with gratitude.
5. **The order of the fleeing**: from brother to children, rising in closeness and in shock.
6. **Bright faces and dusty faces**: a final picture that stays in the mind.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd muttasil**: in *ja'ahu* (verse 2) and *sha'a* (verse 22), where the hamzah follows the long vowel in the same word.
- **Ghunnah**: in *anna* (verse 25), on the noon with shaddah.
- **Iqlab**: in *fa-anbatna* (verse 27), the noon sakinah before ba' turns into a hidden meem with ghunnah.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *fa-aqbarahu* (verse 21), the qaf sakinah bounces lightly.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah An-Nazi'at ended by saying the Prophet ﷺ is "only a warner for whoever fears it", and 'Abasa begins with a blind man who came to him "fearing". Surah At-Takwir after it describes the Day of Resurrection in more detail, continuing the scene of as-Sakhkhah.

## Lessons for Teachers and Callers to Islam
1. **Give priority to the sincere learner**: whatever his social standing.
2. **Do not judge people by appearances**: the blind man became a muezzin and a governor of Madinah.
3. **Watch your face**: a frown is noticed even by those who cannot see it, and Allah sees it.
4. **Your duty is to convey**: guidance belongs to Allah.
5. **Admit mistakes**: the Prophet ﷺ honoured Ibn Umm Maktum after this, and the verses remain in the Quran for all to recite.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Welcome those who come to you for knowledge**, especially the poor and the weak.
2. **Keep reciting even if you stumble**: you have two rewards.
3. **Look at your food and thank Allah** before and after eating.
4. **Remember your beginning and your end**: a drop of fluid and a grave.
5. **Prepare for the Day when nobody can help you**: your deeds are your only companion.
6. **Smile**: in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Do not belittle any good deed, even meeting your brother with a cheerful face."

## How to Memorise Surah 'Abasa
- **Day 1**: verses 1–16.
- **Day 2**: verses 17–32.
- **Day 3**: verses 33–42, then revise the whole surah.

## Teaching Children
- **Tell them the story of the blind man** and how Allah honoured him.
- **Play "where does my food come from?"** using verses 24–32.
- **Teach them to smile at people**: it is charity.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Who is the blind man in Surah 'Abasa?
'Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum, an early Muslim who later became a muezzin of the Prophet ﷺ.

### Why did Allah reproach the Prophet ﷺ in this surah?
Because he turned away from the blind man who came seeking guidance while he was busy with the leaders of Quraysh, hoping they would accept Islam.

### Who are the "noble and dutiful scribes"?
The angels who carry and write the honoured scrolls of revelation.

### What is as-Sakhkhah?
The Deafening Blast, a name for the Day of Resurrection.

### Why will a man flee from his family on the Day of Resurrection?
Because every person will be too occupied with his own account to care for anyone else.

### What does "Then let man look at his food" teach?
To reflect on how Allah produces our food from the earth with rain, and to be grateful.

### How many verses are in Surah 'Abasa?
42 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

### What does "He has not yet fulfilled what He commanded him" mean?
That despite all of Allah's favours, man has not done everything Allah asked of him, so he should never feel self-satisfied.

## Conclusion
Surah 'Abasa teaches that the person who sincerely seeks Allah is worth more than the most powerful person who turns away. It asks us to look at our beginning, our food and our end, and it leaves us with the picture of a Day when even our families cannot help us. Let us be among the faces that are bright, laughing and rejoicing.
