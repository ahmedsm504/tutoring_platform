Muslims recite the Quran in many situations: sitting in the mosque, lying in bed before sleep, walking down the street, in the car, and in prayer. Each situation raises questions: Is it disrespectful to recite lying down? Why can I not recite in sujud, when it is the closest I am to Allah? What if someone greets me in the middle of my recitation, or the adhan begins? What should I do if I yawn?

This article gathers the rulings for these situations in one place, with authentic evidence and the views of scholars, so you can recite in every state with clarity.

## Reciting in Every Position: Standing, Sitting and Lying Down
The basic principle is that reciting the Quran is **permitted in every bodily position**: standing, sitting, lying down and walking. Allah praises:

﴿ٱلَّذِينَ يَذۡكُرُونَ ٱللَّهَ قِيَٰمٗا وَقُعُودٗا وَعَلَىٰ جُنُوبِهِمۡ وَيَتَفَكَّرُونَ فِي خَلۡقِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ رَبَّنَا مَا خَلَقۡتَ هَٰذَا بَٰطِلٗا سُبۡحَٰنَكَ فَقِنَا عَذَابَ ٱلنَّارِ﴾ (Quran 3:191)

This verse praises those who remember Allah in all these states, and the Quran is the greatest remembrance.

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah said: "The Prophet ﷺ would recline in my lap while I was menstruating and recite the Quran." So the Prophet ﷺ himself recited while reclining.

### Is reciting lying down disrespectful?
Scholars say that the **most complete** way is to sit facing the qiblah, with humility, calm and dignity, because that is the greatest form of reverence and helps reflection. But reciting lying down is **permitted without dislike** according to the majority of scholars, especially when there is a need such as illness, tiredness or preparing to sleep. Imam an-Nawawi mentioned that reciting while lying down is permitted, citing this verse and the hadith of 'A'ishah.

**Etiquette for reciting lying down**:
- Cover your legs if your position would expose them.
- Do not put the Mushaf on the floor by your feet; hold it, or place it somewhere raised.
- Keep your heart present, and do not use the recitation merely as a way to fall asleep.

### Reciting while walking
Reciting while walking is permitted, and it is a good use of time, especially reciting from memory. Keep your voice low enough not to attract attention, and stay aware of your path and safety.

### Reciting on public transport
Permitted, from memory, a Mushaf or a phone, in a low voice that does not disturb other passengers.

### Reciting while driving
**Reading from a Mushaf or a phone while driving is not permitted**, because it endangers yourself and others, and preserving life is one of the great objectives of the Shari'ah. **Reciting from memory** or **listening** while driving is fine, as long as it does not distract you from the road.

### Reciting while working
Reciting from memory while doing manual tasks such as cooking, cleaning or crafts is permitted, and it brings blessing to your time. It is better during tasks that do not demand much mental attention, so your heart still has a share of reflection.

## Why Not in Ruku' and Sujud?
This is an important issue that many people who pray do not know. In Sahih Muslim, Ibn 'Abbas reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: **"I have been forbidden to recite the Quran while bowing or prostrating. As for bowing, glorify the Lord in it; as for prostration, strive hard in supplication, for it is most likely that you will be answered."**

Also in Sahih Muslim, 'Ali ibn Abi Talib said: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade me to recite the Quran while bowing or prostrating."

### Why this prohibition?
Scholars have mentioned several reasons:
1. **Each pillar of the prayer has its own remembrance**: standing is for recitation, bowing is for glorifying Allah, and prostration is for supplication. Keeping these distinct is part of the perfection of the prayer.
2. **Bowing and prostration are states of lowliness and submission**, suited to glorification and supplication, while the Quran, the speech of Allah the Most High, is recited while standing upright, out of reverence for it.

### What if someone recites in ruku' or sujud?
The majority of scholars hold that reciting in bowing and prostration is **disliked** and does not invalidate the prayer, though it goes against the Sunnah. Some scholars held it to be forbidden based on the apparent meaning of the prohibition, while there is near agreement that the prayer remains valid.

## Making Du'a with Quranic Verses in Sujud
This is a frequent question: may I say in sujud "Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire", which is a verse of the Quran?

Many scholars hold that it is **permitted to make du'a in prostration with Quranic supplications when the intention is supplication, not recitation**. The prohibition is about reciting the Quran as recitation; here the person intends du'a, and their words happen to match the words of the Quran. Examples:

﴿وَمِنۡهُم مَّن يَقُولُ رَبَّنَآ ءَاتِنَا فِي ٱلدُّنۡيَا حَسَنَةٗ وَفِي ٱلۡأٓخِرَةِ حَسَنَةٗ وَقِنَا عَذَابَ ٱلنَّارِ﴾ (Quran 2:201)

﴿رَبَّنَا لَا تُزِغۡ قُلُوبَنَا بَعۡدَ إِذۡ هَدَيۡتَنَا وَهَبۡ لَنَا مِن لَّدُنكَ رَحۡمَةًۚ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلۡوَهَّابُ﴾ (Quran 3:8)

﴿قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمۡنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمۡ تَغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَتَرۡحَمۡنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلۡخَٰسِرِينَ﴾ (Quran 7:23)

It is best to fill your prostration with the authentic prophetic supplications, and add Quranic du'as with the intention of supplication.

### The prostration of recitation
If the imam recites a verse of prostration and prostrates, that is a separate prostration in which the usual remembrances of prostration are said. It is not reciting in prostration.

## What to Say in Ruku' and Sujud Instead
### In ruku'
- "Subhana Rabbiyal-'Azim" (Glory be to my Lord, the Most Great), three times or more, reported by Muslim from Hudhayfah.
- "Subbuhun Quddusun, Rabbul-mala'ikati war-ruh" (Perfect, Holy, Lord of the angels and the Spirit), reported by Muslim.

### In sujud
- "Subhana Rabbiyal-A'la" (Glory be to my Lord, the Most High), reported by Muslim.
- "Allahummaghfir li dhanbi kullahu, diqqahu wa jillahu, wa awwalahu wa akhirahu, wa 'alaniyatahu wa sirrahu" (O Allah, forgive me all my sins, small and great, first and last, open and secret), reported by Muslim.
- Any du'a for the good of this world and the next, because the Prophet ﷺ said: "The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so increase in supplication," reported by Muslim.

## Interrupting Your Recitation: When Is It Allowed?
The general principle is to avoid interrupting recitation with speech without a need, out of respect for the Quran. But there are situations in which you may pause.

### 1. Returning a greeting
If someone greets you while you are reciting, should you reply? Scholars have two views:
- **Many scholars**: reply to the greeting, because replying is obligatory, then seek refuge again and continue. This was an-Nawawi's view.
- **Others**: it is not recommended to greet someone absorbed in recitation; if they are greeted, they may reply by gesture or by word.

It is better for someone arriving not to greet a person reciting aloud if it would interrupt them; but if they do greet, the reciter replies.

### 2. Hearing the adhan
If you hear the adhan while reciting, the majority of scholars say it is better to **pause your recitation and repeat after the muezzin**, because repeating the adhan is a Sunnah tied to its time that will be missed when the adhan ends, while your recitation will not be missed. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "When you hear the call, say what the muezzin says." Then return to your recitation after the du'a that follows the adhan.

### 3. Sneezing
If you sneeze, say "Al-hamdu lillah". If someone else sneezes and praises Allah, reply "Yarhamuk Allah", then return to your recitation.

### 4. Yawning
It is recommended to **stop reciting until the yawn has passed**, then continue, so the letters are not distorted, and out of respect for the Quran. In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "When one of you yawns, let him hold his hand over his mouth, for Shaytan enters." Some of the early scholars mentioned that the reciter should stop until the yawn ends.

### 5. A need to speak
If you need to speak about something important, such as an urgent question or warning someone of danger, you may do so, then return.

### Do you repeat the isti'adhah after interrupting?
- **If the interruption was brief and related to the recitation**, such as asking about a verse or correcting a mistake, there is no need to repeat it.
- **If the interruption was unrelated speech or a long break**, it is recommended to repeat the isti'adhah, as the next recitation is like a new one.
- **If you paused for a reason such as the adhan or a sneeze** and returned immediately, most scholars say there is no need to repeat it, though repeating it is good.

### Where do you resume?
It is best to resume from **a place where it is good to begin**, such as the start of the verse or the start of a complete phrase, not from the middle of a word or a phrase connected to what came before.

## Other Situations People Ask About
### Reciting while eating
Do not recite with food in your mouth, because the letters will not come out correctly and it is not fitting etiquette. Between mouthfuls or after eating is fine.

### Reciting in noisy public places
Permitted with a low voice, while avoiding places with open wrongdoing or a lot of idle talk, out of respect for the Quran.

### Reciting in the bathroom
Reciting aloud in a place where one relieves oneself is not permitted, out of respect for the Quran.

### Reciting with jokes and laughter
The Quran should not be mixed with jokes and laughter, nor should verses be quoted in a mocking or frivolous context; this is a form of disrespect.

## Reciting When Ill
A sick person who cannot sit up may recite lying down or on their side. Someone who cannot hold a Mushaf may recite from memory or listen. Someone whose tongue is heavy may recite as much as they can. In Sahih al-Bukhari: **"When a servant falls ill or travels, there is written for him the like of what he used to do when he was resident and healthy."** So if illness prevents you from your usual daily portion, the reward of your regular habit continues, by Allah's grace.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can I recite the Quran lying down?
Yes. The Prophet ﷺ recited while reclining, though sitting is more complete.

### Can I recite the Quran in ruku' or sujud?
No. The Prophet ﷺ said he was forbidden to recite while bowing or prostrating, as reported in Sahih Muslim.

### Can I make du'a with a Quranic verse in sujud?
Yes, with the intention of supplication, according to many scholars.

### Should I stop reciting when I hear the adhan?
Yes, it is better to pause, repeat after the muezzin, then return to your recitation.

### Do I repeat the isti'adhah after speaking during recitation?
If the break was long or about something unrelated, it is recommended; for a short related pause it is not needed.

### Can I recite while driving?
From memory or by listening, yes; never read from a phone or Mushaf while driving.

### Does reciting in sujud invalidate the prayer?
The majority of scholars hold that it is disliked but does not invalidate the prayer.

## Conclusion
You may recite the Quran standing, sitting, lying down or walking, and the Prophet ﷺ himself recited while reclining, though sitting with humility is the most complete. In prayer, recitation belongs to standing: bowing is for glorifying Allah and prostration for supplication, so do not recite in ruku' or sujud, though you may make du'a with Quranic words in prostration. Pause for the adhan and a yawn, return greetings, resume from a fitting place, and keep the Quran with you in every state of your day.
