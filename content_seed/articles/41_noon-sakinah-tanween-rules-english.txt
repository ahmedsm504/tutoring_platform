If you are learning Tajweed, the rules of **Noon Sakinah and Tanween** are usually the first chapter you study, because they appear on almost every page of the Quran. Master these four rules and your recitation will improve noticeably within weeks.

## What are Noon Sakinah and Tanween?
- **Noon Sakinah (نْ)** is a Noon with no vowel on it, such as in ﴿مِنۡ﴾ (*min*).
- **Tanween** is the double vowel at the end of a noun (ـً ـٌ ـٍ). It is pronounced as an extra Noon Sakinah that is not written, for example ﴿عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ﴾ (*ʿalīmun ḥakīm*).

Because Tanween is really a hidden Noon Sakinah, both follow exactly the same rules. **What decides the rule is the letter that comes right after the Noon or Tanween.**

## Rule 1: Izhar (clear pronunciation)
Pronounce the Noon clearly, without extra nasal sound (ghunnah), when it is followed by one of the **six throat letters**: ء ه ع ح غ خ.

Examples:
- ﴿مَنۡ ءَامَنَ﴾ (*man āmana*)
- ﴿مِنۡهُمۡ﴾ (*minhum*)
- ﴿مِّنۡ غِلٍّ﴾ (*min ghillin*)

**Tip:** say the Noon clearly, then move straight to the next letter. Do not add a pause or bounce.

## Rule 2: Idgham (merging)
The Noon merges into the next letter, so the two become one doubled letter. Idgham happens with six letters, remembered in the word **يرملون** (*yarmalūn*).

### Idgham with ghunnah
With the four letters of **ينمو** (ي ن م و), the Noon merges and a nasal sound of **two counts** remains:
- ﴿مَن يَقُولُ﴾ is read *may-yaqūl*
- ﴿مِّن مَّالٍ﴾ is read *mim-māl*

### Idgham without ghunnah
With **ل** and **ر**, the Noon merges completely with no nasal sound:
- ﴿مِّن رَّبِّهِمۡ﴾ is read *mir-rabbihim*
- ﴿غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ﴾ is read *ghafūrur-raḥīm*

**Important exception:** Idgham only happens **between two words**. Inside a single word, the Noon stays clear. There are only four such words in the Quran: ﴿ٱلدُّنۡيَا﴾, ﴿بُنۡيَٰنٌ﴾, ﴿قِنۡوَانٌ﴾ and ﴿صِنۡوَانٌ﴾.

## Rule 3: Iqlab (changing)
When the Noon is followed by **ب (Baa)**, it changes into a hidden **Meem** with ghunnah of two counts. In the Mushaf you'll see a small Meem above the Noon.
- ﴿مِنۢ بَعۡدِ﴾ is read *mim-baʿdi*
- ﴿سَمِيعُۢ بَصِيرٌ﴾

**How:** close your lips gently (don't press), hold the nasal sound for two counts, then say the Baa.

## Rule 4: Ikhfa (hiding)
Ikhfa is between clear pronunciation and merging. You don't pronounce a full Noon, and you don't merge it either. Instead, you **hide** it with a ghunnah of two counts while your tongue gets ready for the next letter. It applies to the **remaining 15 letters**:

ص ذ ث ك ج ش ق س د ط ز ف ت ض ظ

Examples:
- ﴿يُنفِقُونَ﴾ (*yunfiqūn*)
- ﴿أَنتُمۡ﴾ (*antum*)
- ﴿مِن قَبۡلُ﴾ (*min qabl*)

**Tip:** in Ikhfa, the tip of your tongue should **not** press firmly behind your top teeth as it does for a normal "n".

## Common beginner mistakes
1. **Holding the ghunnah too long**, so it sounds like a long vowel.
2. **Pronouncing a clear "n" where Ikhfa is required**, as in *antum*. This is the most common mistake we hear.
3. **Pressing the lips hard** in Iqlab.
4. **Merging inside one word**, for example saying *ad-dunyā* as *ad-duyyā*.
