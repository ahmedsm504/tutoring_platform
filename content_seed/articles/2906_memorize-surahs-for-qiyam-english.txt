Many Muslims pray Tarawih or night prayer at home in Ramadan: mothers who cannot leave young children, people who are unwell, those who live far from a mosque, and those who want to add more prayer in the last part of the night. Sooner or later they face the same question: **what should I recite?** If you only know the shortest surahs, night prayer can turn into a quick repetition of Al-Ikhlas and the two Mu'awwidhat, and you miss what makes qiyam special: standing long before Allah with verses that move your heart.

This guide explains why it is worth praying with what you have memorized, which surahs suit each level, how to memorize them in the weeks before Ramadan, how to spread them across the rak'ahs, when you might read from a mushaf, and the most common mistakes.

## Why Pray With What You Have Memorized?

Night prayer is among the greatest acts of worship. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever stands in prayer in Ramadan out of faith and seeking reward will have his past sins forgiven" (al-Bukhari and Muslim). And Allah describes His servants:

﴿وَٱلَّذِينَ يَبِيتُونَ لِرَبِّهِمۡ سُجَّدٗا وَقِيَٰمٗا﴾ (Quran 25:64)

Praying with memorized surahs has advantages you will not find elsewhere:
- **Focus.** Reciting from memory lets you lower your gaze to your place of prostration and give your heart to the meaning.
- **Retention.** Every verse you recite in prayer settles in your memory more than ten readings outside it. Many hafiz say that what is not prayed with slips away.
- **Length without boredom.** With a stock of medium-length surahs, you can stand long without repeating yourself.
- **Portability.** You need no mushaf or phone, so you can pray anywhere: travelling, at relatives' homes, in the dark.

The Prophet ﷺ prayed long at night. Hudhayfah said he prayed with him one night and he recited Al-Baqarah, then An-Nisa', then Al 'Imran in a single rak'ah, reciting slowly (Muslim). We cannot do that, but we learn from it that **night prayer is unhurried recitation**, not a race.

## Know Your Level First

| Your level | What you know now | Your goal before Ramadan |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Al-Fatiha and short surahs | Complete Juz Amma, or its main medium surahs |
| Intermediate | All of Juz Amma | Juz Tabarak, or part of it |
| Advanced | Juz Amma and Tabarak | Selected surahs such as Ya-Sin, Ar-Rahman, Al-Waqi'ah, Al-Kahf |
| Hafiz | Many juz or the whole Quran | Focused review of what you will pray with each night |

Do not underestimate yourself as a beginner. Someone who knows Juz Amma can pray eight unhurried rak'ahs without repeating a single surah, as the plan below shows.

## Suggested Surahs for Each Level

### Beginners: the medium surahs of Juz Amma
If you only know the shortest surahs, start with these medium-length ones, full of meanings that suit the night:
- **Al-A'la** (19 verses): the Prophet ﷺ recited it in witr (Abu Dawud and an-Nasa'i, graded sahih by al-Albani).
- **Al-Ghashiyah** (26 verses).
- **Al-Fajr** (30 verses).
- **Ash-Shams, Al-Layl and Ad-Duha**.
- **Al-Buruj and At-Tariq**.
- **An-Naba' and An-Nazi'at**, the two longest surahs of the juz, if you can.

### Intermediate: Juz Tabarak
Juz Tabarak is wonderful for night prayer: Makkan surahs with short verses and a strong rhythm.
- **Al-Mulk** (30 verses): recited every night, and a gift that stays with you after Ramadan.
- **Al-Muzzammil** (20 verses): revealed about night prayer itself, so it is the most fitting surah to learn for qiyam.
- **Al-Insan** (31 verses): describing the righteous and their reward.
- **Nuh, Al-Jinn, Al-Qalam, Al-Haqqah and Al-Ma'arij**.

The opening of Al-Muzzammil commands the Prophet ﷺ to pray at night:

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلۡمُزَّمِّلُ﴾ ﴿قُمِ ٱلَّيۡلَ إِلَّا قَلِيلٗا﴾ ﴿نِّصۡفَهُۥٓ أَوِ ٱنقُصۡ مِنۡهُ قَلِيلًا﴾ ﴿أَوۡ زِدۡ عَلَيۡهِ وَرَتِّلِ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَ تَرۡتِيلًا﴾ (Quran 73:1-4)

What surah could be more fitting to memorize for night prayer than the one that commands it?

### Advanced: selected surahs
- **Ya-Sin** (83 verses).
- **Ar-Rahman** (78 verses) and **Al-Waqi'ah** (96 verses): their short verses make them easy to memorize.
- **Al-Kahf**, if you want a bigger project.
- **As-Sajdah and Al-Insan**: the Prophet ﷺ recited them in Fajr on Fridays (al-Bukhari and Muslim).
- **The endings of Al-Baqarah and Al 'Imran**: the Prophet ﷺ would recite the last ten verses of Al 'Imran when he woke at night (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

## A Four-Week Memorization Plan Before Ramadan

If you start a month before Ramadan, you can build a comfortable stock for night prayer. This plan is for the intermediate level; adjust it to yours:

| Week | New memorization | Review |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Surah Al-Mulk: five verses a day | Juz Amma: a quarter-hizb a day |
| 2 | Al-Muzzammil and the start of Al-Insan | Al-Mulk + a quarter-hizb of Juz Amma |
| 3 | The rest of Al-Insan, and Surah Nuh | Al-Mulk and Al-Muzzammil + Juz Amma |
| 4 | Surah Al-Jinn, and a full review | All new surahs twice, and one full night's prayer with them |

In the final week, **pray with your new surahs**, even two rak'ahs a night. That is the real test: many people memorize a surah sitting down and forget it standing in prayer, because prayer needs a different kind of presence. And prayer itself is the strongest way to fix a surah in memory. Our [Quran revision system](/blog/quran-revision-system-english/) explains how to keep new surahs from slipping.

### A simple daily memorization routine
1. **Listen** to the new passage from a precise reciter three times while following in the mushaf.
2. **Read it** aloud from the mushaf five times.
3. **Repeat it** verse by verse until you can say it without looking, then link each verse to the one before.
4. **Pray with it** in a sunnah or voluntary prayer the same day.
5. **Review it** once before sleeping.

## How to Divide Your Surahs Across the Rak'ahs

A common question: "I only know Juz Amma. How can I pray eight rak'ahs without boring repetition?" Here is a suggested division:

| Rak'ahs | First rak'ah | Second rak'ah |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | An-Naba' | An-Nazi'at |
| 3–4 | 'Abasa and At-Takwir | Al-Infitar and Al-Mutaffifin |
| 5–6 | Al-Inshiqaq and Al-Buruj | At-Tariq, Al-A'la and Al-Ghashiyah |
| 7–8 | Al-Fajr and Al-Balad | Ash-Shams, Al-Layl, Ad-Duha and Ash-Sharh |
| Witr | Al-A'la | Al-Kafirun, then Al-Ikhlas |

With this division you pray almost the whole of Juz Amma in one night. The next night, choose a different order, or recite more slowly. Reciting Al-A'la, Al-Kafirun and Al-Ikhlas in witr is reported from the Prophet ﷺ (Abu Dawud and an-Nasa'i, graded sahih by al-Albani).

If you know Juz Tabarak, give each night two of its surahs plus one from Juz Amma, cycling through everything you know each week.

### Can I repeat the same surah?
Yes, repeating a surah or verse in prayer is permissible. It is better to vary your recitation if you can, and to lengthen your prayer through slow recitation rather than repetition. But if a verse captures your heart, repeat it: that is reflection.

## Reading From a Mushaf in Night Prayer

If your memorization is not enough yet, do not deprive yourself of night prayer. A number of scholars permitted reading from a mushaf in voluntary prayers such as qiyam and Tarawih, citing a report in al-Bukhari (without a full chain) that 'A'ishah was led in prayer by her servant Dhakwan reading from a mushaf. Scholars differ on the issue, so it is best to work on memorizing and use the mushaf as support for lengthening the prayer rather than a permanent substitute.

If you do read from a mushaf:
- Place it on a raised stand or table to minimise movement.
- Prepare your page before you start, so you are not searching mid-prayer.
- Make at least part of your prayer from memory, even the final two rak'ahs.

## Verses to Pause On in Night Prayer

It is from the Sunnah to pause with the verses: to ask Allah at verses of mercy, seek refuge at verses of punishment, and glorify Him at verses of praise, as Hudhayfah described the Prophet's ﷺ night recitation (Muslim). From Surah Al-Mulk:

﴿تَبَٰرَكَ ٱلَّذِي بِيَدِهِ ٱلۡمُلۡكُ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيۡءٖ قَدِيرٌ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ ٱلۡمَوۡتَ وَٱلۡحَيَوٰةَ لِيَبۡلُوَكُمۡ أَيُّكُمۡ أَحۡسَنُ عَمَلٗاۚ وَهُوَ ٱلۡعَزِيزُ ٱلۡغَفُورُ﴾ (Quran 67:1-2)

Pause at "to test which of you is best in deed", and ask yourself: was my deed today the best it could be? Allah did not say "most in deed" but "best": quality comes before quantity.

From Surah Al-Insan, describing the righteous:

﴿وَيُطۡعِمُونَ ٱلطَّعَامَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِۦ مِسۡكِينٗا وَيَتِيمٗا وَأَسِيرًا﴾ ﴿إِنَّمَا نُطۡعِمُكُمۡ لِوَجۡهِ ٱللَّهِ لَا نُرِيدُ مِنكُمۡ جَزَآءٗ وَلَا شُكُورًا﴾ (Quran 76:8-9)

In Ramadan this verse has a special flavour, since feeding others is among the greatest deeds of the month. When you recite it at night, ask Allah to make you one of them, and intend to feed a fasting person the next day.

## Common Mistakes

- **Rushing.** Some people recite faster alone than with a congregation because nobody is listening. Night prayer is for unhurried recitation. See our guide to [the three speeds of recitation](/blog/quran-recitation-speeds-english/).
- **Neglecting tajweed in the name of focus.** Focus does not mean dropping the rules of madd and ghunnah; correct recitation increases focus.
- **No basmalah between surahs.** When you recite two surahs in one rak'ah in the riwayah of Hafs, say the basmalah between them, except between Al-Anfal and At-Tawbah.
- **Exhausting yourself in the first nights.** Someone who prays two hours on the first night often stops by the second week. Start with what you can sustain and build up. The Prophet ﷺ said the most beloved deeds to Allah are the most consistent, even if small (al-Bukhari and Muslim).
- **Memorizing without reviewing.** A new surah every night, and by the last ten you have forgotten the first week's. Give every new surah a review.

## For Mothers Praying at Home

- **Split your night prayer.** Two rak'ahs after Isha, two after the children sleep, and two plus witr before suhoor. It does not have to be continuous.
- **Start with Al-Mulk and Al-Muzzammil.** Short verses, perfect for praying at any point in the night.
- **Let the children pray beside you.** Even if a child prays one rak'ah and wanders off, they have learned that Ramadan nights are for prayer.
- **Listen in the kitchen.** Play the surah you are memorizing while preparing iftar, so it is fresh in your mind before prayer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many rak'ahs should I pray at home?
There is no fixed limit for night prayer. It is authentically reported that the Prophet ﷺ did not pray more than eleven rak'ahs in Ramadan or at other times (al-Bukhari and Muslim). Pray what you can, and focus on consistency and presence of heart.

### Should I pray Tarawih at home or in the mosque?
Praying Tarawih in congregation at the mosque is a practice of the Companions and has great merit. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever prays with the imam until he finishes, the reward of a whole night's prayer is written for him" (at-Tirmidhi, graded sahih by al-Albani). Whoever cannot attend may pray at home and will be rewarded for their intention if they have a genuine excuse.

### I only know the short surahs. Is my prayer deficient?
No. Your prayer is valid and complete. But memorizing longer surahs helps you pray longer with more focus, so let this Ramadan be the start, even with one new surah a week.

### Should I recite aloud or quietly at night?
Both are permitted in night prayer. The recommended way is in between, loud enough to help you focus without disturbing anyone sleeping.
