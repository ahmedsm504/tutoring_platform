**Surah Al-Fatiha** is the greatest surah of the Quran and a pillar of every unit of prayer. If you pray the five daily prayers, you recite it **at least 17 times a day**. That's why correcting it is the very first thing our teachers do with every new student, child or adult.

## Why Al-Fatiha matters so much
- The Prophet ﷺ said: "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book" (Bukhari and Muslim).
- He ﷺ said: "Al-Ḥamdu lillāhi Rabbil-ʿĀlamīn is the seven oft-repeated verses and the Mighty Quran that I have been given" (Bukhari).
- In a ḥadīth qudsī, Allah says: "I have divided the prayer between Myself and My servant into two halves, and My servant shall have what he asks for" (Muslim), and then Allah responds to each verse.

In Al-Fatiha you are speaking to your Lord, and He answers you verse by verse. Anyone who prays the five obligatory prayers recites it at least seventeen times a day, so a single mistake is repeated seventeen times a day. That is why correcting it is the first thing we do with every new student.

## 1. The 14 shaddas
Counting the Basmala, Al-Fatiha contains **14 shaddas** (doubled letters). Dropping a shadda means dropping a whole letter.

1. ﴿ٱللَّهِ﴾ in the Basmala
2. ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ﴾ in the Basmala
3. ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ in the Basmala
4. ﴿لِلَّهِ﴾
5. ﴿رَبِّ﴾
6. ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ﴾
7. ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾
8. ﴿ٱلدِّينِ﴾
9. ﴿إِيَّاكَ﴾ (first)
10. ﴿وَإِيَّاكَ﴾ (second)
11. ﴿ٱلصِّرَٰطَ﴾
12. ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ﴾
13. the Ḍaad in ﴿ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾
14. the Laam in ﴿ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾

## 2. Letter mistakes
### ح (Ḥaa) pronounced as ه (Haa)
In ﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ﴾, ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ﴾ and ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾, many learners say a light English "h". Ḥaa comes from the middle of the throat with friction. See [the hardest Arabic letters for non-Arabs](/blog/arabic-letters-pronunciation-for-non-arabs/#The-10-Arabic-Letters-Non-Arabs-Find-Hardest-in-Quran-Recita).

### ع (ʿAyn) dropped or turned into a glottal stop
In ﴿ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾, ﴿نَعۡبُدُ﴾, ﴿نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾ and ﴿أَنۡعَمۡتَ﴾.

### ض (Ḍaad)
In ﴿ٱلۡمَغۡضُوبِ﴾ and ﴿ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾, often pronounced as a heavy "d" or as ظ.

### ص vs س
﴿ٱلصِّرَٰطَ﴾ needs a heavy Ṣaad, while the س in ﴿نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾ and ﴿ٱلۡمُسۡتَقِيمَ﴾ must stay light.

## 3. Vowel mistakes that change the meaning
### ﴿أَنۡعَمۡتَ﴾ with a fatha on the Taa
It means "**You** (O Allah) have favoured". Saying *anʿamtu* (with a damma) changes the meaning to "**I** have favoured". This is a serious mistake (*laḥn jalī*).

### ﴿إِيَّاكَ﴾ with a fatha on the Kaaf
It addresses Allah: "You alone". A kasra on the Kaaf is a mistake.

### The shadda on ﴿إِيَّاكَ﴾
Pronouncing *iyāka* with a single Yaa instead of *iyyāka* is a well-known mistake that scholars specifically warned about.

## 4. Tajweed points
- **Meem Sakinah** in ﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ﴾ and ﴿أَنۡعَمۡتَ﴾ is pronounced clearly (Iẓhār Shafawī).
- **Noon Sakinah** in ﴿أَنۡعَمۡتَ﴾ is clear too, because it's followed by ʿAyn, a throat letter (Iẓhār).
- **Raa** is heavy in ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ﴾ and ﴿ٱلصِّرَٰطَ﴾, but light in ﴿غَيۡرِ﴾ (because it has a kasra).
- **Laam** in ﴿لِلَّهِ﴾ is light because a kasra comes before it.
- **The silent Haa in ﴿ٱهۡدِنَا﴾** must be heard: a light breath from the deepest part of the throat. A common mistake is dropping it (*idinā*) or giving it a vowel (*ihadinā*).
- **No qalqalah in Al-Fatiha** when you stop at the verse ends: no letter of *quṭbu jad* is silent in it. The daal in ﴿ٱهۡدِنَا﴾ has a kasrah, the daal in ﴿نَعۡبُدُ﴾ a ḍammah, and the baa in ﴿ٱلۡمَغۡضُوبِ﴾ a kasrah, so don't add a bounce where there is none.

## 5. Madd (elongation)
- ﴿ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾: **6 counts**. Shortening it is a clear mistake.
- Stopping at the end of verses (﴿ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾, ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾, ﴿ٱلدِّينِ﴾, ﴿نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾): 2, 4 or 6 counts, but keep them consistent.
- ﴿مَٰلِكِ﴾: in Hafs, the Meem is elongated naturally, 2 counts.

More in our guide to [Madd rules](/blog/madd-rules-tajweed-english/).

## A verse-by-verse walkthrough
### Verse 1: ﴿بِسۡمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾
- In the Kufan count, which the narration of Hafs follows, the Basmala is the first verse of Al-Fatiha, so the Madinah Mushaf numbers it 1.
- The **seen** in ﴿بِسۡمِ﴾ is silent and light.
- The **laam of Allah's name** is light here, because a kasrah comes before it.
- **Three shaddas**: the laam in ﴿ٱللَّهِ﴾ and the raa in ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ﴾ and ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾.
- The **ḥaa** in ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ﴾ is silent: don't let it turn into a light "h".

### Verse 2: ﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾
- The silent **ḥaa** comes from the middle of the throat.
- The silent **meem** before daal is clear.
- The **laam** in ﴿لِلَّهِ﴾ is light.
- The **baa** in ﴿رَبِّ﴾ is doubled; don't shorten it.
- The **ʿayn** in ﴿ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾ is a clear ʿayn, not a glottal stop.

### Verse 3: ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾
- The **raa** is heavy in both words, because it has a fatḥah.
- The madd in ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ﴾ is natural, two counts; stopping on ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ gives a madd due to stopping.

### Verse 4: ﴿مَٰلِكِ يَوۡمِ ٱلدِّينِ﴾
- ﴿مَٰلِكِ﴾ has a natural madd on the meem in Hafs: *Māliki*, not *Maliki*.
- The waw in ﴿يَوۡمِ﴾ is a soft letter and is not stretched when joining.
- The **daal** in ﴿ٱلدِّينِ﴾ is doubled, because the laam before it is silent (a "sun letter").

### Verse 5: ﴿إِيَّاكَ نَعۡبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾
- The **doubled yaa** in ﴿إِيَّاكَ﴾ twice.
- The **kaaf** has a fatḥah both times.
- The silent **ʿayn** in ﴿نَعۡبُدُ﴾ and the silent, light **seen** in ﴿نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾.

### Verse 6: ﴿ٱهۡدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلۡمُسۡتَقِيمَ﴾
- The silent **haa** is heard with a light breath.
- The **alif** at the end of ﴿ٱهۡدِنَا﴾ drops when joining, because a silent laam follows: *ihdinaṣ-ṣirāṭ*.
- The **ṣaad** is heavy and doubled, and the **raa** is heavy.
- The **seen** in ﴿ٱلۡمُسۡتَقِيمَ﴾ is light, and the **qaf** is heavy.

### Verse 7: ﴿صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ غَيۡرِ ٱلۡمَغۡضُوبِ عَلَيۡهِمۡ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾
- The **ṣaad** is heavy again; don't read it as seen.
- The **taa** of ﴿أَنۡعَمۡتَ﴾ has a fatḥah, and the noon and meem before it are clear.
- The **raa** of ﴿غَيۡرِ﴾ is light because it has a kasrah.
- The **ghayn** is heavy, and the silent **ḍaad** in ﴿ٱلۡمَغۡضُوبِ﴾ is heavy, from the side of the tongue.
- ﴿عَلَيۡهِمۡ وَلَا﴾: the meem before waw is clear; don't hide it.
- The **6-count madd** in ﴿ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾, with the doubled ḍaad and laam.

## Heavy and light letters in Al-Fatiha
It helps to know every heavy letter in the surah, since everything else is light:
- **Ṣaad**: in ﴿ٱلصِّرَٰطَ﴾ and ﴿صِرَٰطَ﴾.
- **Ṭaa**: in ﴿ٱلصِّرَٰطَ﴾ and ﴿صِرَٰطَ﴾.
- **Qaf**: in ﴿ٱلۡمُسۡتَقِيمَ﴾.
- **Ghayn**: in ﴿غَيۡرِ﴾ and ﴿ٱلۡمَغۡضُوبِ﴾.
- **Ḍaad**: in ﴿ٱلۡمَغۡضُوبِ﴾ and ﴿ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾.
- **Heavy raa**: in ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ﴾, ﴿ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾, ﴿رَبِّ﴾, ﴿ٱلصِّرَٰطَ﴾ and ﴿صِرَٰطَ﴾.
- **Light raa**: in ﴿غَيۡرِ﴾ only.
- **The laam of Allah's name**: light in both places.

A common mistake is making neighbouring light letters heavy, such as the seen or taa in ﴿ٱلۡمُسۡتَقِيمَ﴾ because of the qaf next to them.

## Stopping at the end of each verse
The Sunnah is to stop at the end of each verse: Umm Salamah described the Prophet's ﷺ recitation of Al-Fatiha as **stopping verse by verse** (Abu Dawud and At-Tirmidhi). Joining verses is also allowed; then the last word of a verse keeps its vowel, as in *al-ʿālamīnar-raḥmān*.

## Saying "Ameen" after Al-Fatiha
"Ameen" is not part of the Quran. It is said after Al-Fatiha as a Sunnah, with a brief pause after ﴿وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾ so it isn't joined to the surah. It can be said with a long first vowel (*āmīn*) or a short one (*amīn*), and means "O Allah, answer". The mistake to avoid is **doubling the meem** (*āmmīn*), which changes the meaning to "those heading towards".

## Mistakes typical of non-Arabs
- **ʿAyn** becomes a glottal stop or disappears: *naʾbudu* instead of ﴿نَعۡبُدُ﴾.
- **Ḥaa** becomes a light English "h".
- **Ṣaad, ṭaa and ḍaad** become light, turning into seen, taa and daal.
- **Raa** is pronounced the way your native language does it.
- **Long and short vowels** get swapped, so ﴿مَٰلِكِ﴾ loses its long vowel.
- **Shaddas** are shortened because your language doesn't double letters.

These are corrected with repetition and a teacher in a few weeks. Many non-Arab students end up reciting Al-Fatiha better than native speakers.

## The ten most common mistakes at a glance
| Mistake | What you hear | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| ḥaa as haa | *al-hamd* | ﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ﴾ from the middle of the throat |
| ʿayn as hamza | *naʾbudu* | ﴿نَعۡبُدُ﴾ with a clear ʿayn |
| single yaa | *iyāka* | ﴿إِيَّاكَ﴾ with the shadda |
| ḍammah on taa | *anʿamtu* | ﴿أَنۡعَمۡتَ﴾ with fatḥah |
| light ṣaad | *as-sirāṭ* | ﴿ٱلصِّرَٰطَ﴾ with heavy ṣaad |
| ḍaad as daal or ẓaa | *ad-dāllīn* | ﴿ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾ from the side of the tongue |
| short madd lazim | a quick *ḍāllīn* | 6 counts |
| short ﴿مَٰلِكِ﴾ | *maliki* | *Māliki*, 2 counts |
| heavy raa in ﴿غَيۡرِ﴾ | a thick raa | a light raa |
| hidden meem before waw | ﴿عَلَيۡهِمۡ وَلَا﴾ with a hum | a clear meem |

## Does a mistake affect the prayer?
Many jurists state that a **mistake that changes the meaning** in Al-Fatiha, such as a ḍammah or kasrah on the taa of ﴿أَنۡعَمۡتَ﴾, affects the validity of the prayer of someone who is able to learn and neglects it. Someone who has not yet been able to learn recites as best they can and must keep trying to learn. For detailed rulings, ask a scholar you trust.

This does not mean you should worry about every small tajweed imperfection. Hidden mistakes, such as a slightly short ghunnah, are one thing; mistakes that change a letter or the meaning are another. Fix the second first, then perfect the first.

## A two-week correction plan
- **Days 1-2**: the Basmala and verse 2: ḥaa, ʿayn and the shaddas.
- **Day 3**: verses 3 and 4: the heavy raa, and the long vowel in ﴿مَٰلِكِ﴾.
- **Days 4-5**: verse 5: the shadda of ﴿إِيَّاكَ﴾, ʿayn and seen.
- **Day 6**: verse 6: the silent haa, ṣaad, seen and qaf.
- **Days 7-8**: verse 7: the taa of ﴿أَنۡعَمۡتَ﴾, the raa of ﴿غَيۡرِ﴾, the ḍaad, and the meem before waw.
- **Days 9-10**: all the madds, with consistent lengths at the verse ends.
- **Days 11-12**: the whole surah slowly, recording yourself.
- **Days 13-14**: recite to a qualified teacher and fix what remains.

## Teaching Al-Fatiha to children
- **Start with listening**: let the child hear a skilled reciter every day before reading.
- **One verse every two days**: don't rush; a Fatiha learned correctly stays for life.
- **Letters first**: ḥaa, ʿayn, ḍaad and ṣaad before the madds.
- **Let them pray with it**: a Fatiha recited in prayer settles faster.

## Frequently asked questions
### Does my prayer need perfect tajweed?
What is required is that your recitation is free of **clear mistakes** that change a letter or a vowel. Full perfection, such as exact ghunnah and madd lengths, is something to strive for; don't abandon prayer or panic over it.

### How long does correcting Al-Fatiha take?
For most students one to two weeks with a teacher. Non-Arabs may need three weeks or more, depending on their difficult letters.

### Should I repeat old prayers if I made mistakes?
That is a question of fiqh, so ask a scholar you trust. What matters most now is to start correcting your recitation today: every coming prayer with a correct Fatiha is a great gain.

### Which reciter should I learn from?
Choose a skilled, slow and clear reciter, such as Sheikh Al-Husary's teaching recitation, which leaves time to repeat after him.

## How to check your Fatiha
1. Listen to [Surah Al-Fatiha](/quran/1/) by Sheikh Al-Husary (the *Mu'allim* teaching version is ideal) and repeat each verse five times.
2. Record yourself and compare verse by verse.
3. Recite it to a qualified teacher at least once. Letter mistakes are almost impossible to spot on your own.
