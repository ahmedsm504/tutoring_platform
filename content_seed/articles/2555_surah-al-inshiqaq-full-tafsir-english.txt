Surah Al-Inshiqaq contains a sentence that sums up every human life: "O man, indeed you are labouring towards your Lord with great exertion, and you will meet Him." Whether you are a doctor, a student, a farmer or a parent, every day you are working, struggling and moving, and all of that movement is heading in one direction: towards meeting Allah. The surah begins with the sky and the earth obeying their Lord as they should, then shows the two ways a person can arrive: with his record in his right hand or behind his back. It ends with a verse of prostration, rebuking those who hear the Quran and do not bow.

This guide explains Surah Al-Inshiqaq verse by verse in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Inshiqaq
- **Number of verses**: 25.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 84, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: Al-Inshiqaq means "the splitting open", from the first verse.
- **Prostration of recitation**: at verse 21.
- **Main themes**: the obedience of the universe, the inevitable meeting with Allah, the receiving of records, and the stages of human life.

## The Prostration in This Surah
In Sahih Muslim, Abu Hurayrah said: "We prostrated with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in *When the sky is split open* and *Read in the name of your Lord*." And in Sahih al-Bukhari, Abu Rafi' said he prayed 'Isha behind Abu Hurayrah, who recited this surah and prostrated. When asked, Abu Hurayrah said he had prostrated in it behind Abu al-Qasim ﷺ, and he would continue to do so until he met him. So when you reach verse 21, it is Sunnah to prostrate.

## A Surah That Lets You See the Day of Judgement
At-Tirmidhi reported from Ibn 'Umar that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever would like to look at the Day of Resurrection as if he were seeing it with his own eyes, let him read *When the sun is wrapped up*, *When the sky splits apart* and *When the sky is split open*." At-Tirmidhi graded it *hasan*.

## Verses 1–5: The Obedient Sky and Earth
﴿إِذَا ٱلسَّمَآءُ ٱنشَقَّتۡ﴾ ﴿وَأَذِنَتۡ لِرَبِّهَا وَحُقَّتۡ﴾ ﴿وَإِذَا ٱلۡأَرۡضُ مُدَّتۡ﴾ ﴿وَأَلۡقَتۡ مَا فِيهَا وَتَخَلَّتۡ﴾ ﴿وَأَذِنَتۡ لِرَبِّهَا وَحُقَّتۡ﴾ (Quran 84:1-5)

**"When the sky is split open, and listens to its Lord, and it is right that it should."** The phrase *adhinat li-rabbiha* means it listened and obeyed, as one who hears an order and carries it out without hesitation. **"Wa huqqat"**: and it is fitting and right for it to obey.

**"And when the earth is stretched out, and throws out what is in it and becomes empty"**: the mountains are levelled, the earth is spread flat, and it throws out the dead and the treasures buried inside it, then becomes empty. And it too **"listens to its Lord, and it is right that it should"**.

**The message**: the vast sky and the solid earth obey Allah the moment He commands. The question that follows is clear: will you, the small human being, obey Him as they do?

## Verse 6: Labouring Towards Your Lord
﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ إِنَّكَ كَادِحٌ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ كَدۡحٗا فَمُلَٰقِيهِ﴾ (Quran 84:6)

**"O man"**: every human being is addressed.

**"You are labouring towards your Lord with great exertion"**: *kadh* means hard toil that leaves its mark on a person. Every life is full of effort: earning a living, raising children, studying, facing illness and worry. Nobody's life is free of toil. **"Towards your Lord"**: all of this effort is moving towards a destination. **"And you will meet Him"**: there is no escape from the meeting.

**The practical question** is not whether you will labour. You will, whatever you do. The question is what you are labouring for. The same hours of work can be an act of worship or a waste, depending on your intention and on whether it is within what Allah has permitted.

## Verses 7–9: The One Given His Record in His Right Hand
﴿فَأَمَّا مَنۡ أُوتِيَ كِتَٰبَهُۥ بِيَمِينِهِۦ﴾ ﴿فَسَوۡفَ يُحَاسَبُ حِسَابٗا يَسِيرٗا﴾ ﴿وَيَنقَلِبُ إِلَىٰٓ أَهۡلِهِۦ مَسۡرُورٗا﴾ (Quran 84:7-9)

**"He will be judged with an easy account"**: what is the easy account? In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever is examined in his account will be punished." She said: "Does Allah not say: *he will be judged with an easy account*?" He said: "That is only the presentation (of deeds). But whoever is questioned in detail about his account will be destroyed."

**So the easy account is the presentation**: his deeds are shown to him, his sins are covered and forgiven, and he is not interrogated about every detail. Then **"he will return to his people in happiness"**: to his family in Paradise, rejoicing.

## Verses 10–15: The One Given His Record Behind His Back
﴿وَأَمَّا مَنۡ أُوتِيَ كِتَٰبَهُۥ وَرَآءَ ظَهۡرِهِۦ﴾ ﴿فَسَوۡفَ يَدۡعُواْ ثُبُورٗا﴾ ﴿وَيَصۡلَىٰ سَعِيرًا﴾ ﴿إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ فِيٓ أَهۡلِهِۦ مَسۡرُورًا﴾ ﴿إِنَّهُۥ ظَنَّ أَن لَّن يَحُورَ﴾ ﴿بَلَىٰٓۚ إِنَّ رَبَّهُۥ كَانَ بِهِۦ بَصِيرٗا﴾ (Quran 84:10-15)

**"Behind his back"**: in another surah it is described as the left hand. Commentators explain that his left hand will be twisted behind his back, a sign of humiliation. **"He will call out for destruction"**: he will cry "Woe to me, ruin!" wishing he could perish.

**Why?** **"Indeed, he used to be among his people in happiness"**: carefree in this world, never thinking of the hereafter. **"He thought he would never return"** to his Lord.

**The beautiful contrast**: the believer will be happy with his family in the hereafter, and the disbeliever was happy with his family in this world. Happiness in this world that forgets Allah turns into regret; and the worry that the believer felt in this world (in Surah At-Tur the people of Paradise say: "We used to be fearful among our families") turns into happiness.

## Verses 16–19: Stage After Stage
﴿فَلَآ أُقۡسِمُ بِٱلشَّفَقِ﴾ ﴿وَٱلَّيۡلِ وَمَا وَسَقَ﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡقَمَرِ إِذَا ٱتَّسَقَ﴾ ﴿لَتَرۡكَبُنَّ طَبَقًا عَن طَبَقٖ﴾ (Quran 84:16-19)

Allah swears by **the twilight** (the redness after sunset), by **the night and what it gathers**, and by **the moon when it becomes full**. All three are about change: the light fading into twilight, night gathering everything to rest, the moon growing from a crescent to full.

**"You will surely ride stage after stage"**: in Sahih al-Bukhari, Ibn 'Abbas said about this verse: "State after state. Your Prophet ﷺ said this." Human life moves through stages: a drop of fluid, an embryo, a child, a youth, an adult, old age, death, the grave, the resurrection, the reckoning. Just as the twilight changes and the moon grows, you are constantly moving from one condition to the next, and the last stage is the meeting with Allah.

## Verses 20–25: Why Do They Not Believe?
﴿فَمَا لَهُمۡ لَا يُؤۡمِنُونَ﴾ ﴿وَإِذَا قُرِئَ عَلَيۡهِمُ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانُ لَا يَسۡجُدُونَۤ۩﴾ ﴿بَلِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ يُكَذِّبُونَ﴾ ﴿وَٱللَّهُ أَعۡلَمُ بِمَا يُوعُونَ﴾ ﴿فَبَشِّرۡهُم بِعَذَابٍ أَلِيمٍ﴾ ﴿إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ لَهُمۡ أَجۡرٌ غَيۡرُ مَمۡنُونِۭ﴾ (Quran 84:20-25)

**"So what is the matter with them that they do not believe? And when the Quran is recited to them, they do not prostrate?"** After all this evidence, what is stopping them? This is the verse of prostration. Hearing the Quran should bring the heart, and the forehead, down in humility.

**"Rather, those who disbelieve deny, and Allah knows best what they gather"** in their hearts of disbelief and envy. **"So give them tidings of a painful punishment"**: the word "tidings" is normally for good news, and using it here is biting irony.

**"Except for those who believe and do righteous deeds; for them is a reward that is never cut off."** The door is always open: whoever believes and does good will have an unending reward.

## Word Meanings
- **Inshaqqat**: split open.
- **Adhinat**: listened and obeyed.
- **Huqqat**: it is right and fitting for it.
- **Muddat**: stretched out, levelled.
- **Kadih**: one who toils and labours hard.
- **Hisaban yasira**: an easy account.
- **Thubur**: destruction, ruin.
- **Yahur**: return.
- **Ash-Shafaq**: the twilight, the redness after sunset.
- **Wasaq**: what it gathers and envelops.
- **Ittasaq**: became full.
- **Tabaqan 'an tabaq**: stage after stage, state after state.
- **Ghayru mamnun**: never cut off.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **The sky "listened to its Lord"**: personification that shows perfect obedience.
2. **"You are labouring... and you will meet Him"**: a whole life summed up in one verse.
3. **Happiness in two places**: the believer happy in the hereafter, the disbeliever happy only in this world.
4. **Oaths by changing things**: twilight, night and the moon, matching "stage after stage".
5. **"Give them tidings of a painful punishment"**: irony that stings.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd muttasil**: in *as-sama'u* (verse 1).
- **Ghunnah**: in *innahu* (verses 13–15), on the noon with shaddah.
- **Ikhfa'**: in *kadhan fa-mulaqih* (verse 6), the tanween is hidden before the fa'.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *yad'u* (verse 11), the dal sakinah bounces lightly.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Al-Mutaffifin before it described the records of the wicked in Sijjin and the righteous in 'Illiyyin, and Al-Inshiqaq shows how those records are handed out: in the right hand or behind the back. Surah Al-Buruj after it moves from the fate of individuals to the story of believers who were burnt for their faith, showing what the "labour towards your Lord" can cost.

## Turning Your Labour Into Worship
Since you will labour anyway, make it count:
1. **Correct your intention**: work to support your family, to avoid asking people, to benefit the Muslims.
2. **Earn from halal**: the quality of the source matters more than the amount.
3. **Pray on time even at work**: do not let your labour push the prayer aside.
4. **Be patient with hardship**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said that no fatigue, illness, worry or grief afflicts a Muslim, even a thorn that pricks him, except that Allah expiates some of his sins by it.
5. **Remember the destination**: "and you will meet Him".

## Practical Lessons
1. **Obey Allah as the sky and earth obey Him**: immediately and without argument.
2. **Ask Allah for the easy account**: and prepare for it with good deeds.
3. **Do not let your happiness in this world make you forget the hereafter**.
4. **Accept the stages of life**: every stage has its duties and its tests.
5. **Prostrate when you reach verse 21**.
6. **Let the Quran move you**: if it does not, ask Allah to soften your heart.

## How to Memorise Surah Al-Inshiqaq
- **Day 1**: verses 1–9.
- **Day 2**: verses 10–19.
- **Day 3**: verses 20–25, then revise the whole surah.

## Teaching Children
- **Show them the twilight and the moon**, and explain that we too change "stage after stage".
- **Teach them the prostration of recitation** when they reach verse 21.
- **Explain receiving the book in the right hand** as a reward for good deeds.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does "the sky listened to its Lord" mean?
It obeyed His command immediately, as it is right for it to do.

### What does "you are labouring towards your Lord" mean?
Every human being is toiling through life and moving towards the inevitable meeting with Allah.

### What is the "easy account"?
It is the presentation of deeds without detailed questioning, as the Prophet ﷺ explained to 'A'ishah in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### What does "stage after stage" mean?
State after state: man moves through the stages of life, death, the grave and resurrection. Ibn 'Abbas explained it this way in Sahih al-Bukhari.

### Is there a prostration in Surah Al-Inshiqaq?
Yes, at verse 21. Abu Hurayrah prostrated in it with the Prophet ﷺ, as reported in Sahih Muslim.

### What is ash-shafaq?
The redness of the sky after sunset.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Inshiqaq?
25 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Inshiqaq tells you the truth about your life in a single sentence: you are labouring towards your Lord, and you will meet Him. The sky and the earth obey Him without hesitation; the question is whether you will. Make your labour an act of worship, ask Allah for the easy account, and bow down when His words are recited.
