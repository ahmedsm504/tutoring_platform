Surah An-Naba' opens the thirtieth juz, which is why that juz is called *Juz 'Amma*, after its first word. For many Muslims it is the first long surah they memorise as children. It begins with a question: what are they asking one another about? The answer is "the great news", the Resurrection, about which people disagree. Then the surah presents nine signs of Allah's power, each one an argument that the One who did this can raise the dead, before describing the Day of Sorting, the Fire lying in wait, the rewards of the righteous, and the regret of the disbeliever who wishes he were dust.

This guide explains the surah section by section in English.

## Quick Facts About Surah An-Naba'
- **Number of verses**: 40.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 78, the first surah of the thirtieth juz.
- **Names**: An-Naba' (The News), 'Amma, and *'Amma yatasa'alun*.
- **Central theme**: the certainty of the Resurrection, proved by the signs of creation.

At-Tirmidhi recorded from Ibn 'Abbas, grading it *hasan gharib*, that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Hud, Al-Waqi'ah, Al-Mursalat, *'Amma yatasa'alun* and *Idha ash-shamsu kuwwirat* have made me grey." The scenes of the Last Day in these surahs weighed heavily on him.

## Verses 1–5: The Great News
﴿عَمَّ يَتَسَآءَلُونَ﴾ ﴿عَنِ ٱلنَّبَإِ ٱلۡعَظِيمِ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي هُمۡ فِيهِ مُخۡتَلِفُونَ﴾ ﴿كَلَّا سَيَعۡلَمُونَ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ كَلَّا سَيَعۡلَمُونَ﴾ (Quran 78:1-5)

**Meaning:** About what are they asking one another? About the great news, about which they are in disagreement. No! They are going to know. Then, no! They are going to know.

The question is not because Allah does not know; it is to magnify the subject and draw attention. "The great news" is the Resurrection and what follows it, which the Meccans argued over: some denied it, some doubted. The answer is a double warning: they will know, then they will know, when they see it with their own eyes.

## Verses 6–16: Nine Signs of Allah's Power
Instead of arguing about the Resurrection directly, the surah points to things everyone sees every day.

﴿أَلَمۡ نَجۡعَلِ ٱلۡأَرۡضَ مِهَٰدٗا﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡجِبَالَ أَوۡتَادٗا﴾ (Quran 78:6-7)

**Meaning:** Have We not made the earth a resting place, and the mountains as pegs?

1. **The earth as a bed** (*mihad*): spread out, stable and suitable for life.
2. **The mountains as pegs** (*awtad*): holding the earth steady, as tent pegs hold a tent. The Quran elsewhere says the mountains keep the earth from shaking with people.

﴿وَخَلَقۡنَٰكُمۡ أَزۡوَٰجٗا﴾ ﴿وَجَعَلۡنَا نَوۡمَكُمۡ سُبَاتٗا﴾ ﴿وَجَعَلۡنَا ٱلَّيۡلَ لِبَاسٗا﴾ ﴿وَجَعَلۡنَا ٱلنَّهَارَ مَعَاشٗا﴾ (Quran 78:8-11)

**Meaning:** And We created you in pairs, and made your sleep a rest, and made the night a covering, and made the day for livelihood.

3. **Pairs**: male and female, through which life continues.
4. **Sleep as rest** (*subat*): a daily cutting-off from activity that restores the body. Sleep is also a small picture of death, and waking a small picture of resurrection.
5. **Night as a covering**: its darkness wraps people for rest.
6. **Day for livelihood**: a time to work and earn.

﴿وَبَنَيۡنَا فَوۡقَكُمۡ سَبۡعٗا شِدَادٗا﴾ ﴿وَجَعَلۡنَا سِرَاجٗا وَهَّاجٗا﴾ ﴿وَأَنزَلۡنَا مِنَ ٱلۡمُعۡصِرَٰتِ مَآءٗ ثَجَّاجٗا﴾ ﴿لِّنُخۡرِجَ بِهِۦ حَبّٗا وَنَبَاتٗا﴾ ﴿وَجَنَّـٰتٍ أَلۡفَافًا﴾ (Quran 78:12-16)

**Meaning:** And We built above you seven strong heavens, and made a blazing lamp, and sent down from the rain clouds pouring water, to bring forth by it grain and plants, and gardens of dense growth.

7. **Seven strong heavens** above us, built firmly.
8. **A blazing lamp**: the sun, giving light and heat.
9. **Pouring rain**, bringing out grain, plants and thick gardens.

**The argument**: the One who made all of this, the earth, the mountains, you yourselves, sleep, night and day, the heavens, the sun and the rain, can easily bring you back to life. The nine signs move from the ground beneath you, to your own self, to the sky above, and back to the earth with rain, a full circle of evidence.

## Verses 17–20: The Day of Sorting
﴿إِنَّ يَوۡمَ ٱلۡفَصۡلِ كَانَ مِيقَٰتٗا﴾ ﴿يَوۡمَ يُنفَخُ فِي ٱلصُّورِ فَتَأۡتُونَ أَفۡوَاجٗا﴾ ﴿وَفُتِحَتِ ٱلسَّمَآءُ فَكَانَتۡ أَبۡوَٰبٗا﴾ ﴿وَسُيِّرَتِ ٱلۡجِبَالُ فَكَانَتۡ سَرَابًا﴾ (Quran 78:17-20)

**Meaning:** Indeed, the Day of Sorting is an appointed time: the Day the trumpet is blown and you come forth in crowds, and the heaven is opened and becomes gateways, and the mountains are moved away and become a mirage.

*Yawm al-Fasl*, the Day of Sorting, is when truth is separated from falsehood and the people of Paradise from the people of Hell. It has a fixed time. The trumpet is blown, and people come forth in groups. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Between the two blasts is forty." The mountains that were pegs are moved until they become like a mirage: they look like something, but there is nothing there.

## Verses 21–30: Hell Lying in Wait
﴿إِنَّ جَهَنَّمَ كَانَتۡ مِرۡصَادٗا﴾ ﴿لِّلطَّـٰغِينَ مَـَٔابٗا﴾ ﴿لَّـٰبِثِينَ فِيهَآ أَحۡقَابٗا﴾ (Quran 78:21-23)

**Meaning:** Indeed, Hell has been lying in wait, a destination for the transgressors, in which they will remain for ages.

Hell is *mirsad*, waiting and watching for its people. The transgressors will stay in it for *ahqab*, ages after ages. They will taste in it neither coolness nor drink, except boiling water and foul pus, "a fitting recompense".

﴿إِنَّهُمۡ كَانُواْ لَا يَرۡجُونَ حِسَابٗا﴾ ﴿وَكَذَّبُواْ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا كِذَّابٗا﴾ (Quran 78:27-28)

**Meaning:** Indeed, they were not expecting an account, and they denied Our verses with emphatic denial.

The cause is stated plainly: they did not expect to be held to account, so they lived as they pleased, and they denied Allah's signs completely. Meanwhile "everything We have recorded in a book." Then comes the most severe sentence: "So taste, for We will not increase you except in punishment."

## Verses 31–36: The Success of the Righteous
﴿إِنَّ لِلۡمُتَّقِينَ مَفَازًا﴾ ﴿حَدَآئِقَ وَأَعۡنَٰبٗا﴾ ﴿وَكَوَاعِبَ أَتۡرَابٗا﴾ ﴿وَكَأۡسٗا دِهَاقٗا﴾ ﴿لَّا يَسۡمَعُونَ فِيهَا لَغۡوٗا وَلَا كِذَّـٰبٗا﴾ ﴿جَزَآءٗ مِّن رَّبِّكَ عَطَآءً حِسَابٗا﴾ (Quran 78:31-36)

**Meaning:** Indeed, for the righteous is success: gardens and grapevines, and companions of equal age, and a full cup. They will hear in it no idle talk and no lying, as a reward from your Lord, a generous gift made due by account.

*Mafaz*, a place of success and safety. The contrast with the previous section is exact: the transgressors have boiling water and pus; the righteous have a full cup. The transgressors hear "taste"; the righteous hear no idle talk or lies. Notice that their reward is "a gift" (*'ata'*) "by account" (*hisaba*): a gift from Allah's generosity, yet given in full measure, more than enough.

## Verses 37–38: The Day the Spirit and the Angels Stand in Rows
﴿رَّبِّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَمَا بَيۡنَهُمَا ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِۖ لَا يَمۡلِكُونَ مِنۡهُ خِطَابٗا﴾ ﴿يَوۡمَ يَقُومُ ٱلرُّوحُ وَٱلۡمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ صَفّٗاۖ لَّا يَتَكَلَّمُونَ إِلَّا مَنۡ أَذِنَ لَهُ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنُ وَقَالَ صَوَابٗا﴾ (Quran 78:37-38)

**Meaning:** The Lord of the heavens and the earth and whatever is between them, the Most Merciful. They will not have from Him authority to speak. The Day the Spirit and the angels will stand in rows, they will not speak except one whom the Most Merciful permits, and he will say what is correct.

Even Jibril, *ar-Ruh*, and all the angels will stand silent in rows. No one speaks except with permission from the Most Merciful, and only what is correct. This is the setting of intercession: it belongs only to those Allah permits.

## Verses 39–40: "I Wish I Were Dust"
﴿ذَٰلِكَ ٱلۡيَوۡمُ ٱلۡحَقُّۖ فَمَن شَآءَ ٱتَّخَذَ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِۦ مَـَٔابًا﴾ ﴿إِنَّآ أَنذَرۡنَٰكُمۡ عَذَابٗا قَرِيبٗا يَوۡمَ يَنظُرُ ٱلۡمَرۡءُ مَا قَدَّمَتۡ يَدَاهُ وَيَقُولُ ٱلۡكَافِرُ يَٰلَيۡتَنِي كُنتُ تُرَٰبَۢا﴾ (Quran 78:39-40)

**Meaning:** That is the True Day; so whoever wills may take a way back to his Lord. Indeed, We have warned you of a near punishment, the Day a man will see what his hands have sent ahead, and the disbeliever will say: Oh, I wish that I were dust!

The door is still open now: "whoever wills may take a way back to his Lord". But on that Day, each person will see what he sent ahead. Commentators mention that animals will settle their scores and then be turned to dust; in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Rights will be paid to their owners on the Day of Resurrection, even the hornless sheep will be avenged against the horned sheep." When the disbeliever sees animals become dust, he wishes he could share that fate rather than face what awaits him.

## Word Meanings
- **Yatasa'alun**: ask one another.
- **An-Naba' al-'Azim**: the great news.
- **Mihad**: a bed, a resting place.
- **Awtad**: pegs.
- **Subat**: rest, a break.
- **Libas**: a covering.
- **Siraj wahhaj**: a blazing lamp.
- **Al-Mu'sirat**: rain clouds.
- **Thajjaj**: pouring.
- **Yawm al-Fasl**: the Day of Sorting.
- **Mirsad**: lying in wait.
- **Ahqab**: ages.
- **Mafaz**: success, a place of safety.
- **Dihaq**: full to the brim.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **Opening with a question** that grabs attention.
2. **"No! They will know" repeated**: a double warning.
3. **Nine signs in quick succession**, each a short, powerful line.
4. **A precise contrast** between the Fire and the Garden in drink, speech and outcome.
5. **"A gift by account"**: generosity and justice together.
6. **Closing with a single wish**: "I wish I were dust", one of the most haunting endings in the Quran.

## Tajweed Notes
- **The opening word *'amma***: it is *'an* + *ma* merged; the nun is assimilated into the meem, giving a doubled meem with two counts of ghunnah.
- **Ikhfa** in *anzalna* (verse 14): the nun sakinah is hidden before *zay* with a nasal sound.
- **Izhar shafawi** in *nawmakum subata* (verse 9): the meem sakinah is pronounced clearly before *sin*.
- **Madd muttasil** in *as-sama'* (verse 19), *jaza'an* (verses 26 and 36) and *'ata'an* (verse 36): four or five counts.
- **Madd 'iwad** when stopping on verse endings with tanween such as *mihada*, *awtada* and *azwaja*: the tanween becomes a two-count alif, which gives the surah its distinctive rhythm.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before An-Naba' comes Al-Mursalat, with its refrain "Woe that Day to the deniers" and its mention of the Day of Sorting; An-Naba' continues by naming that Day and describing it. After it comes An-Nazi'at, which begins with oaths by the angels who take souls and continues with the Resurrection and the Great Overwhelming Event.

## Why Children Start With Juz 'Amma
Children usually begin memorising from Juz 'Amma because its surahs are short, rhythmic and easy to repeat, and because they cover the foundations of faith: Allah, the Resurrection, Paradise and Hell. Surah An-Naba', as the first surah of the juz, is often a child's first major milestone.

## A Memorisation Plan for An-Naba'
1. **Week 1**: verses 1–16, the question and the nine signs.
2. **Week 2**: verses 17–30, the Day of Sorting and Hell.
3. **Week 3**: verses 31–40, Paradise and the final scene, then full revision.
Tip: memorise the nine signs as a list, and notice that most verses end in *-a*, which helps the rhythm stick.

## Practical Lessons
1. Do not argue about the Hereafter; prepare for it.
2. Read the signs around you every day: the earth, mountains, sleep, night, day, sun and rain.
3. Use sleep as a reminder of death and waking as a reminder of resurrection.
4. Use the day for lawful work, as Allah made it for livelihood.
5. Expect the account: forgetting it is what ruins people.
6. Return to your Lord now, while the door is open.
7. Send good ahead: you will see it on that Day.

## Teaching Children
- Make a poster of the nine signs and match each with a picture.
- At bedtime, read "and made your sleep a rest".
- Explain the Day of Sorting simply: good and bad will be separated.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What is "the great news"?
The Resurrection and what follows it, which the disbelievers disputed.

### Why is Juz 30 called Juz 'Amma?
Because it begins with Surah An-Naba', whose first word is *'amma*.

### What are the nine signs in Surah An-Naba'?
The earth as a bed, mountains as pegs, creation in pairs, sleep as rest, night as a covering, day for livelihood, seven strong heavens, the blazing lamp, and pouring rain.

### What is the Day of Sorting?
The Day of Judgement, when truth and falsehood, and the people of Paradise and Hell, are separated.

### Why will the disbeliever wish he were dust?
Because when he sees the animals settle their scores and become dust, he wishes he could escape his own fate the same way.

### How many verses are in Surah An-Naba'?
40 verses.

## Conclusion
Surah An-Naba' answers the question people keep asking: is the Resurrection real? It does so by pointing to the bed you sleep on, the mountains, the night and day, the sun and the rain, and then shows you the Day when everything is sorted. Its last words are a warning and an invitation at once: the Day is true, so whoever wills, let him take a way back to his Lord, before the moment comes when the only wish left is "I wish I were dust".
