Have you ever tasted a fresh fig? Or dipped bread in olive oil? These two foods are so special that Allah begins a whole surah by swearing by them: "By the fig and the olive." Then He swears by Mount Sinai, where Allah spoke to Musa, and by the safe city of Makkah. And after these four oaths comes an amazing statement: "We have certainly created the human being in the best form."

That means you! Allah made you in the best shape and form, with a mind to think, a heart to love, and a body that can do amazing things. But the surah also teaches that people can fall from this beautiful state through bad choices, and the way to stay at our best is through faith and good deeds. In this guide for parents, we explain Surah At-Tin to children in simple English, with fun activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah At-Tin

- **Position**: surah 95, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: eight.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Name**: at-Tin means "the fig".

### From the Sunnah
Al-Bara' ibn 'Azib said: "I heard the Prophet ﷺ recite 'Wat-tini waz-zaytun' in the 'Isha prayer, and I have never heard anyone with a more beautiful voice or recitation than him" (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

## The Whole Surah

﴿وَٱلتِّينِ وَٱلزَّيۡتُونِ﴾ ﴿وَطُورِ سِينِينَ﴾ ﴿وَهَٰذَا ٱلۡبَلَدِ ٱلۡأَمِينِ﴾ ﴿لَقَدۡ خَلَقۡنَا ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ فِيٓ أَحۡسَنِ تَقۡوِيمٖ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ رَدَدۡنَٰهُ أَسۡفَلَ سَٰفِلِينَ﴾ ﴿إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ فَلَهُمۡ أَجۡرٌ غَيۡرُ مَمۡنُونٖ﴾ ﴿فَمَا يُكَذِّبُكَ بَعۡدُ بِٱلدِّينِ﴾ ﴿أَلَيۡسَ ٱللَّهُ بِأَحۡكَمِ ٱلۡحَٰكِمِينَ﴾ (Quran 95:1-8)

## Verse 1: By the Fig and the Olive

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah swears by two blessed fruits: the fig and the olive. Figs are sweet, soft and full of goodness. Olives give us olive oil, which we cook with and even use on our skin. Some scholars also say these refer to the lands where figs and olives grow, like Palestine and Syria, where many prophets lived."

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Eat olive oil and use it on your skin, for it comes from a blessed tree" (at-Tirmidhi; graded authentic by al-Albani).

### Activity: fig and olive tasting
Prepare a small tasting plate: fresh or dried figs, a few olives, and bread with olive oil. As your child tastes each one, say "Bismillah" before and "Alhamdulillah" after, and talk about why Allah mentioned them in the Quran.

### Activity: inside a fig
Cut a fig in half and look closely at the tiny seeds inside. Count them if you can! Say: "Allah put hundreds of tiny seeds in one small fruit. SubhanAllah!"

## Verse 2: By Mount Sinai

### Explaining it to your child
"Tur Sinin is Mount Sinai, the mountain where Allah spoke to Prophet Musa and gave him the Tawrah. It's a special, blessed place."

## Verse 3: By This Safe City

### Explaining it to your child
"Al-balad al-amin is the safe city: Makkah, where the Kaaba is and where the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was born. Allah made it a place of safety."

### A beautiful connection
"Scholars noticed that these places are connected to three great prophets: the land of figs and olives with 'Isa, Mount Sinai with Musa, and Makkah with Muhammad ﷺ."

### Activity: the map of blessings
On a map of the Middle East, help your child find Palestine, the Sinai Peninsula and Makkah. Mark each one and label which prophet it's connected to.

## Verse 4: Created in the Best Form

### Explaining it to your child
"Ahsan taqwim means the best form and shape. Allah created human beings standing upright, with hands that can make and build, eyes that see colours, ears that hear, a tongue that speaks, and a brain that thinks, learns and creates. No other creature on earth is like us!"

### Lesson for kids: love the way Allah made you
"Allah made you in the best form. You might be tall or short, have curly or straight hair, light or dark skin, but Allah made you beautifully. Never say bad things about how you look, and never make fun of how others look."

### Activity: amazing me
Make a list with your child of ten amazing things their body can do: run, jump, draw, read, taste, smell, hug, think, remember, pray. After each, say "Alhamdulillah". Then make a second list: ten amazing things their heart and mind can do: love, forgive, imagine, learn, care.

## Verse 5: Then We Returned Him to the Lowest of the Low

### Explaining it to your child
"Scholars explain this in two ways. Some say it means old age, when the body becomes weak. Others say it means that people who choose bad things fall very low, even though Allah made them in the best form. Being created in the best form is a gift, and our choices decide whether we stay at our best."

### Lesson for kids
"A beautiful car can end up in a scrapyard if no one takes care of it. A beautiful person can lose their beauty inside if they lie, cheat and hurt others. Good choices keep our inner beauty shining."

## Verse 6: Except Those Who Believe and Do Good

### Explaining it to your child
"But those who believe and do good deeds will have a reward that never ends! Ghayru mamnun means never cut off, never stopping. Their reward keeps going forever."

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said that when a believer becomes ill or travels and can't do the good deeds they used to do, Allah still writes for them the reward of what they used to do when healthy and at home (al-Bukhari). That's a reward that doesn't stop!

## Verses 7–8: Isn't Allah the Wisest of Judges?

### Explaining it to your child
"After all this, who can deny the Day of Judgement? Isn't Allah the wisest and fairest of judges? Yes! He knows everything, sees everything, and is perfectly fair."

### What to say after this verse
Scholars mention that when reciting "Alaysallahu bi-ahkamil-hakimin", it is good to say: "Bala, wa ana 'ala dhalika minash-shahidin", "Yes indeed, and I am among those who bear witness to that." A narration about this is reported by Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi, and scholars discuss its chain, but many encourage saying it outside obligatory prayer.

## Taking Care of the "Best Form"

If Allah created us in the best form, then looking after our bodies is a way of thanking Him. Islam teaches many habits that keep the body healthy and clean:

- **Wudu** five times a day keeps hands, face, mouth, nose and feet clean.
- **Using the miswak or brushing teeth**: the Prophet ﷺ said that if it weren't hard on his nation, he would have commanded the miswak at every prayer (al-Bukhari and Muslim).
- **Eating in moderation**: the Prophet ﷺ taught us not to fill our stomachs completely, leaving room for water and air (at-Tirmidhi, who graded it hasan sahih).
- **Sleeping early** and waking for Fajr.
- **Moving and playing**: running, swimming and games keep us strong. The believer who is strong is better and more beloved to Allah (Muslim).

### Activity: the best-form checklist
Make a daily checklist with your child: "I brushed my teeth", "I ate fruit or vegetables", "I played outside", "I slept on time", "I made wudu". Each tick is a "thank you" to Allah for the best form He gave them.

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Mirror"

Nadia stood in front of the mirror every morning and frowned. "My nose is too big. My hair is too curly. I wish I looked like the girls in the adverts." Some days she didn't even want to go to school.

One evening, her mother sat with her and read Surah At-Tin. When she reached "Laqad khalaqnal-insana fi ahsani taqwim", she stopped. "Nadia, Allah says He created people in the best form. That includes you."

"But I don't look like the girls in the adverts," said Nadia. Her mother smiled. "Many of those pictures are changed with computers. And anyway, Allah made each of us different on purpose, like flowers in a garden. A rose and a sunflower are both beautiful, but in different ways."

Then she asked: "What can your body do?" Nadia thought. "I can run really fast. I can draw. I can hug Grandma." "And what can your heart do?" "I can be kind. I help my friend with maths." Her mother hugged her. "That's ahsan taqwim. The best form, inside and out."

The next morning, Nadia looked in the mirror, smiled and said: "Alhamdulillah, Allah made me in the best form." Then she added quietly: "And I'll keep my inside beautiful too."

Ask your child: "Why was Nadia sad at the mirror? What helped her feel better?"

## What We Learn From Surah At-Tin

- **The fig and olive** are blessed foods.
- **Blessed places** are connected to great prophets.
- **Every person** was created in the best form.
- **Love the way Allah made you**, and never mock others' looks.
- **Bad choices** can make people fall low.
- **Faith and good deeds** keep us at our best.
- **The reward of believers** never ends.
- **Allah is the wisest** and fairest judge.

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Why does Allah swear by fruits?
Allah swears by things to show their importance and to draw our attention to His blessings. The fig and olive are nourishing, blessed foods, and are linked to blessed lands.

### How can I use this surah to help with body image?
Verse 4 is a powerful reminder that every person is created in the best form. Combine it with focusing on what bodies can do, avoiding negative talk about appearance at home, and praising character.

### Is it a sunnah to eat figs?
There's no specific hadith commanding figs, but they are mentioned positively in the Quran, and olive oil is mentioned in authentic hadith as blessed. Both are healthy foods to enjoy with gratitude.


### Which prayer is good for practising this surah?
The Prophet ﷺ recited it in 'Isha while travelling, so your child can recite it in 'Isha too, after Al-Fatiha, and feel they are following his Sunnah.


### Is At-Tin a good surah for beginners?
Yes. Its eight short verses have a gentle rhythm, so most children memorize it within a week.
