Many children are afraid of the dark. When the lights go off at bedtime, shadows look scary and every little sound seems loud. Some children are afraid of bad dreams, of being alone, or of things they can't explain. Allah, who created children and knows their hearts, gave us a beautiful surah to recite when we feel afraid: Surah Al-Falaq. It teaches us to say: "I seek refuge in the Lord of daybreak," in Allah, who brings morning light after every night.

Surah Al-Falaq and Surah An-Nas are called "al-Mu'awwidhatayn", the two surahs of seeking refuge. The Prophet ﷺ said no one has ever sought protection with anything like them. In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Al-Falaq to children in simple English, verse by verse, with a bedtime routine from the Sunnah, gentle ways to help scared children, activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Falaq

- **Position**: surah 113, the second-to-last surah in the Quran.
- **Number of verses**: five.
- **Type**: Meccan according to many scholars, and Madinan according to others.
- **Name**: al-Falaq means "the daybreak", the moment the light splits the darkness at dawn.

### Its virtue
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Have you not seen verses revealed tonight, the like of which has never been seen? Qul a'udhu bi Rabbil-falaq and Qul a'udhu bi Rabbin-nas" (Muslim).

## The Whole Surah

﴿قُلۡ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلۡفَلَقِ﴾ ﴿مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ﴾ ﴿وَمِن شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ﴾ ﴿وَمِن شَرِّ ٱلنَّفَّـٰثَٰتِ فِي ٱلۡعُقَدِ﴾ ﴿وَمِن شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ﴾ (Quran 113:1-5)

## What Does "I Seek Refuge" Mean?

### Explaining it to your child
"A'udhu means 'I seek refuge', I run to someone strong for protection. Imagine a little chick that sees a cat: it runs under its mother's wings. When we say A'udhu billah, we are running to Allah, the strongest protector of all. Nothing can hurt us unless Allah allows it."

### Activity: the safe place
Ask your child: "When you're scared, where do you run?" Maybe to Mum's arms or Dad's lap. Then say: "Allah is even stronger and closer than Mum and Dad. Saying A'udhu billah is like running into Allah's protection."

## Verse 1: Say, I Seek Refuge in the Lord of Daybreak

### Explaining it to your child
"Al-Falaq is the moment the dark night splits open and the light of morning comes out. Every night, no matter how dark, ends with dawn. Allah is the Lord of daybreak: He's the One who brings light after darkness. So when you feel scared at night, remember that the One you're asking for protection is the One who brings the morning."

### Activity: watch the dawn
On a weekend or during Ramadan, wake your child gently before Fajr and watch the sky together as it changes from black to dark blue to orange. Say: "Look, this is al-falaq! Allah brings the light every single day."

## Verse 2: From the Evil of What He Created

### Explaining it to your child
"We ask Allah to protect us from the harm of anything He created: harmful animals, dangerous things, people who might hurt us, and Shaytan. Allah created everything, so He can protect us from everything."

### Lesson for kids
"This doesn't mean everything is bad. Most of what Allah created is good. But some things can hurt us, like a fire, a stinging insect or a busy road, so we ask Allah to keep us safe, and we are careful too."

## Verse 3: From the Evil of Darkness When It Settles

### Explaining it to your child
"Ghasiq means the darkness of the night. At night, some harmful creatures come out, and people often feel more scared. So Allah taught us to ask for protection from the harm that comes with the dark."

### Tip for parents: helping a child afraid of the dark
- **Don't laugh at the fear**: say "I understand, the dark can feel scary."
- **A small night light** is fine and helps many children.
- **Recite together**: Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, then Ayat al-Kursi.
- **Remind them**: "Allah never sleeps, and He is watching over you."
- **Stay a few minutes** until they feel calm, then leave gently.

## Verse 4: From Those Who Blow on Knots

### Explaining it to your child
"Some bad people try to harm others with magic. They tie knots and blow on them, saying bad words. Allah taught us to ask for protection from them. And the good news is: when we say these verses, Allah protects us."

### Tip for parents
Keep this simple and calm. Children don't need detailed explanations of magic. It's enough to say: "Some bad people try to harm others in secret ways, and Allah protects us when we say His words." The focus should be on safety and Allah's protection, not fear.

## Verse 5: From the Evil of an Envier When He Envies

### Explaining it to your child
"Hasad, envy, is when someone sees something good you have and wishes you didn't have it. Envy can cause harm, so we ask Allah to protect us from envious people. And we also make sure that we ourselves don't envy others."

### Lesson for kids: protecting others from our envy
"When you see something nice that a friend has, say: Masha'Allah, barakAllahu lak, may Allah bless it for you. That protects your friend from envy, and protects your own heart too."

### Activity: the Masha'Allah challenge
For one day, every time your child sees something nice that someone else has, a new bag, a good drawing, a toy, they say "Masha'Allah, barakAllahu lak." At the end of the day, ask how many times they said it.

## The Sunnah Bedtime Routine

'A'ishah reported that every night when the Prophet ﷺ went to bed, he would put his palms together, blow lightly into them, recite Qul huwallahu ahad, Qul a'udhu bi Rabbil-falaq and Qul a'udhu bi Rabbin-nas, then wipe over as much of his body as he could, starting with his head, face and the front of his body. He did this three times (al-Bukhari).

### Activity: the bedtime three
1. Your child cups their hands together.
2. They recite Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas.
3. They blow lightly into their palms.
4. They wipe over their head, face and body.
5. Repeat three times.
Young children love doing this with a parent, and it becomes a comforting, safe ending to every day.

## Morning and Evening Protection

The Prophet ﷺ told 'Abdullah ibn Khubayb: "Say Qul huwallahu ahad and the two Mu'awwidhat three times in the evening and in the morning; they will suffice you against everything" (Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi, who graded it hasan sahih).

### Activity: the morning shield
Make a small card for your child's school bag with the three Quls written on it. Before leaving home each morning, they recite each one three times. Call it their "morning shield".

## When Your Child Is Sick

'A'ishah reported that when the Prophet ﷺ was ill, he would recite the Mu'awwidhat over himself and blow (al-Bukhari and Muslim). Parents can do the same for a sick child: recite Al-Falaq and An-Nas, blow gently, and wipe over them, while also giving them medicine and care.

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Shadow on the Wall"

Every night, five-year-old Hana saw a strange shadow on her bedroom wall. It looked like a big monster with long arms. She would pull the blanket over her head and call: "Mummy!"

One night, her mother came, sat on the bed, and said: "Let's find out what the monster is." She turned on the light, and they looked together. The "monster" was just the shadow of the tree outside the window, with its branches moving in the wind! Hana laughed a little.

Then her mother said: "Even when it's dark, Allah is with you. Let me teach you what the Prophet ﷺ did every night." She showed Hana how to cup her hands, recite Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, blow gently and wipe over herself three times. "Al-Falaq means daybreak. When you say Qul a'udhu bi Rabbil-falaq, you're asking for protection from the One who brings the morning after every night."

Hana tried it. When she reached "wa min sharri ghasiqin idha waqab", her mother explained: "That means: from the harm of the darkness. Allah protects you in the dark." Hana snuggled under her blanket and looked at the shadow on the wall. It didn't look so scary anymore.

The next night, Hana didn't call her mother. She cupped her hands and recited the three Quls by herself. In the morning, she ran into the kitchen: "Mummy, I did it all by myself! And look, the sun came out, just like al-falaq!"

Ask your child: "What was the shadow on Hana's wall? What did she recite before sleeping?"

## What We Learn From Surah Al-Falaq

- **Run to Allah** for protection when we're afraid.
- **Allah brings light** after every darkness.
- **Allah can protect us** from everything He created.
- **Night fears** are eased by remembering Allah.
- **Allah protects us** from hidden harm.
- **Say Masha'Allah** to protect others from envy.
- **The bedtime three** is a Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.
- **The morning and evening Quls** are a daily shield.

## One-Week Plan: Memorize and Practise

- **Day 1**: memorize verses 1 and 2, and talk about "seeking refuge".
- **Day 2**: memorize verse 3, and do the safe-place activity.
- **Day 3**: memorize verses 4 and 5.
- **Day 4**: recite the whole surah and start the bedtime three.
- **Day 5**: make the morning shield card.
- **Day 6**: do the Masha'Allah challenge.
- **Day 7**: recite Al-Falaq in prayer and celebrate!

## Questions to Ask Your Child

1. What does "A'udhu" mean?
2. What is al-falaq?
3. Why do we ask for protection from the darkness?
4. What is envy, and how can we protect others from it?
5. What did the Prophet ﷺ do every night before sleeping?
6. How many times do we recite the three Quls in the morning and evening?
7. Who is the strongest protector?

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Will talking about magic and envy scare my child?
Not if you keep the focus on Allah's protection. Explain briefly and calmly, and spend more time on the comforting routine than on the details of harm.

### My child still fears the dark after reciting. Is the surah not working?
Fear is natural, and reciting is one part of helping. Combine it with reassurance, a night light if needed, and staying close for a while. Over time, the routine itself becomes comforting.

### Can I recite Al-Falaq over my baby?
Yes. Parents can recite the Mu'awwidhat over their children and blow gently, as the Prophet ﷺ did for himself and as he sought protection for his grandsons al-Hasan and al-Husayn with Allah's perfect words (al-Bukhari).


### Is it okay for my child to be scared at all?
Yes. Fear is a natural feeling that Allah created to keep us safe. The goal is not to shame a child for being afraid, but to give them tools: remembering Allah, talking to a parent, and knowing that Allah is always near.
