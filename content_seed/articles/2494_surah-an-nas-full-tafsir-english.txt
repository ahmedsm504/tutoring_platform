Surah An-Nas is the final surah of the Quran in the order of the mushaf, and the second of the two surahs of refuge. Where Al-Falaq protects you from harms that come from outside, An-Nas protects you from the harm that works from inside: the whisper that enters the heart and tries to turn it away from Allah.

It is also unusual in its structure. It calls upon Allah with three names, Lord, King and God, to seek refuge from a single evil: the whisperer who retreats. This guide explains the surah verse by verse in English and gathers the authentic guidance of the Prophet ﷺ on dealing with whispers.

## Quick Facts About Surah An-Nas
- **Number of verses**: 6.
- **Type**: Meccan in the standard mushaf; scholars have discussed the point.
- **Position**: Surah 114, the last surah of the Quran.
- **Name**: from *an-nas*, mankind, which appears five times in it.
- **Paired with**: Surah Al-Falaq; together they are *al-mu'awwidhatayn*.

## The Full Text of Surah An-Nas
﴿قُلۡ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿إِلَٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿مِن شَرِّ ٱلۡوَسۡوَاسِ ٱلۡخَنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي يُوَسۡوِسُ فِي صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿مِنَ ٱلۡجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ﴾ (Quran 114:1-6)

**Meaning:** Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind, the King of mankind, the God of mankind, from the evil of the retreating whisperer, who whispers in the chests of mankind, from among the jinn and mankind.

## Verses 1–3: Lord, King and God of Mankind
﴿قُلۡ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿إِلَٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ (Quran 114:1-3)

The believer seeks refuge with three of Allah's attributes, each adding strength to the one before:
1. **Rabb an-nas, Lord of mankind**: their Creator, Sustainer and Nurturer. This is His lordship.
2. **Malik an-nas, King of mankind**: the One who owns them and has full authority over them.
3. **Ilah an-nas, God of mankind**: the only One who deserves their worship.

**Why this order?** A person first recognises Allah through His blessings and care, so Lordship comes first. Whoever is your Lord is your King who governs you. And whoever is your Lord and King deserves your worship. The three together are a complete statement of tawhid.

**Why three names for one evil?** In Al-Falaq, one name, "Lord of daybreak", is used against four evils. Here three names are used against one. Scholars point out that this reflects how dangerous the whisper is: the evils of Al-Falaq mostly harm the body and worldly life, while the whisper harms faith and the heart, and the safety of one's religion is more important.

## Verse 4: The Retreating Whisperer
﴿مِن شَرِّ ٱلۡوَسۡوَاسِ ٱلۡخَنَّاسِ﴾ (Quran 114:4)

**Al-waswas** is Satan, who whispers constantly. The word's form suggests repetition: he never tires of whispering.

**Al-khannas** is the one who retreats and hides. When a person remembers Allah, the whisperer shrinks back; when he becomes heedless, it returns.

This single word contains the remedy. If the whisperer retreats at the remembrance of Allah, then remembrance is your strongest weapon.

## Verse 5: Who Whispers in the Chests of People
﴿ٱلَّذِي يُوَسۡوِسُ فِي صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ (Quran 114:5)

He casts evil thoughts into hearts: doubts about faith, attraction to sin, laziness about worship, and suspicion and hostility between people. The "chest" is mentioned because it contains the heart.

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Safiyyah bint Huyayy reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Satan flows through the son of Adam as blood flows." And in Sahih Muslim, Ibn Mas'ud reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There is none of you except that a companion from among the jinn has been assigned to him."

## Verse 6: From Among Jinn and People
﴿مِنَ ٱلۡجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ﴾ (Quran 114:6)

The whisperer can be a devil from the jinn, who whispers directly into the heart, or a devil from among people, who whispers through words, pressure and making evil attractive, like a companion who keeps encouraging you to sin. Surah Al-An'am speaks of "devils from among mankind and jinn, inspiring one another with decorative speech to deceive."

## Word Meanings
- **Rabb**: Lord, Sustainer.
- **Malik**: King, Sovereign.
- **Ilah**: God, the one worshipped.
- **Al-Waswas**: the persistent whisperer.
- **Al-Khannas**: the one who retreats and hides.
- **Yuwaswisu**: whispers secretly.
- **Sudur**: chests, the seat of the heart.
- **Al-Jinnah**: the jinn.

## The Prophetic Remedy for Waswasah
1. **Seek refuge and stop.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Satan comes to one of you and says: Who created this? Who created that? until he says: Who created your Lord? When he reaches that, let him seek refuge with Allah and stop."
2. **Hating the whisper is a sign of faith.** In Sahih Muslim, some companions told the Prophet ﷺ that they found thoughts in themselves so terrible they could not bear to speak of them. He asked: "Do you really find that?" They said yes. He said: "That is clear faith." Your distress at the whisper shows that your faith is alive.
3. **Whispers in prayer.** In Sahih Muslim, 'Uthman ibn Abi al-'As complained that Satan came between him and his prayer and confused his recitation. The Prophet ﷺ said: "That is a devil called Khinzab. When you sense him, seek refuge with Allah from him and spit lightly to your left three times." He did so, and Allah took it away from him.
4. **You are not held accountable for passing thoughts.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah has pardoned my Ummah for what their souls whisper to them, as long as they do not act on it or speak of it."
5. **Do not argue with the whisper.** Cut it off with seeking refuge and turn to something beneficial.

**An important note:** obsessive-compulsive patterns of waswasah can be a genuine condition that benefits from professional treatment alongside the adhkar. There is no conflict between ruqyah and seeking medical help; both are legitimate means.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **No "and" between the three names**: they describe one Being.
2. **"Mankind" five times**: emphasis, and honour to humanity in being linked to their Lord.
3. **Waswas and khannas**: attack when you are heedless, retreat when you remember.
4. **The Quran ends with "mankind"**, the very ones it was revealed to guide.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Lam shamsiyyah** in *an-nas* every time it appears.
- **Ghunnah** on the doubled *nun* in *an-nas*, *al-khannas* and *al-jinnah*, two counts each.
- **Ikhfa** in *min sharri*: the nun is hidden before *shin*.
- **Madd 'arid** when stopping on *an-nas* and *al-khannas*.
- **Malik** is read in the narration of Hafs without an alif after the *meem*, so there is no madd in it.

## Why the Quran Ends With An-Nas
The Quran opens with Al-Fatihah, in which the believer says "You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help" and "Guide us to the straight path." It closes with seeking refuge from the whisperer who tries to pull people away from that path. The reader begins by asking for guidance and ends by seeking protection for it.

## Guarding Your Heart: A Practical Programme
1. **Morning and evening adhkar**, including the three surahs three times.
2. **Seek refuge when angry.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said of a man in a rage: "I know a word which, if he said it, what he feels would leave him: I seek refuge in Allah from Satan the accursed."
3. **Say bismillah when entering home and eating.** In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said that when a man mentions Allah on entering and at his meal, Satan says: "No lodging for you and no supper."
4. **Recite Surah Al-Baqarah at home.** In Sahih Muslim: "Satan flees from the house in which Surah Al-Baqarah is recited."
5. **Ayat al-Kursi before sleep**, as in Sahih al-Bukhari: a guardian from Allah stays with you and no devil comes near until morning.
6. **Choose good company**, to protect yourself from the human whisperers.

## The Last Three Surahs as a Complete Shield
The mushaf closes with Al-Ikhlas protecting your belief through tawhid, Al-Falaq protecting your body and life from external harm, and An-Nas protecting your heart from inner whispers. That is why the Prophet ﷺ recited all three together in the morning, the evening and before sleep.

## Practical Lessons
1. Remember Allah often; the whisperer retreats at His remembrance.
2. Do not follow or debate whispers; cut them off.
3. Do not panic over doubts about faith; hating them is a sign of faith.
4. Beware of human whisperers.
5. Protect your home with remembrance and Al-Baqarah.
6. End your day with the mu'awwidhat.
7. Seek help if whispers become obsessive.

## Teaching Children
- Teach them to say "a'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim" when angry.
- Teach them bismillah before eating and entering the house.
- Explain *al-khannas* simply: Satan runs away when we remember Allah.
- Make the three surahs part of bedtime.

## Recognising Human Whisperers
The final verse warns that whisperers can be human. Common signs include:
- **Making sin look small**: "Just once, it won't hurt."
- **Discouraging obedience**: "You are too young to be so religious."
- **Spreading doubt** about faith, scholars and settled truths.
- **Damaging relationships** through gossip and tale-bearing.

The remedy is distance and better company. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Musa reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The example of a good companion and a bad companion is like that of a seller of musk and a blacksmith's bellows." A friend can be like an angel helping you to good, or like a devil whispering to you to do evil.

## Remembrance That Makes the Whisperer Retreat
Since the whisperer is *khannas*, retreating at remembrance, the most powerful protection is regular dhikr:
1. **La ilaha illallahu wahdahu la sharika lah, lahul-mulku wa lahul-hamd, wa huwa 'ala kulli shay'in qadir**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, whoever says it a hundred times in a day has "a protection from Satan for that day until evening."
2. **Ayat al-Kursi** before sleeping.
3. **The last two verses of Al-Baqarah** at night: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, "whoever recites them at night, they will suffice him."
4. **Bismillah** at the start of every action.
5. **The mu'awwidhat** morning, evening and after prayers.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does "al-waswas al-khannas" mean?
The whisperer who whispers persistently and retreats when Allah is remembered.

### Why are three names of Allah mentioned against one evil?
Because the whisper threatens faith and the heart, which matter more than worldly harms.

### What does "from among the jinn and mankind" mean?
The whisperer can be a devil of the jinn or a person who encourages evil.

### How do I deal with waswasah?
Seek refuge in Allah, stop following the thought, remember Allah often, and seek professional help if it becomes obsessive.

### Do evil thoughts about faith count against me?
No, if you hate and reject them. The Prophet ﷺ called that distress "clear faith".

### Why is An-Nas the last surah of the Quran?
So that the reader, having asked for guidance in Al-Fatihah, ends by seeking refuge from the one who tries to take it away.

### How many verses are in Surah An-Nas?
6 verses.

### Does Satan have power over believers?
Not over those who believe and rely on their Lord, as Surah An-Nahl states (16:99). His authority is over those who take him as an ally.

### What is the difference between Al-Falaq and An-Nas?
Al-Falaq seeks refuge from external harms like darkness, magic and envy; An-Nas seeks refuge from the internal harm of whispering in the heart.

## Conclusion
Surah An-Nas is the final word of the mushaf, and it reads like a parting instruction: guard your heart. The enemy does not only come from outside; he whispers within. Your shelter is your Lord, your King and your God, and your weapon is His remembrance, at which the whisperer retreats. Keep these six verses close, morning, evening and night, and live inside the fortress of Allah.
