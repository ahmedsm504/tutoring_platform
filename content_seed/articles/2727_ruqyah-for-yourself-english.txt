When a Muslim feels unwell, anxious, or fears the evil eye, a common first thought is: "Where can I find someone to do ruqyah for me?" People search for a sheikh to recite over them, and some fall into the hands of charlatans who mix Quran with talismans and charge large sums. Yet the **Sunnah** teaches that a Muslim can **perform ruqyah for himself and his family**, and that ruqyah is not a secret known only to specialists.

This guide explains self-ruqyah step by step: its conditions, what is authentically reported, how to perform it, and how to recite over children, with a warning about charlatans and the importance of combining ruqyah with medical care.

## What Is Ruqyah?
Ruqyah is **reciting the Quran and permitted supplications over a sick or afflicted person**, seeking cure from Allah. The Prophet ﷺ recited ruqyah over himself and his Companions, Jibril recited it over him, and he approved his Companions' ruqyah.

In Sahih Muslim, 'Awf ibn Malik al-Ashja'i said: "We used to perform ruqyah in the days of ignorance, so we said: O Messenger of Allah, what do you think of that? He said: 'Show me your ruqyah. There is no harm in ruqyah as long as it contains no shirk.'"

## Conditions
Scholars mention three basic conditions:
1. **It must be with the words of Allah**, His names and attributes, or authentic supplications.
2. **It must be in Arabic or understood language**: no talismans or unknown words.
3. **One must believe it has no effect by itself**: the One who cures is Allah.

## Why Do It Yourself?
- **The Prophet ﷺ did ruqyah on himself**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah reported that when he was unwell he would recite the Mu'awwidhat over himself and blow.
- **It reflects complete reliance on Allah**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, describing the seventy thousand who enter Paradise without reckoning: "They are those who do not ask for ruqyah, do not take bad omens, do not use cauterisation, and rely upon their Lord." They do not ask others for ruqyah, though it is permitted.
- **It protects you from charlatans**.
- **Your sincere supplication for yourself** is closer to being answered.

There is no harm in asking a trustworthy person for ruqyah when needed; Jibril recited over the Prophet ﷺ, and in al-Bukhari the Prophet ﷺ instructed 'A'ishah to seek ruqyah for the evil eye.

## What to Recite
### Al-Fatihah
﴿بِسۡمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ ﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾ ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ ﴿مَٰلِكِ يَوۡمِ ٱلدِّينِ﴾ ﴿إِيَّاكَ نَعۡبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾ ﴿ٱهۡدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلۡمُسۡتَقِيمَ﴾ ﴿صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ غَيۡرِ ٱلۡمَغۡضُوبِ عَلَيۡهِمۡ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾ (Quran 1:1-7)

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, a Companion recited Al-Fatihah over a tribal chief stung by a scorpion, and he recovered "as if released from a tether". The Prophet ﷺ said: "How did you know that it is a ruqyah?"

### Ayat al-Kursi
﴿ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ٱلۡحَيُّ ٱلۡقَيُّومُۚ لَا تَأۡخُذُهُۥ سِنَةࣱ وَلَا نَوۡمࣱۚ لَّهُۥ مَا فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِۗ مَن ذَا ٱلَّذِي يَشۡفَعُ عِندَهُۥٓ إِلَّا بِإِذۡنِهِۦۚ يَعۡلَمُ مَا بَيۡنَ أَيۡدِيهِمۡ وَمَا خَلۡفَهُمۡۖ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيۡءٖ مِّنۡ عِلۡمِهِۦٓ إِلَّا بِمَا شَآءَۚ وَسِعَ كُرۡسِيُّهُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضَۖ وَلَا يَـُٔودُهُۥ حِفۡظُهُمَاۚ وَهُوَ ٱلۡعَلِيُّ ٱلۡعَظِيمُ﴾ (Quran 2:255)

### The last two verses of Al-Baqarah
﴿ءَامَنَ ٱلرَّسُولُ بِمَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيۡهِ مِن رَّبِّهِۦ وَٱلۡمُؤۡمِنُونَۚ كُلٌّ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ وَمَلَـٰٓئِكَتِهِۦ وَكُتُبِهِۦ وَرُسُلِهِۦ لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيۡنَ أَحَدٖ مِّن رُّسُلِهِۦۚ وَقَالُواْ سَمِعۡنَا وَأَطَعۡنَاۖ غُفۡرَانَكَ رَبَّنَا وَإِلَيۡكَ ٱلۡمَصِيرُ﴾ ﴿لَا يُكَلِّفُ ٱللَّهُ نَفۡسًا إِلَّا وُسۡعَهَاۚ لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتۡ وَعَلَيۡهَا مَا ٱكۡتَسَبَتۡۗ رَبَّنَا لَا تُؤَاخِذۡنَآ إِن نَّسِينَآ أَوۡ أَخۡطَأۡنَاۚ رَبَّنَا وَلَا تَحۡمِلۡ عَلَيۡنَآ إِصۡرٗا كَمَا حَمَلۡتَهُۥ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبۡلِنَاۚ رَبَّنَا وَلَا تُحَمِّلۡنَا مَا لَا طَاقَةَ لَنَا بِهِۦۖ وَٱعۡفُ عَنَّا وَٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَٱرۡحَمۡنَآۚ أَنتَ مَوۡلَىٰنَا فَٱنصُرۡنَا عَلَى ٱلۡقَوۡمِ ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ﴾ (Quran 2:285-286)

### The Mu'awwidhat
Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas. An-Nasa'i reported, and al-Albani graded authentic, that the Prophet ﷺ said of Al-Falaq and An-Nas: "No one seeks refuge with anything like these two."

### Anything else from the Quran
The whole Quran is healing: "And We send down of the Quran that which is healing and mercy for the believers."

## Authentic Supplications
### "Remove the harm"
"Allahumma Rabban-nas, adhhibil-ba's, ishfi antash-Shafi, la shifa'a illa shifa'uk, shifa'an la yughadiru saqama" (O Allah, Lord of mankind, remove the harm and cure; You are the Healer; there is no cure but Your cure, a cure that leaves no illness). Al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### Hand on the place of pain
In Sahih Muslim, 'Uthman ibn Abi al-'As complained to the Prophet ﷺ of pain in his body. He told him: "Put your hand on the place where you feel pain and say 'Bismillah' three times, and say seven times: 'A'udhu billahi wa qudratihi min sharri ma ajidu wa uhadhir' (I seek refuge with Allah and His power from the evil of what I feel and fear)."

### The ruqyah of Jibril
"Bismillahi arqika, min kulli shay'in yu'dhika, min sharri kulli nafsin aw 'aynin hasid, Allahu yashfik, bismillahi arqik" (In the name of Allah I perform ruqyah for you, from everything that harms you, from the evil of every soul or envious eye; may Allah cure you; in the name of Allah I perform ruqyah for you). Muslim.

### Seeking refuge in Allah's perfect words
"A'udhu bi kalimatillahit-tammati min sharri ma khalaq" (I seek refuge in the perfect words of Allah from the evil of what He created). Muslim.

## Step by Step
1. **Make wudu** if possible.
2. **Sit somewhere quiet** and remind yourself that the Healer is Allah.
3. **Recite Al-Fatihah** seven times, or as you are able.
4. **Recite Ayat al-Kursi** and the last two verses of Al-Baqarah.
5. **Recite the Mu'awwidhat** three times.
6. **Blow lightly** into your palms with a little moisture, then **wipe** as much of your body as you can, starting with your head and face.
7. **Place your hand on the place of pain** and say the supplication above.
8. **Supplicate** with the other prophetic supplications.
9. **Repeat** morning and evening, or whenever needed.

## Ruqyah for Children
The Prophet ﷺ used to seek protection for al-Hasan and al-Husayn. In al-Bukhari, Ibn 'Abbas said the Prophet ﷺ used to say over them: "I seek protection for you both in the perfect words of Allah from every devil and every poisonous creature, and from every evil eye," and he said: "Your father (Ibrahim) used to seek protection with these words for Isma'il and Ishaq."

**How to do it**:
- Recite Al-Fatihah, the Mu'awwidhat and Ayat al-Kursi over the child.
- Blow into your palms and wipe the child's head and body.
- Say the words with which the Prophet ﷺ protected al-Hasan and al-Husayn.
- Make it a bedtime habit.

## The Evil Eye
**The evil eye is real**, as in al-Bukhari and Muslim. Prevention includes:
- **The morning and evening adhkar**.
- **The Mu'awwidhat** morning and evening.
- **Saying "Ma sha' Allah, tabarak Allah"** when you admire something in yourself or others, or praying for blessing.

Treatment includes:
- **Ruqyah with Al-Fatihah and the Mu'awwidhat**.
- **Washing**: if the person who caused it is known, he is asked to wash and the water is poured over the affected person, as in the story of Sahl ibn Hunayf reported by Malik and Ahmad with an authentic chain.

## Recitation over Water and Oil
Reciting over water and then drinking it or bathing with it is **permitted by many scholars**, and is reported from some of the early generations. Likewise reciting over olive oil and applying it. But **the basic method** is direct recitation and blowing over the sick person, as the Prophet ﷺ did.

## Warning Signs of Charlatans
Beware of anyone performing "ruqyah" who:
1. **Asks for your mother's name**.
2. **Asks for something belonging to the patient**, such as clothing or hair.
3. **Asks for an animal to be slaughtered** in a particular way, or not in Allah's name.
4. **Writes talismans** or strings of unexplained letters.
5. **Mutters unintelligible words**.
6. **Gives an amulet** with unknown contents.
7. **Claims knowledge of the unseen**: who did the magic or where it is.
8. **Tells the patient to stop praying** or to isolate in darkness.
9. **Demands large sums of money**.

Ahmad and the authors of the Sunan reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever goes to a fortune-teller or soothsayer and believes what he says has disbelieved in what was revealed to Muhammad."

## Ruqyah and Medicine
It is a serious mistake to abandon medical treatment and rely on ruqyah alone. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Seek treatment, O servants of Allah, for Allah has not created a disease without creating a cure for it." Reported by Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi, who graded it authentic. A Muslim combines ruqyah and medicine, and in both cases the Healer is Allah.

It is also important not to attribute every illness to the evil eye, magic or jinn. Many symptoms have physical or psychological causes that need a doctor.

## Daily Protection Adhkar
Prevention is better than cure, and a Muslim who keeps the daily remembrances is protected by Allah's permission:
- **Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas** three times morning and evening. Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi reported, and at-Tirmidhi graded authentic, that they "will suffice you against everything".
- **"Bismillahil-ladhi la yadurru ma'asmihi shay'un fil-ardi wa la fis-sama'i wa huwas-Sami'ul-'Alim"** three times morning and evening (In the name of Allah, with whose name nothing on earth or in heaven can cause harm, and He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing). Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi; "nothing will harm him."
- **"A'udhu bi kalimatillahit-tammati min sharri ma khalaq"** three times in the evening.
- **Ayat al-Kursi** after each prayer and before sleep; in al-Bukhari, whoever recites it at bedtime has a guardian from Allah until morning.
- **The last two verses of Al-Baqarah** at night; "they will suffice him" (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

## A Short Daily Ruqyah Routine
A routine of about ten minutes for yourself and your family:
1. Al-Fatihah three times.
2. Ayat al-Kursi once.
3. The last two verses of Al-Baqarah once.
4. The three Quls three times.
5. Blow into your palms and wipe your body.
6. "Allahumma Rabban-nas, adhhibil-ba's..."
7. "A'udhu bi kalimatillahit-tammati min sharri ma khalaq."

Parents can do this over their children at bedtime, making it a family habit.

## Ruqyah for Women
A woman can do ruqyah for herself, her children and her husband, and does not need a male raqi alone with her, as Islam forbids such seclusion. If she needs one, she should be accompanied by a mahram, in a safe setting, with a trustworthy raqi known for following the Sunnah. During menstruation she can use the prophetic supplications and adhkar without disagreement; reciting the Quran itself is subject to the well-known scholarly difference.

## Avoiding Obsession
Some people become obsessed, believing that every pain, tiredness or family argument is caused by magic, the evil eye or jinn. They live in constant fear and move from one raqi to another. This is a form of waswasa that needs treatment:
- **Most illnesses have natural causes**: see a doctor first.
- **Keep the adhkar** as protection, without living in fear.
- **Do not become attached to raqis**.
- **Rely on Allah**: "Whoever relies upon Allah, He is sufficient for him."

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can I do ruqyah on myself?
Yes; it is the Sunnah, as the Prophet ﷺ recited the Mu'awwidhat over himself.

### What should I recite?
Al-Fatihah, Ayat al-Kursi, the last two verses of Al-Baqarah, the Mu'awwidhat and the prophetic supplications.

### How do I do ruqyah for my child?
Recite Al-Fatihah and the Mu'awwidhat, blow and wipe, and say "I seek protection for you in the perfect words of Allah from every devil and poisonous creature and from every evil eye."

### Is reciting over water allowed?
Many scholars permit it; direct recitation is the basic method.

### How do I recognise a charlatan?
Asking for your mother's name, talismans, claims of the unseen and requests for your belongings are warning signs.

### Should I stop taking medicine?
No. The Prophet ﷺ commanded seeking treatment.

### What are the daily protection adhkar?
The three Quls three times morning and evening, "Bismillahil-ladhi la yadurru..." three times, Ayat al-Kursi, and the last two verses of Al-Baqarah.

### Is every illness caused by the evil eye or magic?
No. Most illnesses have natural causes; see a doctor and keep the adhkar without obsession.

## Conclusion
Ruqyah is not a secret held by specialists but a Sunnah every Muslim can practise for himself and his family: Al-Fatihah, Ayat al-Kursi, the Mu'awwidhat and the prophetic supplications, with blowing and wiping and certainty that the Healer is Allah. Do it for yourself and your children every day, beware of charlatans, and combine ruqyah with medical treatment. That is the prophetic way of seeking cure.
