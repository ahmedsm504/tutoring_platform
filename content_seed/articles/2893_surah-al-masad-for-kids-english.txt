Imagine standing on a small hill and calling all your relatives: "If I told you an army was behind this hill about to attack, would you believe me?" They all say: "Yes, you've never lied to us!" Then you tell them the most important message in the world. And your own uncle shouts: "May you perish! Is this why you gathered us?" That really happened to the Prophet ﷺ, and his uncle was Abu Lahab. Surah Al-Masad was revealed about him.

It's an unusual surah: it names a real person and tells what will happen to him because of his cruelty to the Prophet ﷺ and his message. For children, the lessons are clear and important: being related to someone good doesn't make you good; what matters is how you act. Money and status can't save anyone from the results of their choices. And we never hurt people who call to good. In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Al-Masad to children in simple English, with activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Masad

- **Position**: surah 111, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: five.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Names**: Al-Masad ("palm fibre") and Al-Lahab ("the flame"), also known as "Tabbat" after its first word.

## The Whole Surah

﴿تَبَّتۡ يَدَآ أَبِي لَهَبٖ وَتَبَّ﴾ ﴿مَآ أَغۡنَىٰ عَنۡهُ مَالُهُۥ وَمَا كَسَبَ﴾ ﴿سَيَصۡلَىٰ نَارٗا ذَاتَ لَهَبٖ﴾ ﴿وَٱمۡرَأَتُهُۥ حَمَّالَةَ ٱلۡحَطَبِ﴾ ﴿فِي جِيدِهَا حَبۡلࣱ مِّن مَّسَدِۭ﴾ (Quran 111:1-5)

## The Story: The Call From Mount Safa

### Explaining it to your child
"When Allah told the Prophet ﷺ to warn his close relatives, he climbed Mount Safa, a small hill near the Kaaba, and called the tribes of Quraysh. When they gathered, he asked: If I told you that an army was behind this valley about to attack you, would you believe me? They said: Yes, we have never known you to lie. He said: Then I am a warner to you of a severe punishment ahead. His uncle Abu Lahab shouted: May you perish all day! Is this why you gathered us? Then Allah revealed this surah" (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### Lesson for kids: the honest one
"Everyone in Makkah knew the Prophet ﷺ was truthful. They called him al-Amin, the trustworthy. Even his enemies admitted he never lied. Being known for honesty is one of the most precious things a person can have."

## Who Was Abu Lahab?

### Explaining it to your child
"Abu Lahab was the Prophet's own uncle, his father's brother. His real name was 'Abd al-'Uzza, and he was called Abu Lahab, 'father of the flame', because his face was bright and reddish. He should have been the Prophet's biggest supporter, like his other uncle Abu Talib, who protected the Prophet ﷺ. Instead, he became one of his worst enemies: he followed him around telling people not to listen to him, and he mocked and hurt him."

## Verse 1: May the Hands of Abu Lahab Perish

### Explaining it to your child
"Tabbat means 'may they be ruined' or 'they are ruined'. Abu Lahab used his hands and his power to stop the message of Islam. Allah said that his efforts would fail and he would be ruined. And the next part, 'wa tabb', means: and he is already ruined."

## Verse 2: His Wealth and What He Earned Did Not Help Him

### Explaining it to your child
"Abu Lahab was rich and important. But Allah said his money and everything he gained, including his children, couldn't help him. Money can buy many things, but it can't buy goodness or save someone from the results of their bad choices."

### Lesson for kids
"Being rich, popular or important doesn't make someone good or safe with Allah. What matters is your heart and your actions."

## Verse 3: He Will Burn in a Fire of Flame

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah told us that Abu Lahab would enter the Fire because he rejected the truth and hurt the Prophet ﷺ until the end of his life. Notice his nickname was 'father of the flame', and his punishment is a fire of flame."

### Tip for parents
For young children, you don't need to dwell on the punishment. Focus on the choices Abu Lahab made and the lessons for us.

## Verses 4–5: His Wife, the Carrier of Firewood

### Explaining it to your child
"Abu Lahab's wife, Umm Jamil, joined him in hurting the Prophet ﷺ. Scholars mention that she used to throw thorns and harmful things on his path at night, and spread gossip and lies to cause trouble. That's why she is called 'the carrier of firewood': she carried things that made the fire of trouble burn bigger. The verse says she will have a rope of palm fibre around her neck."

### Lesson for kids: don't spread harm
"Spreading gossip and making trouble between people is like carrying firewood to make a fire bigger. Instead, we try to put out fires: we make peace and say good things."

## An Amazing Sign: The Quran's Words Came True

This surah was revealed years before Abu Lahab died. It said he would die without believing. Abu Lahab could have simply said "I believe" to prove the Quran wrong, even pretending. But he never did. He died as the surah said.

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah told everyone in advance what would happen to Abu Lahab, and it happened exactly like that. Only Allah knows the future. This is one of the many signs that the Quran is truly from Allah."

## Lessons for Kids

### Family names don't make us good
"Abu Lahab was the Prophet's uncle, but that didn't help him because of his choices. Meanwhile, Bilal, an Abyssinian slave, and Salman, from Persia, were not related to the Prophet ﷺ at all, but they became among his most beloved companions. Our actions matter more than who our family is."

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said to his own family: "O Fatimah, daughter of Muhammad, ask me for whatever you want of my wealth, but I cannot avail you anything before Allah" (al-Bukhari and Muslim). Each person is responsible for their own deeds.

### Never hurt someone who calls to good
"If someone reminds you to pray or to be kind, thank them, even if you don't feel like listening right then. Abu Lahab shouted at the one calling him to good, and that was his biggest mistake."

### Use your hands for good
"Abu Lahab used his hands to harm. Let's use our hands to help: to give, to build, to hug, to write kind notes."

## Activities

### Activity: hands for good
Trace both of your child's hands on paper. On each finger, write one good thing hands can do: help, share, give charity, write kindly, make wudu, carry groceries, plant, hug, clean, pray. Hang it on the wall.

### Activity: firewood or water?
Read situations and ask your child whether each action is "firewood" (makes trouble bigger) or "water" (calms it down):
- Telling a friend what someone said about them. (firewood)
- Saying "Let's be friends again." (water)
- Laughing when two people argue. (firewood)
- Telling a teacher kindly to help two friends make up. (water)

### Activity: Mount Safa role-play
Act out the story: your child plays the Prophet ﷺ asking "Would you believe me?", and family members answer. Discuss why honesty made everyone trust him.

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Famous Cousin"

Omar's cousin Khalid was the best Quran reciter in the whole city. He had won competitions and everyone admired him. At school, Omar loved telling people: "Khalid is my cousin!" But Omar himself rarely practised his own Quran, often skipped his prayers, and was sometimes unkind to his younger sister.

One day, his Quran teacher said: "Omar, you haven't learned your verses again." Omar replied: "It's fine, my cousin is a champion reciter!" The teacher smiled gently and said: "Do you know Surah Al-Masad?"

She told him the story of Abu Lahab, the Prophet's own uncle. "He was the uncle of the best person who ever lived, but that didn't help him, because of his own choices. And Bilal, who wasn't related to the Prophet ﷺ at all, became one of his dearest companions because of his faith and actions."

Omar went quiet. That evening he asked Khalid: "Can you help me practise?" Khalid was delighted. Over the next months, Omar practised every day, prayed on time, and even started helping his little sister with her homework.

At the end of the year, Omar recited a whole juz to his teacher. She said: "Well done, Omar. Now people don't just say: that's Khalid's cousin. They say: that's Omar, who works hard and is kind."

Ask your child: "What did Omar think at first? What did he learn from the story of Abu Lahab?"

## What We Learn From Surah Al-Masad

- **The Prophet ﷺ** was known for his honesty.
- **Being related to good people** doesn't make us good; our actions do.
- **Money and status** can't save anyone from the results of their choices.
- **Don't hurt** people who call to good.
- **Don't spread gossip** or "carry firewood".
- **Use your hands** for good.
- **The Quran's words** came true, a sign it's from Allah.

- **Day 3**: memorize verse 3.

2. What happened on Mount Safa?
3. Why couldn't Abu Lahab's money help him?
4. Why was his wife called "the carrier of firewood"?
5. How do we know the Quran's words about Abu Lahab came true?

7. How can you use your hands for good today?

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Is it okay to teach children a surah about punishment?
Yes, with the right focus. Emphasize the choices Abu Lahab made and the lessons about honesty, kindness and personal responsibility, rather than the details of punishment.

### Why does the Quran name Abu Lahab?
Because his opposition was extreme and public, and the surah's prediction about him became a clear sign of the Quran's truth. It also teaches that closeness to the Prophet ﷺ by blood is not what saves; faith and deeds are.

### My child asked if Abu Lahab's children became Muslim. Did they?
Some of his children later accepted Islam, which shows that each person makes their own choice, regardless of their parents.


### How old should my child be to learn this story?
Children from about six can understand the Mount Safa story and the lesson about choices. Younger children can simply memorize the surah.
