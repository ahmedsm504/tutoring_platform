Surah Al-Ikhlas is four short verses, yet it answers the most important question a person can ask: who is God? It is Allah's description of Himself in the fewest possible words. He is One. He is the Self-Sufficient on whom everything depends. He has no child and no parent. Nothing is comparable to Him.

The Prophet ﷺ said it equals a third of the Quran, and when a companion said he loved it because it describes the Most Merciful, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Tell him that Allah loves him." This guide explains the surah in English, verse by verse, with its authentic virtues and the occasions on which the Prophet ﷺ recited it.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Ikhlas
- **Number of verses**: 4.
- **Type**: Meccan in the standard mushaf; scholars have discussed the point.
- **Position**: Surah 112, near the end of the Quran.
- **Names**: Al-Ikhlas (Sincerity or Purity of Faith), At-Tawhid (Oneness), As-Samad, and *Qul huwa Allahu ahad*.
- **Why "Al-Ikhlas"?** Because it is purely about the oneness of Allah, and because believing in it purifies a person from associating partners with Him.

## The Full Text of Surah Al-Ikhlas
﴿قُلۡ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ﴾ ﴿ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ﴾ ﴿لَمۡ يَلِدۡ وَلَمۡ يُولَدۡ﴾ ﴿وَلَمۡ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدُۢ﴾ (Quran 112:1-4)

**Meaning:** Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Self-Sufficient on whom all depend. He neither begets nor was He begotten. And there is none comparable to Him.

## The Virtues of Surah Al-Ikhlas
**1. It equals a third of the Quran.** In Sahih al-Bukhari, Abu Sa'id al-Khudri reported that a man heard another man reciting *Qul huwa Allahu ahad* repeatedly. In the morning he came to the Prophet ﷺ and mentioned it, as if he thought it little. The Prophet ﷺ said: "By the One in whose hand is my soul, it equals a third of the Quran."

**2. A third of the Quran in one night.** In Sahih Muslim, Abu ad-Darda' reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Is any of you unable to recite a third of the Quran in a night?" They asked how. He said: "*Qul huwa Allahu ahad* equals a third of the Quran."

**3. Loving it brings Allah's love.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah reported that the Prophet ﷺ sent a man to lead a military expedition. He used to lead his companions in prayer and end his recitation with *Qul huwa Allahu ahad*. When they returned, they mentioned it to the Prophet ﷺ, who said: "Ask him why he does that." He said: "Because it is the description of the Most Merciful, and I love to recite it." The Prophet ﷺ said: "Tell him that Allah loves him."

**4. Loving it leads to Paradise.** Al-Bukhari mentions without a full chain, and at-Tirmidhi records with a chain he graded *hasan sahih gharib*, from Anas, that a man of the Ansar used to recite it in every rak'ah along with another surah. When asked, he said: "I love it." The Prophet ﷺ said: "Your love for it has admitted you into Paradise."

### Why does it equal a third of the Quran?
Scholars explain that the meanings of the Quran fall broadly into three areas: the oneness of Allah and His attributes, stories and news, and laws and commands. Surah Al-Ikhlas contains the first area in its entirety, so it equals a third in meaning and reward. It does not replace reading the whole Quran: reciting it three times is not the same as completing the Quran in terms of what you learn and the rulings that depend on full recitation.

## Verse 1: Say, He Is Allah, the One
﴿قُلۡ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ﴾ (Quran 112:1)

**"Say"** is a command to the Prophet ﷺ and to every Muslim to declare this belief openly. Tawhid is not a private opinion; it is a truth to be proclaimed.

**"He is Allah"**: the One who alone deserves worship, to whom hearts turn in love and reverence.

**"Ahad", the One**: a stronger word than *wahid*. *Ahad* denies any partner, any equal, and any division. He is one in His essence, one in His attributes, one in His actions, and one in deserving worship. He is not made of parts and has no counterpart.

## Verse 2: Allah, as-Samad
﴿ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ﴾ (Quran 112:2)

*As-Samad* is one of the greatest names of Allah. The early scholars explained it in two ways that complement each other:
1. **The Master to whom all turn in their needs.** Ibn 'Abbas explained it as the One who is perfect in His knowledge, power, wisdom and every attribute, so that every creature turns to Him for what it needs.
2. **The One who has no hollowness.** He does not eat or drink and has no need of anything.

Put together: He needs nothing, and everything needs Him. When you understand *as-Samad*, you stop hanging your heart on people who are as needy as you are, and you turn to the One who is never in need.

## Verse 3: He Neither Begets Nor Is Born
﴿لَمۡ يَلِدۡ وَلَمۡ يُولَدۡ﴾ (Quran 112:3)

**"He neither begets"** rejects every claim that Allah has a child: the claim of some pagan Arabs that the angels were His daughters, the Christian claim about 'Isa, and the claim attributed to some Jews about 'Uzayr. **"Nor was He begotten"** rejects that He has any parent or origin. He is the First, before whom there is nothing.

Birth implies need, resemblance and a beginning. Allah is free of all three.

## Verse 4: None Is Comparable to Him
﴿وَلَمۡ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدُۢ﴾ (Quran 112:4)

*Kufuwan* means an equal, a match, a counterpart. In the narration of Hafs it is read *kufuwan* with a *waw*. Nothing in creation resembles Him in His essence, attributes or actions. This matches the verse in Surah Ash-Shura: "There is nothing like Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing."

## Word Meanings
- **Qul**: say, declare.
- **Ahad**: the One, unique, indivisible.
- **As-Samad**: the Self-Sufficient Master to whom all turn.
- **Lam yalid**: He did not beget.
- **Lam yulad**: He was not begotten.
- **Kufuwan**: an equal or match.

## The Three Categories of Tawhid in the Surah
Scholars note that this short surah touches all three aspects of tawhid:
1. **Oneness of Lordship**: in *as-Samad*, the One who manages and sustains all things.
2. **Oneness of worship**: in the name *Allah*, the only One deserving worship.
3. **Oneness of names and attributes**: in affirming *Ahad* and *as-Samad*, and negating any child, parent or equal.

## When to Recite Surah Al-Ikhlas
1. **In the two sunnah rak'ahs before Fajr.** In Sahih Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ recited *Qul ya ayyuha al-kafirun* and *Qul huwa Allahu ahad* in them.
2. **In the two rak'ahs after tawaf**, as in Jabir's description of the Prophet's Hajj in Sahih Muslim.
3. **Before sleeping.** In Sahih al-Bukhari, 'A'ishah reported that every night when the Prophet ﷺ went to bed, he joined his palms, blew into them, recited Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, and wiped over as much of his body as he could, starting with his head and face, three times.
4. **Morning and evening.** Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi recorded, with at-Tirmidhi grading it *hasan sahih gharib*, that the Prophet ﷺ told 'Abdullah ibn Khubayb: "Recite *Qul huwa Allahu ahad* and the two *mu'awwidhat* in the evening and morning three times; they will suffice you against everything."
5. **After the obligatory prayers.** 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir said: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ commanded me to recite the *mu'awwidhat* after every prayer." Recorded by Abu Dawud and an-Nasa'i and graded authentic by al-Albani.
6. **In the witr prayer**, along with Al-A'la and Al-Kafirun, as reported by Abu Dawud and an-Nasa'i with an authentic chain.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"Say"**: tawhid is announced, not hidden.
2. **"He"**: the pronoun before the name builds anticipation and reverence.
3. **The definite *as-Samad***: only He is truly the Self-Sufficient.
4. **"Begets" before "begotten"**: the claim that Allah has a child was the one people actually made, so it is negated first.
5. **The surah opens and closes with *ahad***: oneness surrounds the whole surah.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Tafkhim of the name Allah** in *huwa Allahu*, because it follows a *damma*.
- **The connecting nun**: when joining verse 1 to verse 2, the tanween of *ahadun* meets the hamzat al-wasl of *Allah*, so it is pronounced *ahadunil-Lahus-Samad*.
- **Qalqalah** when stopping on *ahad*, *as-Samad*, *yalid* and *yulad*, because they end in *dal*.
- **Izhar shafawi** in *lam yalid*, *lam yulad* and *wa lam yakun*: the meem is pronounced clearly before *ya*.
- **Idgham without ghunnah** in *yakun lahu*: the nun merges into the lam.
- **Izhar** in *kufuwan ahad*: the tanween is clear before the hamza.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before Al-Ikhlas comes Al-Masad, the story of Abu Lahab who opposed tawhid. Al-Ikhlas then defines the tawhid he opposed. After it come Al-Falaq and An-Nas: once you know your Lord as the One and the Self-Sufficient, you are taught to seek refuge in Him from every evil.

## Living the Meaning of Al-Ikhlas
- **Worship none but Him**, because He is *Ahad*.
- **Ask none but Him** for what only He can give, because He is *as-Samad*. In a hadith of Ibn 'Abbas graded *hasan sahih* by at-Tirmidhi, the Prophet ﷺ said: "When you ask, ask Allah; and when you seek help, seek help from Allah."
- **Do not liken Allah to His creation**, because none is comparable to Him.
- **Purify your intentions**, living up to the name of the surah: sincerity.

## Practical Lessons
1. Recite it three times morning and evening with the mu'awwidhatayn.
2. Recite it before sleep with blowing into the palms and wiping the body.
3. Recite it in the sunnah of Fajr.
4. Love it; that love is a means to Allah's love.
5. Turn to *as-Samad* first in every need.
6. Renew your tawhid daily.

## Teaching Children
- It is usually among the first surahs children learn; teach the meaning with it.
- Use a simple sentence: Allah is One, everyone needs Him, He needs no one, and nothing is like Him.
- Make the bedtime routine of the three surahs a family habit.
- Tell them about the companion whom Allah loved because he loved this surah.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Does Surah Al-Ikhlas really equal a third of the Quran?
Yes, as reported in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.

### Does reciting it three times equal completing the Quran?
It equals a third of the Quran in meaning and reward, but it does not replace reading the whole Quran.

### What does "as-Samad" mean?
The Self-Sufficient Master to whom all creatures turn in their needs, who needs nothing; some early scholars also explained it as the One who has no hollowness.

### Why is it called Al-Ikhlas?
Because it is purely about Allah's oneness and purifies the believer from associating partners with Him.

### When did the Prophet ﷺ recite it?
In the sunnah of Fajr, after tawaf, in witr, before sleep, and in the morning and evening adhkar.

### What is the difference between "ahad" and "wahid"?
*Ahad* is stronger in negating any partner, division or likeness.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Ikhlas?
4 verses.

### Is Surah Al-Ikhlas Meccan or Medinan?
It is listed as Meccan in the standard mushaf, and scholars have discussed reports on both sides. Its message of pure tawhid fits the themes of the Meccan period.

### Can I recite Al-Ikhlas in every rak'ah?
Yes. The Prophet ﷺ approved of the companion who recited it in every rak'ah along with another surah.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Ikhlas is the identity card of your Lord: One without partner, Self-Sufficient and the refuge of all, without child or parent, beyond comparison. When you know Him like this, you love Him, you worship Him alone, and you hang your heart on no one else. Recite it with love and certainty, and hope that Allah says of you what He said of that companion: tell him that Allah loves him.
