"He has a newer phone than me." "She has more followers." "Their house is bigger." "I want the latest game too!" Do these sound familiar? Wanting more and more, and always comparing ourselves with others, is something everyone struggles with, children and adults. Allah describes this feeling in Surah At-Takathur: "Competing for more has distracted you." The surah warns that this race for more can keep us busy until the end of our lives, if we're not careful.

The surah ends with a powerful reminder: one day, we will be asked about every blessing we enjoyed. Not to scare us, but to help us notice and be grateful. Even a glass of cool water is a blessing we'll be asked about! In this guide for parents, we explain Surah At-Takathur to children in simple English, with practical lessons about comparison, gratitude and contentment, plus activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah At-Takathur

- **Position**: surah 102, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: eight.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Name**: at-Takathur means "competing for more", trying to have more than others: more money, more things, more people on your side.

## The Whole Surah

﴿أَلۡهَىٰكُمُ ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ﴾ ﴿حَتَّىٰ زُرۡتُمُ ٱلۡمَقَابِرَ﴾ ﴿كَلَّا سَوۡفَ تَعۡلَمُونَ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ كَلَّا سَوۡفَ تَعۡلَمُونَ﴾ ﴿كَلَّا لَوۡ تَعۡلَمُونَ عِلۡمَ ٱلۡيَقِينِ﴾ ﴿لَتَرَوُنَّ ٱلۡجَحِيمَ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ لَتَرَوُنَّهَا عَيۡنَ ٱلۡيَقِينِ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ لَتُسۡـَٔلُنَّ يَوۡمَئِذٍ عَنِ ٱلنَّعِيمِ﴾ (Quran 102:1-8)

## Verses 1–2: Competing for More Has Distracted You

### Explaining it to your child
"Alhakum means 'it has distracted you'. At-takathur means trying to have more than others. Allah says: this race for more has distracted you, until you visit the graves, meaning until the end of your life. Some people spend their whole lives collecting more and more, and forget what's truly important: Allah, family, kindness and good deeds."

### Lesson for kids
"Having nice things isn't wrong. Allah gives us good things to enjoy. The problem is when wanting more becomes the most important thing, and we forget to be thankful for what we have."

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ recited this surah and said: "The son of Adam says: My wealth, my wealth! But do you have anything of your wealth except what you ate and used up, what you wore and wore out, or what you gave in charity and sent ahead?" (Muslim).

### Explaining it to your child
"The only things we really keep are what we enjoyed and what we gave to others. Everything else we leave behind. So giving is like sending treasure ahead to Paradise!"

## Verses 3–4: No! You Will Come to Know

### Explaining it to your child
"Kalla means 'No!', like Allah saying: Stop, this is the wrong way. Then He says twice: You will soon know. One day, everyone will understand what was truly important, and the race for 'more' will seem very small."

## Verses 5–7: If Only You Knew With Certainty

### Explaining it to your child
"If you really knew, with certainty, what is coming after this life, you would not be distracted by collecting more things. 'Ilm al-yaqin is certain knowledge, and 'ayn al-yaqin is seeing with your own eyes. On the Day of Judgement, people will see the truth with their own eyes."

### Tip for parents
Keep this part calm and gentle with young children. The main message for them is: one day we'll see clearly what matters, so let's focus on what matters now.

## Verse 8: You Will Be Asked About the Blessings

### Explaining it to your child
"Then, on that Day, you will surely be asked about an-na'im, the blessings. What blessings? Everything Allah gave us: food, water, health, family, time, our eyes and ears."

### From the Sunnah
One day, the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr and 'Umar were hungry and went out. A man from the Ansar welcomed them, gave them dates and cool water, and slaughtered a sheep for them. After they ate and drank, the Prophet ﷺ said: "By the One in whose hand is my soul, you will be asked about this blessing on the Day of Resurrection" (Muslim).

### Explaining it to your child
"After a simple meal of dates, meat and cool water, the Prophet ﷺ said: we will be asked about this blessing. So every meal, every drink, every comfy bed is a blessing we should notice and thank Allah for."

### Activity: the blessings scavenger hunt
Give your child five minutes to walk around the house and find ten blessings: a tap with clean water, a fridge with food, a warm blanket, their eyes reading a book, shoes, a toy. For each one, they say "Alhamdulillah".

## The Comparison Trap

Today, children see other people's lives constantly: on screens, on social media, at school. It's easy to feel that everyone else has more. The surah gives us a powerful perspective.

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Look at those who have less than you, and don't look at those who have more than you, so that you don't belittle Allah's blessings on you" (Muslim).

### Explaining it to your child
"When you look at people who have more than you, you start to feel you don't have enough. When you remember people who have less, you see how much Allah has given you. Both are true, but one makes you grateful and the other makes you sad."

### Activity: the two lists
Help your child make two lists:
- **"I wish I had..."**: things they want.
- **"I'm so glad I have..."**: things they already have.
Usually, the second list is much longer! Talk about how it feels to read each list.

### Tip for parents: screens and social media
- Talk openly: "Pictures online often show only the best moments."
- Set healthy limits on screen time.
- Model contentment yourself; children copy what they see.
- Celebrate experiences and kindness, not just new things.

## Contentment Is Real Wealth

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Wealth is not having many possessions; real wealth is the richness of the soul" (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### Explaining it to your child
"A truly rich person isn't someone with lots of stuff; it's someone whose heart is happy and satisfied with what Allah gave them. That kind of richness, nobody can take away."

## What We Can Collect That Lasts

If competing for "more" distracts us, what should children collect instead? The surah points us towards things that last beyond this life.

- **Good deeds**: each kind act is saved with Allah.
- **Quran in the heart**: every verse memorized is a treasure no one can take.
- **Good friends**: friends who remind us of Allah.
- **Knowledge**: what we learn and teach others keeps benefiting.
- **Du'a from others**: people pray for those who were kind to them.

### Activity: the treasure box
Make a "forever treasure box" from a shoebox. Every time your child does something that "lasts", like memorizing a verse, helping someone or giving charity, they write it on a slip and put it in. Compare it with a toy box: toys break and get old, but these treasures last forever with Allah.

### Explaining it to your child
"Toys, clothes and gadgets are nice, but they get old and break. The things in this treasure box never get old. That's the kind of 'more' we should compete for!"

### A better kind of competition
Allah says in Surah Al-Mutaffifin that for the blessings of Paradise, "let those who compete, compete." So competing isn't always bad; it depends on what we compete for. Racing to do good is the best competition of all.

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Sneakers"

At school, everyone was talking about the new sneakers with lights on the soles. "I've got them!" said one boy. "Me too!" said another. Ziyad looked down at his own sneakers. They were clean and comfortable, but they didn't light up. Suddenly, they looked old and boring.

At home, Ziyad told his mother: "I need the light-up sneakers. Everyone has them." His mother listened, then said: "Let's read a short surah first." They read Surah At-Takathur together, and she explained: "Alhakumut-takathur means wanting more and more can distract us from what's important."

Then she told him the hadith about the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr and 'Umar, and the simple meal of dates and water, after which the Prophet ﷺ said: you will be asked about this blessing. "Ziyad," she said, "your sneakers are a blessing too. Some children don't have any shoes at all."

Ziyad thought for a while. "Mum, can we give my old sneakers that are too small to a charity? And maybe I'll save my pocket money for new ones, if I still want them in a month."

A month later, Ziyad had almost forgotten about the light-up sneakers. Instead, he had saved enough money to buy a football that he shared with his whole class at break. "This is better," he told his mum. "Everyone gets to enjoy it."

Ask your child: "Why did Ziyad suddenly dislike his sneakers? What helped him feel grateful again?"

## What We Learn From Surah At-Takathur

- **Wanting more and more** can distract us from what matters.
- **We only keep** what we used and what we gave away.
- **One day**, everyone will see clearly what was important.
- **We will be asked** about every blessing, even water.
- **Look at those who have less** to stay grateful.
- **Real wealth** is a content heart.
- **Sharing** makes blessings bigger.

## One-Week Plan: Memorize and Practise

- **Day 1**: memorize verses 1–2.
- **Day 2**: memorize verses 3–4.
- **Day 3**: memorize verses 5–7.
- **Day 4**: memorize verse 8 and do the blessings scavenger hunt.
- **Day 5**: make the two lists.
- **Day 6**: choose something to give to charity.
- **Day 7**: recite At-Takathur in prayer and celebrate!

## Questions to Ask Your Child

1. What does "at-takathur" mean?
2. What can wanting more and more distract us from?
3. What things do we really keep from our wealth?
4. What will we be asked about on the Day of Judgement?
5. What did the Prophet ﷺ say after the meal of dates and water?
6. Should we look at people who have more or less than us? Why?
7. What is real wealth?

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Is it wrong to buy my child nice things?
No. Enjoying halal blessings is good. The surah warns against making "more" the goal of life and forgetting gratitude. Balance gifts with gratitude, sharing and experiences.

### How do I handle "everyone has it" pressure?
Acknowledge the feeling, talk about the surah and the hadith about looking at those with less, and consider a waiting period before big purchases. Often, the desire fades.

### Will talking about being "asked about blessings" scare my child?
Frame it positively: "Allah wants us to notice and enjoy His blessings and say thank you." The aim is gratitude, not fear.


### At what age can children understand this surah?
From about six, children can grasp "wanting more" and gratitude through daily examples. The deeper meanings about the Hereafter can grow with them over time.
