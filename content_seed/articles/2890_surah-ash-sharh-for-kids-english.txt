Every child has hard days: homework that seems impossible, a friend who isn't being nice, a test that went badly, or a new school that feels scary. On days like these, Allah has given us a surah with one of the most encouraging promises in the whole Quran: "Indeed, with hardship comes ease. Indeed, with hardship comes ease." Allah says it twice, so we never forget!

Surah Ash-Sharh, also called Al-Inshirah, was revealed to the Prophet ﷺ in Makkah during very difficult years, when people were rejecting and hurting him. Allah reminded him of the gifts He had already given him, and promised that ease always comes with hardship. In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Ash-Sharh to children in simple English, with practical tips for hard days, activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Ash-Sharh

- **Position**: surah 94, in Juz 'Amma, right after Surah Ad-Duha.
- **Number of verses**: eight.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Names**: Ash-Sharh or Al-Inshirah, meaning "the expanding" or "the opening up" of the chest, and sometimes called "Alam Nashrah" after its first words.

### Connection with Ad-Duha
Many scholars note that Ash-Sharh continues the comfort of Surah Ad-Duha. Ad-Duha says "your Lord has not abandoned you", and Ash-Sharh adds "with hardship comes ease".

## The Whole Surah

﴿أَلَمۡ نَشۡرَحۡ لَكَ صَدۡرَكَ﴾ ﴿وَوَضَعۡنَا عَنكَ وِزۡرَكَ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِيٓ أَنقَضَ ظَهۡرَكَ﴾ ﴿وَرَفَعۡنَا لَكَ ذِكۡرَكَ﴾ ﴿فَإِنَّ مَعَ ٱلۡعُسۡرِ يُسۡرًا﴾ ﴿إِنَّ مَعَ ٱلۡعُسۡرِ يُسۡرٗا﴾ ﴿فَإِذَا فَرَغۡتَ فَٱنصَبۡ﴾ ﴿وَإِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَٱرۡغَب﴾ (Quran 94:1-8)

## Verse 1: Did We Not Expand Your Chest?

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah asks the Prophet ﷺ: Did We not open up your chest for you? This means Allah filled his heart with peace, wisdom and patience, so he could carry his big mission. When your heart feels 'tight' with worry, you can ask Allah to open it up too, like Musa did: Rabbish-rahli sadri, my Lord, expand my chest."

### Activity: tight and open
Ask your child to clench their fist really tight, then slowly open it and relax their hand. "Worry makes our hearts feel tight like a fist. Allah can make them open and relaxed like an open hand."

## Verses 2–3: The Heavy Load Removed

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah says: and We removed from you your burden, which weighed down your back. The Prophet ﷺ felt the weight of his mission, like carrying a heavy backpack. Allah made it lighter for him."

### Activity: the heavy backpack
Fill a backpack with a few books and let your child carry it around the room. Then take the books out one by one. "That's how worry feels, and that's how Allah lightens it when we turn to Him."

## Verse 4: We Raised Your Name

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah raised the name of the Prophet ﷺ high. Think about it: five times a day, in every country, in the adhan, people say: Ashhadu anna Muhammadan rasulullah. Because the world is round and prayer times move, somewhere on earth the adhan is being called right now, every minute! His name is always being mentioned."

### Activity: the adhan around the world
Look at a world map with your child. Explain that as the sun moves around the earth, Fajr starts in one country, then the next, and so on, so the adhan never stops anywhere in the world. "The Prophet's name is being called somewhere right now!"

## Verses 5–6: With Hardship Comes Ease

### Explaining it to your child
"These are the most famous verses of the surah: Fa inna ma'al-'usri yusra. Inna ma'al-'usri yusra. Indeed, with hardship comes ease. Indeed, with hardship comes ease. Notice that it says 'with' hardship, not just 'after' it. Even inside the hard time, Allah puts some ease."

### A beautiful detail scholars noticed
In Arabic, "al-'usr" (the hardship) comes with "al", meaning it's the same hardship both times, while "yusra" (ease) comes without "al", meaning it's a new ease each time. So scholars said: one hardship, two eases! A famous saying attributed to companions is: "One hardship will never overcome two eases."

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah mentions hardship twice, but it's the same hardship. And He mentions ease twice, and it's two different eases! So every hard thing you face comes with more than one ease."

### Activity: finding the ease
When your child is going through something hard, sit with them and look for the "eases" together:
- "Your homework is hard, but your teacher said you can ask her tomorrow." (ease)
- "You're sick, but Mum is looking after you and you get to rest." (ease)
- "Your friend moved away, but you can video-call on weekends." (ease)
This helps children see that Allah's ease is often already there.

## Verses 7–8: When You Finish, Keep Going, and Turn to Your Lord

### Explaining it to your child
"When you finish one task, don't just stop and waste time; move on to the next good thing, and turn to your Lord with hope. For example: when you finish your homework, pray; when you finish praying, help at home; when you finish helping, read Quran. A believer's day is full of good, one thing after another."

### Lesson for kids: not lazy, but balanced
"This doesn't mean you never rest. Rest is good too! But we don't waste time doing nothing useful. And whatever we do, we hope for Allah's reward."

## Practical Tips for Hard Days

Here's a simple plan you can teach your child for when things feel hard:

1. **Say it**: "Inna ma'al-'usri yusra." Allah has promised ease.
2. **Breathe**: take three slow, deep breaths.
3. **Make du'a**: "Rabbish-rahli sadri wa yassir li amri."
4. **Break it into small steps**: one question at a time, one page at a time.
5. **Ask for help**: from a parent, teacher or friend.
6. **Look for the ease**: what good is already there?

### Activity: the hard-day card
Write these six steps on a small card for your child's pencil case or school bag. When they feel overwhelmed, they can take it out and follow the steps.

## Hardship and Ease in the Prophet's Life

The Prophet ﷺ lived this surah. In Makkah, he faced years of hardship: people mocked him, hurt his companions, and even surrounded his family in a valley for three years with very little food. Then came the hijrah to Madinah, a long and dangerous journey. But each hardship came with ease: loyal friends like Abu Bakr, the warm welcome of the Ansar in Madinah, and in the end, the peaceful return to Makkah.

### Explaining it to your child
"Even the best of people, the Prophet ﷺ, had very hard days. But Allah's promise came true for him again and again. If it came true for him, it will come true for you too, in sha' Allah."

### Activity: my hard-then-easy timeline
Help your child draw a timeline of their own life with a few hard moments, like a first day at school, an illness or a move, and next to each one, the ease that came: a new friend, getting better, a new home they love. Seeing their own story helps them trust the promise.

## The Du'a for Ease

The Prophet ﷺ taught a short du'a for difficult moments: "Allahumma la sahla illa ma ja'altahu sahla, wa anta taj'alul-hazna idha shi'ta sahla", "O Allah, nothing is easy except what You make easy, and You make the difficult easy if You will" (Ibn Hibban, graded authentic). Teach it to your child for exams, hard homework and new challenges.

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Impossible Puzzle"

For her birthday, Nour got a 500-piece puzzle of a mountain landscape. She tipped all the pieces onto the table and stared. "This is impossible!" she groaned. After twenty minutes, she had only joined four pieces. She pushed the box away, frustrated.

Her grandmother sat down beside her. "Do you know Surah Alam Nashrah?" Nour nodded and recited: "Fa inna ma'al-'usri yusra, inna ma'al-'usri yusra." "What does it mean?" asked her grandmother. "With hardship comes ease," said Nour. "Exactly. So let's find the ease in this puzzle."

Her grandmother showed her a trick: "First, find all the edge pieces, the ones with a straight side." That was easier! Soon the frame of the puzzle was done. "Now, let's sort the pieces by colour: blue for the sky, green for the trees, white for the snow." Each small pile felt much less scary.

Over the next three days, Nour worked on the puzzle a little at a time. Whenever she got stuck, she whispered "inna ma'al-'usri yusra" and tried a different piece. On the fourth day, she placed the last piece and shouted: "I did it!"

Her grandmother smiled. "You see? The hardship was one big puzzle, but the ease came in many small steps." Nour looked at the finished mountain and said: "One hardship, two eases. Actually, lots of eases!"

Ask your child: "Why did Nour want to give up? How did her grandmother help her find the ease?"

## What We Learn From Surah Ash-Sharh

- **Allah can open up our hearts** when we feel worried.
- **Allah lightens** heavy burdens.
- **The Prophet's name** is raised everywhere in the world.
- **With hardship comes ease**, twice!
- **One hardship can't beat two eases**.
- **Keep going** from one good thing to the next.
- **Turn to Allah** with hope in everything.
- **Break hard things** into small steps.

## One-Week Plan: Memorize and Practise

- **Day 1**: memorize verses 1–3 and do the backpack activity.
- **Day 2**: memorize verse 4 and look at the adhan world map.
- **Day 3**: memorize verses 5–6.
- **Day 4**: memorize verses 7–8.
- **Day 5**: recite the whole surah and make the hard-day card.
- **Day 6**: practise "finding the ease" with a real challenge.
- **Day 7**: recite Ash-Sharh in prayer and celebrate!

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Does "with hardship comes ease" mean problems will disappear immediately?
Not always immediately. It means ease accompanies hardship and follows it, and Allah's help is present even during difficulty. Teaching children to notice small eases builds real resilience.

### Is the "one hardship, two eases" saying a hadith?
It is reported from companions such as 'Umar and Ibn Mas'ud as an explanation, and some versions are attributed to the Prophet ﷺ with weak chains. The meaning is well established by scholars of tafsir from the wording of the verses.

### Can this surah help an anxious child?
Yes. Pair the surah with practical tools: deep breathing, breaking tasks into steps, and talking about worries. The surah gives hope; the tools give control.
