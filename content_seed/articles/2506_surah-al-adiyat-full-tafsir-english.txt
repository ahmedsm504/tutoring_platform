Surah Al-'Adiyat opens with a thrilling scene: horses charging at full speed, panting, their hooves striking sparks from the rocks, raiding at dawn, raising clouds of dust and plunging into the midst of the enemy. Then the scene cuts suddenly to man: "Indeed, man is ungrateful to his Lord." The horse gives its life in loyalty to its owner who feeds it; man, overwhelmed with countless blessings, often denies his Lord's favours.

This guide explains the surah verse by verse in English and reflects on the disease of ingratitude and its cure.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-'Adiyat
- **Number of verses**: 11.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 100, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: from *al-'adiyat*, the charging horses, in the first verse.

## The Full Text of Surah Al-'Adiyat
﴿وَٱلۡعَٰدِيَٰتِ ضَبۡحٗا﴾ ﴿فَٱلۡمُورِيَٰتِ قَدۡحٗا﴾ ﴿فَٱلۡمُغِيرَٰتِ صُبۡحٗا﴾ ﴿فَأَثَرۡنَ بِهِۦ نَقۡعٗا﴾ ﴿فَوَسَطۡنَ بِهِۦ جَمۡعًا﴾ ﴿إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لِرَبِّهِۦ لَكَنُودࣱ﴾ ﴿وَإِنَّهُۥ عَلَىٰ ذَٰلِكَ لَشَهِيدࣱ﴾ ﴿وَإِنَّهُۥ لِحُبِّ ٱلۡخَيۡرِ لَشَدِيدٌ﴾ ﴿۞أَفَلَا يَعۡلَمُ إِذَا بُعۡثِرَ مَا فِي ٱلۡقُبُورِ﴾ ﴿وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِي ٱلصُّدُورِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ رَبَّهُم بِهِمۡ يَوۡمَئِذٖ لَّخَبِيرُۢ﴾ (Quran 100:1-11)

**Meaning:** By the chargers, panting, striking sparks, raiding at dawn, stirring up dust thereby, and penetrating into the midst of a host: indeed, man is ungrateful to his Lord, and he is a witness to that, and he is intense in his love of wealth. Does he not know that when what is in the graves is scattered, and what is in the chests is brought out, their Lord on that Day will be fully aware of them?

## Verses 1–5: The Oath by the Horses
﴿وَٱلۡعَٰدِيَٰتِ ضَبۡحٗا﴾ ﴿فَٱلۡمُورِيَٰتِ قَدۡحٗا﴾ ﴿فَٱلۡمُغِيرَٰتِ صُبۡحٗا﴾ ﴿فَأَثَرۡنَ بِهِۦ نَقۡعٗا﴾ ﴿فَوَسَطۡنَ بِهِۦ جَمۡعًا﴾ (Quran 100:1-5)

Allah swears by horses in five successive descriptions that paint a charge:
1. **"By the chargers, panting"** (*dabhan*): horses running hard, with the sound of their breath.
2. **"Striking sparks"** (*qadhan*): their hooves strike rocks and throw out sparks.
3. **"Raiding at dawn"** (*subhan*): attacking in the early morning, the Arabs' custom in raids.
4. **"Stirring up dust thereby"** (*naq'an*): clouds of dust raised by their gallop.
5. **"Penetrating into the midst of a host"** (*jam'an*): breaking into the centre of the enemy.

**According to most commentators**, these are the horses of those striving in the path of Allah. Some, including a report from 'Ali, said they are camels on the Hajj; the first view is stronger given the panting, the sparks and the raid.

**Why swear by horses?** Because of their great benefit, because they were the means of striving in Allah's path, and because they show loyalty and obedience to their owners. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'Urwah al-Bariqi reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Good is tied to the forelocks of horses until the Day of Resurrection: reward and spoils."

## Verse 6: Man Is Ungrateful to His Lord
﴿إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لِرَبِّهِۦ لَكَنُودࣱ﴾ (Quran 100:6)

This is the answer to the oath. *Kanud* means ungrateful, one who denies favours. Al-Hasan al-Basri said: *al-kanud* is the one who counts his misfortunes and forgets the blessings of his Lord.

**The contrast**: the horse risks its life in obedience to the owner who feeds and waters it, while man, flooded with favours beyond counting, denies them and disobeys his Lord with them. Which of the two is more loyal?

**Forms of ingratitude in our lives:**
- Remembering one bad day and forgetting years of health.
- Constant complaining about provision, people and circumstances.
- Using blessings in disobedience, like the eyes for forbidden looks or wealth for extravagance.
- Forgetting the Giver in ease and remembering Him only in hardship.

## Verse 7: He Is a Witness to It
﴿وَإِنَّهُۥ عَلَىٰ ذَٰلِكَ لَشَهِيدࣱ﴾ (Quran 100:7)

Two explanations of the pronoun:
1. **Man himself testifies** to his ingratitude, through his state and actions, and cannot deny it.
2. **Allah is witness** to man's ingratitude; nothing is hidden from Him.
The first fits the surrounding verses about man; the second is also strong, and both are true.

## Verse 8: His Intense Love of Wealth
﴿وَإِنَّهُۥ لِحُبِّ ٱلۡخَيۡرِ لَشَدِيدٌ﴾ (Quran 100:8)

**"Al-khayr" here is wealth**, as in Surah Al-Baqarah: "if he leaves *khayr*, to make a bequest". **"Intense"**: his love of wealth is severe, or, as some explain, he is miserly because of it. Wealth is called *khayr* because people consider it good, and it is good when spent in Allah's obedience.

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "If the son of Adam had two valleys of wealth, he would want a third, and nothing fills the belly of the son of Adam except dust; and Allah accepts the repentance of whoever repents."

## Verses 9–11: When the Graves Are Scattered
﴿۞أَفَلَا يَعۡلَمُ إِذَا بُعۡثِرَ مَا فِي ٱلۡقُبُورِ﴾ ﴿وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِي ٱلصُّدُورِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ رَبَّهُم بِهِمۡ يَوۡمَئِذٖ لَّخَبِيرُۢ﴾ (Quran 100:9-11)

**"Does he not know"**: does this ungrateful lover of wealth not know where he is going? **"When what is in the graves is scattered"**: when the graves are overturned and the dead brought out for the Resurrection. **"And what is in the chests is brought out"**: the intentions and desires in hearts are gathered and exposed, good and bad. **"Their Lord on that Day will be fully aware of them"**: He knows them and their deeds and will recompense them.

**Why mention the chests?** Because ingratitude and greed are diseases of the heart before they are actions. The Reckoning includes what is inside, not only the outside, which is why intention is the basis of every deed.

**A subtle point**: "on that Day" specifies the Day, though Allah is always fully aware. The meaning is that His awareness will be shown on that Day through recompense.

## Word Meanings
- **Al-'Adiyat**: the charging horses.
- **Dabhan**: the panting breath of running horses.
- **Al-Muriyat**: those striking sparks.
- **Qadhan**: striking stones to make sparks.
- **Al-Mughirat**: the raiders.
- **Subhan**: at dawn.
- **Atharna**: stirred up.
- **Naq'an**: dust.
- **Wasatna**: penetrated into the midst.
- **Kanud**: ungrateful.
- **Al-Khayr**: wealth.
- **Bu'thira**: was scattered, overturned.
- **Hussila**: was brought out, gathered and exposed.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **The repeated *fa*** in *fal-muriyat*, *fal-mughirat*, *fa-atharna*, *fa-wasatna* shows rapid succession: the whole charge happens in moments.
2. **Sound and sight**: panting is heard, sparks are seen, dust is felt; the scene is cinematic.
3. **Horse and man**: loyalty against ingratitude.
4. **Four emphatic *la***: *la-kanud*, *la-shahid*, *la-shadid*, *la-khabir*.
5. **Passive verbs** *bu'thira* and *hussila*, focusing on the event, not the doer.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Qalqalah sughra** in *dabhan* and *subhan* (*ba* with sukun), *qadhan* (*dal* with sukun) and *naq'an* (*qaf* with sukun).
- **Qalqalah kubra** when stopping on *la-kanud*, *la-shahid* and *la-shadid* (dal).
- **Ikhfa** of tanween before *fa* in *dabhan fal-muriyat*, *qadhan fal-mughirat*, *subhan fa-atharna* and *naq'an fa-wasatna*.
- **Izhar** in *jam'an inna*: tanween clear before hamza.
- **Izhar shafawi** in *bihim yawma'idh*: meem clear before ya.
- **Idgham without ghunnah** in *yawma'idhin la-khabir*: tanween merges into lam.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before Al-'Adiyat comes Az-Zalzalah: "and the earth brings out its burdens", matched here by "when what is in the graves is scattered". Az-Zalzalah speaks of seeing deeds; Al-'Adiyat adds the exposure of what is in the chests. After it comes Al-Qari'ah, describing that Day and its scales.

## The Cure for Ingratitude
Gratitude is the opposite of ingratitude: "If you are grateful, I will surely increase you" (14:7). Ways to cure *kunud*:
1. **Count your blessings**: set aside time each day to remember three.
2. **Look at those below you.** In Sahih Muslim: "Look at those below you and do not look at those above you; that is more likely to keep you from belittling Allah's favour upon you."
3. **Thank people.** Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi recorded, with at-Tirmidhi grading it *hasan sahih*: "Whoever does not thank people does not thank Allah."
4. **Use blessings in obedience**: this is practical gratitude.
5. **Praise Allah often.** In Sahih Muslim: "Allah is pleased with a servant who eats a meal and praises Him for it, or drinks a drink and praises Him for it."
6. **Pray at night in gratitude**, as the Prophet ﷺ did until his feet swelled, saying: "Should I not be a grateful servant?" (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

## Love of Wealth: Between Nature and Excess
Loving wealth is natural, and Islam does not fight it; it refines it. Good wealth is a blessing; the problem is "intensity", when love of wealth overrides love of Allah, leading to withholding rights, miserliness and earning from the forbidden. The cure: remember that wealth belongs to Allah and you are a trustee; give regularly, even a little; remember the grave, where no wealth enters with you; and seek contentment. In Sahih Muslim: "Successful is the one who accepts Islam, is given sufficient provision, and Allah makes him content with what He has given him."

## Horses in the Quran and Sunnah
The Quran mentions horses as beauty and benefit (16:8) and as preparation for defence (8:60). In Sahih al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever keeps a horse in the path of Allah, out of faith in Allah and belief in His promise, its feed, its drink, its dung and its urine will be in his scale on the Day of Resurrection." And in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn 'Umar reported that the Prophet ﷺ held horse races.

## Practical Lessons
1. Be loyal to your Lord, as the horse is loyal to its owner.
2. Write down Allah's blessings to cure forgetfulness.
3. Do not be a constant complainer.
4. Control your love of wealth and give from it.
5. Purify what is in your chest; intentions will be exposed.
6. Remember the grave so what you gather looks small.
7. Thank people; it is part of thanking Allah.

## Teaching Children
- Show them videos of horses galloping and explain panting, sparks and dust.
- Play "three blessings" before bed.
- Use the loyal horse to explain why we obey Allah.

## What Is in the Chests: Deeds of the Heart
"And what is in the chests is brought out" draws attention to the actions of the heart, which are the foundation of religion. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, an-Nu'man ibn Bashir reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Truly, in the body there is a morsel of flesh; if it is sound, the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupt, the whole body is corrupt. Truly, it is the heart." Among the actions of the heart that will be exposed: sincerity and showing off, love and hatred, envy and a clean heart, arrogance and humility, gratitude and ingratitude. Give your heart its share of self-examination, as you examine your limbs.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What are "al-'adiyat"?
Charging horses, according to most commentators the horses of those striving in Allah's path.

### What does "inna al-insana li-rabbihi la-kanud" mean?
Man is ungrateful to his Lord: he counts his misfortunes and forgets the blessings.

### What is "al-khayr" in verse 8?
Wealth.

### What does "what is in the chests is brought out" mean?
The intentions and desires of hearts will be gathered and exposed.

### How do I cure ingratitude?
Count blessings, praise Allah, look at those below you, thank people, and use blessings in obedience.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-'Adiyat?
11 verses.

### Is loving wealth always blameworthy?
No. Loving wealth is natural; what is blamed is excess that makes a person withhold rights or put wealth before obeying Allah. Lawful wealth spent in good is a blessing.

### Why does the surah move from horses to man?
To contrast the loyalty of the horse to its owner with the ingratitude of man to his Lord, so that a person feels ashamed of his state.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-'Adiyat holds up a mirror. The horse charges in loyalty to its owner, risking its life; what have you offered the Lord who gave you everything? Cure ingratitude with gratitude, refine your love of wealth with giving, and purify your heart before what is in it is brought out, for your Lord on that Day will be fully aware of you.
