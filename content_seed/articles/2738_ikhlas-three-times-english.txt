Many people say that whoever recites Surah Al-Ikhlas three times "has completed the whole Quran". Some recite it three times a day and say, "I finished the Quran today." Some imams recite it three times when completing the Quran in Taraweeh. What is the basis for this? What does the authentic hadith that "Qul huwallahu ahad equals a third of the Quran" mean? And can it replace reading the Quran?

This guide explains the hadith, the scholars' views on its meaning, the important difference between **reward** and **sufficiency**, the ruling on repeating it three times at a completion, and its authentic virtues.

## The Authentic Hadiths
### Abu Sa'id al-Khudri
Al-Bukhari reported from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri that a man heard another man reciting "Qul huwallahu ahad" repeatedly. In the morning he came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and mentioned it, as if he thought it was little. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **"By the One in whose hand is my soul, it equals a third of the Quran."**

### Abu ad-Darda'
Muslim reported from Abu ad-Darda' that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Is any of you unable to recite a third of the Quran in a night?" They said: "How could one recite a third of the Quran?" He said: **"Qul huwallahu ahad equals a third of the Quran."**

### Abu Hurayrah
Muslim reported from Abu Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Gather, for I will recite to you a third of the Quran." He came out and recited "Qul huwallahu ahad", then said: "It equals a third of the Quran."

﴿قُلۡ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ﴾ ﴿ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ﴾ ﴿لَمۡ يَلِدۡ وَلَمۡ يُولَدۡ﴾ ﴿وَلَمۡ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدُۢ﴾ (Quran 112:1-4)

## What Does "Equal to a Third" Mean?
Scholars offered several explanations:

### 1. In meaning and content
The Quran contains three kinds of meanings:
1. **Tawhid**: knowing Allah, His names and attributes.
2. **Rulings**: commands and prohibitions.
3. **Stories and reports**: about past nations and the Hereafter.

Surah Al-Ikhlas contains **tawhid** in its most concise form, so it equals a third of the Quran **in its meanings**. Many scholars mentioned this, and a number of careful scholars preferred it.

### 2. In reward
The reciter receives **the reward of reciting a third of the Quran**, out of Allah's generosity. This is the apparent wording of Abu ad-Darda's hadith: "Is any of you unable to recite a third of the Quran in a night?"

### 3. Combining both
It equals a third of the Quran in meaning, and the reciter receives a great reward befitting that, but not equal in every respect. Ibn Taymiyyah wrote an entire treatise on this question, examining these views in detail.

## Reward vs Sufficiency
This is the most important point, and scholars emphasised it:
- **Reward** (*ajr*): the good deeds Allah records.
- **Sufficiency** (*ijza'*): that an act discharges an obligation or commitment.

Even if Al-Ikhlas equals a third of the Quran in reward, **it does not substitute for it**. Scholars gave the example of the Prophet's statement that whoever says "La ilaha illallahu wahdahu la sharika lah..." ten times is like one who freed four slaves from the descendants of Isma'il, as in al-Bukhari and Muslim. He receives the reward of freeing slaves, but if he owed the expiation of freeing a slave, these words would not discharge it.

### Practical consequences
1. **It does not replace Al-Fatihah in prayer**: whoever recites Al-Ikhlas three times instead of Al-Fatihah has not prayed validly according to the majority.
2. **It does not fulfil a vow to complete the Quran**: whoever vowed to complete the Quran must complete it in full.
3. **It does not replace reading the Quran**: whoever stops reading the Quran and suffices with Al-Ikhlas three times a day has neglected the Quran and deprived himself of immense good.
4. **It is not a "completion"**: someone who recites it three times should not say, "I finished the Quran."

## Why Not Just Recite Al-Ikhlas?
- **The whole Quran is guidance and light**, and we are commanded to read and reflect on all of it: "Do they not reflect upon the Quran?"
- **Every letter brings ten good deeds**, and reading the whole Quran brings countless good deeds.
- **The Companions**, who knew this hadith best, did not suffice with Al-Ikhlas; they completed the Quran monthly, weekly or every three days.
- **The Quran contains rulings and stories** a Muslim needs for his religion, which are not found in Al-Ikhlas.

## Repeating Al-Ikhlas Three Times at a Completion
Some imams and reciters, when completing the Quran, recite Al-Ikhlas **three times** before Al-Falaq and An-Nas.
- **Some reciters approved of it**, and it became the practice in some places.
- **Many careful scholars objected**, stating that it is not prescribed, because it is not reported from the Prophet ﷺ or the Companions, it adds to what is in the Mushaf, and it may give the impression that the completion is not complete without it.

It is better to recite Al-Ikhlas **once**, as in the Mushaf, then Al-Falaq and An-Nas, then make du'a.

## The Authentic Virtues of Al-Ikhlas
### Loving it brings Allah's love and Paradise
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah reported that the Prophet ﷺ sent a man to lead an expedition, and he would end his recitation in prayer with "Qul huwallahu ahad". When they returned, they mentioned this to the Prophet ﷺ, who said: "Ask him why he does that." He said: "Because it is the description of the Most Merciful, and I love to recite it." The Prophet ﷺ said: **"Tell him that Allah loves him."**

And in a report mentioned by al-Bukhari and narrated in full by at-Tirmidhi from Anas, about a man who recited it in every rak'ah, the Prophet ﷺ said: **"Your love for it will admit you to Paradise."**

### In the morning, evening and bedtime adhkar
Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi reported from 'Abdullah ibn Khubayb that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Recite Qul huwallahu ahad and the two Mu'awwidhat in the evening and morning three times; they will suffice you against everything." At-Tirmidhi graded it authentic.

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, when the Prophet ﷺ went to bed he would cup his hands, blow into them, recite Al-Ikhlas and the Mu'awwidhat, and wipe as much of his body as he could.

### In prayers
The Prophet ﷺ recited it in the Sunnah of Fajr, the Sunnah of Maghrib, the two rak'ahs after tawaf, and in witr.

### The ten-times hadith
Ahmad reported from Mu'adh ibn Anas that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever recites Qul huwallahu ahad until he completes it ten times, Allah will build for him a palace in Paradise." Its chain has weakness, and some hadith scholars graded it hasan due to supporting reports.

## The Meanings in Brief
- **"Say: He is Allah, One"**: Allah is One in His essence, names, attributes and actions, with no partner.
- **"Allah, as-Samad"**: the perfect Master to whom all turn in their needs.
- **"He neither begets nor is born"**: rejecting any claim that Allah has a child or parent.
- **"Nor is there anyone comparable to Him"**: He has no equal or likeness.

It is the surah of pure tawhid, called **Al-Ikhlas** (sincerity/purity) because it purifies its reciter from shirk, or because it is purely about Allah's description.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Saying "I completed the Quran"** after reciting Al-Ikhlas three times.
2. **Sufficing with it** and abandoning the Quran.
3. **Believing it fulfils** a vow to complete the Quran or replaces Al-Fatihah.
4. **Repeating it three times at a completion** as if it were Sunnah.
5. **Reciting it without reflection**, though it is the surah of tawhid.

## Al-Ikhlas and Al-Kafirun: The Two Surahs of Tawhid
The Prophet ﷺ recited **Al-Kafirun** and **Al-Ikhlas** in the two rak'ahs of the Sunnah of Fajr, as in Sahih Muslim, and in the two rak'ahs after tawaf and the Sunnah of Maghrib. Scholars note that the two surahs together cover both aspects of tawhid:
- **Al-Ikhlas**: tawhid of knowledge and affirmation, knowing Allah through His names and attributes.
- **Al-Kafirun**: tawhid of intention and worship, devoting worship to Allah alone and disowning the worship of others.

Whoever begins the day with them in the Sunnah of Fajr renews both aspects of tawhid.

## Its Names
Commentators mention several names, reflecting its greatness: **Al-Ikhlas** (purity), **At-Tawhid** (oneness), **As-Samad**, **Al-Asas** (the foundation, since tawhid is the foundation of the religion), and **Al-Ma'rifah** (knowledge of Allah).

## Responding to Distortions
The surah answers many deviations in understanding Allah:
- **Polytheists** who associated partners: "Say: He is Allah, One."
- **Those who attributed a child to Allah**: "He neither begets."
- **Those who imagined Allah has an origin**: "nor is He born."
- **Those who likened Allah to His creation**: "nor is there anyone comparable to Him."

This is why it has always been central to teaching belief, and why children learn it first.

## How to Reflect on Al-Ikhlas
Because we recite it so often, we may recite it without reflection. Ask yourself:
- **"He is Allah, One"**: is my heart attached to anyone besides Allah, hoping in them or fearing them as I should hope in and fear Allah?
- **"Allah, as-Samad"**: do I turn to Allah first in my needs, or to people?
- **"He neither begets nor is born"**: Allah needs nothing, and everything needs Him.
- **"Nor is there anyone comparable to Him"**: do I revere anything as I revere Allah?

Recited with these questions, it becomes a daily renewal of tawhid in your heart.

## Is Al-Ikhlas Greater Than Al-Fatihah?
Al-Fatihah is **the greatest surah in the Quran**, as in Sahih al-Bukhari from Abu Sa'id ibn al-Mu'alla: "I will teach you the greatest surah in the Quran: Al-hamdu lillahi rabbil-'alamin; it is the seven oft-repeated verses and the great Quran I have been given." Ayat al-Kursi is **the greatest verse**, as in Sahih Muslim. And Al-Ikhlas **equals a third of the Quran**. Each has an established virtue, with no conflict.

## Teaching Children Al-Ikhlas
Al-Ikhlas is one of the first surahs a Muslim child memorises. Explain it simply:
- **"Qul huwallahu ahad"**: Allah is One; there is no other god.
- **"Allahus-Samad"**: we ask Allah for everything we need.
- **"Lam yalid wa lam yulad"**: Allah has no father, mother or children.
- **"Wa lam yakun lahu kufuwan ahad"**: no one is like Allah.

You can tell the child: "Allah loves this surah, and whoever loves it, Allah loves them."

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Does reciting Al-Ikhlas three times equal a completion of the Quran?
It equals a third of the Quran in meaning or reward, but it does not substitute for completing the Quran, and reciting it three times is not called a completion.

### What does "equal to a third of the Quran" mean?
The best-known explanation is that it contains tawhid, which is a third of the Quran's meanings; others say it refers to reward.

### Can Al-Ikhlas replace Al-Fatihah in prayer?
No. Al-Fatihah is a pillar of prayer according to the majority.

### Is repeating Al-Ikhlas three times at a khatm allowed?
Some reciters approved of it, but many careful scholars objected; it is better to recite it once as in the Mushaf.

### What is the virtue of loving Al-Ikhlas?
"Tell him that Allah loves him," and "Your love for it will admit you to Paradise."

### When is Al-Ikhlas recited?
In the morning, evening and bedtime adhkar, and in the Sunnah of Fajr and Maghrib, witr, and the two rak'ahs after tawaf.

### Which surahs are recited in the Sunnah of Fajr?
Al-Kafirun in the first rak'ah and Al-Ikhlas in the second, as in Sahih Muslim.

### Is Al-Ikhlas greater than Al-Fatihah?
Al-Fatihah is the greatest surah; Al-Ikhlas equals a third of the Quran. Each has its own established virtue.

### How do I explain Al-Ikhlas to a child?
Allah is One, we ask Him for what we need, He has no parents or children, and no one is like Him.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Ikhlas equals a third of the Quran according to the authentic hadith, because it contains tawhid, and its reciter has a great reward. But this is in reward, not sufficiency: it does not replace Al-Fatihah, does not fulfil a vow to complete the Quran, does not replace reading the Quran, and reciting it three times is not a completion. Love it, recite it in your adhkar and prayers, reflect on its meanings, and read the whole Quran too, for every letter brings ten good deeds.
