Surah Ash-Sharh, also called Al-Inshirah, is eight short verses of comfort sent to the Prophet ﷺ in one of the hardest periods of his mission in Makkah. It reminds him of three gifts already given: a chest expanded, a burden removed, and a name raised high. Then it delivers one of the most quoted promises in the Quran, twice in a row: "For indeed, with hardship there is ease. Indeed, with hardship there is ease." Finally it tells him what to do with that ease: when you finish one task, stand up for the next in worship, and turn all your longing to your Lord. Millions of Muslims recite it in moments of distress, and it speaks to them as it spoke to him.

This guide explains Surah Ash-Sharh verse by verse in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons for times of difficulty.

## Quick Facts About Surah Ash-Sharh
- **Number of verses**: 8.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 94, in the thirtieth juz (Juz 'Amma).
- **Names**: Ash-Sharh and Al-Inshirah, both meaning "the expansion", and Alam Nashrah, from its first words.
- **Main themes**: Allah's care for His Messenger ﷺ, comfort in hardship, the certainty of relief, and continuous devotion.

## The Context of Revelation
The surah was revealed in Makkah, after Surah Ad-Duha, which it closely resembles. The early years of the mission were heavy: rejection by his own people, mockery, the persecution of the weak believers, and the weight of a message meant for all humanity. Ad-Duha told him that his Lord had not abandoned him; Ash-Sharh goes further and lists what Allah had already done for him, then promises more. Some early scholars even treated the two surahs as one continuous message of comfort.

## Verse 1: Have We Not Expanded Your Chest?
﴿أَلَمۡ نَشۡرَحۡ لَكَ صَدۡرَكَ﴾ (Quran 94:1)

**"Did We not expand for you your breast?"** The question is not seeking information; it is affirming a fact: We certainly did. The expansion of the chest (*sharh as-sadr*) means opening the heart to receive revelation, to bear the burdens of the mission, and to remain patient and generous with people.

**The physical opening of the chest**: in Sahih Muslim, Anas ibn Malik reported that Jibril came to the Prophet ﷺ as a boy while he was playing with other boys, laid him down, opened his chest, took out his heart and removed a clot from it, saying: "This is the share of Satan in you." Then he washed it in a basin of gold with Zamzam water and returned it. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, a similar opening is reported before the Night Journey. Scholars say the spiritual meaning of the verse is primary, and these events are among its signs.

**Musa asked for it**: in Surah Ta-Ha, Musa prayed: "My Lord, expand for me my breast and ease for me my task." What Musa asked for, the Prophet ﷺ was given without asking.

## Verses 2–3: The Burden Removed
﴿وَوَضَعۡنَا عَنكَ وِزۡرَكَ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِيٓ أَنقَضَ ظَهۡرَكَ﴾ (Quran 94:2-3)

**"And We removed from you your burden, which had weighed upon your back."** *Wizr* is a heavy load. Commentators explained it as the heavy concerns of the early mission, the worry for his people's guidance, and anything that weighed on him, all of which Allah lightened and forgave. The image is a load so heavy that one hears the back creak under it (*anqada*), and then it is lifted.

## Verse 4: Your Mention Raised High
﴿وَرَفَعۡنَا لَكَ ذِكۡرَكَ﴾ (Quran 94:4)

**"And raised high for you your repute."** His name is joined with the name of Allah in the testimony of faith, in the call to prayer five times a day from every minaret on earth, in the iqamah, in the tashahhud of every prayer, and in the khutbah of every Friday. At every moment, somewhere on the planet, the adhan is being called and his name is being mentioned. Whoever prays sends blessings on him; whoever enters Islam testifies to his message. No human being in history has had his name raised like this.

## Verses 5–6: With Hardship Comes Ease
﴿فَإِنَّ مَعَ ٱلۡعُسۡرِ يُسۡرًا﴾ ﴿إِنَّ مَعَ ٱلۡعُسۡرِ يُسۡرٗا﴾ (Quran 94:5-6)

**"For indeed, with hardship there is ease. Indeed, with hardship there is ease."**

**Why repeated?** For emphasis and certainty, so that no hardship can shake the believer's confidence in relief.

**"With", not "after"**: the verse says ease comes *with* (*ma'a*) hardship, not only after it. Ease is already present inside the difficulty, close enough to touch: patience, reward, the help of Allah, the lessons learned and the sins forgiven.

**One hardship, two eases**: scholars of the Arabic language noted that *al-'usr* (the hardship) comes with the definite article both times, pointing to the same hardship, while *yusr* (ease) is indefinite both times, pointing to two different eases. From this came a saying reported from some of the Companions: "One hardship will never overcome two eases."

**From the Sunnah**: in a hadith collected by Ahmad from Ibn 'Abbas, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Know that victory comes with patience, relief with distress, and with hardship comes ease." Scholars considered this part of the hadith authentic.

## Verse 7: When You Have Finished, Stand Up
﴿فَإِذَا فَرَغۡتَ فَٱنصَبۡ﴾ (Quran 94:7)

**"So when you have finished, then stand up."** The Arabic *fansab* means to exert yourself, to stand up tired. When you finish one duty, begin the next; when you finish the affairs of this world, stand for worship; when you finish your prayer, devote yourself to du'a. The believer's life has no empty idleness: rest is only a transition from one good to another.

## Verse 8: To Your Lord Direct Your Longing
﴿وَإِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَٱرۡغَب﴾ (Quran 94:8)

**"And to your Lord direct your longing."** Placing "to your Lord" first in the Arabic gives the meaning: to Him alone. Hope, desire and attention all directed to Allah, not to people, not to results, not to praise.

## The Message of the Surah in One Line
You have been given much (verses 1–4); difficulty will not last, and ease is already with you (verses 5–6); so keep working and keep your heart turned to Allah (verses 7–8).

## Word Meanings
- **Nashrah**: We expand, open.
- **Sadr**: chest, breast.
- **Wada'na**: We removed.
- **Wizr**: burden.
- **Anqada**: weighed heavily upon, made creak.
- **Zahr**: back.
- **Rafa'na**: We raised.
- **Dhikr**: mention, repute.
- **Al-'Usr**: hardship.
- **Yusr**: ease.
- **Faraghta**: you have finished.
- **Fansab**: stand up, exert yourself.
- **Farghab**: direct your longing.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"Laka" (for you) repeated**: in verses 1 and 4, everything is "for you", a sign of special care.
2. **Questions that affirm**: "Did We not...?" means "We certainly did."
3. **A heavy image**: a burden that made the back creak.
4. **Repetition with precision**: definite hardship, indefinite ease.
5. **"With", not "after"**: relief hidden inside the trial.
6. **Fronting "to your Lord"**: exclusivity of devotion.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Izhar shafawi**: in *alam nashrah* (verse 1), the mim sakinah before the noon is pronounced clearly.
- **Ikhfa'**: in *'anka* (verse 2), the noon sakinah is hidden before the kaf.
- **Ikhfa'**: in *fansab* (verse 7), the noon sakinah is hidden before the sad.
- **Qalqalah kubra**: on stopping at *fansab* (verse 7) and *farghab* (verse 8), the ba' bounces strongly.
- **Ghunnah**: in *inna* (verses 5–6), the doubled noon is held for two counts.

## Connection With the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Ad-Duha before it promises that the end will be better than the beginning and lists three gifts of the past: shelter for the orphan, guidance and sufficiency. Ash-Sharh adds three more gifts: the expanded chest, the removed burden and the raised mention. Surah At-Tin after it speaks of the human being created in the best form.

## How to Live Surah Ash-Sharh
1. **In distress, recite it slowly**, letting "with hardship comes ease" settle in your heart.
2. **Look for the ease inside your hardship**: what reward, lesson or closeness is in it now?
3. **Count the gifts already given**, as the first four verses do.
4. **Send blessings on the Prophet ﷺ** whose mention Allah raised.
5. **Do not waste free time**: move from one good deed to the next.
6. **Make Allah the destination of your hopes.**
7. **Ask for an expanded chest**, with Musa's du'a.

## How to Memorise Surah Ash-Sharh
- **One sitting**: verses 1–4 (the gifts), then verses 5–8 (the promise and the command). It can be memorised in one day and reviewed with Surah Ad-Duha.

## Teaching Children
- **Ask them about a hard day** that got better, and read verses 5–6.
- **Listen to the adhan together** and point out the Prophet's name in it: "We raised high your mention."
- **Teach them to fill free time** with something good.

## Ease in the Life of the Prophet ﷺ
The promise of verses 5–6 was not abstract for the Prophet ﷺ; his life was a series of hardships each accompanied by ease.

**The boycott**: for about three years, Banu Hashim were besieged in the valley of Abu Talib, cut off from trade and marriage. Then the boycott collapsed, and the believers came out.

**The year of grief**: he lost his wife Khadijah and his uncle Abu Talib, then was rejected and stoned at Ta'if. Soon after came the Night Journey and Ascension, an honour no human had received before.

**The migration**: he left Makkah secretly, hunted by his own people, hiding in the cave of Thawr with Abu Bakr. He said: "Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us." Within a few years, Madinah had become the capital of a growing community.

**Hudaybiyah**: the Muslims were turned away from 'Umrah under terms that seemed unfair. Allah called it "a clear victory", and within two years Makkah was opened without a battle.

In each case, the ease was already present in the hardship: in the patience of the believers, in the sincerity it produced, and in the doors it opened that no one could have predicted.

## A Du'a for Difficult Moments
Alongside reciting this surah, the Prophet ﷺ taught a du'a for anxiety and hardship. Anas ibn Malik reported that he would say: "O Allah, there is no ease except what You make easy, and You make the difficult easy if You wish." Collected by Ibn Hibban; scholars graded it authentic.

## Common Misunderstandings
- **"Ease means the problem disappears immediately"**: not necessarily. Ease may come as patience, as reward, as a changed perspective, or as a later relief. The verse promises that hardship never comes alone.
- **"Hardship means Allah is displeased"**: the surah was revealed to the most beloved of Allah's creation during his hardest years. Difficulty is often a sign of honour and a means of raising rank.
- **"Free time is for doing nothing"**: verse 7 teaches that rest is a transition, not a destination.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Is it called Ash-Sharh or Al-Inshirah?
Both names are used; they mean the expansion of the chest.

### What does "Did We not expand your chest?" mean?
Allah opened the Prophet's heart to bear revelation and its burdens; the physical opening of his chest in Sahih Muslim is one of its signs.

### Why is "with hardship comes ease" repeated?
For emphasis and certainty; scholars also noted from the grammar that one hardship is accompanied by two eases.

### How was the Prophet's mention raised?
In the testimony of faith, the adhan, the iqamah, the tashahhud and the khutbah, all over the world.

### What does "when you have finished, stand up" mean?
Move from one duty to the next, and from worldly work to worship, without idle emptiness.

### Is Ash-Sharh connected to Ad-Duha?
Yes, both are comfort to the Prophet ﷺ, and they complement each other.

### How many verses are in Surah Ash-Sharh?
8 verses.

### Did the Prophet ﷺ experience hardship followed by ease?
Yes, repeatedly: the boycott, the year of grief, the migration and Hudaybiyah were each followed by relief and victory.

### Does hardship mean Allah is displeased with me?
No. The surah was revealed to the Prophet ﷺ in his hardest years; trials often raise a believer's rank.

## Conclusion
Surah Ash-Sharh is eight verses that you can carry into any difficulty. It reminds you of what Allah has already given, assures you twice that ease is with your hardship, and tells you not to sit still: finish, then stand, and keep your heart turned to your Lord. Whatever weighs on your back today, the One who lifted the Prophet's burden is able to lift yours.
