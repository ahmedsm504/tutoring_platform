A huge army marches across the desert towards Makkah. In front of it walks something the Arabs had rarely seen: a gigantic elephant, trained for war. The army's leader, Abrahah, has one goal: to knock down the Kaaba. The people of Makkah are few and can't fight such an army, so they leave the city and climb the mountains. But Allah sends something no one expected: flocks of small birds, each carrying tiny stones. And the mighty army is destroyed.

This is the story of Surah Al-Fil, "The Elephant". It happened in the year the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was born, which the Arabs called "the Year of the Elephant". In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Al-Fil to children in simple English, tell the full story in scenes they'll love, and add activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Fil

- **Position**: surah 105, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: five.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Name**: al-Fil means "The Elephant".
- **When did it happen?** In the Year of the Elephant, around 570 CE, the year the Prophet ﷺ was born.

## The Whole Surah

﴿أَلَمۡ تَرَ كَيۡفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِأَصۡحَٰبِ ٱلۡفِيلِ﴾ ﴿أَلَمۡ يَجۡعَلۡ كَيۡدَهُمۡ فِي تَضۡلِيلٖ﴾ ﴿وَأَرۡسَلَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ طَيۡرًا أَبَابِيلَ﴾ ﴿تَرۡمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٖ مِّن سِجِّيلٖ﴾ ﴿فَجَعَلَهُمۡ كَعَصۡفٖ مَّأۡكُولِۭ﴾ (Quran 105:1-5)

## The Full Story in Six Scenes

### Scene 1: A jealous king
"In Yemen, there was a ruler called Abrahah. He saw that Arabs from everywhere travelled to Makkah to visit the Kaaba, and he was jealous. So he built a huge, beautiful church in Yemen and wanted everyone to go there instead. But people kept going to the Kaaba. Abrahah became furious and decided: I will destroy the Kaaba!"

### Scene 2: The army and the elephant
"Abrahah gathered a massive army, and at the front he placed a huge elephant, some reports say more than one, to pull down the walls of the Kaaba. The Arabs had never seen anything like it. Tribes along the way tried to stop him, but they were defeated."

### Scene 3: Abdul-Muttalib and the camels
"Near Makkah, Abrahah's soldiers took the camels of the people of Makkah, including two hundred camels belonging to Abdul-Muttalib, the leader of Makkah and the grandfather of the Prophet ﷺ. Abdul-Muttalib went to meet Abrahah. Abrahah thought he had come to beg him to spare the Kaaba. But Abdul-Muttalib asked only for his camels! Abrahah was surprised: You ask about your camels and not about the House of your religion? Abdul-Muttalib answered: I am the owner of the camels, and the House has a Lord who will protect it."

### Lesson for kids: trusting Allah
"Abdul-Muttalib knew that the Kaaba belongs to Allah, and that Allah would protect it. When something is too big for us, we do what we can and trust Allah with the rest."

### Scene 4: The elephant refuses
"In the morning, Abrahah ordered the army to march into Makkah. But when they turned the elephant towards the Kaaba, it knelt down and refused to move! They hit it and tried everything, but it wouldn't budge. When they turned it towards Yemen, it got up and walked. When they turned it back towards the Kaaba, it knelt again."

### From the Sunnah
At the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, when the Prophet's camel knelt and would not move, he said: "She was held back by the One who held back the elephant" (al-Bukhari). This shows the story of the elephant was well known and true.

### Scene 5: The birds Ababil
"Then the sky filled with flocks of birds, coming from the direction of the sea. Each bird carried three small stones: one in its beak and one in each claw. The stones were made of baked clay. The birds dropped the stones on the army, and every soldier hit by a stone was destroyed."

### Scene 6: Like eaten straw
"The great army was left like 'asfin ma'kul: like straw or leaves that animals have chewed and spat out. Abrahah himself was struck and died on the way back. And the Kaaba stood safe, as it still stands today."

## Verse by Verse

### Verse 1: "Have you not seen how your Lord dealt with the companions of the elephant?"
"Allah asks the Prophet ﷺ: Have you not seen? He wasn't born yet when it happened, but the story was so famous that it was as if everyone had seen it with their own eyes."

### Verse 2: "Did He not make their plan go astray?"
"Abrahah planned everything: the army, the elephant, the route. But Allah ruined his plan completely. No plan can defeat Allah's will."

### Verse 3: "And He sent against them birds in flocks"
"Ababil means flocks, group after group. Allah used tiny birds to defeat a huge army!"

### Verse 4: "Striking them with stones of baked clay"
"Sijjil means hard baked clay. Small stones, but by Allah's command, they were stronger than any weapon."

### Verse 5: "And He made them like eaten straw"
"The mighty army became as weak and worthless as chewed-up straw."

## Lessons for Kids

### Allah protects His House
"The Kaaba is the House of Allah, and Allah protected it in a way no one could have imagined. Today, millions of Muslims face the Kaaba in every prayer."

### Big doesn't mean strong
"The army was huge, with a giant elephant, but Allah defeated it with small birds and small stones. Being big or having lots of things doesn't make anyone truly strong. True strength belongs to Allah."

### Jealousy leads to harm
"Abrahah's story started with jealousy. He was jealous that people loved the Kaaba, and his jealousy led him to try to destroy it, and in the end it destroyed him."

### Allah prepared the world for the Prophet ﷺ
"This happened in the year the Prophet ﷺ was born. It's as if Allah was preparing Makkah for the arrival of the last Prophet, protecting the city where he would be born."

## Activities

### Activity: story cards
Draw six cards with your child, one for each scene: the jealous king, the army and the elephant, Abdul-Muttalib and the camels, the elephant kneeling, the birds with stones, and the army like straw. Mix them up and let your child put them in order while retelling the story.

### Activity: the Kaaba direction
Use a qibla app or compass with your child to find the direction of the Kaaba from your home. Say: "This is the House Allah protected from the elephant army. Every time we pray, we face it."

### Activity: elephant facts
Look up elephant facts together: how heavy they are, how strong their trunks are. Then say: "Even the strongest animal knelt down when Allah willed."

## Why Did Allah Use Birds?

Allah could have destroyed the army with a storm, an earthquake or anything else. But He chose small birds and tiny stones. Scholars mention wisdom in this: it showed everyone that the army's power meant nothing before Allah, and that the victory came from Allah alone, not from the people of Makkah, who had gone up into the mountains.

### Explaining it to your child
"If the people of Makkah had fought and won, they might have said: We are strong! But they didn't fight at all. Tiny birds did the work, so everyone knew it was Allah. When something good happens to you, remember to say: this is from Allah."

### Activity: small but mighty
Make a list with your child of small things that do big jobs: a bee that pollinates flowers, an ant that carries food many times its weight, a seed that becomes a tree, a single kind word that makes a sad friend smile. After each one, say: "SubhanAllah, Allah gives small things great power."

## The Kaaba Today

The Kaaba that Allah protected from the elephant army is the same Kaaba Muslims visit today for Hajj and 'Umrah. It was first built by Ibrahim and Isma'il, and Allah has protected it throughout history. Show your child photos or videos of pilgrims circling the Kaaba, and tell them: "One day, in sha' Allah, you will stand there too."

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Sandcastle"

Ibrahim spent the whole morning at the beach building a big sandcastle with towers, walls and a moat. He was very proud of it. Then a group of older boys came along, and one of them said: "Let's knock it down!" Ibrahim was small and couldn't stop them, and he started to cry.

His father, who was sitting nearby, called the boys over kindly and asked them to help build an even bigger castle instead. To Ibrahim's surprise, they agreed, and soon everyone was building together, laughing.

On the way home, Ibrahim asked: "Dad, how did you know they would stop?" His father smiled. "I didn't. I did what I could, and I trusted Allah with the rest. Do you remember Abdul-Muttalib? When the elephant army came to destroy the Kaaba, he said: The House has a Lord who will protect it."

Ibrahim's eyes widened. "And Allah sent the birds!" "Exactly," said his father. "Allah protected His House with tiny birds. He can protect us in ways we never expect."

That night, Ibrahim recited Surah Al-Fil before sleeping, and when he reached "tayran ababil", he imagined thousands of little birds flying over the desert.

Ask your child: "What did Ibrahim's father do when the boys wanted to knock down the castle? What did Abdul-Muttalib say about the Kaaba?"

## What We Learn From Surah Al-Fil

- **Allah protects** His House, the Kaaba.
- **No plan** can beat Allah's will.
- **Big and powerful** isn't the same as truly strong.
- **Allah can use small things** to do great things.
- **Jealousy** leads to harm.
- **Trust Allah** with what you can't control.
- **The Year of the Elephant** was the year the Prophet ﷺ was born.

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Is the story of the elephant historically confirmed?
Yes. It was so well known among the Arabs that they named the year after it, and the Quran mentions it to people who had heard about it from eyewitnesses. The Prophet ﷺ also referred to it in an authentic hadith.

### How do I tell the story without scaring my child?
Focus on Allah's protection, the elephant refusing to move, and the birds. Describe the army's defeat simply, without graphic details.

### Why does the surah ask "Have you not seen?" if the Prophet ﷺ wasn't born yet?
Because the event was so famous and certain that knowing about it was like seeing it. It was fresh in everyone's memory in Makkah.
