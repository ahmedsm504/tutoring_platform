Juz 4 begins with ﴿۞كُلُّ ٱلطَّعَامِ كَانَ حِلّٗا لِّبَنِيٓ إِسۡرَـٰٓءِيلَ﴾, "All food was lawful to the Children of Israel." Many memorizers call it **Juz Lan Tanalu**, after the famous verse just before it ("You will never attain righteousness..."), while others call it by its first words. It runs from **Al Imran 3:93 to An-Nisa 4:23**, ending with ﴿حُرِّمَتۡ عَلَيۡكُمۡ أُمَّهَٰتُكُمۡ وَبَنَاتُكُمۡ﴾, "Forbidden to you are your mothers and your daughters..." In the Madinah Mushaf it fills **pages 62 to 81**.

This juz holds the Quran's longest account of the **Battle of Uhud**, with its early victory, its reversal, and its lessons in obedience, leadership and patience. It closes Surah Al Imran with the verses on **people of understanding**, then opens **Surah An-Nisa** with rulings on family, orphans and inheritance.

## The big idea

Juz 4 revolves around **the unity of the community and its steadfastness in hardship**. It reminds the People of the Book that the first house set up for mankind was the Kaaba, then commands the believers to be God-conscious, to hold fast to Allah's rope and not to divide, and describes them as the best nation. Then comes the practical test at Uhud: how does a community behave when defeat follows victory? Al Imran closes with the model believer who reflects, endures and stands guard. An-Nisa then begins building the family, the core of the community.

The message: **a community united on Allah's rope, patient in trial, and just in its homes.**

## Part one: Al Imran 93-120

### 1. Food, the Sacred House and Hajj (93-101)
Allah answers those who claimed certain food prohibitions went back to Ibrahim, explaining that all food was lawful to the Children of Israel except what Israel (Ya'qub) forbade himself before the Torah was revealed. Then He declares: ﴿إِنَّ أَوَّلَ بَيۡتٖ وُضِعَ لِلنَّاسِ لَلَّذِي بِبَكَّةَ مُبَارَكٗا وَهُدٗى لِّلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾, "The first house established for mankind was the one at Bakkah, blessed and a guidance for the worlds." In it is the Station of Ibrahim, and whoever enters it is safe. Then the obligation of Hajj: ﴿وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ حِجُّ ٱلۡبَيۡتِ مَنِ ٱسۡتَطَاعَ إِلَيۡهِ سَبِيلٗاۚ﴾, "Pilgrimage to the House is a duty owed to Allah by all who are able to make the journey."

In Al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Dharr asked the Prophet ﷺ which mosque was first placed on earth; he said: "Al-Masjid al-Haram", then al-Masjid al-Aqsa, with forty years between them.

### 2. Commands for unity (102-109)
One of the greatest passages on building a community:
- ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ حَقَّ تُقَاتِهِۦ وَلَا تَمُوتُنَّ إِلَّا وَأَنتُم مُّسۡلِمُونَ﴾, "O you who believe, fear Allah as He should be feared, and do not die except as Muslims."
- ﴿وَٱعۡتَصِمُواْ بِحَبۡلِ ٱللَّهِ جَمِيعٗا وَلَا تَفَرَّقُواْۚ﴾, "And hold firmly to the rope of Allah all together and do not become divided", remembering how Allah joined their hearts when they were enemies. The Aws and Khazraj had fought long wars before Islam.
- ﴿وَلۡتَكُن مِّنكُمۡ أُمَّةࣱ يَدۡعُونَ إِلَى ٱلۡخَيۡرِ وَيَأۡمُرُونَ بِٱلۡمَعۡرُوفِ وَيَنۡهَوۡنَ عَنِ ٱلۡمُنكَرِۚ﴾, "Let there be among you a group inviting to good, enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong."
- ﴿وَلَا تَكُونُواْ كَٱلَّذِينَ تَفَرَّقُواْ وَٱخۡتَلَفُواْ مِنۢ بَعۡدِ مَا جَآءَهُمُ ٱلۡبَيِّنَٰتُۚ﴾, "Do not be like those who divided and differed after clear proofs came to them."
- Then the scene of the Day when some faces turn bright and others dark.

**The order is deliberate**: personal God-consciousness, then collective holding together, then a group that calls to good, then a warning against division. There is no unity without taqwa, and taqwa is incomplete without calling to good.

### 3. The best nation and the People of the Book (110-120)
﴿كُنتُمۡ خَيۡرَ أُمَّةٍ أُخۡرِجَتۡ لِلنَّاسِ تَأۡمُرُونَ بِٱلۡمَعۡرُوفِ وَتَنۡهَوۡنَ عَنِ ٱلۡمُنكَرِ وَتُؤۡمِنُونَ بِٱللَّهِۗ﴾, "You are the best nation brought forth for mankind: you enjoin what is right, forbid what is wrong and believe in Allah." Being "best" is conditional on these qualities, not granted by birth. Read [You Are the Best Nation: 3:110](/blog/kuntum-khayra-umma-meaning-english/#You-Are-the-Best-Nation-Brought-Forth-for-Mankind-Quran-3-11).

The Quran is fair to the People of the Book: ﴿۞لَيۡسُواْ سَوَآءٗۗ مِّنۡ أَهۡلِ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ أُمَّةࣱ قَآئِمَةࣱ يَتۡلُونَ ءَايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ ءَانَآءَ ٱلَّيۡلِ وَهُمۡ يَسۡجُدُونَ﴾, "They are not all alike: among the People of the Book is an upright community who recite Allah's verses in the night and prostrate." It then forbids taking as close confidants those who wish the believers harm, and gives the remedy: ﴿وَإِن تَصۡبِرُواْ وَتَتَّقُواْ لَا يَضُرُّكُمۡ كَيۡدُهُمۡ شَيۡـًٔاۗ﴾, "If you are patient and God-conscious, their plotting will not harm you at all."

## Part two: the Battle of Uhud (121-179)

This is the longest continuous passage about a battle in the Quran, around sixty verses. Uhud took place in Shawwal of the third year after the Hijrah.

### 1. The start, and a reminder of Badr (121-129)
﴿وَإِذۡ غَدَوۡتَ مِنۡ أَهۡلِكَ تُبَوِّئُ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ مَقَٰعِدَ لِلۡقِتَالِۗ﴾, "And when you left your household in the morning to post the believers at their stations for battle." Two groups almost lost heart, and Allah reminds them of Badr: ﴿وَلَقَدۡ نَصَرَكُمُ ٱللَّهُ بِبَدۡرٖ وَأَنتُمۡ أَذِلَّةࣱۖ﴾, "Allah had already given you victory at Badr when you were few and weak." Then the promise of angels if they were patient and God-conscious, and the rule: ﴿وَمَا ٱلنَّصۡرُ إِلَّا مِنۡ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡعَزِيزِ ٱلۡحَكِيمِ﴾, "Victory is only from Allah, the Mighty, the Wise."

### 2. A moral pause in the middle of battle (130-138)
In the middle of the battle narrative comes a ban on riba multiplied many times, then: ﴿۞وَسَارِعُوٓاْ إِلَىٰ مَغۡفِرَةٖ مِّن رَّبِّكُمۡ وَجَنَّةٍ عَرۡضُهَا ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ وَٱلۡأَرۡضُ أُعِدَّتۡ لِلۡمُتَّقِينَ﴾, "And hasten to forgiveness from your Lord and a Garden as wide as the heavens and the earth, prepared for the God-conscious." Their qualities follow:
- ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ فِي ٱلسَّرَّآءِ وَٱلضَّرَّآءِ وَٱلۡكَٰظِمِينَ ٱلۡغَيۡظَ وَٱلۡعَافِينَ عَنِ ٱلنَّاسِۗ وَٱللَّهُ يُحِبُّ ٱلۡمُحۡسِنِينَ﴾, "who spend in ease and hardship, who restrain anger and pardon people; and Allah loves those who do good."
- ﴿وَٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا فَعَلُواْ فَٰحِشَةً أَوۡ ظَلَمُوٓاْ أَنفُسَهُمۡ ذَكَرُواْ ٱللَّهَ فَٱسۡتَغۡفَرُواْ لِذُنُوبِهِمۡ﴾, "and those who, when they commit an immorality or wrong themselves, remember Allah and seek forgiveness for their sins."

**Why here?** Some commentators note that the setback came from love of spoils, so the Quran treats love of money by banning riba and urging spending; and after Uhud the believers needed to restrain anger, forgive and seek forgiveness. Read [Hasten to Forgiveness: 3:133](/blog/kuntum-khayra-umma-meaning-english/#Hasten-to-Forgiveness-from-Your-Lord-Quran-3-133-134-Explain).

### 3. The law of alternating days (139-148)
﴿وَلَا تَهِنُواْ وَلَا تَحۡزَنُواْ وَأَنتُمُ ٱلۡأَعۡلَوۡنَ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤۡمِنِينَ﴾, "Do not weaken and do not grieve, for you will be superior if you are believers." Then: ﴿إِن يَمۡسَسۡكُمۡ قَرۡحࣱ فَقَدۡ مَسَّ ٱلۡقَوۡمَ قَرۡحࣱ مِّثۡلُهُۥۚ وَتِلۡكَ ٱلۡأَيَّامُ نُدَاوِلُهَا بَيۡنَ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾, "If a wound has touched you, a similar wound has touched the other side; and these days We alternate among people." Victory and defeat take turns, and the wisdom is to distinguish the believers, take martyrs from among them and purify hearts.

Then the verse Abu Bakr recited on the day the Prophet ﷺ died, as reported in Sahih Al-Bukhari: ﴿وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولࣱ قَدۡ خَلَتۡ مِن قَبۡلِهِ ٱلرُّسُلُۚ أَفَإِيْن مَّاتَ أَوۡ قُتِلَ ٱنقَلَبۡتُمۡ عَلَىٰٓ أَعۡقَٰبِكُمۡۚ﴾, "Muhammad is no more than a messenger; messengers have passed before him. If he dies or is killed, will you turn back on your heels?" A rumour had spread at Uhud that the Prophet ﷺ had been killed, and Allah made clear that the religion does not depend on any one person remaining. Then comes the example of the devoted men who fought alongside earlier prophets and never weakened.

### 4. The moment of disobedience (149-155)
﴿وَلَقَدۡ صَدَقَكُمُ ٱللَّهُ وَعۡدَهُۥٓ إِذۡ تَحُسُّونَهُم بِإِذۡنِهِۦۖ حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا فَشِلۡتُمۡ وَتَنَٰزَعۡتُمۡ فِي ٱلۡأَمۡرِ وَعَصَيۡتُم مِّنۢ بَعۡدِ مَآ أَرَىٰكُم مَّا تُحِبُّونَۚ﴾, "Allah had fulfilled His promise to you when you were killing them by His permission, until you lost courage, disputed about the order and disobeyed after He had shown you what you love." In Sahih Al-Bukhari, al-Bara' reports that the Prophet ﷺ put 'Abdullah ibn Jubayr over the archers and ordered them not to leave their post. When they saw the enemy fleeing, most of them went down for the spoils, and Khalid ibn al-Walid, not yet Muslim, circled behind.

The Quran names three stages of the mistake: **losing courage**, then **disputing**, then **disobeying**. It mentions the calm drowsiness that covered a group of believers, exposes the hypocrites' thoughts, and announces pardon for those who turned away on the day the two armies met.

### 5. Merciful leadership (156-164)
﴿فَبِمَا رَحۡمَةٖ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ لِنتَ لَهُمۡۖ وَلَوۡ كُنتَ فَظًّا غَلِيظَ ٱلۡقَلۡبِ لَٱنفَضُّواْ مِنۡ حَوۡلِكَۖ فَٱعۡفُ عَنۡهُمۡ وَٱسۡتَغۡفِرۡ لَهُمۡ وَشَاوِرۡهُمۡ فِي ٱلۡأَمۡرِۖ﴾, "By mercy from Allah you were gentle with them; had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have scattered from around you. So pardon them, ask forgiveness for them and consult them in the matter."

Reflect: this came after some Companions had erred, yet the Prophet ﷺ was told to be gentle, to pardon and to seek forgiveness for them, and even to keep consulting them, although consultation before Uhud had led to going out of Madinah. A mistake does not cancel the principle of consultation. Then: ﴿فَإِذَا عَزَمۡتَ فَتَوَكَّلۡ عَلَى ٱللَّهِۚ﴾, "And once you have decided, put your trust in Allah." Read [By Mercy from Allah: 3:159](/blog/kuntum-khayra-umma-meaning-english/#By-Mercy-from-Allah-You-Were-Gentle-with-Them-Quran-3-159-Ex).

### 6. The cause of the setback and the life of martyrs (165-175)
When the believers asked, "How did this happen?", the answer came: ﴿قُلۡ هُوَ مِنۡ عِندِ أَنفُسِكُمۡۗ﴾, "Say: it is from yourselves." The hypocrites who withdrew before the battle are exposed. Then: ﴿وَلَا تَحۡسَبَنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ قُتِلُواْ فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ أَمۡوَٰتَۢاۚ بَلۡ أَحۡيَآءٌ عِندَ رَبِّهِمۡ يُرۡزَقُونَ﴾, "Never think of those killed in the way of Allah as dead; rather they are alive with their Lord, receiving provision."

Then the march to Hamra' al-Asad the next day, when the wounded Muslims answered Allah and the Messenger: ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ قَالَ لَهُمُ ٱلنَّاسُ إِنَّ ٱلنَّاسَ قَدۡ جَمَعُواْ لَكُمۡ فَٱخۡشَوۡهُمۡ فَزَادَهُمۡ إِيمَٰنٗا وَقَالُواْ حَسۡبُنَا ٱللَّهُ وَنِعۡمَ ٱلۡوَكِيلُ﴾, "Those to whom people said, 'The people have gathered against you, so fear them', but it only increased them in faith, and they said: Allah is sufficient for us, and He is the best Disposer of affairs." In Sahih Al-Bukhari, Ibn 'Abbas said Ibrahim said these words when thrown into the fire, and Muhammad ﷺ said them when told this. Read ["Allah Is Sufficient for Us, and He Is the Best Disposer of Affairs": Quran 3:173 Explained](/blog/kuntum-khayra-umma-meaning-english/#Allah-Is-Sufficient-for-Us-and-He-Is-the-Best-Disposer-of-Af).

### 7. The wisdom of trial (176-179)
Do not be grieved by those who hurry into disbelief, and let the disbelievers not think that being given time is good for them. Then: ﴿مَّا كَانَ ٱللَّهُ لِيَذَرَ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ عَلَىٰ مَآ أَنتُمۡ عَلَيۡهِ حَتَّىٰ يَمِيزَ ٱلۡخَبِيثَ مِنَ ٱلطَّيِّبِۗ﴾, "Allah would not leave the believers in the state you are in until He separates the bad from the good."

**Summary of the Quran's lessons from Uhud**:
1. Obedience brings victory; disobedience brings defeat.
2. Love of this world damages unity.
3. Days alternate, and the believer does not lose heart.
4. The religion remains even after the Messenger ﷺ.
5. A leader pardons, consults and trusts in Allah.
6. Setbacks come "from yourselves", so review starts within.
7. Martyrs are alive, and trial sorts people.

## Part three: the close of Al Imran (180-200)

- **Miserliness**: ﴿سَيُطَوَّقُونَ مَا بَخِلُواْ بِهِۦ يَوۡمَ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِۗ﴾, "what they withheld will be hung around their necks on the Day of Resurrection."
- **Death comes to all**: ﴿كُلُّ نَفۡسٖ ذَآئِقَةُ ٱلۡمَوۡتِۗ وَإِنَّمَا تُوَفَّوۡنَ أُجُورَكُمۡ يَوۡمَ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِۖ فَمَن زُحۡزِحَ عَنِ ٱلنَّارِ وَأُدۡخِلَ ٱلۡجَنَّةَ فَقَدۡ فَازَۗ﴾, "Every soul will taste death, and you will be paid your full reward on the Day of Resurrection; whoever is drawn away from the Fire and admitted to Paradise has succeeded." Read [Every Soul Will Taste Death](/blog/kuntum-khayra-umma-meaning-english/#Every-Soul-Will-Taste-Death-Quran-3-185-Explained).
- **The law of hardship**: ﴿۞لَتُبۡلَوُنَّ فِيٓ أَمۡوَٰلِكُمۡ وَأَنفُسِكُمۡ﴾, "You will surely be tested in your wealth and yourselves", with the advice to be patient and God-conscious.

### People of understanding (190-195)
﴿إِنَّ فِي خَلۡقِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَٱخۡتِلَٰفِ ٱلَّيۡلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ لَأٓيَٰتٖ لِّأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَلۡبَٰبِ﴾, "In the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of night and day are signs for people of understanding." Their description: ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ يَذۡكُرُونَ ٱللَّهَ قِيَٰمٗا وَقُعُودٗا وَعَلَىٰ جُنُوبِهِمۡ وَيَتَفَكَّرُونَ فِي خَلۡقِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ﴾, "who remember Allah standing, sitting and lying on their sides, and reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth." Five prayers follow, each beginning "Our Lord", and then the answer: ﴿أَنِّي لَآ أُضِيعُ عَمَلَ عَٰمِلࣲ مِّنكُم مِّن ذَكَرٍ أَوۡ أُنثَىٰۖ﴾, "I will not let the deed of any doer among you be lost, whether male or female."

In Al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn 'Abbas spent a night at the Prophet's house; when the Prophet ﷺ woke at night he recited the last ten verses of Al Imran. Reciting them on rising for night prayer is a Sunnah.

### The close (196-200)
Do not be deceived by the disbelievers moving freely through the land; then fairness to those of the People of the Book who believe; then the last verse: ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱصۡبِرُواْ وَصَابِرُواْ وَرَابِطُواْ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تُفۡلِحُونَ﴾, "O you who believe, be patient, outlast in patience, stand guard and fear Allah, so that you may succeed." The surah that narrated Uhud closes with four commands that are the provision of every striver. Read [Be Patient and Outlast in Patience: 3:200](/blog/kuntum-khayra-umma-meaning-english/#Be-Patient-Outlast-in-Patience-and-Stand-Guard-Quran-3-200-E).

## Part four: the opening of Surah An-Nisa (1-23)

An-Nisa is a Madani surah of 176 verses, named "The Women" for its many rulings concerning women. It opens with a call to all people: ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱتَّقُواْ رَبَّكُمُ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفۡسٖ وَٰحِدَةٖ وَخَلَقَ مِنۡهَا زَوۡجَهَا وَبَثَّ مِنۡهُمَا رِجَالٗا كَثِيرٗا وَنِسَآءٗۚ﴾, "O mankind, fear your Lord who created you from one soul, created from it its mate, and spread from them many men and women", then ﴿وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ ٱلَّذِي تَسَآءَلُونَ بِهِۦ وَٱلۡأَرۡحَامَۚ﴾, "and fear Allah, through whom you ask of one another, and the ties of kinship." The Prophet ﷺ recited this verse in the sermon of need (khutbat al-hajah). For the whole surah see [Surah An-Nisa Meaning in English](/blog/khalaqakum-min-nafsin-wahida-meaning-english/#Surah-An-Nisa-Meaning-Rights-Justice-and-Care-for-the-Vulner).

**The link with Al Imran**: Al Imran ended with taqwa, and An-Nisa begins with taqwa twice in one verse. Al Imran built the unity of the community; An-Nisa builds the unity of the family on which that community rests.

### 1. The wealth of orphans (2-10)
After Uhud there were many orphans, and the rulings came: give orphans their property and do not exchange the bad for the good. Then: ﴿وَإِنۡ خِفۡتُمۡ أَلَّا تُقۡسِطُواْ فِي ٱلۡيَتَٰمَىٰ فَٱنكِحُواْ مَا طَابَ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلنِّسَآءِ مَثۡنَىٰ وَثُلَٰثَ وَرُبَٰعَۖ فَإِنۡ خِفۡتُمۡ أَلَّا تَعۡدِلُواْ فَوَٰحِدَةً﴾, "If you fear you will not deal justly with orphan girls, then marry women who please you, two, three or four; but if you fear you will not be just, then one." In Sahih Al-Bukhari, 'A'ishah explained that it concerns an orphan girl under her guardian's care whom he wished to marry for her wealth and beauty without giving her a fair dowry.

Then: give women their dowries as a free gift, do not hand wealth to those unable to manage it, test orphans when they reach maturity, and the severe warning: ﴿إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَأۡكُلُونَ أَمۡوَٰلَ ٱلۡيَتَٰمَىٰ ظُلۡمًا إِنَّمَا يَأۡكُلُونَ فِي بُطُونِهِمۡ نَارٗاۖ﴾, "Those who consume the wealth of orphans unjustly are only filling their bellies with fire." See also [Do Not Oppress the Orphan](/blog/surah-al-fajr-meaning-english/#As-for-the-Orphan-Do-Not-Oppress-As-for-the-One-Who-Asks-Do-).

### 2. The inheritance verses (11-14)
﴿يُوصِيكُمُ ٱللَّهُ فِيٓ أَوۡلَٰدِكُمۡۖ لِلذَّكَرِ مِثۡلُ حَظِّ ٱلۡأُنثَيَيۡنِۚ﴾, "Allah instructs you concerning your children: for the male, the share of two females." Verses 11 and 12 set out the shares of children, parents, spouses and maternal siblings, and the surah closes with the kalalah verse (176). Some beautiful details:
- They open with "Allah instructs you", for Allah is more merciful to children than their own parents.
- ﴿ءَابَآؤُكُمۡ وَأَبۡنَآؤُكُمۡ لَا تَدۡرُونَ أَيُّهُمۡ أَقۡرَبُ لَكُمۡ نَفۡعٗاۚ﴾, "Your parents and your children: you do not know which of them is nearer to you in benefit." The division is Allah's, not human guesswork.
- The repeated reminder that division comes after any bequest and debt.
- They end with "These are the limits of Allah", with promise and warning.

### 3. Repentance and living with wives in kindness (15-21)
- Rulings on immorality, then the conditions of accepted repentance: ﴿إِنَّمَا ٱلتَّوۡبَةُ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ لِلَّذِينَ يَعۡمَلُونَ ٱلسُّوٓءَ بِجَهَٰلَةࣲ ثُمَّ يَتُوبُونَ مِن قَرِيبࣲ﴾, "Repentance accepted by Allah is for those who do wrong out of ignorance and then repent soon after", and that repentance is not accepted once death arrives.
- ﴿لَا يَحِلُّ لَكُمۡ أَن تَرِثُواْ ٱلنِّسَآءَ كَرۡهٗاۖ﴾, "It is not lawful for you to inherit women against their will", ending a pre-Islamic custom.
- ﴿وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِٱلۡمَعۡرُوفِۚ فَإِن كَرِهۡتُمُوهُنَّ فَعَسَىٰٓ أَن تَكۡرَهُواْ شَيۡـٔٗا وَيَجۡعَلَ ٱللَّهُ فِيهِ خَيۡرٗا كَثِيرٗا﴾, "Live with them in kindness. If you dislike them, perhaps you dislike something in which Allah has placed much good."
- No taking back any of the dowry at separation: ﴿وَأَخَذۡنَ مِنكُم مِّيثَٰقًا غَلِيظٗا﴾, "and they have taken from you a solemn covenant."

### 4. Women one may not marry (22-23)
The juz ends with those one may not marry: the father's wife, then ﴿حُرِّمَتۡ عَلَيۡكُمۡ أُمَّهَٰتُكُمۡ وَبَنَاتُكُمۡ وَأَخَوَٰتُكُمۡ وَعَمَّـٰتُكُمۡ وَخَٰلَٰتُكُمۡ﴾, "Forbidden to you are your mothers, daughters, sisters, paternal aunts and maternal aunts...", through those forbidden by lineage, nursing and marriage, and marrying two sisters at once.

## Tajweed points in Juz 4

### Ikhfa' in ﴿مِّنكُمۡ﴾, ﴿كُنتُمۡ﴾ and ﴿فَٱنكِحُواْ﴾
Sakin noon before kaf and ta is hidden with a two-count ghunnah. It occurs constantly in this juz.

### Qalqalah in ﴿بِبَدۡرٖ﴾
The sakin dal in the middle of the phrase gets a light echo (qalqalah sughra).

### Merging in ﴿إِذۡ هَمَّت طَّآئِفَتَانِ﴾
The ta merges completely into the following ta' (a heavy t), a case of idgham between letters from the same articulation point.

### Separated madd in ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا﴾
Calls are frequent in this juz; the separated madd is four or five counts for Hafs by way of Ash-Shatibiyyah.

### ﴿أَفَإِيْن مَّاتَ﴾
It is written with an extra ya that is not pronounced: read *afa-in mata*. Beginners often stumble on this spelling.

## Similar verses to watch

### 1. The good news of angels: Al Imran vs Al-Anfal
- Al Imran 126: ﴿إِلَّا بُشۡرَىٰ لَكُمۡ وَلِتَطۡمَئِنَّ قُلُوبُكُم بِهِۦۗ وَمَا ٱلنَّصۡرُ إِلَّا مِنۡ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡعَزِيزِ ٱلۡحَكِيمِ﴾.
- Al-Anfal 10: ﴿إِلَّا بُشۡرَىٰ وَلِتَطۡمَئِنَّ بِهِۦ قُلُوبُكُمۡۚ وَمَا ٱلنَّصۡرُ إِلَّا مِنۡ عِندِ ٱللَّهِۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ﴾.
**Key**: Al Imran has *lakum*, *qulubukum bihi* and "al-'Aziz al-Hakim" as attributes; Al-Anfal drops *lakum*, has *bihi qulubukum*, and ends "inna Allaha 'azizun hakim".

### 2. "Hasten" and "race"
- Al Imran 133: ﴿وَسَارِعُوٓاْ﴾ (hasten), ﴿عَرۡضُهَا ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ وَٱلۡأَرۡضُ أُعِدَّتۡ لِلۡمُتَّقِينَ﴾.
- Al-Hadid 21: ﴿سَابِقُوٓاْ﴾ (race), ﴿عَرۡضُهَا كَعَرۡضِ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ أُعِدَّتۡ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ بِٱللَّهِ وَرُسُلِهِۦۚ﴾.

### 3. "Every soul will taste death" in three surahs
- Al Imran 185: followed by ﴿وَإِنَّمَا تُوَفَّوۡنَ أُجُورَكُمۡ يَوۡمَ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِۖ﴾.
- Al-Anbiya 35: followed by ﴿وَنَبۡلُوكُم بِٱلشَّرِّ وَٱلۡخَيۡرِ فِتۡنَةٗۖ﴾.
- Al-'Ankabut 57: followed by ﴿ثُمَّ إِلَيۡنَا تُرۡجَعُونَ﴾.

### 4. Verse endings in the Uhud passage
Endings such as "Allah knows what is in the breasts", "Allah sees what you do" and "Allah is aware of what you do" are close. Link each to its verse's meaning: when intentions are mentioned, "what is in the breasts"; when actions, "what you do".

## How to memorize Juz 4 with understanding

1. **Read a short account of Uhud before memorizing its verses**; the verses will then narrate what you already know.
2. **List the events of Uhud on a sheet**: setting out, the two groups, the archers, the rumour, the drowsiness, the hypocrites, Hamra' al-Asad, and write verse numbers next to each.
3. **Memorize the qualities of the God-conscious (133-136)** as one unit; they are often quoted in sermons and prayers.
4. **Learn the verses on people of understanding** intending to recite them when you wake for night prayer.
5. **Understand inheritance before memorizing it**: write a table of shares next to the verses.
6. **Revise the opening of An-Nisa with Juz 5**, since the family theme continues there.

For a fuller method see [How to Memorize the Quran Step by Step](/blog/how-to-memorize-quran-step-by-step/).

## Practical lessons from Juz 4

- **Hold to Allah's rope with your brothers and sisters**, and never be a cause of division.
- **The nation's excellence lies in action**: enjoining good, forbidding wrong, and faith.
- **Restrain your anger and pardon**: this is the mark of those who do good.
- **When you slip, seek forgiveness and do not persist.**
- **Love of the world ruins teamwork**, as it did with the archers.
- **Leadership means gentleness, pardon and consultation.**
- **Say "Hasbuna Allah wa ni'mal wakil" when afraid.**
- **Reflect on the heavens and the earth**; do not pass them by heedlessly.
- **Protect the rights of orphans and wives**: it is a solemn covenant.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is Juz 4 called "Lan Tanalu"?
Many memorizers name it after the famous verse just before it, ﴿لَن تَنَالُواْ ٱلۡبِرَّ﴾, while others name it by its first words, ﴿كُلُّ ٱلطَّعَامِ﴾.

### How many verses discuss the Battle of Uhud?
The Uhud passage in Al Imran runs from about verse 121 to verse 179, around sixty verses.

### Where does Juz 4 end?
At An-Nisa 4:23. Juz 5 begins with ﴿۞وَٱلۡمُحۡصَنَٰتُ مِنَ ٱلنِّسَآءِ﴾, "And forbidden are married women..."

### What is special about the end of Al Imran?
In Al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ recited the last ten verses of Al Imran when he rose at night.

### Why does An-Nisa begin with creation from one soul?
Because the surah goes on to detail family, kinship and inheritance, so it begins by reminding people that they are one family from one origin, which obliges them to mercy and justice.

## Conclusion

Juz 4 is a school of steadfastness. It teaches that unity rests on taqwa, that excellence is earned by action, that defeat is a lesson rather than an end, that a merciful leader keeps people together, that reflecting on creation leads to prayer and action, and that just families are the foundation of a strong community.
