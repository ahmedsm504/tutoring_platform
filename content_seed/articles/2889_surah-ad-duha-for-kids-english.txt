Have you ever felt sad and alone, as if nobody cared about you? Everyone feels like that sometimes, even grown-ups. Even the Prophet ﷺ once felt sad when the revelation stopped coming for a while, and some people mocked him, saying: "Your Lord has left you!" So Allah sent down a beautiful surah to comfort him: "Your Lord has not abandoned you, nor has He become displeased." That surah is Surah Ad-Duha.

Surah Ad-Duha is like a warm hug from Allah. It reminds us that after every dark night comes a bright morning, that Allah always cares for us, and that better things are coming. It also teaches us to pass that kindness on: to orphans, to people who ask for help, and by talking about Allah's blessings. In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Ad-Duha to children in simple English, with activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Ad-Duha

- **Position**: surah 93, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: eleven.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Name**: ad-Duha means the bright morning, the time when the sun has risen and the day is full of light.

## The Whole Surah

﴿وَٱلضُّحَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَٱلَّيۡلِ إِذَا سَجَىٰ﴾ ﴿مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ وَمَا قَلَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَلَلۡأٓخِرَةُ خَيۡرࣱ لَّكَ مِنَ ٱلۡأُولَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَلَسَوۡفَ يُعۡطِيكَ رَبُّكَ فَتَرۡضَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَلَمۡ يَجِدۡكَ يَتِيمٗا فَـَٔاوَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَوَجَدَكَ ضَآلّٗا فَهَدَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَوَجَدَكَ عَآئِلٗا فَأَغۡنَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَأَمَّا ٱلۡيَتِيمَ فَلَا تَقۡهَرۡ﴾ ﴿وَأَمَّا ٱلسَّآئِلَ فَلَا تَنۡهَرۡ﴾ ﴿وَأَمَّا بِنِعۡمَةِ رَبِّكَ فَحَدِّثۡ﴾ (Quran 93:1-11)

## Why Was It Revealed?

Jundub ibn Sufyan said that the Prophet ﷺ was unwell and did not get up to pray at night for a night or two. A woman came and said: "O Muhammad, I think your devil has left you." So Allah revealed: "By the morning brightness, and the night when it grows still, your Lord has not abandoned you, nor has He become displeased" (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### Explaining it to your child
"For a little while, the revelation paused, and some unkind people said: Allah has left you! The Prophet ﷺ felt sad. Then Allah sent this surah to tell him: I haven't left you, and I'm not upset with you. Allah never abandons the people He loves."

## Verses 1–2: By the Morning and the Night

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah swears by the bright morning and by the calm, still night. Why these two? Because the night is dark and quiet, and then the morning comes full of light. Just like that, sad times are followed by happy times. The night never lasts forever."

### Activity: night and morning
Draw a picture divided into two halves: a dark, quiet night with stars on one side, and a bright, sunny morning on the other. In the middle, write: "After every night, a morning comes."

## Verse 3: Your Lord Has Not Abandoned You

### Explaining it to your child
"Ma wadda'aka means: Allah hasn't said goodbye to you. Wa ma qala means: and He isn't displeased with you. When you feel alone, remember this verse. Allah is always with you, always hearing you, always caring for you."

### Lesson for kids
"Sometimes we feel that nobody understands us. But Allah always understands. You can talk to Him any time, in your heart or in your du'a."

## Verse 4: The Later Is Better Than the First

### Explaining it to your child
"What comes later is better for you than what came before. For the Prophet ﷺ, this meant that his life would get better and better, and that the Hereafter would be best of all. For us, it means: keep going, keep doing good, and trust that Allah has good things ahead."

## Verse 5: Your Lord Will Give You, and You Will Be Satisfied

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah promised the Prophet ﷺ that He would give him so much that he would be completely happy. Scholars mention that this includes the Prophet's intercession for his nation on the Day of Judgement. Allah's gifts are so big, they make the heart full."

## Verses 6–8: Allah's Care for the Prophet ﷺ

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah reminds the Prophet ﷺ of three ways He took care of him:
1. **Did He not find you an orphan and give you shelter?** The Prophet's father died before he was born, and his mother died when he was six. Allah gave him a loving grandfather, then a caring uncle.
2. **Did He not find you unaware and guide you?** Before revelation, he didn't know the details of Allah's message, and Allah guided him.
3. **Did He not find you in need and make you self-sufficient?** He was poor, and Allah gave him what he needed."

### Lesson for kids
"Allah took care of the Prophet ﷺ every step of the way, and He takes care of you too. Think about all the people Allah has given you: parents, grandparents, teachers, friends."

### Activity: my care map
Draw your child in the middle of a page, and around them, draw all the people and things Allah has given to look after them: family, home, school, doctor, food. Say: "Allah cares for you through all of these."

## Verses 9–11: Three Commands

### Verse 9: As for the orphan, do not be harsh with him
"The Prophet ﷺ was an orphan, so Allah told him: be gentle with orphans. Never be rough or unkind to a child who has lost a parent."

### Verse 10: As for the one who asks, do not turn him away
"If someone asks you for help, or asks you a question to learn, don't push them away rudely. Help if you can, and if you can't, say no kindly."

### Verse 11: As for the blessing of your Lord, speak of it
"Talk about Allah's blessings! Not to show off, but to thank Him. Say: Alhamdulillah, Allah gave me a lovely family. Alhamdulillah, I passed my test. Talking about blessings makes our hearts grateful."

### Notice the beautiful match
Scholars point out a lovely connection:
- "Did He not find you an orphan?" → "So don't be harsh with the orphan."
- "Did He not find you in need?" → "So don't turn away the one who asks."
- "Did He not guide you?" → "So speak of your Lord's blessing."

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah was kind to you, so be kind to others in the same way. That's how we say thank you."

## Activities

### Activity: the blessings talk
At dinner, each family member shares one blessing Allah gave them today. This is "wa amma bi ni'mati Rabbika fa-haddith" in action.

### Activity: comfort notes
Write little notes with verse 3's meaning, "Your Lord has not abandoned you", and let your child give one to a friend or family member who is having a hard time.

### Activity: the kind answer
Practise with your child how to respond kindly when someone asks for something: "Sure, here you go!" or "I'm sorry, I can't right now, but maybe later." Never "Go away!"

## When to Recite Ad-Duha

Many people love to recite Surah Ad-Duha when they feel sad or worried, because its words are so comforting. Teach your child: "When you're sad, read Surah Ad-Duha, and remember: Allah hasn't left you."

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Worst Week"

It was the worst week ever for Ibrahim. His best friend moved to another city, he got a low mark in maths, and his grandmother was in hospital. On Thursday night, he sat on his bed and cried quietly.

His father came in and sat beside him. "Want to tell me what's wrong?" Ibrahim told him everything. "It feels like nothing is going right. Like nobody cares."

His father put his arm around him. "Do you know that the Prophet ﷺ once felt sad too? People said Allah had left him. So Allah sent him a surah." He opened the mushaf to Surah Ad-Duha and read slowly: "Wad-duha, wal-layli idha saja, ma wadda'aka Rabbuka wa ma qala." "Your Lord has not abandoned you."

"Look," said his father, "Allah swears by the night and then the morning. Every night ends. This week is your night, and the morning is coming."

Ibrahim wiped his eyes. Over the next weeks, things slowly got better: his grandmother came home, he started video-calling his friend every Friday, and with extra practice, his maths mark improved. One day, he told his father: "Wa lal-akhiratu khayrun laka minal-ula. The later really was better!"

Then Ibrahim noticed a new boy at school sitting alone, whose father had passed away. He remembered verse 9 and went to sit with him. "Want to play with us?" he asked. Now he was the one bringing the morning to someone else's night.

Ask your child: "Why was Ibrahim sad? Which verse comforted him? How did he help someone else?"

## What We Learn From Surah Ad-Duha

- **Allah never abandons** those who turn to Him.
- **After every night** comes a morning.
- **Better days** are ahead.
- **Allah cares for us** through people and blessings.
- **Be gentle with orphans**.
- **Don't turn away** those who ask.
- **Talk about Allah's blessings** with gratitude.
- **Pass kindness on** the way Allah was kind to us.

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Is it okay for my child to feel sad?
Yes. Sadness is a natural feeling, and even the Prophet ﷺ felt it. The surah teaches that sadness passes and that Allah is near, which helps children feel understood rather than ashamed.

### Does "speak of your Lord's blessing" mean showing off?
No. It means acknowledging and thanking Allah, not boasting. Teach your child to say "Alhamdulillah" when sharing good news, and to be sensitive to friends who may not have the same blessings.

### When should my child memorize Ad-Duha?
It's a lovely surah for ages five and up. Its comforting meaning makes it especially valuable for children going through changes like moving home, starting school or losing a loved one.


### Can Surah Ad-Duha help a grieving child?
Its message that Allah has not abandoned us, and that later will be better, can be very comforting. Read it gently together, and pair it with lots of love, listening and patience.
