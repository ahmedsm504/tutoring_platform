Arabic has letters pronounced with a **full, heavy** sound (tafkhīm) and others with a **thin, light** sound (tarqīq). English has nothing exactly like this, so it takes practice.

## The seven always-heavy letters
Gathered in: **خُصَّ ضَغۡطٍ قِظۡ** (*khuṣṣa ḍaghṭin qiẓ*).
| Letter | Name |
|---|---|
| خ | Khaa |
| ص | Ṣaad |
| ض | Ḍaad |
| غ | Ghayn |
| ط | Ṭaa |
| ق | Qaf |
| ظ | Ẓaa |
- **How:** raise the back of the tongue toward the roof of the mouth.
- **Strongest:** ṭaa, ḍaad, ṣaad and ẓaa, because the tongue also covers the roof of the mouth.

## Letters that change
| Letter | Heavy when | Light when |
|---|---|---|
| ر Raa | With fatḥah or ḍammah, as in ﴿رَبِّ﴾ | With kasrah, as in ﴿رِجَالࣱ﴾ |
| ل in "Allah" | After fatḥah or ḍammah: ﴿قَالَ ٱللَّهُ﴾ | After kasrah: ﴿بِسۡمِ ٱللَّهِ﴾ |
| ا Alif | After a heavy letter: ﴿قَالَ﴾ | After a light letter: ﴿مَٰلِكِ﴾ |

## All other letters are light
- Including ﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ﴾: the ḥaa is light even though it comes from the throat.

## Tips for English speakers
1. **Don't make light letters heavy** before alif: say *kitāb* lightly, not *kotob*.
2. **Keep heavy letters heavy** even with kasrah: ﴿قِيلَ﴾ stays heavy, though a little less.
3. **Record yourself** and compare with a teacher.
