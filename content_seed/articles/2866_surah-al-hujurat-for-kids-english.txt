If we wanted to write a "code of good manners" for a classroom, a home or a sports team, we could not find anything more complete than Surah Al-Hujurat. In just eighteen verses, it teaches us how to speak to adults, what to do when we hear a piece of news, how to make peace between people who are fighting, why we should never mock anyone or call them hurtful names, and why we don't spy on people or talk about them behind their backs. That is why some scholars call it "the surah of manners".

Near its end is a verse that fits every school in the world where children come from different countries: Allah made people into nations and tribes so that they may get to know one another, and the most honoured of them in the sight of Allah is the most mindful of Him. Nobody is better because of their colour, country or money, only because of their taqwa. In this guide, we explain Surah Al-Hujurat to children in simple English, with activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Hujurat

- **Position**: surah 49, in juz 26.
- **Number of verses**: 18.
- **Type**: Madinan.
- **Name**: al-hujurat means "the private rooms", the small rooms where the Prophet's wives lived next to the masjid. Some visitors once stood outside them shouting: "O Muhammad, come out to us!" Allah taught them better manners.

### Five calls to the believers
The surah has five calls that begin "O you who believe", and each one teaches a new manner, followed by one call to all of humanity, "O mankind". Ibn Mas'ud said: when you hear Allah say "O you who believe", listen carefully, because it is either something good He commands or something bad He forbids.

### Activity: count the calls
Read the surah with your child and ask them to raise a hand every time they hear "Ya ayyuhal-ladhina amanu". At the end they count: five, plus one "Ya ayyuhan-nas".

## Manner 1: Don't Put Yourself Ahead of Allah and His Messenger

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ لَا تُقَدِّمُواْ بَيۡنَ يَدَيِ ٱللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِۦۖ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمࣱ﴾ (Quran 49:1)

### Explaining it to your child
"Don't put your opinion before what Allah and His Messenger said. If we know Allah commanded something, we obey. We don't say: But I think differently. Allah's words and His Messenger's words come first."

## Manner 2: Don't Raise Your Voice

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ لَا تَرۡفَعُوٓاْ أَصۡوَٰتَكُمۡ فَوۡقَ صَوۡتِ ٱلنَّبِيِّ وَلَا تَجۡهَرُواْ لَهُۥ بِٱلۡقَوۡلِ كَجَهۡرِ بَعۡضِكُمۡ لِبَعۡضٍ أَن تَحۡبَطَ أَعۡمَٰلُكُمۡ وَأَنتُمۡ لَا تَشۡعُرُونَ﴾ (Quran 49:2)

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah taught the companions not to raise their voices above the Prophet's voice, and not to speak to him loudly as they spoke to each other. It is good manners to lower our voices with adults: parents, teachers and grandparents. And when we hear a hadith of the Prophet ﷺ, we listen respectfully."

### From the seerah
Thabit ibn Qays naturally had a loud voice. When this verse was revealed, he sat at home crying, afraid his deeds were lost. The Prophet ﷺ asked about him and then gave him the good news that he was among the people of Paradise (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### Activity: the respect voice
Agree on a "respect voice" with adults: calm and clear. Try saying the same sentence to Grandpa loudly, then in the respect voice. Which sounds nicer?

## Manner 3: Check Before You Believe

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓاْ إِن جَآءَكُمۡ فَاسِقُۢ بِنَبَإٖ فَتَبَيَّنُوٓاْ أَن تُصِيبُواْ قَوۡمَۢا بِجَهَٰلَةٖ فَتُصۡبِحُواْ عَلَىٰ مَا فَعَلۡتُمۡ نَٰدِمِينَ﴾ (Quran 49:6)

### Explaining it to your child
"If someone brings you news, especially someone you don't trust, don't believe it straight away. Check first. If you believe it and act on it, you might hurt someone unfairly, and then regret it."

### Lesson for kids: news on phones
"Today we get lots of news from phones and friends. If someone says: So-and-so said something bad about you, don't get angry straight away. Check first. And if you get a strange message, don't forward it until you know it's true."

### Activity: broken telephone
Sit in a circle. The first person whispers a sentence to the next, and so on. The last person says it out loud. It will have changed a lot! Then say: "That's how news changes as it travels, and that's why Allah told us to check."

## Manner 4: Make Peace Between People

﴿وَإِن طَآئِفَتَانِ مِنَ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ ٱقۡتَتَلُواْ فَأَصۡلِحُواْ بَيۡنَهُمَاۖ فَإِنۢ بَغَتۡ إِحۡدَىٰهُمَا عَلَى ٱلۡأُخۡرَىٰ فَقَٰتِلُواْ ٱلَّتِي تَبۡغِي حَتَّىٰ تَفِيٓءَ إِلَىٰٓ أَمۡرِ ٱللَّهِۚ فَإِن فَآءَتۡ فَأَصۡلِحُواْ بَيۡنَهُمَا بِٱلۡعَدۡلِ وَأَقۡسِطُوٓاْۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحِبُّ ٱلۡمُقۡسِطِينَ﴾ ﴿إِنَّمَا ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنُونَ إِخۡوَةࣱ فَأَصۡلِحُواْ بَيۡنَ أَخَوَيۡكُمۡۚ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تُرۡحَمُونَ﴾ (Quran 49:9-10)

### Explaining it to your child
"If two groups of believers argue, make peace between them. If one side is being unfair, stand with the one being wronged until the unfair side returns to what is right, then make peace fairly. Because the believers are brothers and sisters, and family doesn't leave each other fighting."

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Shall I not tell you of something better in rank than fasting, prayer and charity?" They said: "Yes." He said: "Making peace between people" (Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi, who graded it authentic).

### Activity: the peacemaker
Make your child the family's "peacemaker": when siblings or friends fight, they go to them and say: "We're brothers, let's make up," and suggest a fair solution. Praise them every time.

## Manner 5: No Mocking, No Hurtful Nicknames

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ لَا يَسۡخَرۡ قَوۡمࣱ مِّن قَوۡمٍ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَكُونُواْ خَيۡرٗا مِّنۡهُمۡ وَلَا نِسَآءࣱ مِّن نِّسَآءٍ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَكُنَّ خَيۡرٗا مِّنۡهُنَّۖ وَلَا تَلۡمِزُوٓاْ أَنفُسَكُمۡ وَلَا تَنَابَزُواْ بِٱلۡأَلۡقَٰبِۖ بِئۡسَ ٱلِٱسۡمُ ٱلۡفُسُوقُ بَعۡدَ ٱلۡإِيمَٰنِۚ وَمَن لَّمۡ يَتُبۡ فَأُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلظَّـٰلِمُونَ﴾ (Quran 49:11)

### Explaining it to your child
"No one should mock anyone else. The person you laugh at might be better than you in Allah's sight! Don't find fault with each other, and don't call people by nicknames they hate, like 'fatty', 'shorty' or 'four-eyes'. Whoever doesn't stop doing this is a wrongdoer."

### Lesson for kids: bullying
"Mocking and hurtful nicknames are what we call bullying today, and they hurt people's hearts deeply. Allah forbade it more than 1,400 years ago! If you see someone mocking a classmate, say: That's not okay. They might be better than us in Allah's sight."

### Activity: kind names
Make a list with your child of kind ways to address friends and siblings: "my friend", "champ", "my dear brother". Agree that they will only call people by names they like.

## Manner 6: No Suspicion, No Spying, No Backbiting

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱجۡتَنِبُواْ كَثِيرٗا مِّنَ ٱلظَّنِّ إِنَّ بَعۡضَ ٱلظَّنِّ إِثۡمࣱۖ وَلَا تَجَسَّسُواْ وَلَا يَغۡتَب بَّعۡضُكُم بَعۡضًاۚ أَيُحِبُّ أَحَدُكُمۡ أَن يَأۡكُلَ لَحۡمَ أَخِيهِ مَيۡتٗا فَكَرِهۡتُمُوهُۚ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ تَوَّابࣱ رَّحِيمࣱ﴾ (Quran 49:12)

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah forbade three things:
1. **Bad suspicion**: thinking badly of someone without proof, like 'I bet he took my pencil'.
2. **Spying**: digging into people's secrets, like opening your sister's bag or reading someone's messages.
3. **Backbiting**: talking about someone behind their back in a way they would hate, even if it is true.
Then Allah gave an example: backbiting is like eating your dead brother's flesh! Would you like that? Of course not, you'd hate it. So hate backbiting too."

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ asked: "Do you know what backbiting is?" They said: "Allah and His Messenger know best." He said: "Mentioning your brother in a way he dislikes." Someone asked: "What if what I say about him is true?" He said: "If it is true, you have backbitten him, and if it is not true, you have slandered him" (Muslim).

### Activity: the backbiting test
Before talking about someone who isn't there, your child asks themselves: "Would they like to hear this if they were here?" If not, it is backbiting, so they stay quiet.

## Manner 7: So That You May Know One Another

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ إِنَّا خَلَقۡنَٰكُم مِّن ذَكَرٖ وَأُنثَىٰ وَجَعَلۡنَٰكُمۡ شُعُوبٗا وَقَبَآئِلَ لِتَعَارَفُوٓاْۚ إِنَّ أَكۡرَمَكُمۡ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ أَتۡقَىٰكُمۡۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلِيمٌ خَبِيرࣱ﴾ (Quran 49:13)

### Explaining it to your child
"All people come from one father and one mother: Adam and Hawwa. Allah made us into different nations and tribes, with different colours and languages. Why? So we can get to know each other and learn from each other, not so we can show off. The most honoured person with Allah is not the richest, the best-looking or the one from the 'best' country, but the one with the most taqwa."

### Activity: a friend from another country
If your child has a classmate from another country, encourage them to ask about their country, food and traditions. Or look up a faraway Muslim country together: what do they eat? What do they wear? Say: "This is what 'that you may know one another' means."

### From the Sunnah
In his farewell sermon, the Prophet ﷺ said: "O people, your Lord is one and your father is one. There is no superiority of an Arab over a non-Arab, nor a non-Arab over an Arab, nor a red-skinned over a black, nor a black over a red-skinned, except by taqwa" (Ahmad; graded authentic by al-Albani).

## Manner 8: Faith Lives in the Heart

﴿۞قَالَتِ ٱلۡأَعۡرَابُ ءَامَنَّاۖ قُل لَّمۡ تُؤۡمِنُواْ وَلَٰكِن قُولُوٓاْ أَسۡلَمۡنَا وَلَمَّا يَدۡخُلِ ٱلۡإِيمَٰنُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمۡۖ وَإِن تُطِيعُواْ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥ لَا يَلِتۡكُم مِّنۡ أَعۡمَٰلِكُمۡ شَيۡـًٔاۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَفُورࣱ رَّحِيمٌ﴾ (Quran 49:14)

### Explaining it to your child
"Some Bedouins came and said: We believe. Allah said: Say rather, we have submitted, because faith has not yet entered your hearts. Iman is something deep in the heart that grows with obedience and knowledge. So we ask Allah to fill our hearts with iman."

## Let's Read Together: The Brotherhood Verses

﴿إِنَّمَا ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنُونَ إِخۡوَةࣱ فَأَصۡلِحُواْ بَيۡنَ أَخَوَيۡكُمۡۚ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تُرۡحَمُونَ﴾ ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ لَا يَسۡخَرۡ قَوۡمࣱ مِّن قَوۡمٍ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَكُونُواْ خَيۡرٗا مِّنۡهُمۡ وَلَا نِسَآءࣱ مِّن نِّسَآءٍ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَكُنَّ خَيۡرٗا مِّنۡهُنَّۖ وَلَا تَلۡمِزُوٓاْ أَنفُسَكُمۡ وَلَا تَنَابَزُواْ بِٱلۡأَلۡقَٰبِۖ بِئۡسَ ٱلِٱسۡمُ ٱلۡفُسُوقُ بَعۡدَ ٱلۡإِيمَٰنِۚ وَمَن لَّمۡ يَتُبۡ فَأُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلظَّـٰلِمُونَ﴾ ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱجۡتَنِبُواْ كَثِيرٗا مِّنَ ٱلظَّنِّ إِنَّ بَعۡضَ ٱلظَّنِّ إِثۡمࣱۖ وَلَا تَجَسَّسُواْ وَلَا يَغۡتَب بَّعۡضُكُم بَعۡضًاۚ أَيُحِبُّ أَحَدُكُمۡ أَن يَأۡكُلَ لَحۡمَ أَخِيهِ مَيۡتٗا فَكَرِهۡتُمُوهُۚ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ تَوَّابࣱ رَّحِيمࣱ﴾ (Quran 49:10-12)

### Activity: the class charter
Write a "Manners Charter" from Surah Al-Hujurat with your child, which they can share with their teacher:
1. We speak in a calm voice.
2. We check news before we share it.
3. We make peace between people who argue.
4. We don't mock anyone.
5. We don't use hurtful nicknames.
6. We don't think badly of others, spy, or backbite.
7. We get to know classmates from every country.

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The New Nickname"

A new boy named Anas joined Hamza's class. He wore thick glasses. At break time, one boy shouted: "Look, Four-Eyes!" Some children laughed, and soon everyone was calling him that. Anas sat alone at break, looking sad.

At home, Hamza told his father the story, laughing. But his father didn't laugh. He opened the mushaf to Surah Al-Hujurat and read the verse forbidding mocking and nicknames. Then he said: "Hamza, how would you feel if they called you a name you hated? And Allah says they might be better than you. Maybe Anas is better than all of you in Allah's sight."

Hamza felt ashamed. "I didn't call him that, but I laughed." His father said: "Laughing encourages them. So what will you do now?"

The next day, Hamza went to Anas at break and said: "Assalamu alaykum, Anas, want to play with us?" Then he told the others: "Guys, Allah forbade hurtful nicknames. His name is Anas." The children went quiet, and the boy who started it apologized.

A week later, everyone discovered that Anas was brilliant at maths and he started helping them with the hard problems. Hamza told his dad: "You were right. They might be better than us."

Ask your child: "Why was Anas sad? What did Hamza do? What would you do if you saw someone being called names?"

## What We Learn From Surah Al-Hujurat

- **Allah and His Messenger** come first.
- **Lower your voice** with adults.
- **Check news** before you share it.
- **Make peace** between people.
- **Believers are brothers and sisters**.
- **No mocking** and no hurtful nicknames.
- **No suspicion, spying or backbiting**.
- **Get to know people** from every country.
- **The most honoured** is the most mindful of Allah.

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### How do I teach my child to avoid backbiting when adults do it around them?
Being a role model matters most. When someone talks about an absent person, gently say: "May Allah guide them, let's talk about something else." Your child learns how to stop backbiting politely.

### Is telling the teacher about a classmate's mistake backbiting?
If it is to prevent harm, like bullying or danger, it is not blameworthy backbiting; it is sincere help. Blameworthy backbiting is talking about people for entertainment or to shame them.

### My child is being bullied. How can I use this surah?
Reassure them that Allah said the mocked person may be better than those mocking, teach them to tell you and their teacher, and contact the school. The verse makes mockery a wrong that must be stopped.
