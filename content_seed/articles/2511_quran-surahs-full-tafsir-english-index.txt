This page is an **index of our complete English tafsir articles**: one long article for each surah, explaining it from beginning to end. It now covers **all 114 surahs of the Quran**, arranged in **Mushaf order**.

If you want the meaning of a single verse instead, use our [index of Quran verses explained in English](/blog/quran-verses-explained-english-index/). If you are looking for an overview of a whole juz, start with the juz summaries, then come back here for the surah you want to study in depth.

## What every full tafsir article contains
Each article follows the same structure, so once you have read one you can find your way around all of them:
1. **Quick facts**: the number of verses, whether the surah is Meccan or Medinan, its position in the Mushaf and its names.
2. **The full text or the key passages** in the Uthmani script, inserted exactly as in the Madinah Mushaf, each followed by a clear English rendering of the meaning.
3. **Verse-by-verse or section-by-section explanation**, drawing on the classical commentators such as Ibn Kathir, with the reasons for revelation where they are reliably reported.
4. **Authentic hadith only**: narrations from al-Bukhari and Muslim, or from other collections with their grading stated. Where a popular narration about a surah's virtue is weak, the article says so plainly.
5. **Word meanings** for the key Arabic terms in the surah.
6. **Rhetorical beauty**: the features of the Arabic that a translation cannot carry.
7. **Tajweed notes** with examples taken from the surah itself.
8. **The connection** between the surah and the ones before and after it.
9. **Practical lessons**, a section on teaching children, a memorisation plan and frequently asked questions.

## How to study a surah with these articles
- **Read the quick facts and the introduction first**, so you know what the surah is about before you begin.
- **Recite the surah from the Mushaf**, or listen to it on our [Quran pages](/quran/), keeping that theme in mind.
- **Read the explanation one section at a time**, and compare it with what you understood on your own.
- **Pick one practical lesson** and apply it this week.
- **Recite the surah in your prayers** afterwards. You will find you recite it with a different heart.

## Our method and sources
The Quranic text in every article is taken directly from the Madinah Mushaf data, never typed from memory, so the Arabic is exact. The English is a rendering of the meaning written for clarity, not a word-for-word translation, and it should not replace consulting a qualified teacher on matters of law. For hadith we rely on al-Bukhari and Muslim first; when we quote another collection, we name the scholar who graded the narration. We avoid stories from Isra'iliyyat that have no reliable basis, and we prefer the explanation of the Companions and the major commentators.

## What Is Tafsir, and Why Read a Full Tafsir?
*Tafsir* is the explanation of the meaning of the Quran: what its words mean, what situation a passage addressed, how its verses relate to one another, and what guidance it carries for life. A translation tells you roughly what a verse says; tafsir tells you what it means, why it was revealed, and how it connects to the rest of the surah.

Reading single verses in isolation is useful, but every surah has a structure and a central purpose that you only see when you read it as a whole. Surah Al-Kahf is built around four trials; Surah Ar-Rahman lists the favours of Allah one after another with a repeated question; Surah Yusuf is one continuous story from a dream to its fulfilment. A full tafsir shows you how the pieces fit together, so that when you recite the surah you follow its flow instead of reciting disconnected sentences.

## Meccan and Medinan Surahs
Every article tells you whether the surah is Meccan or Medinan, following the classification printed in the standard Madinah Mushaf. This matters for understanding:
- **Meccan surahs**, revealed before the Hijrah, focus on the foundations of faith: the oneness of Allah, the truth of the Messenger, the Resurrection, Paradise and Hell, and the stories of earlier prophets. They are often shorter, with powerful rhythm and vivid scenes, which is why most of Juz 'Amma is Meccan.
- **Medinan surahs**, revealed after the Hijrah, also include laws for the community: worship, family, transactions, and relations with others, because the Muslims now lived as a society.
For some surahs the scholars differ on whether they are Meccan or Medinan; where that is the case, the article says so.

## How the Surahs Are Ordered
The surahs in the Mushaf are not arranged in the order of revelation. The first verses revealed were the opening of Surah Al-'Alaq, which sits near the end of the Mushaf, while Surah Al-Baqarah, revealed in Madinah, comes second. The order of the surahs follows the arrangement known from the Prophet ﷺ and agreed upon by the Companions. Our index follows the order of the Mushaf, so you can move through it the same way you move through your copy of the Quran.

## Reading Tafsir With the Right Expectations
A tafsir article is a guide to understanding, not a replacement for the Quran itself or for learning from qualified teachers. Use these articles to deepen your reflection, prepare for a study circle, or understand what you recite in prayer. When a question of law or practice arises, ask a scholar you trust. And always return to the recitation itself: the goal of understanding is to recite with presence of heart and to act on what you learn.

## Suggested reading paths
### If you are new to the Quran
Start with **Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas**, the three surahs the Prophet ﷺ recited every night before sleeping, then **Al-Asr** and **Al-Kawthar**. They are short, and each one opens a door to a big idea: who Allah is, how to seek His protection, what success means, and how to be grateful.

### If you want to understand the Hereafter
Read **An-Naba'**, **Az-Zalzalah**, **Al-Qari'ah** and **Al-Waqi'ah** in that order. Together they describe the Resurrection, the weighing of deeds and the three groups of people on the Last Day.

### If you are going through a hard time
Read **Ad-Duha**, revealed to comfort the Prophet ﷺ during a pause in revelation, then **Yusuf**, the story of patience that ends in reunion, and **Maryam**, a surah of mercy in moments of loneliness and weakness.

### If you are preparing for Ramadan
Read **Al-Qadr** for the Night of Decree, and **Al-Mulk**, which many people recite every night.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Is this a complete translation of the Quran?
No. Each article explains the meaning of a surah in depth, quoting its key verses. For a complete translation, use a reliable printed translation alongside these articles.

### Does this index cover every surah?
Yes. Every one of the 114 surahs has its own full English tafsir article, listed here in Mushaf order, and each one links to its Arabic counterpart.

### Are the hadith in these articles authentic?
We quote al-Bukhari and Muslim first, and for other collections we state the grading. Weak narrations that are popular are mentioned only to point out that they are weak.

### Can I use these articles to teach my children?
Yes. Every article has a section on teaching the surah to children, and a memorisation plan you can follow together.

### Which surah should I start with?
If you are unsure, start with the short surahs of Juz 'Amma that you already recite in your daily prayers, such as Al-Ikhlas, Al-'Asr and Al-Kawthar. You recite them every day, so understanding them changes your prayer immediately.

### How long does it take to read one article?
Most articles take between fifteen and twenty minutes to read in full. You can also read one section a day alongside your recitation of that part of the surah.

### Is there an Arabic version?
Yes. Every one of these surahs also has a full Arabic tafsir, which you can reach from the language link at the top of each article.

## Conclusion
A surah you understand is a surah you recite differently. Choose one surah from this index, read its full tafsir slowly, recite it in your prayers this week, and let its lessons settle before moving to the next.
