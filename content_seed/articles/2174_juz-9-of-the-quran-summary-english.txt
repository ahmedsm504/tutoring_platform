Juz 9 begins with ﴿۞قَالَ ٱلۡمَلَأُ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسۡتَكۡبَرُواْ مِن قَوۡمِهِۦ لَنُخۡرِجَنَّكَ يَٰشُعَيۡبُ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ مَعَكَ مِن قَرۡيَتِنَآ﴾, "The arrogant chiefs of his people said: we will surely drive you out of our town, O Shu'ayb, and those who believe with you", so it is called **Juz Qal al-Mala'**. It runs from **Al-A'raf 7:88 to Al-Anfal 8:40**. In the Madinah Mushaf it fills **pages 162 to 181**.

This juz holds the longest continuous account of **Musa and Pharaoh** in the Quran, then closes **Surah Al-A'raf** with some of its greatest guidance on calling to Allah and character, including the **first prostration of recitation** in the order of the Mushaf. It then opens **Surah Al-Anfal**, revealed about the Battle of Badr.

## The big idea

The juz can be summed up as **the confrontation between truth and tyranny, with victory for believers who respond**. It begins with the chiefs threatening to expel Shu'ayb, sets out Allah's law for towns that deny, then presents the most extreme model of tyranny, Pharaoh, and how he fell before Musa's staff and the faith of the magicians. It then exposes the ailments of the Children of Israel after their rescue, and closes Al-A'raf with the root of human nature: the covenant ﴿أَلَسۡتُ بِرَبِّكُمۡۖ﴾, "Am I not your Lord?" Al-Anfal then gives a practical application at Badr: a small group responsive to Allah and His Messenger is given victory over a large, arrogant one.

## Part one: the close of Al-A'raf (88-206)

### 1. The end of Shu'ayb's story (88-93)
The chiefs said: we will drive you out of our town, O Shu'ayb, and those who believe with you, unless you return to our religion. Shu'ayb replied: ﴿وَسِعَ رَبُّنَا كُلَّ شَيۡءٍ عِلۡمًاۚ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ تَوَكَّلۡنَاۚ﴾, "Our Lord encompasses all things in knowledge; upon Allah we rely", and prayed: ﴿رَبَّنَا ٱفۡتَحۡ بَيۡنَنَا وَبَيۡنَ قَوۡمِنَا بِٱلۡحَقِّ وَأَنتَ خَيۡرُ ٱلۡفَٰتِحِينَ﴾, "Our Lord, judge between us and our people in truth, and You are the best of judges." The outcome: ﴿فَأَخَذَتۡهُمُ ٱلرَّجۡفَةُ فَأَصۡبَحُواْ فِي دَارِهِمۡ جَٰثِمِينَ﴾, "So the earthquake seized them, and they lay lifeless in their home."

### 2. Allah's laws for towns (94-102)
After the five stories comes a general comment:
- No town received a prophet without its people being seized with hardship so they might humble themselves; then Allah replaced the bad with good until they grew many and forgot.
- ﴿وَلَوۡ أَنَّ أَهۡلَ ٱلۡقُرَىٰٓ ءَامَنُواْ وَٱتَّقَوۡاْ لَفَتَحۡنَا عَلَيۡهِم بَرَكَٰتٖ مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ﴾, "Had the people of the towns believed and been God-conscious, We would have opened to them blessings from the heaven and the earth."
- ﴿أَفَأَمِنُواْ مَكۡرَ ٱللَّهِۚ فَلَا يَأۡمَنُ مَكۡرَ ٱللَّهِ إِلَّا ٱلۡقَوۡمُ ٱلۡخَٰسِرُونَ﴾, "Do they feel secure from Allah's plan? None feels secure from Allah's plan except the losing people."

**Lesson**: true blessing is the fruit of faith and taqwa, and feeling safe from Allah's punishment while persisting in sin is a sign of loss.

### 3. Musa and Pharaoh (103-137)
This is the fullest continuous account of the confrontation in the Quran, moving in stages:

**a. The call and two miracles (103-108)**: Musa tells Pharaoh he is a messenger from the Lord of the worlds and asks him to release the Children of Israel. Pharaoh demands a sign: ﴿فَأَلۡقَىٰ عَصَاهُ فَإِذَا هِيَ ثُعۡبَانࣱ مُّبِينࣱ﴾, "So he threw his staff, and it became a serpent, clear", then ﴿وَنَزَعَ يَدَهُۥ فَإِذَا هِيَ بَيۡضَآءُ لِلنَّـٰظِرِينَ﴾, "and he drew out his hand, and it was white for the onlookers."

**b. Gathering the magicians (109-119)**: the chiefs accuse Musa of magic and summon the magicians, who ask: ﴿إِنَّ لَنَا لَأَجۡرًا إِن كُنَّا نَحۡنُ ٱلۡغَٰلِبِينَ﴾, "We will surely have a reward if we are the winners." They throw and bewitch people's eyes; then Musa throws his staff and it swallows what they faked, and "the truth was established and what they were doing was abolished."

**c. The magicians believe (120-126)**: ﴿وَأُلۡقِيَ ٱلسَّحَرَةُ سَٰجِدِينَ﴾, "and the magicians fell down in prostration", ﴿قَالُوٓاْ ءَامَنَّا بِرَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾ ﴿رَبِّ مُوسَىٰ وَهَٰرُونَ﴾, "They said: we believe in the Lord of the worlds, the Lord of Musa and Harun." Pharaoh threatens to cut off their hands and feet on opposite sides and crucify them, and they answer with timeless words: ﴿وَمَا تَنقِمُ مِنَّآ إِلَّآ أَنۡ ءَامَنَّا بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّنَا لَمَّا جَآءَتۡنَاۚ رَبَّنَآ أَفۡرِغۡ عَلَيۡنَا صَبۡرٗا وَتَوَفَّنَا مُسۡلِمِينَ﴾, "You resent us only because we believed in the signs of our Lord when they came to us. Our Lord, pour patience upon us and let us die as Muslims."

**Reflect on the change**: at the start of the day they came asking Pharaoh for a reward; by its end they were asking Allah for patience. That is what truth does when it touches the heart.

**d. Musa's advice to his people (127-129)**: ﴿ٱسۡتَعِينُواْ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱصۡبِرُوٓاْۖ إِنَّ ٱلۡأَرۡضَ لِلَّهِ يُورِثُهَا مَن يَشَآءُ مِنۡ عِبَادِهِۦۖ وَٱلۡعَٰقِبَةُ لِلۡمُتَّقِينَ﴾, "Seek help from Allah and be patient. The earth belongs to Allah; He gives it as inheritance to whom He wills of His servants, and the best outcome is for the God-conscious."

**e. The detailed signs (130-135)**: Allah struck Pharaoh's people with years of drought and loss of crops, then: ﴿فَأَرۡسَلۡنَا عَلَيۡهِمُ ٱلطُّوفَانَ وَٱلۡجَرَادَ وَٱلۡقُمَّلَ وَٱلضَّفَادِعَ وَٱلدَّمَ ءَايَٰتٖ مُّفَصَّلَٰتٖ﴾, "So We sent upon them the flood, locusts, lice, frogs and blood as distinct signs." Each time punishment fell they asked Musa to pray, and when it was lifted they broke their word.

**f. The drowning and the inheritance (136-137)**: Allah drowned Pharaoh and his people and made the oppressed inherit the east and west of the land: ﴿وَتَمَّتۡ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ ٱلۡحُسۡنَىٰ عَلَىٰ بَنِيٓ إِسۡرَـٰٓءِيلَ بِمَا صَبَرُواْۖ﴾, "and the good word of your Lord was fulfilled for the Children of Israel because of their patience."

### 4. The Children of Israel after the rescue (138-171)
**a. Asking for an idol (138-141)**: no sooner had they crossed the sea than they saw people devoted to idols and said: make for us a god as they have gods. Musa said: you are an ignorant people.

**b. The appointment and the request to see Allah (142-147)**: Allah appointed thirty nights for Musa and completed them with ten, and Musa left Harun in charge. Then: ﴿قَالَ رَبِّ أَرِنِيٓ أَنظُرۡ إِلَيۡكَۚ قَالَ لَن تَرَىٰنِي وَلَٰكِنِ ٱنظُرۡ إِلَى ٱلۡجَبَلِ فَإِنِ ٱسۡتَقَرَّ مَكَانَهُۥ فَسَوۡفَ تَرَىٰنِيۚ فَلَمَّا تَجَلَّىٰ رَبُّهُۥ لِلۡجَبَلِ جَعَلَهُۥ دَكّٗا وَخَرَّ مُوسَىٰ صَعِقٗاۚ﴾, "He said: My Lord, show me Yourself that I may look at You. He said: You will not see Me, but look at the mountain; if it stays in place, you will see Me. When his Lord revealed Himself to the mountain, He made it crumble, and Musa fell unconscious." Then Musa received the tablets containing instruction and explanation of all things.

**c. The calf (148-154)**: they made from their ornaments a calf, a body that lowed. When Musa returned angry and grieved, he threw down the tablets and seized his brother's head, and Harun said: ﴿ٱبۡنَ أُمَّ إِنَّ ٱلۡقَوۡمَ ٱسۡتَضۡعَفُونِي وَكَادُواْ يَقۡتُلُونَنِي﴾, "Son of my mother, the people overpowered me and nearly killed me." Musa prayed for himself and his brother, and when his anger calmed he took up the tablets.

**d. The seventy and the all-embracing mercy (155-158)**: Musa chose seventy men for Allah's appointment and prayed: ﴿۞وَٱكۡتُبۡ لَنَا فِي هَٰذِهِ ٱلدُّنۡيَا حَسَنَةٗ وَفِي ٱلۡأٓخِرَةِ إِنَّا هُدۡنَآ إِلَيۡكَۚ﴾, "Decree for us good in this world and in the Hereafter; we have turned to You." The answer: ﴿وَرَحۡمَتِي وَسِعَتۡ كُلَّ شَيۡءٖۚ فَسَأَكۡتُبُهَا لِلَّذِينَ يَتَّقُونَ وَيُؤۡتُونَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَٱلَّذِينَ هُم بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا يُؤۡمِنُونَ﴾, "My mercy encompasses all things, and I will decree it for those who are God-conscious, pay zakah and believe in Our signs." Then, in the middle of Musa's story, comes mention of Muhammad ﷺ: ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ يَتَّبِعُونَ ٱلرَّسُولَ ٱلنَّبِيَّ ٱلۡأُمِّيَّ ٱلَّذِي يَجِدُونَهُۥ مَكۡتُوبًا عِندَهُمۡ فِي ٱلتَّوۡرَىٰةِ وَٱلۡإِنجِيلِ﴾, "those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, whom they find written in the Torah and the Gospel", followed by the universal call: ﴿قُلۡ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ إِنِّي رَسُولُ ٱللَّهِ إِلَيۡكُمۡ جَمِيعًا﴾, "Say: O mankind, I am the Messenger of Allah to you all."

**Why mention the Prophet ﷺ here?** Because the wide mercy Musa asked for his people was decreed for those who follow this Prophet, linking the two messages.

**e. The Sabbath-breakers (163-166)**: a town by the sea where fish came to them openly on their Sabbath and not on other days, so they devised a trick. Its people split into three: transgressors, those who forbade it, and silent ones who said to the forbidders: ﴿لِمَ تَعِظُونَ قَوۡمًا ٱللَّهُ مُهۡلِكُهُمۡ أَوۡ مُعَذِّبُهُمۡ عَذَابٗا شَدِيدٗاۖ﴾, "Why do you advise a people Allah will destroy or severely punish?" The forbidders replied: ﴿مَعۡذِرَةً إِلَىٰ رَبِّكُمۡ وَلَعَلَّهُمۡ يَتَّقُونَ﴾, "To be absolved before your Lord, and perhaps they will become God-conscious." Allah saved those who forbade evil.

**Lesson**: forbidding wrong is an excuse before Allah and a hope for reform, even when it seems useless.

### 5. The covenant of human nature (172-174)
﴿وَإِذۡ أَخَذَ رَبُّكَ مِنۢ بَنِيٓ ءَادَمَ مِن ظُهُورِهِمۡ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمۡ وَأَشۡهَدَهُمۡ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ أَلَسۡتُ بِرَبِّكُمۡۖ قَالُواْ بَلَىٰ شَهِدۡنَآۚ﴾, "And when your Lord took from the children of Adam, from their loins, their descendants and made them testify about themselves: Am I not your Lord? They said: Yes, we testify." Monotheism is planted in human nature, and no one can excuse himself by saying "our forefathers associated partners before us."

### 6. The man who cast off the signs (175-179)
A chilling parable: a man to whom Allah gave His signs, but he cast them off: ﴿وَلَوۡ شِئۡنَا لَرَفَعۡنَٰهُ بِهَا وَلَٰكِنَّهُۥٓ أَخۡلَدَ إِلَى ٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَٱتَّبَعَ هَوَىٰهُۚ فَمَثَلُهُۥ كَمَثَلِ ٱلۡكَلۡبِ﴾, "Had We willed, We could have raised him by them, but he clung to the earth and followed his desire. His example is that of the dog..." Knowledge without action can become a cause of downfall. Then the heedless: ﴿لَهُمۡ قُلُوبࣱ لَّا يَفۡقَهُونَ بِهَا وَلَهُمۡ أَعۡيُنࣱ لَّا يُبۡصِرُونَ بِهَا وَلَهُمۡ ءَاذَانࣱ لَّا يَسۡمَعُونَ بِهَآۚ أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ كَٱلۡأَنۡعَٰمِ بَلۡ هُمۡ أَضَلُّۚ﴾, "They have hearts with which they do not understand, eyes with which they do not see, and ears with which they do not hear. They are like cattle, rather more astray."

### 7. The Beautiful Names and the Hour (180-198)
﴿وَلِلَّهِ ٱلۡأَسۡمَآءُ ٱلۡحُسۡنَىٰ فَٱدۡعُوهُ بِهَاۖ﴾, "To Allah belong the best names, so call upon Him by them." In Al-Bukhari and Muslim: "Allah has ninety-nine names, one hundred minus one; whoever enumerates them will enter Paradise." Then the question about the Hour and the answer: ﴿إِنَّمَا عِلۡمُهَا عِندَ رَبِّيۖ﴾, "Its knowledge is only with my Lord", and ﴿قُل لَّآ أَمۡلِكُ لِنَفۡسِي نَفۡعٗا وَلَا ضَرًّا إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُۚ﴾, "Say: I have no power to benefit or harm myself except as Allah wills." Then creation from one soul, and a reply to those who worship what creates nothing.

### 8. A charter of character and remembrance (199-206)
- **Three comprehensive commands**: ﴿خُذِ ٱلۡعَفۡوَ وَأۡمُرۡ بِٱلۡعُرۡفِ وَأَعۡرِضۡ عَنِ ٱلۡجَٰهِلِينَ﴾, "Take what is easy, command what is good, and turn away from the ignorant." Some scholars called it the most comprehensive verse on noble character. Read ["Hold to Forgiveness, Enjoin Good, Turn from the Ignorant": Quran 7:199 Explained](/blog/khudh-al-afw-meaning-english/).
- **The remedy for Shaytan's prompting**: seeking refuge in Allah, and ﴿إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّقَوۡاْ إِذَا مَسَّهُمۡ طَـٰٓئِفࣱ مِّنَ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنِ تَذَكَّرُواْ فَإِذَا هُم مُّبۡصِرُونَ﴾, "Those who are God-conscious, when a whisper from Shaytan touches them, remember Allah, and at once they see clearly."
- **The etiquette of listening**: ﴿وَإِذَا قُرِئَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانُ فَٱسۡتَمِعُواْ لَهُۥ وَأَنصِتُواْ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تُرۡحَمُونَ﴾, "When the Quran is recited, listen to it and be silent, that you may receive mercy."
- **The etiquette of remembrance**: ﴿وَٱذۡكُر رَّبَّكَ فِي نَفۡسِكَ تَضَرُّعٗا وَخِيفَةٗ وَدُونَ ٱلۡجَهۡرِ مِنَ ٱلۡقَوۡلِ بِٱلۡغُدُوِّ وَٱلۡأٓصَالِ وَلَا تَكُن مِّنَ ٱلۡغَٰفِلِينَ﴾, "Remember your Lord within yourself humbly and in awe, without loudness, morning and evening, and do not be among the heedless."
- **The first prostration in the Mushaf**: ﴿إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ عِندَ رَبِّكَ لَا يَسۡتَكۡبِرُونَ عَنۡ عِبَادَتِهِۦ وَيُسَبِّحُونَهُۥ وَلَهُۥ يَسۡجُدُونَ﴾, "Those who are near your Lord are not too proud to worship Him; they glorify Him and prostrate to Him." The surah whose great story began with Iblis refusing to prostrate ends with angels who prostrate, and with a prostration for the reciter.

## Part two: the opening of Surah Al-Anfal (1-40)

Al-Anfal is a Madani surah of 75 verses, revealed after the Battle of Badr in the second year after the Hijrah. *Anfal* means the spoils of war, and the surah begins with a question about them. For the whole surah see [Surah Al-Anfal Meaning in English](/blog/surah-al-anfal-meaning-english/).

### 1. The spoils and the qualities of believers (1-4)
﴿يَسۡـَٔلُونَكَ عَنِ ٱلۡأَنفَالِۖ قُلِ ٱلۡأَنفَالُ لِلَّهِ وَٱلرَّسُولِۖ فَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ وَأَصۡلِحُواْ ذَاتَ بَيۡنِكُمۡۖ﴾, "They ask you about the spoils. Say: the spoils belong to Allah and the Messenger, so fear Allah and set things right among yourselves." The surah sets hearts right before dividing wealth. Then the qualities of true believers: ﴿إِنَّمَا ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنُونَ ٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا ذُكِرَ ٱللَّهُ وَجِلَتۡ قُلُوبُهُمۡ وَإِذَا تُلِيَتۡ عَلَيۡهِمۡ ءَايَٰتُهُۥ زَادَتۡهُمۡ إِيمَٰنٗا وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمۡ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ﴾, "The believers are only those whose hearts tremble when Allah is mentioned, whose faith increases when His verses are recited to them, and who rely upon their Lord", followed by prayer and spending.

### 2. Setting out for Badr (5-8)
The Muslims went out seeking Quraysh's caravan, and some disliked fighting: ﴿وَتَوَدُّونَ أَنَّ غَيۡرَ ذَاتِ ٱلشَّوۡكَةِ تَكُونُ لَكُمۡ﴾, "and you wished that the unarmed party would be yours." But Allah willed to establish the truth by His words and cut off the disbelievers. Good lay in what Allah chose, not in what they wished.

### 3. Miracles of victory (9-14)
- ﴿إِذۡ تَسۡتَغِيثُونَ رَبَّكُمۡ فَٱسۡتَجَابَ لَكُمۡ أَنِّي مُمِدُّكُم بِأَلۡفٖ مِّنَ ٱلۡمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ مُرۡدِفِينَ﴾, "When you called upon your Lord for help, He answered: I will reinforce you with a thousand angels in succession." In Sahih Muslim, 'Umar reported that on the day of Badr the Prophet ﷺ faced the qibla and stretched out his hands, calling upon his Lord until his cloak fell from his shoulders.
- Drowsiness as security, and rain to purify them and make their feet firm.
- Allah's inspiration to the angels: ﴿أَنِّي مَعَكُمۡ فَثَبِّتُواْ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْۚ﴾, "I am with you, so strengthen those who believe."

### 4. Standing firm, and attributing the act to Allah (15-19)
A ban on turning your back in battle except to manoeuvre or join another group. Then: ﴿فَلَمۡ تَقۡتُلُوهُمۡ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ قَتَلَهُمۡۚ وَمَا رَمَيۡتَ إِذۡ رَمَيۡتَ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ رَمَىٰ﴾, "You did not kill them, but Allah killed them; and you did not throw when you threw, but Allah threw." The believer takes the means and knows victory is from Allah.

### 5. Responding, and the criterion (20-29)
- ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱسۡتَجِيبُواْ لِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ إِذَا دَعَاكُمۡ لِمَا يُحۡيِيكُمۡۖ﴾, "O you who believe, respond to Allah and the Messenger when he calls you to what gives you life." Responding to Allah is life itself.
- ﴿وَٱتَّقُواْ فِتۡنَةٗ لَّا تُصِيبَنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُواْ مِنكُمۡ خَآصَّةٗۖ﴾, "Beware a trial that will not strike only the wrongdoers among you": when trials come, they spread.
- A reminder of the days of weakness in Makkah: ﴿وَٱذۡكُرُوٓاْ إِذۡ أَنتُمۡ قَلِيلࣱ مُّسۡتَضۡعَفُونَ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ﴾, "Remember when you were few and oppressed in the land."
- A ban on betraying Allah, the Messenger and trusts, and a reminder that wealth and children are a test.
- ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓاْ إِن تَتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ يَجۡعَل لَّكُمۡ فُرۡقَانٗا﴾, "O you who believe, if you fear Allah, He will grant you a criterion": a light to tell truth from falsehood.

### 6. Quraysh's plot and the believers' seeking of forgiveness (30-35)
﴿وَإِذۡ يَمۡكُرُ بِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ لِيُثۡبِتُوكَ أَوۡ يَقۡتُلُوكَ أَوۡ يُخۡرِجُوكَۚ وَيَمۡكُرُونَ وَيَمۡكُرُ ٱللَّهُۖ وَٱللَّهُ خَيۡرُ ٱلۡمَٰكِرِينَ﴾, "And when the disbelievers plotted against you to restrain you, kill you or expel you; they plot, and Allah plots, and Allah is the best of planners", a reference to the night of the Hijrah. Then two safeguards from punishment: ﴿وَمَا كَانَ ٱللَّهُ لِيُعَذِّبَهُمۡ وَأَنتَ فِيهِمۡۚ وَمَا كَانَ ٱللَّهُ مُعَذِّبَهُمۡ وَهُمۡ يَسۡتَغۡفِرُونَ﴾, "Allah would not punish them while you are among them, and Allah would not punish them while they seek forgiveness." The first safeguard ended with the Prophet's death; the second, seeking forgiveness, remains.

### 7. Spending to block the way, and the door of repentance (36-40)
The disbelievers spend their wealth to block Allah's path, and it will become a regret for them. Then hope: ﴿قُل لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓاْ إِن يَنتَهُواْ يُغۡفَرۡ لَهُم مَّا قَدۡ سَلَفَ﴾, "Say to the disbelievers: if they desist, their past will be forgiven." The juz closes: ﴿وَإِن تَوَلَّوۡاْ فَٱعۡلَمُوٓاْ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ مَوۡلَىٰكُمۡۚ نِعۡمَ ٱلۡمَوۡلَىٰ وَنِعۡمَ ٱلنَّصِيرُ﴾, "But if they turn away, know that Allah is your protector, an excellent protector and an excellent helper."

## Tajweed points in Juz 9

### The prostration of recitation at 7:206
The sign ۩ at the end of the verse marks a place of prostration. It is Sunnah for the reciter and listener to prostrate.

### ﴿ٱبۡنَ أُمَّ﴾
Read with a fatha on the meem in the narration of Hafs; the hamzah of *ibn* is a connecting hamzah that drops in continuous reading.

### Ikhfa' in ﴿مَن كَانَ﴾ and ﴿أَنفُسِكُمۡ﴾
Sakin noon before kaf and fa is hidden with ghunnah.

### Compulsory madd in ﴿دَآبَّةٖ﴾
A madd letter followed by a doubled letter: six counts.

### Heavy and light letters in ﴿فِرۡعَوۡنَ﴾ and ﴿ٱلطُّوفَانَ﴾
The ra in *Fir'awn* is light, being sakin after an original kasrah with no heavy letter after it; the ta' in *at-tufan* is heavy, being one of the letters of *itbaq*. For an overview see [A Roadmap for Learning Tajweed](/blog/learn-tajweed-roadmap-english/).

## How to memorize Juz 9 with understanding

1. **Musa's story is two-thirds of the juz**: divide it into the six stages of the confrontation with Pharaoh and the five stages with the Children of Israel.
2. **Make a table of Musa's story in Al-A'raf, Ta-Ha and Ash-Shu'ara'** to tell the differences apart.
3. **Learn the five punishments in order**: flood, locusts, lice, frogs, blood.
4. **The end of Al-A'raf is a run of instructions**: memorize them while acting on them.
5. **Al-Anfal is tied to the events of Badr**: read the story of the battle first.

For technique see [How to Memorize the Quran Step by Step](/blog/how-to-memorize-quran-step-by-step/).

## Practical lessons from Juz 9

- **Faith and taqwa are the key to blessings.**
- **Never feel safe from Allah's plan while persisting in sin.**
- **Truth transforms the heart it touches**, as it did the magicians.
- **Seek help from Allah and be patient**; the outcome is for the God-conscious.
- **Do not play tricks with Allah's rulings** as the Sabbath-breakers did.
- **Forbid wrong as an excuse before your Lord.**
- **Knowledge without action is dangerous**, as the parable of the man who cast off the signs shows.
- **Call upon Allah by His Beautiful Names.**
- **Take what is easy, command good, and turn away from the ignorant.**
- **Be silent when the Quran is recited.**
- **Set things right between people** before dividing wealth.
- **Respond to Allah**: it is your life.
- **Seek forgiveness often**; it is a lasting safeguard.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is Juz 9 called "Qal al-Mala'"?
Because it begins with ﴿۞قَالَ ٱلۡمَلَأُ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسۡتَكۡبَرُواْ مِن قَوۡمِهِۦ﴾.

### Where is the first prostration of recitation in the Mushaf?
At the end of Surah Al-A'raf, verse 206, which is in Juz 9.

### Where does Juz 9 end?
At Al-Anfal 8:40. Juz 10 begins with ﴿۞وَٱعۡلَمُوٓاْ أَنَّمَا غَنِمۡتُم مِّن شَيۡءٖ فَأَنَّ لِلَّهِ خُمُسَهُۥ﴾, "And know that whatever you take as spoils, one fifth belongs to Allah..."

### What were the detailed signs sent upon Pharaoh's people?
The flood, locusts, lice, frogs and blood (7:133), preceded by years of drought and loss of crops.

### What are the two safeguards mentioned in Al-Anfal?
The presence of the Prophet ﷺ among them, and seeking forgiveness (8:33).

## Conclusion

Juz 9 shows the great confrontation between truth and tyranny: from the chiefs' threat against Shu'ayb, to Pharaoh's fall before the staff and the magicians' faith, to the ailments of the Children of Israel after their rescue. It then reminds every person of the covenant of their nature and of the manners of calling to Allah, and ends Al-A'raf with a prostration. Badr then puts the lesson into practice: a group that responds to Allah is given victory, however small.
