Juz 1 is the doorway to the Quran. It opens with **Surah Al-Fatiha** and then covers **Surah Al-Baqarah from verse 1 to verse 141**. In the standard Madinah Mushaf it fills **pages 1 to 21**, and Juz 2 begins with ﴿سَيَقُولُ ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ (2:142), "The foolish among the people will say..."

This guide is not only a memorization timetable. It is a thematic reading of the whole juz: what it builds in the heart of the reader, how its sections connect, and which verses, tajweed rules and similar passages deserve special attention. Understanding comes first, because a passage you understand is a passage you remember.

## Juz 1 at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Starts | Al-Fatiha 1:1 |
| Ends | Al-Baqarah 2:141 |
| Surahs | All of Al-Fatiha, Al-Baqarah 1-141 |
| Pages (Madinah Mushaf) | 1 to 21 |
| Revelation | Al-Fatiha is Makki, Al-Baqarah is Madani |
| Hizbs | Hizb 1 and Hizb 2 |
| Main stories | Adam, Musa and the Children of Israel, the cow, Ibrahim and Ismail building the Kaaba |

The juz is often called by its opening words, **"Alif Lam Mim"**.

## The big idea: guidance asked for, given and tested

If Juz 1 had to be summarized in one line, it would be this: **guidance is requested, then delivered, then tested.**

In Al-Fatiha the servant asks: ﴿ٱهۡدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلۡمُسۡتَقِيمَ﴾, "Guide us to the straight path." The very next page answers: ﴿ذَٰلِكَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبُ لَا رَيۡبَۛ فِيهِۛ هُدٗى لِّلۡمُتَّقِينَ﴾, "This is the Book in which there is no doubt, a guidance for the God-conscious." It is as if Allah says: you asked for the path, and here is the Book that shows it.

The rest of the juz then shows people responding to this guidance in different ways:
- those who **accept it** (the God-conscious),
- those who **reject it** (the disbelievers),
- those who **pretend to accept it** (the hypocrites),
- a nation **given scripture before** who fell short in carrying it (the Children of Israel),
- and the model of **complete submission**, Ibrahim, peace be upon him.

Through these portraits the reader is invited to ask: where do I stand?

## Section 1: Al-Fatiha, the surah of asking

Al-Fatiha has seven verses. It is called **Umm al-Quran** (the Mother of the Quran) and **as-Sab' al-Mathani** (the Seven Oft-Repeated). The Prophet ﷺ said, in a hadith reported by Al-Bukhari and Muslim: "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book."

In Sahih Muslim there is the hadith qudsi: "I have divided the prayer between Myself and My servant into two halves, and My servant shall have what he asks for." The Prophet ﷺ then explained that when the servant says "All praise is for Allah, Lord of the worlds", Allah says "My servant has praised Me", and so on through the surah.

Al-Fatiha has three clear parts:
1. **Praise of Allah**: His praise, lordship, mercy and ownership of the Day of Judgement.
2. **The covenant**: ﴿إِيَّاكَ نَعۡبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾, "You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help". This is the heart of the surah.
3. **The request**: guidance to the straight path, and protection from the path of those who earned anger and those who went astray.

The whole of Juz 1 can be read as a practical commentary on these three parts: knowing Allah, worshipping Him, and walking His path while avoiding the paths of those who strayed. For a fuller explanation read [Surah Al-Fatiha Meaning in English](/blog/surah-al-fatiha-meaning-english/).

## Section 2: Three kinds of people (2:1-20)

Al-Baqarah opens with the separate letters ﴿الٓمٓ﴾, then declares that this Book has no doubt in it. It then divides people into three groups, and scholars have noticed the striking proportion of verses given to each:
- **Believers in four verses (2-5)**: they believe in the unseen, establish prayer, spend from what Allah provided, believe in all revealed books, and are certain of the Hereafter. Their conclusion: ﴿أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ عَلَىٰ هُدٗى مِّن رَّبِّهِمۡۖ وَأُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُفۡلِحُونَ﴾, "Those are upon guidance from their Lord, and it is they who are successful."
- **Disbelievers in two verses (6-7)**: "It is the same to them whether you warn them or not; they will not believe."
- **Hypocrites in thirteen verses (8-20)**, beginning: ﴿وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ ءَامَنَّا بِٱللَّهِ وَبِٱلۡيَوۡمِ ٱلۡأٓخِرِ وَمَا هُم بِمُؤۡمِنِينَ﴾, "And among the people are some who say, 'We believe in Allah and the Last Day', but they are not believers."

Why so much space for the hypocrites? Because their danger is hidden. The open disbeliever's position is clear, while the hypocrite lives among believers with two faces. This section contains two famous parables: the man who **kindled a fire** and then lost its light, and the **rainstorm** full of darkness, thunder and lightning.

**Practical lesson**: the five qualities of the God-conscious at the start of the surah make an excellent daily checklist: faith in the unseen, established prayer, spending, belief in revelation, and certainty of the Hereafter.

## Section 3: The first call to humanity and the challenge (2:21-29)

Here comes the first general call to all people in the order of the Mushaf: ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱعۡبُدُواْ رَبَّكُمُ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَكُمۡ وَٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبۡلِكُمۡ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تَتَّقُونَ﴾, "O mankind, worship your Lord who created you and those before you, so that you may become God-conscious."

Having described the three groups, Allah invites them all to worship, with evidence they can see: their own creation, the earth spread out as a bed, the sky as a canopy, the rain and the fruits it brings.

Then the **challenge**: ﴿وَإِن كُنتُمۡ فِي رَيۡبٖ مِّمَّا نَزَّلۡنَا عَلَىٰ عَبۡدِنَا فَأۡتُواْ بِسُورَةٖ مِّن مِّثۡلِهِۦ﴾, "And if you are in doubt about what We have sent down to Our servant, then produce a surah like it." The section goes on to describe the reward of the believers in Paradise, answers those who mocked a parable about a gnat, and reminds people that they were lifeless and Allah gave them life.

**How the sections connect**: section 2 declared "no doubt in it", and section 3 proves it through the challenge. Seeing this chain helps memorization: the verses are links in one argument, not separate sentences.

## Section 4: The story of Adam (2:30-39)

After reminding people of their creation, Allah tells the story of the **first human being**: ﴿إِنِّي جَاعِلࣱ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ خَلِيفَةٗۖ﴾, "I am placing a successor on the earth." The story includes:
- the angels' question about the wisdom of this, and Allah's answer: ﴿إِنِّيٓ أَعۡلَمُ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ﴾, "I know what you do not know";
- Adam being **taught the names**, showing the honour of knowledge;
- the angels' **prostration** and Iblis's refusal: ﴿أَبَىٰ وَٱسۡتَكۡبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ﴾, "he refused and was arrogant and became one of the disbelievers";
- life in the Garden, the forbidden tree, the slip, and then **repentance**: ﴿فَتَلَقَّىٰٓ ءَادَمُ مِن رَّبِّهِۦ كَلِمَٰتٖ فَتَابَ عَلَيۡهِۚ﴾, "Then Adam received words from his Lord, and He accepted his repentance";
- the descent to earth with a promise: ﴿فَمَن تَبِعَ هُدَايَ فَلَا خَوۡفٌ عَلَيۡهِمۡ وَلَا هُمۡ يَحۡزَنُونَ﴾, "whoever follows My guidance, there will be no fear upon them, nor will they grieve."

Notice that the story ends with the same key word as Al-Fatiha and the opening of Al-Baqarah: **guidance**. Humanity came down to earth carrying a promise that guidance would come, and the Quran is that guidance.

**Lessons**:
- Knowledge is a cause of honour.
- Arrogance was the first sin committed against Allah.
- Mistakes happen; the difference between Adam and Iblis is that Adam repented and Iblis persisted.

## Section 5: Addressing the Children of Israel (2:40-123)

This is the longest section, filling more than half the juz. It begins: ﴿يَٰبَنِيٓ إِسۡرَـٰٓءِيلَ ٱذۡكُرُواْ نِعۡمَتِيَ ٱلَّتِيٓ أَنۡعَمۡتُ عَلَيۡكُمۡ وَأَوۡفُواْ بِعَهۡدِيٓ أُوفِ بِعَهۡدِكُمۡ﴾, "O Children of Israel, remember My favour which I bestowed upon you, and fulfil My covenant, I will fulfil your covenant."

A beautiful structural detail: the section opens and closes with almost identical verses. Verse 47 and verse 122 are word for word the same: ﴿يَٰبَنِيٓ إِسۡرَـٰٓءِيلَ ٱذۡكُرُواْ نِعۡمَتِيَ ٱلَّتِيٓ أَنۡعَمۡتُ عَلَيۡكُمۡ وَأَنِّي فَضَّلۡتُكُمۡ عَلَى ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾, and each is followed by a verse about the Day of Judgement. The whole account is framed inside one repeated reminder.

### Favours given
Allah lists His favours: rescue from Pharaoh's people who slaughtered their sons, the parting of the sea, forgiveness after the calf, the giving of the Book, shade from the clouds, manna and quails, and twelve springs gushing from the rock.

### Failures recorded
Alongside the favours come the failures, recorded so that Muslims learn from them:
- worshipping the calf while Musa was away,
- demanding to see Allah openly,
- changing the word they were told to say when entering the town (they were told to say ﴿حِطَّةࣱ﴾, "relieve us of our burdens"),
- complaining about manna and quails and asking for vegetables,
- transgressing the Sabbath,
- breaking the covenant.

### The cow (2:67-73)
This is the story the surah is named after: ﴿إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَأۡمُرُكُمۡ أَن تَذۡبَحُواْ بَقَرَةٗۖ﴾, "Allah commands you to slaughter a cow." The command was simple, but they made it hard with question after question: what kind is it, what colour, so Allah made it harder for them, and they slaughtered it "though they almost did not". At the end a murdered man is struck with part of it and is brought back to life, a sign of the resurrection.

**Lesson**: hurrying to obey is better than burdening yourself with needless questions.

### Hardened hearts
Right after the story: ﴿ثُمَّ قَسَتۡ قُلُوبُكُم مِّنۢ بَعۡدِ ذَٰلِكَ فَهِيَ كَٱلۡحِجَارَةِ أَوۡ أَشَدُّ قَسۡوَةٗۚ﴾, "Then your hearts became hardened after that, being like stones or even harder." Some stones split and rivers flow from them, and some fall down in awe of Allah. A heart that does not soften before the verses is harder than rock.

### The covenant and the foundations of faith
Verse 83 gathers the foundations of good character: ﴿لَا تَعۡبُدُونَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهَ وَبِٱلۡوَٰلِدَيۡنِ إِحۡسَانٗا وَذِي ٱلۡقُرۡبَىٰ وَٱلۡيَتَٰمَىٰ وَٱلۡمَسَٰكِينِ وَقُولُواْ لِلنَّاسِ حُسۡنٗا﴾: worship none but Allah, be good to parents, relatives, orphans and the poor, and speak kindly to people. These are not commands for one nation only; they are the foundations of every message.

### Answering claims
The Quran answers several claims: that the Fire would touch them only for a few days, and that Paradise was reserved for them: ﴿وَقَالُواْ لَن يَدۡخُلَ ٱلۡجَنَّةَ إِلَّا مَن كَانَ هُودًا أَوۡ نَصَٰرَىٰۗ تِلۡكَ أَمَانِيُّهُمۡۗ﴾, "They say none will enter Paradise except a Jew or a Christian. That is their wishful thinking." Then comes the fair rule: ﴿بَلَىٰۚ مَنۡ أَسۡلَمَ وَجۡهَهُۥ لِلَّهِ وَهُوَ مُحۡسِنࣱ فَلَهُۥٓ أَجۡرُهُۥ عِندَ رَبِّهِۦ﴾, "Yes, whoever submits his face to Allah while doing good will have his reward with his Lord."

This section also mentions **Harut and Marut** and magic (2:102), and the verse of **abrogation**: ﴿مَا نَنسَخۡ مِنۡ ءَايَةٍ أَوۡ نُنسِهَا نَأۡتِ بِخَيۡرٖ مِّنۡهَآ أَوۡ مِثۡلِهَآۗ﴾, which prepares for the change of the qibla at the start of Juz 2. It also contains: ﴿وَلِلَّهِ ٱلۡمَشۡرِقُ وَٱلۡمَغۡرِبُۚ فَأَيۡنَمَا تُوَلُّواْ فَثَمَّ وَجۡهُ ٱللَّهِۚ﴾, "To Allah belong the east and the west; wherever you turn, there is the Face of Allah."

**Why so much space for the Children of Israel?** Because the Muslim community inherited the scripture after them. The Quran shows it the experience of an earlier nation that carried revelation and then fell short, so that it avoids the same mistakes: twisting meanings, finding loopholes in commands, endless argument, and commanding others to do good while forgetting oneself: ﴿أَتَأۡمُرُونَ ٱلنَّاسَ بِٱلۡبِرِّ وَتَنسَوۡنَ أَنفُسَكُمۡ وَأَنتُمۡ تَتۡلُونَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبَۚ﴾, "Do you order people to righteousness and forget yourselves while you recite the Scripture?"

## Section 6: Ibrahim, leader of the monotheists (2:124-141)

The juz closes with the father of the prophets: ﴿وَإِذِ ٱبۡتَلَىٰٓ إِبۡرَٰهِـۧمَ رَبُّهُۥ بِكَلِمَٰتٖ فَأَتَمَّهُنَّۖ قَالَ إِنِّي جَاعِلُكَ لِلنَّاسِ إِمَامٗاۖ﴾, "And when his Lord tested Ibrahim with commands and he fulfilled them, He said: I will make you a leader for mankind." Ibrahim asked for leadership for his descendants too, and the answer came: ﴿لَا يَنَالُ عَهۡدِي ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ﴾, "My covenant does not include the wrongdoers." Leadership in faith is not inherited by blood; it is earned by uprightness.

Then the scene of **building the Kaaba**: ﴿وَإِذۡ يَرۡفَعُ إِبۡرَٰهِـۧمُ ٱلۡقَوَاعِدَ مِنَ ٱلۡبَيۡتِ وَإِسۡمَٰعِيلُ رَبَّنَا تَقَبَّلۡ مِنَّآۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلۡعَلِيمُ﴾, "And when Ibrahim was raising the foundations of the House with Ismail: Our Lord, accept this from us; You are the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing." They were building the greatest house of worship on earth, yet they asked for acceptance. That is one of the deepest lessons in sincerity: a great deed does not remove the fear that it may not be accepted.

Then Ibrahim's prayer: ﴿رَبَّنَا وَٱبۡعَثۡ فِيهِمۡ رَسُولٗا مِّنۡهُمۡ يَتۡلُواْ عَلَيۡهِمۡ ءَايَٰتِكَ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ وَٱلۡحِكۡمَةَ وَيُزَكِّيهِمۡۖ﴾, "Our Lord, raise up among them a messenger from themselves who will recite Your verses to them, teach them the Book and wisdom, and purify them." Allah answered it by sending Muhammad ﷺ from the descendants of Ismail.

The section continues with the final advice of Ibrahim and Yaqub to their sons to die as Muslims, the claim ﴿كُونُواْ هُودًا أَوۡ نَصَٰرَىٰ تَهۡتَدُواْۗ﴾, "Be Jews or Christians and you will be guided", and the reply ﴿قُلۡ بَلۡ مِلَّةَ إِبۡرَٰهِـۧمَ حَنِيفٗاۖ﴾, "Say: rather, the way of Ibrahim, the upright." Then comes the declaration of belief in all the prophets without distinction, and ﴿صِبۡغَةَ ٱللَّهِ وَمَنۡ أَحۡسَنُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ صِبۡغَةٗۖ﴾, "The colouring of Allah, and who is better than Allah in colouring?"

**Why end the juz with Ibrahim?** Because the People of the Book and the Arabs all claimed him. The Quran shows that true belonging to Ibrahim means following his monotheism, and that prepares for turning the qibla to the Kaaba he built, at the start of Juz 2.

## Tajweed points that appear in Juz 1

### The compulsory letter madd in ﴿الٓمٓ﴾
It is read *Alif, Laam, Meem*. The alif has no madd, while **Laam and Meem are stretched for six counts** (al-madd al-lazim al-harfi). The final meem of "Laam" merges into "Meem" with ghunnah.

### The paired stop in ﴿لَا رَيۡبَۛ فِيهِۛ﴾
The three-dot sign ۛ appears twice close together. It means: stop at one of them, not both. You may read "la rayba" and then "fihi hudan lil-muttaqin", or "la rayba fih" and then "hudan lil-muttaqin".

### Iqlab in ﴿مِّنۢ بَعۡدِ﴾
A sakin noon before ba becomes a hidden meem with ghunnah. The small meem written above the noon shows this.

### Idgham without ghunnah
As in ﴿هُدٗى لِّلۡمُتَّقِينَ﴾ and ﴿مِّن رَّبِّهِمۡۖ﴾: tanween and sakin noon before laam and ra merge without ghunnah in the narration of Hafs.

### Connected and separated madd
Very frequent in this juz. Keep your length consistent throughout a recitation.

For a gentle introduction to these rules see [A Roadmap for Learning Tajweed](/blog/learn-tajweed-roadmap-english/).

## Similar verses (mutashabihat) to watch

These are the passages memorizers most often confuse.

### 1. The two Day-of-Judgement verses: 2:48 and 2:123
- 2:48: ﴿وَلَا يُقۡبَلُ مِنۡهَا شَفَٰعَةࣱ وَلَا يُؤۡخَذُ مِنۡهَا عَدۡلࣱ﴾, intercession first, then ransom.
- 2:123: ﴿وَلَا يُقۡبَلُ مِنۡهَا عَدۡلࣱ وَلَا تَنفَعُهَا شَفَٰعَةࣱ﴾, ransom first, then intercession.
**Key**: the first comes at the start of the Bani Israel section, the second at its end, and the order is reversed.

### 2. Verses 47 and 122
Identical, so the only danger is what follows each one (the two verses above).

### 3. Verses 134 and 141
Also identical: ﴿تِلۡكَ أُمَّةࣱ قَدۡ خَلَتۡۖ﴾. When you reach it the second time, the juz is over.

### 4. Entering the town: Al-Baqarah 58 and Al-A'raf 161
- Al-Baqarah: "enter (*udkhulu*) this town, **and eat** (*fa-kulu*) ... **abundantly** (*raghadan*) ... enter the gate bowing, and say *hittah* ... *khatayakum* ... **and** We will increase (*wa-sanazid*)."
- Al-A'raf: "dwell (*uskunu*) in this town, **and eat** (*wa-kulu*) ... say *hittah*, and enter the gate bowing ... *khati'atikum* ... We will increase (*sanazid*, without *wa*)."

### 5. Believers, Jews, Christians and Sabians
- Al-Baqarah 62: ﴿وَٱلنَّصَٰرَىٰ وَٱلصَّـٰبِـِٔينَ﴾, Christians before Sabians.
- Al-Ma'idah 69: ﴿وَٱلصَّـٰبِـُٔونَ وَٱلنَّصَٰرَىٰ﴾, Sabians first, in the nominative.

### 6. Adam in Al-Baqarah and Al-A'raf
- Al-Baqarah 35: ﴿وَكُلَا مِنۡهَا رَغَدًا حَيۡثُ شِئۡتُمَا﴾, with *raghadan*.
- Al-A'raf 19: ﴿فَكُلَا مِنۡ حَيۡثُ شِئۡتُمَا﴾, without it.
**Handy rule**: in both the Adam story and the town story, *raghadan* appears in Al-Baqarah and not in Al-A'raf.

## How to memorize Juz 1 with understanding

1. **Start with the map**: learn the six sections first.
2. **Read a short tafsir** of each section before memorizing it.
3. **Memorize a section as one unit**: do not split the story of Adam across distant days.
4. **Link the end of each section to the start of the next**: section 3 begins with "O mankind" right after the parables of the hypocrites.
5. **Mark the similar verses** in your memorization copy with one colour.
6. **Revise by hizb**: Hizb 1 ends at 2:74 and Hizb 2 runs to 2:141.
7. **Pray with what you memorize**: reciting in salah is one of the strongest ways to make it stick.

For a step-by-step plan see [How to Memorize the Quran Step by Step](/blog/how-to-memorize-quran-step-by-step/).

## The virtue of reciting Al-Baqarah

The Prophet ﷺ said, in Sahih Muslim: "Recite Surah Al-Baqarah, for taking it is a blessing, leaving it is a regret, and the sorcerers cannot withstand it." He also said, in Sahih Muslim: "Do not make your houses graves. Shaytan flees from a house in which Surah Al-Baqarah is recited." Juz 1 is more than a third of this surah, so finishing it is a large step towards that virtue. For an overview of the full surah read [Surah Al-Baqarah Meaning in English](/blog/surah-al-baqarah-meaning-english/).

## Practical lessons from Juz 1

- **Ask for guidance with a present heart**; you say it in every rak'ah.
- **Check yourself against the qualities of the God-conscious** at the start of Al-Baqarah.
- **Beware the small traits of hypocrisy**: lying, showing what you do not hold, and causing harm while claiming to fix things.
- **Hurry to repent like Adam**, and never persist like Iblis.
- **Do not burden yourself with questions** about what Allah did not require, as the people of the cow did.
- **Work and ask for acceptance**, as Ibrahim and Ismail did.
- **Belong to the prophets through deeds, not lineage**: ﴿لَا يَنَالُ عَهۡدِي ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ﴾.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where does Juz 1 end?
At Al-Baqarah 2:141: ﴿وَلَا تُسۡـَٔلُونَ عَمَّا كَانُواْ يَعۡمَلُونَ﴾. Juz 2 begins with ﴿سَيَقُولُ ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ﴾.

### How many pages is Juz 1?
In the Madinah Mushaf it occupies pages 1 to 21. The first two pages are shorter because of their decorated frames.

### What is the most important verse in Juz 1?
Every verse has its place, but scholars often point to ﴿إِيَّاكَ نَعۡبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾, because it gathers the whole religion: worship and seeking help from Allah alone.

### Is Juz 1 good for beginners in memorization?
It suits those who want to start from the beginning of the Mushaf, but its verses are longer than those of Juz Amma, so a beginner needs patience. Many start with Juz Amma and return to it later.

### Why are there so many similar verses in this juz?
Because the stories of Adam and the Children of Israel are repeated in other surahs such as Al-A'raf and Ta-Ha, with small differences in wording. These are best mastered by writing the pairs side by side, not by repetition alone.

## Conclusion

Juz 1 is not a simple introduction to the Quran; it is the roadmap. It begins with a request for guidance, presents the Book that guides, shows the kinds of people who meet it, tells the beginning of humanity, presents the experience of an earlier nation, and closes with the leader of the monotheists. Whoever understands this journey will memorize the juz more firmly, recite it with more presence, and live it more closely.
