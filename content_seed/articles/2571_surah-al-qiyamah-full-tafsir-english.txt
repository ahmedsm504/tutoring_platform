Surah Al-Qiyamah speaks directly to the human being who doubts that he will be brought back. "Does man think that We will not assemble his bones? Yes. We are able even to restore his fingertips." It swears by the Day of Resurrection and by the self-reproaching soul, the conscience inside each of us that blames us when we do wrong. It shows the moment the eyes are dazzled and the moon is eclipsed, when man asks "Where is the escape?" and finds none. It interrupts itself to teach the Prophet ﷺ how to receive revelation, describes faces radiant and looking at their Lord, and ends with the moment the soul reaches the collarbones and the question: does man think he will be left neglected?

This guide explains Surah Al-Qiyamah section by section in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Qiyamah
- **Number of verses**: 40.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 75, in the twenty-ninth juz.
- **Name**: Al-Qiyamah means "the Resurrection", from the first verse.
- **Main themes**: the certainty of resurrection, the conscience, the moment of death, and the vision of Allah.

## Verses 1–6: The Oath and the Doubter
﴿لَآ أُقۡسِمُ بِيَوۡمِ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِ﴾ ﴿وَلَآ أُقۡسِمُ بِٱلنَّفۡسِ ٱللَّوَّامَةِ﴾ ﴿أَيَحۡسَبُ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ أَلَّن نَّجۡمَعَ عِظَامَهُۥ﴾ ﴿بَلَىٰ قَٰدِرِينَ عَلَىٰٓ أَن نُّسَوِّيَ بَنَانَهُۥ﴾ ﴿بَلۡ يُرِيدُ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ لِيَفۡجُرَ أَمَامَهُۥ﴾ ﴿يَسۡـَٔلُ أَيَّانَ يَوۡمُ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِ﴾ (Quran 75:1-6)

**"I swear by the Day of Resurrection, and I swear by the self-reproaching soul."** *An-nafs al-lawwamah* is the soul that blames its owner: for doing wrong, and even for not doing more good. Al-Hasan al-Basri described the believer as someone who constantly calls himself to account. This inner voice is a sign of life in the heart.

**"Does man think that We will not assemble his bones? Yes. We are able even to restore his fingertips."** The fingertips are mentioned for a reason: they are the finest, most detailed part of the body, with patterns that differ from person to person. The One who can restore those fine details can certainly restore the rest.

**"Rather, man desires to continue in sin"**: his denial is not an intellectual problem. He wants to keep sinning, so he denies the Day that would hold him to account. **"He asks: When is the Day of Resurrection?"**, mocking.

## Verses 7–15: Where Is the Escape?
﴿فَإِذَا بَرِقَ ٱلۡبَصَرُ﴾ ﴿وَخَسَفَ ٱلۡقَمَرُ﴾ ﴿وَجُمِعَ ٱلشَّمۡسُ وَٱلۡقَمَرُ﴾ ﴿يَقُولُ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ يَوۡمَئِذٍ أَيۡنَ ٱلۡمَفَرُّ﴾ ﴿كَلَّا لَا وَزَرَ﴾ ﴿إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ يَوۡمَئِذٍ ٱلۡمُسۡتَقَرُّ﴾ ﴿يُنَبَّؤُاْ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ يَوۡمَئِذِۭ بِمَا قَدَّمَ وَأَخَّرَ﴾ ﴿بَلِ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ عَلَىٰ نَفۡسِهِۦ بَصِيرَةࣱ﴾ ﴿وَلَوۡ أَلۡقَىٰ مَعَاذِيرَهُۥ﴾ (Quran 75:7-15)

**The scenes**: **the eyes are dazzled**, **the moon is eclipsed**, **the sun and the moon are joined**. Then man says: **"Where is the escape?"** **"No! There is no refuge. To your Lord, that Day, is the place of permanence."**

**"Man will be informed that Day of what he sent ahead and kept back."** Then a profound statement: **"Rather, man, against himself, will be a witness, even if he presents his excuses."** Deep down, every person knows the truth about himself, whatever excuses he offers.

## Verses 16–19: Do Not Hasten Your Tongue
﴿لَا تُحَرِّكۡ بِهِۦ لِسَانَكَ لِتَعۡجَلَ بِهِۦٓ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ عَلَيۡنَا جَمۡعَهُۥ وَقُرۡءَانَهُۥ﴾ ﴿فَإِذَا قَرَأۡنَٰهُ فَٱتَّبِعۡ قُرۡءَانَهُۥ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ إِنَّ عَلَيۡنَا بَيَانَهُۥ﴾ (Quran 75:16-19)

In the middle of the description of the Day, the surah turns to the Prophet ﷺ. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn 'Abbas said that when Jibril brought revelation, the Prophet ﷺ used to move his tongue and lips with it, finding it hard, out of fear of forgetting. So Allah revealed: **"Move not your tongue with it to hasten with it. Indeed, upon Us is its collection and its recitation."** Ibn 'Abbas said: its collection in your heart, and then you recite it. **"So when We have recited it, then follow its recitation"**: listen attentively. **"Then upon Us is its clarification."** After this, when Jibril came, the Prophet ﷺ would listen, and when Jibril left, he would recite it as Jibril had.

**Why here?** One explanation is the contrast: man hastens towards this world, and the Prophet ﷺ was hastening towards revelation out of love; both are told that what matters is in Allah's hands.

## Verses 20–25: Radiant Faces Looking at Their Lord
﴿كَلَّا بَلۡ تُحِبُّونَ ٱلۡعَاجِلَةَ﴾ ﴿وَتَذَرُونَ ٱلۡأٓخِرَةَ﴾ ﴿وُجُوهࣱ يَوۡمَئِذٖ نَّاضِرَةٌ﴾ ﴿إِلَىٰ رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةࣱ﴾ ﴿وَوُجُوهࣱ يَوۡمَئِذِۭ بَاسِرَةࣱ﴾ ﴿تَظُنُّ أَن يُفۡعَلَ بِهَا فَاقِرَةࣱ﴾ (Quran 75:20-25)

**"No! But you love the immediate and leave the hereafter."** That is the root of the problem: preferring what is fast and close over what is lasting.

**"Faces, that Day, will be radiant, looking at their Lord."** One of the clearest verses on the believers seeing Allah in the hereafter. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Jarir ibn 'Abdullah reported that the Prophet ﷺ looked at the full moon and said: "You will see your Lord as you see this moon, without any difficulty in seeing Him." And in Sahih Muslim, Suhayb reported that after the people of Paradise enter it, the veil will be lifted, "and they will not have been given anything more beloved to them than looking at their Lord".

**"And faces, that Day, will be contorted, expecting that there will be done to them a backbreaking calamity."**

## Verses 26–30: When the Soul Reaches the Collarbones
﴿كَلَّآ إِذَا بَلَغَتِ ٱلتَّرَاقِيَ﴾ ﴿وَقِيلَ مَنۡۜ رَاقٖ﴾ ﴿وَظَنَّ أَنَّهُ ٱلۡفِرَاقُ﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡتَفَّتِ ٱلسَّاقُ بِٱلسَّاقِ﴾ ﴿إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ يَوۡمَئِذٍ ٱلۡمَسَاقُ﴾ (Quran 75:26-30)

**The moment of death**: the soul reaches the collarbones, and those around say: **"Who will cure him?"** The dying man **"is certain that it is the parting"**. **"And the leg is wound about the leg"**: the stillness of death, or the hardship of this world joined with the hardship of the next. **"To your Lord, that Day, will be the procession."**

## Verses 31–35: He Neither Believed Nor Prayed
﴿فَلَا صَدَّقَ وَلَا صَلَّىٰ﴾ ﴿وَلَٰكِن كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ ذَهَبَ إِلَىٰٓ أَهۡلِهِۦ يَتَمَطَّىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَوۡلَىٰ لَكَ فَأَوۡلَىٰ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ أَوۡلَىٰ لَكَ فَأَوۡلَىٰٓ﴾ (Quran 75:31-35)

**"And the disbeliever had not believed, nor had he prayed. But he denied and turned away. And then he went to his people, swaggering."** Then: **"Woe to you, and woe! Then woe to you, and woe!"** a fourfold warning.

## Verses 36–40: Not Left Neglected
﴿أَيَحۡسَبُ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ أَن يُتۡرَكَ سُدًى﴾ ﴿أَلَمۡ يَكُ نُطۡفَةٗ مِّن مَّنِيّٖ يُمۡنَىٰ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ كَانَ عَلَقَةٗ فَخَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ﴾ ﴿فَجَعَلَ مِنۡهُ ٱلزَّوۡجَيۡنِ ٱلذَّكَرَ وَٱلۡأُنثَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَلَيۡسَ ذَٰلِكَ بِقَٰدِرٍ عَلَىٰٓ أَن يُحۡـِۧيَ ٱلۡمَوۡتَىٰ﴾ (Quran 75:36-40)

**"Does man think that he will be left neglected?"** (*suda*): without commands and prohibitions, without accountability. Then the argument from creation: he was a drop of fluid, then a clinging clot, then Allah created and proportioned him, and made from him the two mates, male and female. **"Is not that Being able to give life to the dead?"**

## Word Meanings
- **Al-Qiyamah**: the Resurrection.
- **Al-Lawwamah**: self-reproaching.
- **Banan**: fingertips.
- **Yafjur**: continue in sin.
- **Bariqa**: dazzled.
- **Khasafa**: eclipsed.
- **Al-Mafarr**: the escape.
- **Wazar**: refuge.
- **Basirah**: witness, seeing clearly.
- **Ma'adhir**: excuses.
- **Nadirah**: radiant.
- **Nazirah**: looking.
- **Basirah (verse 24)**: contorted, gloomy.
- **At-Taraqi**: the collarbones.
- **Raq**: one who cures, or one who ascends with the soul.
- **Yatamatta**: swaggering.
- **Suda**: neglected, without purpose.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **Two oaths**: the Day of Resurrection outside, the reproaching soul inside.
2. **"Restore his fingertips"**: the finest detail as proof of the greatest power.
3. **"Where is the escape?"**: three words that capture panic.
4. **"Man against himself will be a witness"**: an inner court.
5. **"Radiant, looking"**: *nadirah, nazirah*, two similar words with two meanings.
6. **"Woe to you, and woe"**: repeated four times.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd munfasil**: in *la uqsimu* (verse 1).
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *najma'a* (verse 3), the jim sakinah bounces lightly.
- **Idgham with ghunnah**: in *wujuhun yawma'idhin* (verses 22 and 24).
- **Saktah**: in *man raq* (verse 27), Hafs makes a brief pause without breath on *man* before *raq*, so the noon is not merged into the ra'.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Al-Muddaththir before it ended with people who "do not fear the hereafter", and Al-Qiyamah answers their doubt directly. Surah Al-Insan after it begins with man's origin, "a drop of mixed fluid", continuing the end of Al-Qiyamah, and describes the reward of the righteous in detail.

## Listening to the Self-Reproaching Soul
1. **Do not silence your conscience**: it is a gift.
2. **Account for yourself daily**: what did I do, and why?
3. **Do not drown it in excuses**: "even if he presents his excuses".
4. **Turn blame into repentance**, not despair.
5. **Keep company that awakens your conscience**, not that numbs it.

## Learning From "Do Not Hasten"
The verses about receiving revelation teach a method for learning the Quran:
1. **Listen first** with full attention.
2. **Then recite** what you heard.
3. **Trust that Allah makes it easy**: "upon Us is its collection".
4. **Seek understanding**: "upon Us is its clarification".

## Practical Lessons
1. **Believe in the resurrection with certainty.**
2. **Listen to your conscience.**
3. **Stop making excuses**: you know yourself.
4. **Do not love the immediate at the cost of the hereafter.**
5. **Long to see your Lord**, and work for it.
6. **Remember the moment of death.**
7. **Know that you were not created without purpose.**

## Loving the Immediate: A Modern Diagnosis
"No! But you love the immediate and leave the hereafter" describes our age with uncanny accuracy. Instant messages, instant food, instant entertainment: everything trains us to want results now. The hereafter, by contrast, requires delayed reward: you pray now and are rewarded later, you give now and see the return later, you hold back from sin now and taste the sweetness later. The surah does not condemn enjoying lawful things in this world; it condemns *leaving* the hereafter for them.

**How to retrain the heart**:
1. **Practise small delays**: wait before buying, before replying in anger, before reaching for your phone.
2. **Fast voluntarily**: fasting is the purest training in delayed reward.
3. **Remind yourself of the long view**: one prayer now is a light in the grave and on the Bridge.
4. **Read about Paradise** regularly, so the hereafter feels real and near.
5. **Visit graves**: in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Visit the graves, for they remind you of the hereafter."

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What is the self-reproaching soul?
The soul that blames its owner for wrongdoing and for falling short in good.

### Why does the surah mention fingertips?
Because they are among the finest details of the body; the One who can restore them can restore everything.

### What does "Move not your tongue with it" mean?
Allah told the Prophet ﷺ not to hurry to repeat revelation while it was being recited, because Allah would gather it in his heart, as Ibn 'Abbas explained in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### Does this surah prove that believers will see Allah?
Yes. "Faces, that Day, radiant, looking at their Lord" is one of the clearest proofs, confirmed by authentic hadith.

### What does "man against himself will be a witness" mean?
Every person knows the truth about himself, whatever excuses he presents.

### What does "left neglected" mean?
Left without commands, prohibitions or accountability, which is not the case.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Qiyamah?
40 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

### What does "the leg is wound about the leg" mean?
It describes the stillness and hardship of death, or the meeting of the last day of this world with the first day of the hereafter.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Qiyamah takes you from your fingertips to the moment your soul reaches your collarbones, and asks whether you really think you will be left without purpose. Listen to the self-reproaching soul inside you, stop hiding behind excuses, and work for the greatest reward: a radiant face looking at its Lord.
