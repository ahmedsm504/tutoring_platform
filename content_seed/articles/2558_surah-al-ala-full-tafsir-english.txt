Surah Al-A'la was one of the surahs the Prophet ﷺ loved to recite. He read it in the Friday prayer, in the two Eid prayers and in the Witr prayer, and he recommended it to an imam who was making the prayer too long. It begins with glorifying Allah, the Most High, and ends with a reminder that the hereafter is better and more lasting. In between it gives a complete path: know your Lord through His creation and guidance, receive the reminder, purify yourself, remember His name and pray.

This guide explains Surah Al-A'la verse by verse in English, with the authentic hadith about where it was recited and practical lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-A'la
- **Number of verses**: 19.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 87, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: Al-A'la means "the Most High", from the first verse. It is also called *Sabbih*.
- **Main themes**: glorifying Allah, His creation and guidance, the preservation of the Quran, the formula of success and preferring the hereafter.

## Where the Prophet ﷺ Recited Surah Al-A'la
1. **In Friday and the Eids**: in Sahih Muslim, an-Nu'man ibn Bashir said: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to recite in the two Eids and in Friday prayer *Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High* and *Has there come to you the report of the Overwhelming?*" He added that when Eid and Friday fell on the same day, he recited both surahs in both prayers.
2. **In Witr**: Abu Dawud and an-Nasa'i reported from Ubayy ibn Ka'b that the Prophet ﷺ used to pray Witr with *Sabbih isma rabbika al-a'la*, *Qul ya ayyuha al-kafirun* and *Qul huwa Allahu ahad*. Al-Albani graded it authentic.
3. **His advice to Mu'adh**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, when Mu'adh made the prayer too long for his people, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Are you causing trouble, O Mu'adh? Why did you not pray with *Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High*, *By the sun and its brightness* and *By the night when it covers*?"
4. **Its early place in Madinah**: in Sahih al-Bukhari, al-Bara' said that the first to come to them in Madinah were Mus'ab ibn 'Umayr and Ibn Umm Maktum, who taught them the Quran, and by the time the Prophet ﷺ arrived, al-Bara' had already learned *Sabbih isma rabbika al-a'la* among other surahs like it.

## Verses 1–5: Glorify the Name of Your Lord
﴿سَبِّحِ ٱسۡمَ رَبِّكَ ٱلۡأَعۡلَى﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ﴾ ﴿وَٱلَّذِي قَدَّرَ فَهَدَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَٱلَّذِيٓ أَخۡرَجَ ٱلۡمَرۡعَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَجَعَلَهُۥ غُثَآءً أَحۡوَىٰ﴾ (Quran 87:1-5)

**"Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High"**: declare Him free of every imperfection, and mention His name with reverence. **Al-A'la**, the Most High, is above everything: in His essence, in His status and in His power.

**Putting it into practice**: in Sahih Muslim, Hudhayfah described the Prophet's ﷺ night prayer and said that in his bowing he said *Subhana rabbiya al-'azim* and in his prostration *Subhana rabbiya al-a'la*. Notice the wisdom: when your forehead is at its lowest point on the ground, you declare that your Lord is the Most High.

**Four descriptions of your Lord**:
1. **"Who created and proportioned"**: He created everything and gave it the right form.
2. **"Who measured and guided"**: He measured out for every creature what suits it, then guided it to it. The newborn finds its mother's milk, the bee builds its hive, the bird finds its way home across continents. Nobody taught them; Allah guided them.
3. **"Who brought out the pasture"**: the green grass for the animals.
4. **"Then made it dark stubble"**: then it dries and turns dark.

**The lesson of the pasture**: green, then dry. That is the story of this world: freshness, then withering. It prepares for the end of the surah: "But you prefer the life of this world, while the hereafter is better and more lasting."

## Verses 6–8: We Will Make You Recite
﴿سَنُقۡرِئُكَ فَلَا تَنسَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُۚ إِنَّهُۥ يَعۡلَمُ ٱلۡجَهۡرَ وَمَا يَخۡفَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَنُيَسِّرُكَ لِلۡيُسۡرَىٰ﴾ (Quran 87:6-8)

**"We will make you recite, and you will not forget"**: a promise to the Prophet ﷺ that the Quran would be preserved in his heart. **"Except what Allah wills"**: everything is subject to Allah's will, including what He chose to abrogate. **"Indeed, He knows what is declared and what is hidden."**

**"And We will ease you towards ease"**: We will make the easiest path smooth for you in all your affairs, and give you a religion of ease. In Sahih al-Bukhari, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The religion is easy, and nobody makes the religion too hard for himself except that it overpowers him. So be balanced, come close to perfection, and receive good news."

## Verses 9–13: Remind, Where Reminding Benefits
﴿فَذَكِّرۡ إِن نَّفَعَتِ ٱلذِّكۡرَىٰ﴾ ﴿سَيَذَّكَّرُ مَن يَخۡشَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَيَتَجَنَّبُهَا ٱلۡأَشۡقَى﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي يَصۡلَى ٱلنَّارَ ٱلۡكُبۡرَىٰ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا وَلَا يَحۡيَىٰ﴾ (Quran 87:9-13)

**"So remind, if the reminder benefits"**: the Prophet ﷺ is told to keep reminding people. Some scholars said the meaning is: remind in every case, whether it benefits or not, because conveying the message is a duty.

**Two responses to the reminder**:
- **"The one who fears will be reminded"**: a heart with fear of Allah benefits.
- **"The most wretched will avoid it"**: the wretched person runs from reminders.

**His end**: **"who will burn in the greatest Fire, then he will neither die in it nor live"**. He does not die and find relief, and he does not live a life worth living.

## Verses 14–15: The Formula of Success
﴿قَدۡ أَفۡلَحَ مَن تَزَكَّىٰ﴾ ﴿وَذَكَرَ ٱسۡمَ رَبِّهِۦ فَصَلَّىٰ﴾ (Quran 87:14-15)

**Three steps to success**:
1. **"Purified himself"** (*tazakka*): cleansed himself of shirk, sins and bad character. Some of the early scholars also explained it as giving the charity of Eid al-Fitr.
2. **"Mentioned the name of his Lord"**: remembered Allah with his heart and tongue.
3. **"And prayed"**: established the prayer.

**The order matters**: purification first, then remembrance, then prayer. A heart that has been cleaned remembers Allah, and the one who remembers Allah prays with presence. Some of the early scholars applied these verses to *zakat al-fitr* followed by the Eid prayer, which is one example of their meaning, not all of it.

## Verses 16–19: The Hereafter Is Better and More Lasting
﴿بَلۡ تُؤۡثِرُونَ ٱلۡحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنۡيَا﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡأٓخِرَةُ خَيۡرࣱ وَأَبۡقَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَفِي ٱلصُّحُفِ ٱلۡأُولَىٰ﴾ ﴿صُحُفِ إِبۡرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ﴾ (Quran 87:16-19)

**"But you prefer the life of this world"**: you put it first. **"While the hereafter is better and more lasting"**: better in its quality and more lasting in its duration. A sensible person does not prefer what perishes over what lasts.

**"Indeed, this is in the former scriptures, the scriptures of Ibrahim and Musa."** The message of purification, success and preferring the hereafter was also in the scrolls of Ibrahim and Musa. It is one of the foundations shared by all the messages.

## Word Meanings
- **Sabbih**: glorify, declare free of imperfection.
- **Al-A'la**: the Most High.
- **Sawwa**: proportioned, gave form.
- **Qaddara**: measured, decreed.
- **Hada**: guided.
- **Al-Mar'a**: pasture.
- **Ghutha'**: dry stubble, debris.
- **Ahwa**: darkened, blackish.
- **Al-Yusra**: ease.
- **Al-Ashqa**: the most wretched.
- **Tazakka**: purified himself.
- **Abqa**: more lasting.
- **Suhuf**: scrolls, scriptures.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"Glorify the name of your Lord"**: glorifying even His name shows deep reverence.
2. **"Created and proportioned, measured and guided"**: balanced pairs.
3. **The green pasture that turns dark**: a picture of this world in two words.
4. **"The one who fears" and "the most wretched"**: a clear contrast.
5. **"Neither die in it nor live"**: denying both extremes to show the worst state.
6. **"Better and more lasting"**: quality and duration together.

## Tajweed Notes
- **No imala in the verse endings**: the endings such as *fa-sawwa* and *fa-hada* are read with a plain fath in the narration of Hafs.
- **Madd 'arid lis-sukun**: at the ends of the verses when stopping.
- **Ghunnah**: in *innahu ya'lamu* (verse 7), on the noon with shaddah.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *wa abqa* (verse 17), the ba' sakinah bounces lightly.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah At-Tariq before it ended with "So give respite to the disbelievers", and Al-A'la opens by glorifying the Most High, whose patience is not weakness. Surah Al-Ghashiyah after it is its companion in Friday and Eid prayers: Al-A'la mentions "the greatest Fire", and Al-Ghashiyah describes the faces in the Fire and the faces in Paradise in detail.

## Allah's Guidance in Creation
"Who measured and guided" is one of the most beautiful verses about Allah's wisdom. Guidance comes in levels:
1. **Natural guidance for every creature**: the bee, the bird, the baby.
2. **The guidance of explanation**: through messengers and books, for all people.
3. **The guidance of success** (*tawfiq*): given to whomever Allah wills, opening his heart to Islam.
4. **Guidance to Paradise** on the Day of Resurrection.

When Pharaoh asked Musa who his Lord was, Musa answered: "Our Lord is He who gave everything its form and then guided it." The One who created you knows best what is good for you, which is why His law is better than any human system.

## Purifying the Soul
"Indeed, whoever purifies himself will succeed." Practical ways to purify:
1. **Pure tawhid**: the first purification.
2. **Sincere repentance**: wiping away sins.
3. **Zakah and charity**: cleansing wealth and the heart.
4. **Daily self-accounting**.
5. **Fighting arrogance, envy and anger**.
6. **Keeping good company**.
7. **Remembering Allah and reciting the Quran**.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Say "Subhana rabbiya al-a'la" in prostration** with your heart present.
2. **If you lead Friday prayer, recite Al-A'la and Al-Ghashiyah** as the Prophet ﷺ did.
3. **Pray Witr with Al-A'la, Al-Kafirun and Al-Ikhlas** at times.
4. **Reflect on how Allah guides His creatures.**
5. **Remind others with wisdom.**
6. **Purify yourself**: this is the path to success.
7. **Put the hereafter first** in your daily decisions.
8. **Keep the prayer light for others**, as the Prophet ﷺ advised Mu'adh.

## How to Memorise Surah Al-A'la
- **Day 1**: verses 1–8.
- **Day 2**: verses 9–15.
- **Day 3**: verses 16–19, then revise the whole surah.

## Teaching Children
- **Teach them the words of prostration**, *Subhana rabbiya al-a'la*, and connect them to this surah.
- **Plant a seed together** and watch it grow green, then dry: "then made it dark stubble".
- **Tell them how animals are guided**: birds migrating, bees making honey.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### When did the Prophet ﷺ recite Surah Al-A'la?
In Friday and Eid prayers with Al-Ghashiyah, as in Sahih Muslim, and in Witr with Al-Kafirun and Al-Ikhlas, as in the hadith of Ubayy ibn Ka'b graded authentic by al-Albani.

### What does "Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High" mean?
Declare your Lord free of every imperfection and mention His name with reverence; the Most High is above everything.

### What does "Who measured and guided" mean?
Allah measured out for every creature what suits it, then guided it to it.

### What does "We will make you recite, and you will not forget" mean?
A promise to the Prophet ﷺ that Allah would preserve the Quran in his heart, except what Allah willed.

### What does "Whoever purifies himself will succeed" mean?
Success belongs to the one who cleanses himself of shirk, sins and bad character; some early scholars also applied it to zakat al-fitr.

### What are the scriptures of Ibrahim and Musa?
Scrolls revealed to Ibrahim and Musa that contained the same foundations taught in this surah.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-A'la?
19 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-A'la is a short journey from glorification to the hereafter. It begins by exalting the Most High, passes through the signs of His creation and guidance, promises ease and the preservation of the Quran, and gives a simple formula for success: purify yourself, remember your Lord and pray. Then it closes with the truth every heart needs to hear each week: the hereafter is better and more lasting.
