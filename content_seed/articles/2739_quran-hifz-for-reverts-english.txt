You have recently embraced Islam, and you have fallen in love with the Quran: its sound, its meanings, the way it speaks to your heart. You want to memorise it, but you cannot read Arabic, you do not know where to start, and you sometimes feel overwhelmed when you hear others recite whole juz' from memory. You may even wonder whether it is realistic for you at all.

It is. Throughout history, countless people who were not born into Arabic, and many who came to Islam as adults, became people of the Quran. The Prophet ﷺ said, in a hadith in al-Bukhari and Muslim, that the one who recites the Quran and struggles with it, finding it difficult, has **two rewards**. Your struggle is not a weakness; it is a source of double reward. This guide gives you a clear path.

## Step 1: What You Need First for Prayer
Your first priority is not a long memorisation plan, but **what you need to pray**:
1. **Al-Fatihah**: recited in every unit (rak'ah) of every prayer.
2. **A few short surahs**: to recite after Al-Fatihah in the first two rak'ahs, such as Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, An-Nas and Al-Kawthar.
3. **The phrases of prayer**: "Allahu akbar", "Subhana Rabbiyal-'Azim" in bowing, "Sami'allahu liman hamidah", "Subhana Rabbiyal-A'la" in prostration, and the tashahhud.

﴿بِسۡمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ ﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾ ﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ ﴿مَٰلِكِ يَوۡمِ ٱلدِّينِ﴾ ﴿إِيَّاكَ نَعۡبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾ ﴿ٱهۡدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلۡمُسۡتَقِيمَ﴾ ﴿صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ غَيۡرِ ٱلۡمَغۡضُوبِ عَلَيۡهِمۡ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾ (Quran 1:1-7)

### Before you have memorised Al-Fatihah
Do not delay your prayers. Abu Dawud and an-Nasa'i reported that a man came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: "I cannot learn anything of the Quran, so teach me something that will suffice me." He said: "Say: Subhan Allah, wal-hamdu lillah, wa la ilaha illallah, wallahu akbar, wa la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah." The hadith is graded hasan. So until you learn Al-Fatihah, say this remembrance in its place, and your prayer is valid, while you work to learn Al-Fatihah as quickly as possible.

## Step 2: Learn the Arabic Letters
Many reverts rely on transliteration for years and never move beyond it. The Arabic alphabet has twenty-eight letters, and most adults can learn to read them in **a few weeks to a few months** with steady practice.
- **Use a foundational primer** such as the Noorani Qaida, which teaches letters, vowels and joining step by step.
- **Learn with a teacher**, even online, because some Arabic sounds do not exist in English and need correction: 'Ayn (ع), Ḥa (ح), Qaf (ق), Ṣad (ص), Ḍad (ض) and others.
- **Practise ten to twenty minutes daily** rather than long sessions once a week.

### What about transliteration?
Transliteration (Arabic written in Latin letters) can be a **temporary bridge** for your first surahs, but it cannot capture Arabic sounds accurately: Ḥa and Ha, 'Ayn and Hamzah, Ṣad and Sin are often written the same way, which leads to mistakes that change meanings. Many scholars and fiqh councils hold that the Quran should not be printed as a Mushaf in Latin letters. So use transliteration only alongside the Arabic and audio, and move to the Arabic script as soon as you can.

## Step 3: Your First Surahs
After Al-Fatihah, start with short surahs from the last part of the Quran (Juz' 'Amma). A good order:
1. **Al-Ikhlas** (4 verses): the surah of tawhid, equal to a third of the Quran in virtue.
2. **Al-Falaq and An-Nas**: protection surahs recited morning, evening and at bedtime.
3. **Al-Kawthar** (3 verses): the shortest surah.
4. **Al-'Asr** (3 verses): a complete guide to success in three verses.
5. **An-Nasr**, **Al-Kafirun**, **Al-Masad**, **Al-Fil**, **Quraysh**, **Al-Ma'un**.
6. Then continue backwards through Juz' 'Amma at your own pace.

﴿وَٱلۡعَصۡرِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لَفِي خُسۡرٍ﴾ ﴿إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلۡحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلصَّبۡرِ﴾ (Quran 103:1-3)

Also aim early for **Ayat al-Kursi** and **the last two verses of Al-Baqarah**, because of their great virtue at bedtime.

## Step 4: Find a Teacher
The Quran was taught orally from the beginning, from Jibril to the Prophet ﷺ and from him to his Companions. For a revert, a teacher is especially important:
- **Correct pronunciation** from the start, before mistakes become habits.
- **Encouragement and accountability**.
- **Answers** to the many questions new Muslims have.

Options include local mosques and Islamic centres, which often run classes for new Muslims, and online one-to-one lessons, which offer flexibility and patient, individual attention. Look for a teacher who is patient, experienced with adult beginners, and willing to explain meanings.

## Step 5: Understand What You Memorise
For a non-Arabic speaker, understanding is the glue of memorisation:
- **Before memorising a surah**, read its translation and a short explanation in your language.
- **Learn key words**: *Allah*, *Rabb* (Lord), *qul* (say), *nas* (people), *yawm* (day), *ar-Rahman* (the Most Merciful). Many words repeat throughout the Quran.
- **Use word-by-word resources** that show the meaning of each Arabic word.
- **Recite the surah in prayer** while thinking of its meaning.

When you understand what you recite, prayer becomes a conversation, and memorisation becomes far easier.

## Step 6: A Simple Memorisation Method
1. **Listen** to the surah several times from a clear reciter, following the text.
2. **Learn one verse at a time**, repeating it after the reciter.
3. **Recite it without audio**; if you get stuck, listen again.
4. **Link** each new verse to the previous one.
5. **Recite the whole surah** several times without mistakes.
6. **Recite it to your teacher** or a knowledgeable friend.
7. **Use it in prayer** the same day.

## A Realistic One-Year Plan
| Period | Focus |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1 to 2 | Al-Fatihah, prayer phrases, the remembrance in place of Al-Fatihah until learned |
| Weeks 3 to 8 | Arabic letters with a primer; Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, An-Nas |
| Months 3 to 4 | Al-Kawthar, Al-'Asr, An-Nasr, Al-Kafirun; reading short words in Arabic |
| Months 5 to 6 | Ayat al-Kursi, Al-Masad, Al-Fil, Quraysh, Al-Ma'un; reading from the Mushaf slowly |
| Months 7 to 12 | Continue through Juz' 'Amma; the last two verses of Al-Baqarah; basic tajweed |

By the end of a year, many reverts can read the Mushaf slowly, have memorised a good portion of Juz' 'Amma, and pray with understanding. Some go faster; some slower. Consistency matters more than speed.

## Overcoming Common Challenges
### "The pronunciation is too hard"
Some sounds take weeks to master. Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity, and the one who struggles has two rewards. Keep practising with a teacher.

### "I forget what I memorise"
Revise before learning anything new, and recite your surahs in your prayers daily; prayer is natural revision.

### "I feel behind other Muslims"
You are on your own journey. Many born Muslims never learned to read the Quran properly; your effort to learn as an adult is precious.

### "I have no time"
Fifteen to twenty minutes a day, consistently, achieves far more than occasional long sessions.

### "I have no Muslim community nearby"
Online teachers and study circles can connect you with others, and many Islamic centres run programmes for new Muslims.

## Supporting a Revert
If you are a born Muslim with a revert friend or family member:
- **Be patient** with their pronunciation and do not mock their mistakes.
- **Offer to listen** to their recitation.
- **Share resources**, not pressure.
- **Celebrate** each surah they learn.

## Making Prayer Your Classroom
For a revert, the five daily prayers are the most powerful memorisation tool:
- **Rotate your surahs**: recite different surahs in different prayers, so each one is revised regularly.
- **Add one new surah to your prayers** as soon as you memorise it.
- **Pray a few voluntary rak'ahs**, such as the Sunnah before Fajr, to recite more of what you know.
- **Think of the meaning** while reciting; this deepens both memory and devotion.

In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "When the companion of the Quran stands and recites it night and day, he remembers it, and if he does not, he forgets it."

## Building Daily Habits
- **A fixed time**: after Fajr or before bed works for many people.
- **A small daily target**: one verse a day is over 300 verses a year.
- **A tracker**: tick off each surah you memorise; seeing progress is motivating.
- **Morning and evening adhkar**: include Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas three times; this alone keeps them strong.
- **Listening in spare moments**: commute, chores and walks.

## Learning Basic Tajweed
Once you can read slowly, learn a few essential tajweed rules that affect most pages:
- **Natural lengthening** (madd) of two counts.
- **Nasalisation** (ghunnah) on doubled noon and meem.
- **Rules of noon sakinah and tanwin**: clear, merged, converted and hidden pronunciation.
- **Echoing letters** (qalqalah).
- **Heavy and light letters**.

There is no need to master theory first; learn each rule when your teacher points it out in the verses you memorise.

## Ramadan as a Revert
Your first Ramadan is a special opportunity:
- **Attend Taraweeh** when you can, even part of it, and listen.
- **Set a modest goal**: perhaps memorising two or three new short surahs, or reading a juz' of translation.
- **Join a community iftar** or study circle for new Muslims.
- **Do not compare** your Quran goals with lifelong Muslims; your sincere effort is what counts.

## A Word of Encouragement
Allah says:

﴿وَلَقَدۡ يَسَّرۡنَا ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَ لِلذِّكۡرِ فَهَلۡ مِن مُّدَّكِرٖ﴾ (Quran 54:17)

The Quran has been made easy for those who sincerely seek to remember it. Many of the greatest scholars of the Quran's language and recitation were not Arabs by birth. Your accent, your slow reading and your stumbling are not barriers; they are part of a journey that Allah rewards at every step.

## Finding the Right Resources
- **A Mushaf with clear script** in the same layout your teacher uses, and an app showing the same pages.
- **A reliable translation** in your language, printed alongside the Arabic.
- **Teaching recordings** with slow, clear recitation, such as al-Husary's teaching Mushaf, where the listener repeats after the reciter.
- **A simple tafsir** for the surahs you memorise.
- **A small notebook** to record new words and their meanings.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What should a new Muslim memorise first?
Al-Fatihah and a few short surahs for prayer, along with the phrases of prayer.

### What if I cannot recite Al-Fatihah yet?
Say "Subhan Allah, wal-hamdu lillah, wa la ilaha illallah, wallahu akbar, wa la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah" in its place until you learn it, as taught in the hadith.

### How long does it take to learn the Arabic letters?
Usually a few weeks to a few months with daily practice and a teacher.

### Can I rely on transliteration?
Only as a temporary bridge alongside the Arabic and audio; move to the Arabic script as soon as possible.

### Do I need a teacher?
It is strongly recommended, especially for pronunciation, and online lessons make it accessible.

### Is it realistic to memorise the whole Quran as a revert?
Yes. Many have done it; start small, stay consistent, and keep going.

### How can I keep my memorised surahs from fading?
Recite them in your daily prayers, rotate surahs between prayers, and include the three Quls in your morning and evening adhkar.

### Do I need to learn tajweed theory before memorising?
No. Learn the essential rules gradually as your teacher points them out in the verses you memorise.

### What resources does a revert need to start?
A clear Mushaf and matching app, a reliable translation, slow teaching recordings, a simple tafsir and a notebook for new words.

## Conclusion
Memorising the Quran as a new Muslim is a beautiful and realistic goal. Start with what you need for prayer, learn the Arabic letters early instead of relying on transliteration, memorise short surahs with their meanings, find a patient teacher, and revise through your daily prayers. Your struggle with the words of Allah earns you two rewards, and every verse you learn becomes a light in your prayer and your life. Begin today, even with one verse.
