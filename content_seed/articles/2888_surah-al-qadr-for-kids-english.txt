Imagine one single night that is better than a thousand months. A thousand months is more than eighty-three years, longer than most people live! If you worship Allah on that one night, it's better than worshipping Him every single day for eighty-three years. That night is Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Power, and Allah tells us about it in Surah Al-Qadr.

It is the night when the Quran was first sent down, a night when angels fill the earth, and a night of peace until the morning. Every Ramadan, Muslim families all over the world look for it in the last ten nights. In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Al-Qadr to children in simple English, with realistic, child-friendly ways to join in during the last ten nights, activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Qadr

- **Position**: surah 97, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: five.
- **Type**: Meccan according to many scholars.
- **Name**: al-Qadr means "power", "honour" or "decree". Laylat al-Qadr is the Night of Power, the Night of Honour, and the night when Allah's decrees for the year are set out.

## The Whole Surah

﴿إِنَّآ أَنزَلۡنَٰهُ فِي لَيۡلَةِ ٱلۡقَدۡرِ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا لَيۡلَةُ ٱلۡقَدۡرِ﴾ ﴿لَيۡلَةُ ٱلۡقَدۡرِ خَيۡرࣱ مِّنۡ أَلۡفِ شَهۡرٖ﴾ ﴿تَنَزَّلُ ٱلۡمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ وَٱلرُّوحُ فِيهَا بِإِذۡنِ رَبِّهِم مِّن كُلِّ أَمۡرٖ﴾ ﴿سَلَٰمٌ هِيَ حَتَّىٰ مَطۡلَعِ ٱلۡفَجۡرِ﴾ (Quran 97:1-5)

## Verse 1: We Sent It Down on the Night of Power

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah says: Indeed, We sent it down on Laylat al-Qadr. 'It' means the Quran. On this special night, in Ramadan, the Quran was sent down from the Preserved Tablet to the nearest heaven, and it was also the night the first verses came to the Prophet ﷺ in the cave of Hira."

### Lesson for kids: Ramadan is the month of the Quran
"That's why Ramadan and the Quran go together. In Ramadan, we read more Quran, listen to more Quran, and pray Taraweeh, where the imam recites the Quran."

## Verse 2: And What Will Make You Know What the Night of Power Is?

### Explaining it to your child
"When Allah asks 'what will make you know?', it means: this is so amazing, you can't fully imagine it! Allah is getting our attention, like a teacher saying: Listen carefully, this is important!"

## Verse 3: Better Than a Thousand Months

### Explaining it to your child
"A thousand months is about eighty-three years and four months. One night of worship on Laylat al-Qadr is better than all that time! That's how generous Allah is with this night."

### Activity: the maths of mercy
Ask your child: "How many months are in a year?" Twelve. "So how many years is a thousand months?" Help them work it out: about 83 years. Then ask: "How old is Grandma or Grandpa?" Compare. "One night can be worth more than a whole lifetime!"

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever stands in prayer on Laylat al-Qadr out of faith and hoping for reward, his past sins will be forgiven" (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

## Verse 4: The Angels and the Spirit Descend

### Explaining it to your child
"On this night, the angels come down to the earth in huge numbers, along with ar-Ruh, which is Jibril, by Allah's permission, with every matter Allah has decreed. Scholars mention that the angels on earth that night are more than the pebbles on the ground!"

### Activity: imagine the angels
Ask your child to close their eyes and imagine the sky full of light and angels coming down to every masjid and every home where people are praying. "On Laylat al-Qadr, angels may be right next to you as you make du'a!"

## Verse 5: Peace Until the Break of Dawn

### Explaining it to your child
"Salam means peace. The whole night is peaceful and full of goodness, from sunset until Fajr. It's a night of calm, safety and mercy."

## When Is Laylat al-Qadr?

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Look for Laylat al-Qadr in the odd nights of the last ten nights of Ramadan" (al-Bukhari). The odd nights are the 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th and 29th. Allah kept the exact night hidden, so we try hard on all of them.

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah hid the night like a treasure, so we keep searching every night of the last ten. The more nights we try, the more sure we are to find it."

### Signs mentioned in hadiths
The Prophet ﷺ mentioned that on the morning after Laylat al-Qadr, the sun rises without strong rays (Muslim). Some other signs are mentioned in hadiths with weaker chains, like the night being calm and mild.

## The Special Du'a

'A'ishah asked the Prophet ﷺ: "If I know which night is Laylat al-Qadr, what should I say?" He said: "Say: Allahumma innaka 'afuwwun tuhibbul-'afwa fa'fu 'anni", "O Allah, You are Pardoning and You love to pardon, so pardon me" (at-Tirmidhi, who graded it hasan sahih).

### Activity: the du'a card
Write this du'a on a beautiful card with your child and decorate it with stars and a crescent. Keep it by their bed during the last ten nights, and say it together every night.

## How Children Can Join the Last Ten Nights

Children don't need to stay up all night, and they shouldn't be exhausted. Here are realistic ideas:

1. **A short night prayer**: two rak'ahs after Isha or before sleeping.
2. **A "Quran minute"**: read or listen to a short surah, like Al-Qadr itself.
3. **The 'afw du'a**: say "Allahumma innaka 'afuwwun..." several times.
4. **A small charity**: put a coin in a charity jar each night, so if it's Laylat al-Qadr, it's like giving every night for 83 years!
5. **Dhikr**: say SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, La ilaha illallah and Allahu Akbar ten times each.
6. **A special night outing**: if suitable, take older children to the masjid for part of the night prayer.

### Activity: the last-ten-nights chart
Make a chart with ten boxes, one for each night. Each night, your child colours a box after doing their small acts: prayer, du'a, charity. Odd nights get a gold star!

### From the Sunnah
'A'ishah said that when the last ten nights began, the Prophet ﷺ would stay up at night, wake his family, and strive hard in worship (al-Bukhari and Muslim). Notice: he woke his family, so including children is a sunnah.

## The Night the Quran Began

On Laylat al-Qadr, the Prophet ﷺ was in the cave of Hira on the Mountain of Light, near Makkah, where he used to go to think and worship. Jibril came to him and said: "Iqra'", "Read!" The Prophet ﷺ said: "I am not one who reads." This happened three times, and then Jibril recited the first verses of Surah Al-'Alaq: "Read in the name of your Lord who created." The full story is in Sahih al-Bukhari.

### Explaining it to your child
"The very first word of the Quran that came down was 'Iqra', Read! So on this special night, the Quran began with a command to read and learn. That's why Muslims love reading and learning, and why Laylat al-Qadr is a perfect night to read some Quran."

### Activity: Iqra' night
On one of the last ten nights, sit with your child and read Surah Al-'Alaq's first five verses and Surah Al-Qadr together. Tell them: "We are reading the first verses ever revealed, on the same special night many centuries later."

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Treasure Hunt"

On the first night of the last ten nights of Ramadan, Maryam's father gathered the children. "We're going on a treasure hunt," he said. The children cheered. "But it's not a normal treasure. It's a night, and it's hidden somewhere in the next ten nights. Whoever finds it gets a reward bigger than eighty-three years!"

"How do we find it?" asked little Ali. Their father explained: "We don't know which night it is, so we look every night. And the way to look is with small acts of worship." He showed them a chart with ten boxes and a charity jar.

Every night, after Isha, the children prayed two rak'ahs, put a coin in the jar, said the du'a "Allahumma innaka 'afuwwun tuhibbul-'afwa fa'fu 'anni" three times, and coloured a box. On the odd nights, their father let them stay up a bit later to listen to the Taraweeh in the masjid.

On the morning after the 27th night, Maryam looked at the sunrise and said: "Dad, the sun looks soft, without strong rays!" Her father smiled: "Maybe last night was the treasure. But we keep looking until the end, just in case."

On Eid, the charity jar was full. The children took it to the masjid together. "We found the treasure, in sha' Allah," said Maryam, "because we looked every night."

Ask your child: "What was the hidden treasure? How did Maryam's family look for it every night?"

## What We Learn From Surah Al-Qadr

- **The Quran was sent down** on Laylat al-Qadr.
- **One night** is better than a thousand months.
- **The angels and Jibril** come down on that night.
- **The night is full of peace** until Fajr.
- **Look for it** in the odd nights of the last ten of Ramadan.
- **Say the 'afw du'a** that the Prophet ﷺ taught.
- **Small, regular acts** each night are the best plan for children.
- **Families worship together** in the last ten nights.

- **Day 6**: prepare the last-ten-nights chart and charity jar.
- **Day 7**: recite Al-Qadr in prayer and celebrate!

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Is Laylat al-Qadr always on the 27th?
Many hope it is the 27th, and some reports point to it, but the Prophet ﷺ told us to look for it in all the odd nights of the last ten, so we try on every one of them.

### Should young children stay up late?
Not necessarily. Short, meaningful acts before bedtime are better than an exhausted child. Older children can stay up a little longer on some nights if they're able.

### Can children's worship count on Laylat al-Qadr?
Yes. Children are rewarded for their good deeds, and parents are rewarded for teaching them. A child's two rak'ahs and du'a on this night are precious.
