Surah Al-'Alaq has an honour no other surah shares: its first five verses were the very first words of the Quran revealed to the Prophet ﷺ, in the cave of Hira. The first word of revelation was "Read". Knowledge and reading were the key to this message from its first moment. The surah then moves to man's arrogance when he sees himself as self-sufficient, shows a man who tried to stop a servant of Allah from praying, and ends with a command: "Do not obey him. Prostrate and draw near."

This guide explains the surah verse by verse in English, with the account of the first revelation as reported in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-'Alaq
- **Number of verses**: 19.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 96, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Order of revelation**: its opening verses were the first revelation.
- **Names**: Al-'Alaq, Iqra', and *Iqra' bismi rabbik*.
- **Prostration of recitation**: at the final verse.

## The Story of the First Revelation
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah described how revelation began:
1. **True dreams**: the first sign of revelation was true dreams in sleep; every dream came true like the break of dawn.
2. **Love of seclusion**: then he came to love being alone, and would retreat to the cave of Hira to worship for many nights.
3. **The angel came**: the truth came to him while he was in the cave. The angel said: "Read." He said: "I am not a reader." He said: "He took hold of me and pressed me until I was exhausted, then released me and said: Read. I said: I am not a reader." This happened three times, then the angel said: "Read in the name of your Lord who created, created man from a clinging clot. Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous."
4. **Return to Khadijah**: he returned with his heart trembling, came to Khadijah and said: "Cover me, cover me," until his fear left him. He said: "I feared for myself."
5. **Khadijah's support**: she said: "Never! By Allah, Allah will never disgrace you. You keep ties of kinship, you bear the burden of the weak, you give to the destitute, you honour guests, and you help those afflicted by calamities."
6. **Waraqah ibn Nawfal**: she took him to her cousin Waraqah, who had become a Christian before Islam and knew the scriptures. When the Prophet ﷺ told him what he had seen, Waraqah said: "This is the Namus (Jibril) whom Allah sent down to Musa."

**Lessons**: prophethood began with reading; a righteous spouse is a great support; his noble character before prophethood showed Allah's care for him; and carrying a message is heavy and needs people who strengthen and help.

## Verses 1–2: Read in the Name of Your Lord
﴿ٱقۡرَأۡ بِٱسۡمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ﴾ ﴿خَلَقَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ مِنۡ عَلَقٍ﴾ (Quran 96:1-2)

**"Read in the name of your Lord"**: read seeking help through His name, beginning with it. Reading in Islam is not just gathering information; it is reading connected to Allah, beginning with His name and aiming to know Him. **"Who created"**: the first attribute mentioned is creation, the clearest evidence of the Creator. Then man is singled out: **"created man from a clinging clot"** (*'alaq*), the stage when the embryo clings to the wall of the womb.

**A subtle point**: mentioning man's humble origin in the very first revelation reminds him of his true size, so he does not become arrogant, which is exactly what the surah warns against shortly afterwards.

## Verses 3–5: Your Lord Is the Most Generous
﴿ٱقۡرَأۡ وَرَبُّكَ ٱلۡأَكۡرَمُ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي عَلَّمَ بِٱلۡقَلَمِ﴾ ﴿عَلَّمَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمۡ يَعۡلَمۡ﴾ (Quran 96:3-5)

**"Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous"**: the command is repeated for emphasis. *Al-Akram*, the Most Generous, whose generosity no one can match, and among His greatest generosity is that He taught man. **"Who taught by the pen"**: the pen preserves and transmits knowledge across generations. **"Taught man what he did not know"**: He brought him out of his mother's womb knowing nothing, then taught him hearing, sight, language and knowledge.

**Reflect**: in the first five verses revealed, reading is mentioned twice, teaching twice, and the pen once. A religion that began like this cannot be a religion of ignorance.

## Verses 6–8: Man Transgresses
﴿كَلَّآ إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لَيَطۡغَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَن رَّءَاهُ ٱسۡتَغۡنَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلرُّجۡعَىٰٓ﴾ (Quran 96:6-8)

**"No!"** (*kalla*): a firm rebuke. **"Man transgresses when he sees himself self-sufficient"**: he oversteps bounds and grows arrogant when he believes he needs no one. Note the wording: "when he *sees himself* self-sufficient", not "when he becomes self-sufficient". Man is never truly independent of his Lord; he only imagines it. **"Indeed, to your Lord is the return"**: whatever his wealth, he will return to Allah and be held to account.

## Verses 9–14: The One Who Forbids a Servant From Praying
﴿أَرَءَيۡتَ ٱلَّذِي يَنۡهَىٰ﴾ ﴿عَبۡدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَرَءَيۡتَ إِن كَانَ عَلَى ٱلۡهُدَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَوۡ أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقۡوَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَرَءَيۡتَ إِن كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَلَمۡ يَعۡلَم بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ﴾ (Quran 96:9-14)

**"Have you seen the one who forbids a servant when he prays?"**: an expression of amazement at Abu Jahl, who tried to stop the Prophet ﷺ from praying at the Ka'bah. Calling the Prophet ﷺ "a servant" honours him: servitude to Allah is the highest rank. **"Have you seen if he is upon guidance or enjoins righteousness?"** And "have you seen if the one who forbids denies and turns away?" **"Does he not know that Allah sees?"**

"Does he not know that Allah sees?" is one of the greatest verses on awareness of Allah. If a person kept it in mind at the moment of every sin, he would stop.

## Verses 15–18: The Forelock
﴿كَلَّا لَئِن لَّمۡ يَنتَهِ لَنَسۡفَعَۢا بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ﴾ ﴿نَاصِيَةٖ كَٰذِبَةٍ خَاطِئَةٖ﴾ ﴿فَلۡيَدۡعُ نَادِيَهُۥ﴾ ﴿سَنَدۡعُ ٱلزَّبَانِيَةَ﴾ (Quran 96:15-18)

**"No! If he does not stop, We will seize him by the forelock"**: drag him by the front of his head, roughly and humiliatingly. **"A lying, sinful forelock"**: the forelock, the front of the head with which a person plans his affairs, is described as lying and sinful, as if the centre of his thinking and decisions is corrupt. **"Let him call his council"**: his associates and people to help him. **"We will call the Zabaniyah"**: the stern angels of punishment.

### The authentic report
In Sahih Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that Abu Jahl asked: "Does Muhammad put his face in the dust among you?" They said yes. He said: "By al-Lat and al-'Uzza, if I see him doing that, I will step on his neck." He came while the Prophet ﷺ was praying, intending to step on his neck, but suddenly retreated, shielding himself with his hands. Asked what happened, he said: "Between me and him is a trench of fire, terror and wings." The Prophet ﷺ said: "Had he come near me, the angels would have snatched him limb by limb." Then Allah revealed "No! Man transgresses..." to the end of the surah.

## Verse 19: Prostrate and Draw Near
﴿كَلَّا لَا تُطِعۡهُ وَٱسۡجُدۡۤ وَٱقۡتَرِب۩﴾ (Quran 96:19)

**"No! Do not obey him"**: continue your prayer. **"Prostrate and draw near"**: prostrate to your Lord and draw closer through obedience. In Sahih Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so make abundant supplication."

**The prostration of recitation**: in Sahih Muslim, Abu Hurayrah said: "We prostrated with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in *Idha as-sama'u-nshaqqat* and *Iqra' bismi rabbik*."

**Reflect on the ending**: the tyrant wants to stop the servant from prostrating, and the divine command is: prostrate more, draw nearer. The answer to tyranny is not retreat but steadfastness and closeness to Allah.

## Word Meanings
- **Iqra'**: read, recite.
- **'Alaq**: a clinging clot.
- **Al-Akram**: the Most Generous.
- **Bil-qalam**: by the pen.
- **Yatgha**: transgresses, exceeds bounds.
- **Istaghna**: considers himself self-sufficient.
- **Ar-Ruj'a**: the return.
- **Nasfa'an**: We will seize and drag.
- **An-Nasiyah**: the forelock.
- **Nadiyah**: his council, his associates.
- **Az-Zabaniyah**: the angels of punishment.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **The first word is a command, "Read"**, announcing the nature of the message.
2. **Beginning and end**: "Read" at the start and "Prostrate" at the end: knowledge, then worship.
3. **"Have you seen" three times**, building suspense and amazement.
4. **"A lying, sinful forelock"**: attributing the lie to the forelock, meaning its owner.
5. **"Let him call his council... We will call the Zabaniyah"**: weak human allies against Allah's mighty soldiers.
6. **"Kalla" three times**, each rejecting a different wrong: transgression, threats, and obeying the tyrant.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Izhar** in *min 'alaq*: nun clear before 'ayn.
- **Qalqalah kubra** when stopping on *khalaq* and *'alaq* (qaf).
- **Lam shamsiyyah** in *alladhi* and *an-nasiyah*; **lam qamariyyah** in *al-insan*, *al-akram*, *al-qalam* and *al-huda*.
- **Ghunnah** on the doubled nun in *inna*.
- **Ikhfa** in *la-nasfa'an* (nun before sin) and *nasiyatin kadhibah* (tanween before kaf).
- **Stopping**: on *khalaq* the qaf is stopped with qalqalah; on *az-zabaniyah* the ta marbutah becomes a sakin *ha*.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before Al-'Alaq comes At-Tin: "We created man in the best form". Al-'Alaq says "created man from a clinging clot": together they combine man's humble origin with his honour. At-Tin warns of falling to the lowest of the low; Al-'Alaq shows the cause: transgression and imagined self-sufficiency. After it comes Al-Qadr, about the night the Quran was sent down: after the first verses revealed, the night of revelation.

## Knowledge in Islam Through Surah Al-'Alaq
The first word of revelation being "Read" means knowledge is the foundation of this religion:
- **Allah raises people of knowledge**: "Allah raises those who believe among you and those given knowledge by degrees" (58:11).
- **The only thing the Prophet ﷺ was told to ask more of**: "Say: My Lord, increase me in knowledge" (20:114).
- **Learning and teaching the Quran.** In Sahih al-Bukhari, 'Uthman reported: "The best of you are those who learn the Quran and teach it."
- **A path to Paradise.** In Sahih Muslim: "Whoever takes a path seeking knowledge, Allah makes easy for him a path to Paradise."

But the surah sets a condition for beneficial knowledge: "in the name of your Lord". Knowledge cut off from Allah can lead to arrogance; just as the rich man transgresses with his wealth, the learned man can transgress with his knowledge if he does not read in his Lord's name.

## The Illusion of Self-Sufficiency
Many people today live with the illusion of not needing Allah. They have wealth, technology and knowledge, and assume they need nothing more. The surah exposes the illusion in three ways: your origin is a clinging clot; your knowledge was taught by Allah; and your return is to Him, where every fortune ends. The cure is to remember your constant need of Allah, and to prostrate and draw near.

## Practical Lessons
1. Make reading a daily habit, especially the Quran and beneficial knowledge.
2. Begin your learning in Allah's name, intending to grow closer to Him.
3. Beware the illusion of self-sufficiency.
4. Remember that Allah sees you.
5. Do not obey anyone who forbids you from obedience to Allah.
6. Prostrate often and make du'a in prostration.
7. Support those carrying a heavy burden, as Khadijah supported the Prophet ﷺ.

## Teaching Children
- Tell the story of the cave of Hira simply, and that the first word was "Read".
- Teach them to say *bismillah* before studying.
- Do the prostration of recitation together at the end of the surah.
- Plant awareness of Allah with "Does he not know that Allah sees?"

## Frequently Asked Questions

The first five verses of Surah Al-'Alaq, as in 'A'ishah's account in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### Where was the first revelation?
In the cave of Hira near Mecca, where the Prophet ﷺ used to worship in seclusion.

### What does "created man from 'alaq" mean?
From a clinging clot, the stage when the embryo clings to the wall of the womb.

### Who was "the one who forbids a servant when he prays"?
Abu Jahl, who tried to stop the Prophet ﷺ from praying at the Ka'bah, as in Sahih Muslim.

### Who are the Zabaniyah?
The stern angels of punishment.

### Is there a prostration in Surah Al-'Alaq?
Yes, at the final verse, and the Prophet ﷺ prostrated there, as in Sahih Muslim.

19 verses.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-'Alaq began the message with "Read" and closed it with "draw near". Between those two words is a complete path: learn in the name of Allah, know your true size so you do not transgress, remember that Allah sees you, and stay firm in prostration whoever tries to stop you. Read in the name of your Lord, and prostrate and draw near.
