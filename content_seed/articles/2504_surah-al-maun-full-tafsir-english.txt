Surah Al-Ma'un is seven verses that expose false religiosity. It begins with a person who denies the Day of Recompense, and shows how that denial appears in his behaviour: he pushes the orphan away and does not care whether the poor are fed. Then it turns to people who pray, but whose prayer is heedless, performed for show, and who will not even lend a neighbour a small household item.

The surah ties belief, prayer and character into a single thread. Religion is not a set of rituals cut off from mercy towards people. This guide explains it verse by verse in English and draws out a measure for the sincerity of our own faith.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Ma'un
- **Number of verses**: 7.
- **Type**: Meccan in the standard mushaf; commentators differ, and some say its first part is Meccan and the rest Medinan.
- **Position**: Surah 107, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Names**: Al-Ma'un, Ara'ayta, and Ad-Din.

## The Full Text of Surah Al-Ma'un
﴿أَرَءَيۡتَ ٱلَّذِي يُكَذِّبُ بِٱلدِّينِ﴾ ﴿فَذَٰلِكَ ٱلَّذِي يَدُعُّ ٱلۡيَتِيمَ﴾ ﴿وَلَا يَحُضُّ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ ٱلۡمِسۡكِينِ﴾ ﴿فَوَيۡلࣱ لِّلۡمُصَلِّينَ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ هُمۡ عَن صَلَاتِهِمۡ سَاهُونَ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ هُمۡ يُرَآءُونَ﴾ ﴿وَيَمۡنَعُونَ ٱلۡمَاعُونَ﴾ (Quran 107:1-7)

**Meaning:** Have you seen the one who denies the Recompense? That is the one who drives away the orphan and does not encourage the feeding of the poor. So woe to those who pray, who are heedless of their prayer, those who make a show, and withhold small kindnesses.

## Verse 1: The One Who Denies the Recompense
﴿أَرَءَيۡتَ ٱلَّذِي يُكَذِّبُ بِٱلدِّينِ﴾ (Quran 107:1)

**"Have you seen"**: a question to arouse wonder and attention. **"The one who denies ad-din"**: *ad-din* here means the Recompense and the Reckoning on the Day of Judgement. Someone who does not believe in a Day of accounting sees no reason to be kind to people who can give him nothing in return.

**Why start with denial?** Because denial of the Hereafter is the root, and what follows are its fruits: harshness to the orphan and indifference to the poor.

## Verses 2–3: Repelling the Orphan, Ignoring the Poor
﴿فَذَٰلِكَ ٱلَّذِي يَدُعُّ ٱلۡيَتِيمَ﴾ ﴿وَلَا يَحُضُّ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ ٱلۡمِسۡكِينِ﴾ (Quran 107:2-3)

**"Drives away the orphan"** (*yadu''u*): pushes him away roughly, denies him his rights, and turns him away when he comes asking. The same word appears in Surah At-Tur for those who are shoved into Hell.

**"Does not encourage the feeding of the poor"**: notice it does not say "does not feed" but "does not encourage". He neither feeds the poor himself nor urges anyone else to do so. He is as far from good as possible.

**The striking link**: Allah makes harshness to orphans and stinginess towards the poor a sign of denying the Recompense. Whoever truly believes in the Reckoning will be merciful to the weak.

## Verses 4–5: Woe to Those Who Pray Heedlessly
﴿فَوَيۡلࣱ لِّلۡمُصَلِّينَ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ هُمۡ عَن صَلَاتِهِمۡ سَاهُونَ﴾ (Quran 107:4-5)

**"So woe to those who pray"**: *wayl* is a severe threat of ruin, and some explain it as a valley in Hell. **"Who are heedless of their prayer"**: careless and neglectful of it.

### What is heedlessness of prayer?
The commentators describe several forms:
1. **Delaying it beyond its time** without excuse.
2. **Neglecting its pillars and humility**, praying without stillness or presence of heart.
3. **Praying sometimes and leaving it other times**, depending on mood.
4. **Not caring** whether it is prayed or not.

### "Of" their prayer, not "in" their prayer
The verse says *'an salatihim*, heedless *of* their prayer, not *fi salatihim*, forgetful *in* their prayer. Forgetfulness within prayer, such as losing count of rak'ahs, happens to everyone, even the Prophet ﷺ, and is repaired with the prostrations of forgetfulness. Heedlessness *of* prayer is carelessness about the prayer itself, and that is what is condemned.

In Sahih Muslim, Anas reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "That is the prayer of the hypocrite: he sits watching the sun until it is between the horns of Satan, then he gets up and pecks four rak'ahs, remembering Allah in them only a little."

## Verse 6: Those Who Show Off
﴿ٱلَّذِينَ هُمۡ يُرَآءُونَ﴾ (Quran 107:6)

**"Those who make a show"** (*yura'un*): they do their deeds to be seen by people, not for Allah. In front of others they pray and act well; alone, they abandon it. *Riya'*, showing off, is among the most dangerous diseases of the heart because it nullifies deeds.

In Sahih Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah, Blessed and Exalted, said: I am the One most free of need of partners. Whoever does a deed in which he associates another with Me, I leave him and his partnership."

## Verse 7: Withholding Small Kindnesses
﴿وَيَمۡنَعُونَ ٱلۡمَاعُونَ﴾ (Quran 107:7)

**"And withhold al-ma'un"**: the small, everyday items people lend each other, such as a pot, a bucket, an axe, a needle, salt or water. Abu Dawud recorded with a *hasan* chain that 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud said: "We used to count al-ma'un, in the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, as lending the bucket and the pot." Some, including 'Ali, explained it as zakat.

**The deeper point**: if refusing to lend a small item is condemned, how much worse is withholding major rights? Ending the surah with something so small shows how stinginess with little reveals a hard heart.

## Word Meanings
- **Ara'ayta**: have you seen?
- **Yukadhdhibu bid-din**: denies the Recompense.
- **Yadu''u**: drives away roughly.
- **Yahuddu**: encourages, urges.
- **Wayl**: woe, ruin.
- **Sahun**: heedless, careless.
- **Yura'un**: make a show.
- **Al-Ma'un**: small items commonly lent; also explained as zakat.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"Have you seen" at the start** creates suspense.
2. **"That is the one"**: a distant demonstrative, showing how low his position is.
3. **"So woe to those who pray"**: a rhetorical surprise. The listener expects praise for those who pray, and instead hears a threat for those with these qualities.
4. **"Of their prayer"**: the preposition *'an* carries the sense of turning away.
5. **Ending with something small**: shows the depth of their stinginess.
6. **Two halves**: one about the denier, one about the heedless worshipper, both condemned.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Lam shamsiyyah** in *alladhi* and *ad-din*; **lam qamariyyah** in *al-yatim*, *al-miskin* and *al-ma'un*.
- **Idgham without ghunnah** in *fa-waylun lil-musallin*: tanween merges into lam.
- **Izhar shafawi** in *hum 'an* and *salatihim sahun*: meem clear before 'ayn and sin; and in *yamna'un*, meem clear before nun.
- **Madd muttasil** in *yura'un*: four or five counts.
- **Madd 'arid** when stopping on *bid-din*, *al-yatim*, *al-miskin*, *sahun* and *al-ma'un*.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before Al-Ma'un comes Quraysh: "who fed them against hunger". Having reminded Quraysh that Allah fed them, Al-Ma'un condemns those who do not encourage feeding the poor. Quraysh says "let them worship the Lord of this House", and Al-Ma'un condemns those heedless of that worship. After it comes Al-Kawthar: "So pray to your Lord and sacrifice", sincere prayer and generous giving, the exact opposite of heedless, showy prayer and withheld kindness.

## Orphans in the Quran and Sunnah
The Quran repeatedly commands kindness to orphans and warns against wronging them: "As for the orphan, do not oppress him" (93:9); "No! But you do not honour the orphan" (89:17); "Those who consume the wealth of orphans unjustly only consume fire into their bellies" (4:10).

In Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahl ibn Sa'd reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "I and the one who looks after an orphan will be in Paradise like this," holding up his index and middle fingers. And in al-Bukhari and Muslim: "The one who strives for the widow and the poor is like one who strives in the path of Allah."

## Showing Off: The Hidden Shirk and Its Cure
**Signs of showing off:** energy in front of people and laziness in private; loving praise for worship; talking about one's good deeds to be admired.

**Remedies:**
1. Remember that people cannot benefit or harm you.
2. Hide some of your good deeds, such as secret charity and night prayer.
3. Ask Allah for sincerity.
4. Remember the hadith in Sahih Muslim of the scholar, the reciter and the one who spent, who did their deeds to be praised, were praised, and were among the first thrown into the Fire.
5. Do not abandon good deeds for fear of showing off. Leaving deeds for that reason is one of Satan's tricks; keep doing them and work on your intention.

## Al-Ma'un in Modern Life
In its wider sense, *al-ma'un* includes every small benefit people need from you:
- Lending household tools to neighbours.
- Sharing a charger or wi-fi.
- Giving directions to someone lost.
- Offering water to a worker or passer-by.
- Helping a colleague with information or experience.
Refusing such simple things reveals a hard heart; giving them gladly earns great reward at little cost.

## Humble Prayer: The Opposite of Heedlessness
The Quran praises those who pray with humility: "Successful indeed are the believers, those who are humble in their prayer" (23:1–2). Practical ways to build humility and avoid heedlessness:
1. Prepare before prayer with careful wudu and arriving early.
2. Understand what you recite, especially Al-Fatihah and short surahs.
3. Be still in every position. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ told the man who prayed badly: "Go back and pray, for you have not prayed," and taught him stillness in every pillar.
4. Look at the place of prostration.
5. Pray as though it were your last prayer.
6. Remove distractions, including your phone.

## Practical Lessons
1. Pray on time; do not delay it out of carelessness.
2. Make your deeds sincerely for Allah.
3. Care for orphans: sponsor, visit, and speak kindly.
4. Encourage feeding the poor, with your money or your words.
5. Do not withhold small kindnesses.
6. Let your worship show in your character.

## Teaching Children
- Teach them that real prayer makes us kind to the weak.
- Encourage them to share toys and tools.
- Sponsor or donate to an orphan together.

## Lending in Islam: The Etiquette of al-Ma'un
In its original sense, *al-ma'un* is what is lent, and lending is a recommended act of kindness and cooperation. Its etiquette: lend readily to those who need it, without reminding them or complaining; the borrower guards the item as a trust and returns it on time and in the condition he took it; and the borrower shows thanks. Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi recorded, with at-Tirmidhi grading it *hasan sahih*, that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever does you a favour, repay him; and if you cannot find anything to repay him with, pray for him until you feel you have repaid him."

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does "denies the Recompense" mean?
Denying the reckoning and reward of the Day of Judgement.

### Who are "those heedless of their prayer"?
Those careless about prayer, who delay it beyond its time, neglect its pillars and humility, or do not care whether they pray.

### What is the difference between forgetting in prayer and heedlessness of prayer?
Forgetting in prayer happens to everyone and is repaired with the prostrations of forgetfulness; heedlessness of prayer is careless neglect of it, which is condemned.

### What is al-ma'un?
Small items people commonly lend, like a bucket or pot; some explained it as zakat.

### What does "those who make a show" mean?
People who do good deeds to be seen and praised by others.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Ma'un?
7 verses.

### Is it enough to pray if I mistreat people?
Correct, humble prayer prevents indecency and wrongdoing (29:45). If prayer does not improve your character, review your prayer and your sincerity.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Ma'un tells you that your religion is not measured only by the number of your rak'ahs, but by your sincerity in them and their effect on how you treat people. When you see an orphan, honour him. When you know of someone hungry, feed him or urge others to. When you pray, be sincere and present. And when your neighbour asks for something small, do not refuse. True religion is humble prayer, a merciful hand and a sincere heart.
