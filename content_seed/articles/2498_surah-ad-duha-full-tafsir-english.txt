Surah Ad-Duha was revealed at a moment of sadness. Revelation had paused, the Prophet ﷺ was unwell, and an enemy mocked him, claiming his Lord had left him. Then these eleven verses came down like morning light after a dark night: your Lord has not abandoned you, and He is not displeased. What is coming is better than what has passed. Your Lord will give you until you are content.

Many Muslims turn to this surah when they feel low, and with good reason. This guide explains it verse by verse in English and draws out its lessons for anyone going through a hard time.

## Quick Facts About Surah Ad-Duha
- **Number of verses**: 11.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 93, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: from *ad-duha*, the bright morning hours, in its first verse.
- **Central theme**: comfort and reassurance after a period of difficulty, and gratitude expressed through kindness.

## The Full Text of Surah Ad-Duha
﴿وَٱلضُّحَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَٱلَّيۡلِ إِذَا سَجَىٰ﴾ ﴿مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ وَمَا قَلَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَلَلۡأٓخِرَةُ خَيۡرࣱ لَّكَ مِنَ ٱلۡأُولَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَلَسَوۡفَ يُعۡطِيكَ رَبُّكَ فَتَرۡضَىٰٓ﴾ ﴿أَلَمۡ يَجِدۡكَ يَتِيمٗا فَـَٔاوَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَوَجَدَكَ ضَآلّٗا فَهَدَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَوَجَدَكَ عَآئِلٗا فَأَغۡنَىٰ﴾ ﴿فَأَمَّا ٱلۡيَتِيمَ فَلَا تَقۡهَرۡ﴾ ﴿وَأَمَّا ٱلسَّآئِلَ فَلَا تَنۡهَرۡ﴾ ﴿وَأَمَّا بِنِعۡمَةِ رَبِّكَ فَحَدِّثۡ﴾ (Quran 93:1-11)

**Meaning:** By the morning brightness, and the night when it grows still, your Lord has not abandoned you, nor has He become displeased. And the Hereafter is better for you than the first life. And your Lord will give you, and you will be satisfied. Did He not find you an orphan and give you shelter? And He found you lost and guided you, and found you in need and made you self-sufficient. So as for the orphan, do not oppress him; and as for the one who asks, do not repel him; and as for the favour of your Lord, speak of it.

## The Story of Its Revelation
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Jundub ibn Sufyan reported that the Prophet ﷺ became ill and did not get up for one or two nights. A woman came and said: "O Muhammad, I think your devil has left you; I have not seen him come near you for two or three nights." Then Allah revealed: "By the morning brightness, and the night when it grows still, your Lord has not abandoned you, nor has He become displeased."

The pause in revelation was painful for the Prophet ﷺ, and the mockery made it worse. The answer came from Allah Himself, swearing by the light of day.

## Verses 1–2: By the Morning and the Night
﴿وَٱلضُّحَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَٱلَّيۡلِ إِذَا سَجَىٰ﴾ (Quran 93:1-2)

Allah swears by *ad-duha*, the bright part of the morning when the sun is up, and by the night when it becomes still and its darkness settles.

**Why these two oaths?** Because they mirror what the Prophet ﷺ was going through. The pause in revelation was like a night; its return is like the morning. And just as night follows day and day follows night as part of a wise order, periods of hardship and ease in a believer's life are part of Allah's plan, not signs of abandonment.

Notice the word chosen for night: *saja*, still and calm, not frightening. Even the night in this surah is gentle.

## Verse 3: Your Lord Has Not Abandoned You
﴿مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ وَمَا قَلَىٰ﴾ (Quran 93:3)

This is the answer to the oath. *Ma wadda'aka*: He has not bid you farewell or left you. *Wa ma qala*: nor is He displeased with you or does He hate you.

Notice also that it says "your Lord" (*rabbuka*), a name that carries care and nurturing. The One who has raised and looked after you all your life has not walked away from you now.

Every believer who feels that Allah is distant in a hard time can take this verse personally. Silence is not abandonment. Delay is not rejection.

## Verse 4: What Comes After Is Better
﴿وَلَلۡأٓخِرَةُ خَيۡرࣱ لَّكَ مِنَ ٱلۡأُولَىٰ﴾ (Quran 93:4)

*Al-akhirah* here can mean the Hereafter, which is better than this life, or, as some scholars note, the later part of his life and mission, which would be better than the earlier part. Both came true. The Prophet's mission grew from a persecuted few in Mecca to the conquest of Mecca and people entering Islam in crowds, and the Hereafter holds for him what no eye has seen.

## Verse 5: You Will Be Satisfied
﴿وَلَسَوۡفَ يُعۡطِيكَ رَبُّكَ فَتَرۡضَىٰٓ﴾ (Quran 93:5)

A promise from Allah: He will keep giving until the Prophet ﷺ is content. In Sahih Muslim, 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr reported that the Prophet ﷺ recited the words of Ibrahim and 'Isa about their people, raised his hands and said: "O Allah, my Ummah, my Ummah," and wept. Allah sent Jibril to ask him why he wept, and then said: "O Jibril, go to Muhammad and say: We will please you concerning your Ummah and will not grieve you."

His satisfaction is bound up with the salvation of his Ummah. That is a source of great hope for every Muslim.

## Verses 6–8: Remembering Past Blessings
﴿أَلَمۡ يَجِدۡكَ يَتِيمٗا فَـَٔاوَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَوَجَدَكَ ضَآلّٗا فَهَدَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَوَجَدَكَ عَآئِلٗا فَأَغۡنَىٰ﴾ (Quran 93:6-8)

Allah reminds the Prophet ﷺ of three stages in his life:
1. **"Did He not find you an orphan and shelter you?"** His father died before he was born, and his mother when he was six. Allah sheltered him through his grandfather 'Abd al-Muttalib and then his uncle Abu Talib.
2. **"He found you unaware and guided you."** *Dallan* here does not mean astray in sin; it means without knowledge of the revelation and the details of the law before prophethood, as Surah Ash-Shura says: "You did not know what the Book was, nor faith."
3. **"He found you in need and made you self-sufficient."** He was poor, and Allah enriched him, partly through his trade and his marriage to Khadijah, and above all with contentment.

**The lesson**: when you are going through hardship, look back at how Allah carried you through earlier ones. The One who sheltered you then will not abandon you now.

## Verses 9–11: Gratitude in Action
﴿فَأَمَّا ٱلۡيَتِيمَ فَلَا تَقۡهَرۡ﴾ ﴿وَأَمَّا ٱلسَّآئِلَ فَلَا تَنۡهَرۡ﴾ ﴿وَأَمَّا بِنِعۡمَةِ رَبِّكَ فَحَدِّثۡ﴾ (Quran 93:9-11)

Three commands correspond to the three blessings:
1. **"As for the orphan, do not oppress him."** You were an orphan and were sheltered, so shelter orphans. In Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahl ibn Sa'd reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "I and the one who looks after an orphan will be in Paradise like this," and he held up his index and middle fingers.
2. **"As for the one who asks, do not repel him."** You were in need, so do not turn away those in need, whether they ask for money or for knowledge. If you cannot give, decline kindly.
3. **"As for the favour of your Lord, speak of it."** You were guided, so speak of Allah's blessings with gratitude, especially the blessing of guidance, by teaching and sharing it.

Gratitude in this surah is not only a feeling. It is a way of treating people.

## Word Meanings
- **Ad-Duha**: the bright morning hours.
- **Saja**: became still and calm.
- **Wadda'aka**: left you, bid you farewell.
- **Qala**: hated, became displeased.
- **Yatim**: orphan.
- **Fa-awa**: gave shelter.
- **Dallan**: unaware, without guidance yet.
- **'A'ilan**: in need.
- **Fa-aghna**: made self-sufficient.
- **Taqhar**: oppress, treat harshly.
- **Tanhar**: repel, scold.
- **Fa-haddith**: speak of it.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **The oaths match the situation**: light after night, revelation after a pause.
2. **"Abandoned you" is said, but "hated you" is said without "you"** in the Arabic (*wa ma qala*), out of courtesy: Allah does not even attach the word "hate" to His Prophet.
3. **Three blessings answered by three commands**, in matching order.
4. **The surah begins with light and ends with speaking of blessings**: the mood moves from comfort to gratitude.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Lam shamsiyyah** in *ad-duha*, *as-sa'il*; **lam qamariyyah** in *al-yatim* and *al-ula*.
- **Madd 'arid** in the rhyming endings *-a*: *ad-duha*, *saja*, *qala*, *al-ula*, *fa-tarda*.
- **Ikhfa** of tanween before *fa* in *yatiman fa-awa*, *dallan fa-hada* and *'a'ilan fa-aghna*: the nasal sound is hidden with a light ghunnah.
- **Madd muttasil** in *'a'ilan*: the alif is followed by a hamza in the same word, four or five counts.
- **Izhar** in *fa-la tanhar*: the nun sakinah is pronounced clearly before the throat letter *ha*.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before Ad-Duha comes Al-Layl, which ends with the promise to the most devout believer: "And he will be satisfied." Ad-Duha continues: "Your Lord will give you, and you will be satisfied." After it comes Ash-Sharh, which continues the comfort: "Did We not expand your chest... indeed, with hardship comes ease."

## Surah Ad-Duha for Hard Times
Many people turn to this surah when they feel anxious or low. Its message for them:
1. **A pause is not abandonment.** If you feel Allah is far, He is not.
2. **What is coming is better.** Trust that later can be better than earlier.
3. **Remember past rescues.** List the times Allah carried you through.
4. **Turn sadness into service.** Help an orphan, give to someone in need, share a blessing.
5. **Talk about blessings, not only problems.**
A note of care: if low mood is persistent or severe, speaking to a trusted person or a professional is also a legitimate means, alongside prayer and the Quran.

## The Duha Prayer
The name of the surah reminds us of the Duha prayer, offered after sunrise until shortly before noon. In Sahih Muslim, Abu Dharr reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that every joint of a person owes a charity each day, and "two rak'ahs prayed in the forenoon suffice for that". In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah said: "My close friend ﷺ advised me to do three things: fast three days of every month, pray the two rak'ahs of Duha, and pray witr before sleeping."

## Practical Lessons
1. Never assume Allah has abandoned you.
2. Trust that what comes later can be better.
3. Remember the blessings of your past.
4. Care for orphans.
5. Never scold someone who asks.
6. Speak of Allah's blessings, especially guidance.
7. Pray the Duha prayer.

## Teaching Children
- Watch the morning light together and read the first verse.
- Teach them that when they feel sad, Allah has not left them.
- Encourage them to give something to an orphan charity.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Why was Surah Ad-Duha revealed?
After a pause in revelation, when a woman mocked the Prophet ﷺ by saying his devil had left him, as reported in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### What does "your Lord has not abandoned you" mean?
Allah has not left the Prophet ﷺ nor become displeased with him; the pause was not rejection.

### What does "He found you dallan and guided you" mean?
Before revelation he did not know the details of the Book and the law, and Allah guided him through revelation. It does not mean he was astray in sin.

### Is Surah Ad-Duha good for depression and sadness?
Its message of comfort and hope is a strong support for anyone who is sad, alongside other means such as speaking to trusted people or professionals when needed.

### What are the three commands at the end?
Do not oppress the orphan, do not repel the one who asks, and speak of your Lord's blessings.

### How many verses are in Surah Ad-Duha?
11 verses.

## Conclusion
Surah Ad-Duha is Allah's reassurance to His Prophet ﷺ, and through him to every believer in a dark season: your Lord has not left you. Morning follows night, what comes is better than what has passed, and your Lord will give you until you are satisfied. Remember how He sheltered, guided and provided for you before, and answer His kindness by being kind: to the orphan, to the one who asks, and in speaking of His favour.
