In many parts of the Muslim world, when a reciter finishes, he says **"Sadaqallahul 'Azim"** ("Allah the Almighty has spoken the truth"). Children learn to say it at the end of every Quran class, and it is heard at the end of recitations on radio and television. But some people say it is an innovation, and others reply that it is simply affirming Allah's truthfulness, so what could be wrong with it?

This article explains the question calmly: where the phrase comes from, what the scholars have said, what the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions are reported to have said after reciting, and how to end your recitation in a way all scholars agree on.

## The Phrase in the Quran Itself
The words "Allah has spoken the truth" appear in the Quran:

﴿قُلۡ صَدَقَ ٱللَّهُۗ فَٱتَّبِعُواْ مِلَّةَ إِبۡرَٰهِيمَ حَنِيفٗاۖ وَمَا كَانَ مِنَ ٱلۡمُشۡرِكِينَ﴾ (Quran 3:95)

﴿وَلَمَّا رَءَا ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنُونَ ٱلۡأَحۡزَابَ قَالُواْ هَٰذَا مَا وَعَدَنَا ٱللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُۥ وَصَدَقَ ٱللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُۥۚ وَمَا زَادَهُمۡ إِلَّآ إِيمَٰنٗا وَتَسۡلِيمٗا﴾ (Quran 33:22)

Affirming that Allah's words are true is at the core of faith, and no Muslim disputes that Allah is truthful in everything He says. Saying "Sadaqallah" in itself is a beautiful form of praise. So the disagreement is **not about the meaning of the phrase**. It is about **making it a fixed habit after every recitation, as though it were part of the etiquette of reciting the Quran**.

## Did the Prophet ﷺ Say It After Reciting?
It is not authentically reported that the Prophet ﷺ or his Companions ended their recitation by saying "Sadaqallahul 'Azim". On the contrary, in al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ asked 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud to recite to him. Ibn Mas'ud recited from Surah An-Nisa' until he reached the verse:

﴿فَكَيۡفَ إِذَا جِئۡنَا مِن كُلِّ أُمَّةِۭ بِشَهِيدٖ وَجِئۡنَا بِكَ عَلَىٰ هَـٰٓؤُلَآءِ شَهِيدٗا﴾ (Quran 4:41)

The Prophet ﷺ said: **"That is enough for now."** Ibn Mas'ud looked and saw that his eyes were shedding tears. Neither of them is reported to have said "Sadaqallahul 'Azim" at that moment, although it was a moment of deep emotion where one might expect it.

The Prophet ﷺ and his Companions recited the Quran constantly, in prayer and outside it, and if they had made a habit of ending with this phrase, it would very likely have been transmitted.

## The Scholars' Views
### View one: making it a fixed habit is disliked or an innovation
A number of contemporary scholars, including the Permanent Committee for Fatwa in Saudi Arabia, hold that **making a fixed habit** of saying "Sadaqallahul 'Azim" after every recitation, as though it were a Sunnah or part of the etiquette of reciting, is an **innovation**, because it was not reported from the Prophet ﷺ or the Companions despite how much they recited, and acts of worship are based on what has been transmitted. They add that there is no problem in saying it occasionally, without making it a fixed practice.

### View two: it is permitted and good
Other scholars, including many scholars of al-Azhar and various fatwa institutions, hold that it is **permitted and good**, because it is praise of Allah and affirmation of His words, Allah commanded "Say: Allah has spoken the truth", and nothing prohibits it. Some cite earlier scholars who mentioned it among the etiquettes of recitation, such as al-Qurtubi.

### A fair summary
**Both sides agree** that the phrase is not part of the Quran, that it is not reported from the Prophet ﷺ after recitation, and that it must not be considered obligatory or part of the recitation. **They differ** on whether making it a habit is an innovation or permitted. The more cautious course is not to make it a permanent habit that gives the impression it is a Sunnah, and not to condemn those who say it, since it is a matter of scholarly difference.

## What Is Authentically Reported After Reciting?
### The expiation of the gathering
One of the most beautiful authentic practices for ending any gathering, including a gathering of recitation, is the **expiation of the gathering** (*kaffarat al-majlis*). At-Tirmidhi reported, and graded authentic, that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever sits in a gathering in which there is much idle talk, and says before he gets up: *Subhanaka Allahumma wa bihamdika, ash-hadu an la ilaha illa anta, astaghfiruka wa atubu ilayk* (Glory be to You, O Allah, and praise; I bear witness that there is no god but You; I seek Your forgiveness and repent to You), will be forgiven for what happened in that gathering."

An-Nasa'i reported in *as-Sunan al-Kubra* from 'A'ishah that the Prophet ﷺ never sat in a gathering, recited Quran or prayed without ending it with certain words. She asked him about them, and he said: "If good was said, they will be a seal on it until the Day of Resurrection, and if otherwise, they will be an expiation for it: *Subhanaka Allahumma wa bihamdika, astaghfiruka wa atubu ilayk*." Ibn Hajar graded its chain authentic.

This is the **established remembrance** for ending a recitation session, and it is more fitting than anything else.

## Responding to Verses While Reciting
One of the established practices is to **respond to what you recite**, especially in the night prayer. In Sahih Muslim, Hudhayfah prayed with the Prophet ﷺ one night. He recited Al-Baqarah, then An-Nisa', then Al 'Imran, reciting slowly: **when he passed a verse of glorification, he glorified Allah; when he passed a verse mentioning asking, he asked; and when he passed a verse mentioning seeking refuge, he sought refuge.**

So the Sunnah is:
- **At verses of glorification**: say "Subhan Allah" or "Subhana Rabbiyal-'Azim".
- **At verses of mercy and Paradise**: ask Allah from His bounty: "Allahumma inni as'alukal-jannah."
- **At verses of punishment and the Fire**: seek refuge: "A'udhu billahi min an-nar."

This is recommended in voluntary prayers and outside prayer. In obligatory prayers it is not reported that the Prophet ﷺ did it, though some scholars permitted it for a person praying alone.

### Specific places where responses are reported
- **In Surah Ar-Rahman**: at-Tirmidhi reported from Jabir that the Prophet ﷺ recited Surah Ar-Rahman to his Companions and they remained silent. He said: "I recited it to the jinn on the night of the jinn, and they responded better than you. Every time I reached His words, 'Which of the favours of your Lord will you deny?', they said: 'We deny none of Your favours, our Lord; to You belongs all praise.'" Al-Albani graded it hasan.

﴿فَبِأَيِّ ءَالَآءِ رَبِّكُمَا تُكَذِّبَانِ﴾ (Quran 55:13)

- **At "Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High"**: it is reported from Ibn 'Abbas that the Prophet ﷺ, when he recited it, said "Subhana Rabbiyal-A'la". Abu Dawud reported it, and scholars differ over whether it goes back to the Prophet ﷺ or stops at Ibn 'Abbas.
- **At the end of Surah At-Tin and Surah Al-Qiyamah**: there are reports of saying "Bala, wa ana 'ala dhalika minash-shahidin" (Yes, and I am among those who bear witness to that) and "Subhanaka fa bala", but their chains are weak. Whoever says them does no wrong, since they are good remembrance that fits the meaning.

## Prostration of Recitation
Another established response is the **prostration of recitation** at verses of prostration. It is a confirmed Sunnah according to the majority of scholars, for both the reciter and the listener. In Sahih al-Bukhari, 'Umar ibn al-Khattab recited Surah An-Nahl on the pulpit one Friday; when he reached the verse of prostration he came down and prostrated, and the people prostrated with him.

## The Du'a of Completing the Quran
### What is established from the early generations
It is authentically reported that **Anas ibn Malik**, when he completed the Quran, would gather his family and children and make du'a for them. Ad-Darimi and others reported this with an authentic chain as the practice of Anas. This is a basis for making du'a at the completion and gathering the family for it.

### The long du'a printed at the end of some Mushafs
The long du'a printed at the end of some copies of the Mushaf, sometimes attributed to Ibn Taymiyyah, **cannot be authentically attributed to him**, nor to the Prophet ﷺ. There is no harm in using it or any other good, comprehensive du'a, provided one does not believe it is a du'a transmitted from the Prophet ﷺ.

### Advice for the completion du'a
- Use comprehensive du'as from the Quran and Sunnah.
- Ask for understanding, acting on the Quran, and its intercession.
- Keep it reasonably brief and sincere, rather than lengthy and rhymed.

## Before Reciting: Seeking Refuge and the Basmalah
Allah says:

﴿فَإِذَا قَرَأۡتَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَ فَٱسۡتَعِذۡ بِٱللَّهِ مِنَ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنِ ٱلرَّجِيمِ﴾ (Quran 16:98)

Seeking refuge (*isti'adhah*) before recitation is a Sunnah according to the majority of scholars, and some consider it obligatory. The Basmalah is said at the beginning of every surah except At-Tawbah. So what is established is clear at the start of recitation and at its end: seek refuge at the start, respond to verses during it, and end with the expiation of the gathering.

## Why Did "Sadaqallahul 'Azim" Become So Widespread?
The phrase likely spread because it is short, beautiful in meaning, and signals clearly that the recitation has ended, which is useful in radio broadcasts and public gatherings. Over generations it became a custom, and many people now assume it is part of the recitation. This is exactly why scholars who discourage making it a habit are concerned: when a custom becomes so fixed that people think it is from the Sunnah, it should be clarified.

## For Teachers: What Should We Teach Children?
- Teach them that "Sadaqallahul 'Azim" is **not part of the Quran**.
- Teach them the **expiation of the gathering** as the way to end a lesson. It is short and easy to memorise.
- Teach them to respond to verses: "Subhan Allah" at verses of glorification, asking for Paradise at verses of mercy.
- Teach them **respect for scholarly difference**, so they do not criticise those who say the phrase.

## Etiquette in Minor Disagreements
Matters like this should not become a cause of division. A person who says "Sadaqallahul 'Azim" intends to affirm Allah's truthfulness; a person who avoids it intends to follow the Sunnah precisely. Both have good intentions. Explain gently, and do not turn a mosque or a classroom into a battlefield over a phrase.

## A Sample Ending to a Recitation Session
1. Finish at the end of a complete meaning, such as the end of a passage or surah.
2. If you passed a verse of mercy or punishment, respond with a brief du'a.
3. Say the expiation of the gathering: *Subhanaka Allahumma wa bihamdika, ash-hadu an la ilaha illa anta, astaghfiruka wa atubu ilayk.*
4. If you completed the whole Quran, gather your family and make du'a, as Anas did.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Is saying "Sadaqallahul 'Azim" after reciting an innovation?
Some scholars consider making it a fixed habit an innovation; others consider it permitted. It is not reported from the Prophet ﷺ after recitation.

### What did the Prophet ﷺ say after reciting?
He ended gatherings, recitation and prayer with "Subhanaka Allahumma wa bihamdika, astaghfiruka wa atubu ilayk", as reported by an-Nasa'i.

### Should I glorify Allah at verses of glorification?
Yes, especially in voluntary prayers and outside prayer, as reported in Sahih Muslim.

### What do I say at "Which of the favours of your Lord will you deny?"
"We deny none of Your favours, our Lord; to You belongs all praise," as in the hadith of Jabir, graded hasan by al-Albani.

### Is the du'a printed at the end of the Mushaf authentic?
It cannot be attributed to the Prophet ﷺ or authentically to Ibn Taymiyyah, but there is no harm in using it as a general du'a.

### Should the listener interrupt to say "Sadaqallahul 'Azim"?
No. The listener listens attentively; interrupting the reciter is not from the etiquette of recitation.

## Conclusion
"Allah has spoken the truth" is true and beautiful in meaning, and it appears in the Quran itself. But ending every recitation with "Sadaqallahul 'Azim" is not reported from the Prophet ﷺ or the Companions, and scholars differ about making it a habit. The safest course is to end your recitation with what is established: respond to verses as you recite, prostrate at verses of prostration, and close with the expiation of the gathering. And when you complete the Quran, gather your family and make du'a, as the Companions did.
