Juz 12 begins with ﴿۞وَمَا مِن دَآبَّةٖ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ إِلَّا عَلَى ٱللَّهِ رِزۡقُهَا وَيَعۡلَمُ مُسۡتَقَرَّهَا وَمُسۡتَوۡدَعَهَاۚ﴾, "There is no creature on earth but that its provision is from Allah, and He knows its dwelling and resting place", so it is called **Juz Wa Ma Min Dabbah**. It runs from **Hud 11:6 to Yusuf 12:52**. In the Madinah Mushaf it fills **pages 222 to 241**.

The juz contains almost all of **Surah Hud**, a surah of successive prophets' stories that strengthen the Prophet's heart, and then the first half of **the story of Yusuf**, which Allah calls ﴿أَحۡسَنَ ٱلۡقَصَصِ﴾, "the best of stories."

## The big idea

The whole juz is about **holding firm on the path of truth, however long it takes**. Surah Hud presents seven prophets who stood firm before their peoples, then tells the Prophet ﷺ plainly: ﴿وَكُلّٗا نَّقُصُّ عَلَيۡكَ مِنۡ أَنۢبَآءِ ٱلرُّسُلِ مَا نُثَبِّتُ بِهِۦ فُؤَادَكَۚ﴾, "Each of these stories of the messengers We relate to you to make your heart firm", and commands: ﴿فَٱسۡتَقِمۡ كَمَآ أُمِرۡتَ﴾, "So stand firm as you have been commanded." The story of Yusuf then shows a different kind of firmness: a young man standing firm against envy, temptation and prison until his innocence comes to light.

The message: **steadfastness is a long road, and the best outcome belongs to the God-conscious.**

## Part one: Surah Hud (6-123)

### 1. Provision, testing and human nature (6-24)
- ﴿وَمَا مِن دَآبَّةٖ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ إِلَّا عَلَى ٱللَّهِ رِزۡقُهَا﴾: reassurance for every heart that fears poverty.
- ﴿وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضَ فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٖ وَكَانَ عَرۡشُهُۥ عَلَى ٱلۡمَآءِ لِيَبۡلُوَكُمۡ أَيُّكُمۡ أَحۡسَنُ عَمَلٗاۗ﴾, "It is He who created the heavens and the earth in six days, and His Throne was upon the water, to test which of you is best in deeds." The measure is the best deeds, not the most.
- Human nature: when mercy is taken away ﴿إِنَّهُۥ لَيَـُٔوسࣱ كَفُورࣱ﴾, "he is despairing and ungrateful"; when blessing comes, he becomes exultant and boastful, ﴿إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ﴾, "except those who are patient and do righteous deeds."
- The challenge to produce ten invented surahs like it.
- Whoever wants only this world and its adornment will be paid in full for their deeds here, with nothing in the Hereafter but the Fire.
- The two groups compared: like the blind and deaf versus the seeing and hearing.

### 2. Nuh and the flood (25-49)
The longest telling of Nuh's story after Surah Nuh itself:
- **The call and the argument**: ﴿وَلَقَدۡ أَرۡسَلۡنَا نُوحًا إِلَىٰ قَوۡمِهِۦٓ إِنِّي لَكُمۡ نَذِيرࣱ مُّبِينٌ﴾, "We sent Nuh to his people: I am a clear warner to you." The chiefs said: we see you as only a man like us, and only the lowest of us follow you. He replied: I ask you for no wealth, and I will not drive away those who believe.
- **Building the ark**: whenever a group of his people passed, they mocked him, and he said: if you mock us, we will mock you as you mock.
- **The flood**: ﴿وَهِيَ تَجۡرِي بِهِمۡ فِي مَوۡجٖ كَٱلۡجِبَالِ وَنَادَىٰ نُوحٌ ٱبۡنَهُۥ وَكَانَ فِي مَعۡزِلٖ يَٰبُنَيَّ ٱرۡكَب مَّعَنَا وَلَا تَكُن مَّعَ ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ﴾, "And it sailed with them through waves like mountains, and Nuh called to his son, who was apart: O my son, ride with us and do not be with the disbelievers." The son answered: ﴿سَـَٔاوِيٓ إِلَىٰ جَبَلٖ يَعۡصِمُنِي مِنَ ٱلۡمَآءِۚ﴾, "I will take refuge on a mountain to protect me from the water." Nuh said: ﴿لَا عَاصِمَ ٱلۡيَوۡمَ مِنۡ أَمۡرِ ٱللَّهِ إِلَّا مَن رَّحِمَۚ﴾, "There is no protector today from Allah's decree except for whom He has mercy", and the waves came between them.
- **The end of the flood**: ﴿وَقِيلَ يَـٰٓأَرۡضُ ٱبۡلَعِي مَآءَكِ وَيَٰسَمَآءُ أَقۡلِعِي وَغِيضَ ٱلۡمَآءُ وَقُضِيَ ٱلۡأَمۡرُ وَٱسۡتَوَتۡ عَلَى ٱلۡجُودِيِّۖ﴾, "And it was said: O earth, swallow your water; O sky, withhold. The water subsided, the matter was settled, and it came to rest on al-Judi." Scholars of rhetoric count it among the most eloquent verses of the Quran.
- **A father's plea**: ﴿رَبِّ إِنَّ ٱبۡنِي مِنۡ أَهۡلِي وَإِنَّ وَعۡدَكَ ٱلۡحَقُّ﴾, "My Lord, my son is of my family, and Your promise is true", and the answer: ﴿إِنَّهُۥ لَيۡسَ مِنۡ أَهۡلِكَۖ إِنَّهُۥ عَمَلٌ غَيۡرُ صَٰلِحٖۖ﴾, "He is not of your family; his conduct was unrighteous." The true family is the family of faith.
- **The close**: ﴿تِلۡكَ مِنۡ أَنۢبَآءِ ٱلۡغَيۡبِ نُوحِيهَآ إِلَيۡكَۖ مَا كُنتَ تَعۡلَمُهَآ أَنتَ وَلَا قَوۡمُكَ مِن قَبۡلِ هَٰذَاۖ فَٱصۡبِرۡۖ إِنَّ ٱلۡعَٰقِبَةَ لِلۡمُتَّقِينَ﴾, "These are accounts of the unseen We reveal to you; neither you nor your people knew them before this. So be patient; the best outcome is for the God-conscious."

**Lesson**: lineage does not save; guidance is in Allah's hand, and even a prophet could not save his son. See [Surah Nuh Meaning in English](/blog/surah-nuh-meaning-english/).

### 3. Hud and 'Ad (50-60)
Hud calls his people and links seeking forgiveness to worldly good: ﴿وَيَٰقَوۡمِ ٱسۡتَغۡفِرُواْ رَبَّكُمۡ ثُمَّ تُوبُوٓاْ إِلَيۡهِ يُرۡسِلِ ٱلسَّمَآءَ عَلَيۡكُم مِّدۡرَارٗا وَيَزِدۡكُمۡ قُوَّةً إِلَىٰ قُوَّتِكُمۡ﴾, "O my people, seek forgiveness from your Lord and repent to Him; He will send the sky upon you in showers and add strength to your strength." When they threatened him, he challenged them all with confidence: ﴿إِنِّي تَوَكَّلۡتُ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ رَبِّي وَرَبِّكُمۚ مَّا مِن دَآبَّةٍ إِلَّا هُوَ ءَاخِذُۢ بِنَاصِيَتِهَآۚ إِنَّ رَبِّي عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٖ مُّسۡتَقِيمٖ﴾, "I have relied on Allah, my Lord and your Lord. There is no creature but that He holds its forelock. My Lord is on a straight path." Then his rescue with those who believed.

### 4. Salih and Thamud (61-68)
﴿هُوَ أَنشَأَكُم مِّنَ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَٱسۡتَعۡمَرَكُمۡ فِيهَا﴾, "He produced you from the earth and settled you in it to build it." The she-camel was a sign; they hamstrung her, and ﴿وَأَخَذَ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُواْ ٱلصَّيۡحَةُ فَأَصۡبَحُواْ فِي دِيَٰرِهِمۡ جَٰثِمِينَ﴾, "the blast seized the wrongdoers, and they lay lifeless in their homes."

### 5. Ibrahim's guests (69-76)
The angels came to Ibrahim with good news; he served them a roasted calf, and when he saw their hands not reaching for it, he grew afraid. They gave his wife the good news of Ishaq and, after him, Ya'qub. She wondered: shall I give birth when I am an old woman and my husband an old man? They said: ﴿أَتَعۡجَبِينَ مِنۡ أَمۡرِ ٱللَّهِۖ رَحۡمَتُ ٱللَّهِ وَبَرَكَٰتُهُۥ عَلَيۡكُمۡ أَهۡلَ ٱلۡبَيۡتِۚ إِنَّهُۥ حَمِيدࣱ مَّجِيدࣱ﴾, "Do you wonder at Allah's decree? The mercy of Allah and His blessings be upon you, people of the house. He is Praiseworthy and Glorious." Ibrahim then pleaded for the people of Lut out of compassion, and Allah praised him: ﴿إِنَّ إِبۡرَٰهِيمَ لَحَلِيمٌ أَوَّـٰهࣱ مُّنِيبࣱ﴾, "Ibrahim was forbearing, tender-hearted and ever turning to Allah."

**Lesson**: honouring guests is a prophetic trait, and compassion even towards sinners is the way of Ibrahim.

### 6. Lut and his people (77-83)
The messengers came to Lut and he was distressed for them, and his people rushed to him. He said: ﴿أَلَيۡسَ مِنكُمۡ رَجُلࣱ رَّشِيدࣱ﴾, "Is there not among you a single right-minded man?" The angels then revealed who they were: ﴿يَٰلُوطُ إِنَّا رُسُلُ رَبِّكَ لَن يَصِلُوٓاْ إِلَيۡكَۖ فَأَسۡرِ بِأَهۡلِكَ بِقِطۡعٖ مِّنَ ٱلَّيۡلِ﴾, "O Lut, we are messengers of your Lord; they will not reach you. Travel with your family in a part of the night", and their appointment was dawn: ﴿أَلَيۡسَ ٱلصُّبۡحُ بِقَرِيبࣲ﴾, "Is not the morning near?" Then: ﴿فَلَمَّا جَآءَ أَمۡرُنَا جَعَلۡنَا عَٰلِيَهَا سَافِلَهَا وَأَمۡطَرۡنَا عَلَيۡهَا حِجَارَةٗ مِّن سِجِّيلٖ مَّنضُودٖ﴾, "When Our command came, We turned the town upside down and rained on it stones of baked clay, layer upon layer."

### 7. Shu'ayb and Madyan (84-95)
Hizb 24 begins with Shu'ayb. He calls his people to monotheism and honest measure, and they mock: ﴿أَصَلَوٰتُكَ تَأۡمُرُكَ أَن نَّتۡرُكَ مَا يَعۡبُدُ ءَابَآؤُنَآ أَوۡ أَن نَّفۡعَلَ فِيٓ أَمۡوَٰلِنَا مَا نَشَـٰٓؤُاْۖ﴾, "Does your prayer command you that we abandon what our fathers worshipped, or stop doing as we please with our wealth?", in other words, what has religion to do with business? Shu'ayb replies with words that became a motto for every reformer: ﴿وَمَآ أُرِيدُ أَنۡ أُخَالِفَكُمۡ إِلَىٰ مَآ أَنۡهَىٰكُمۡ عَنۡهُۚ إِنۡ أُرِيدُ إِلَّا ٱلۡإِصۡلَٰحَ مَا ٱسۡتَطَعۡتُۚ وَمَا تَوۡفِيقِيٓ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِۚ عَلَيۡهِ تَوَكَّلۡتُ وَإِلَيۡهِ أُنِيبُ﴾, "I do not intend to do what I forbid you from. I only want reform as far as I am able, and my success is only through Allah. In Him I trust and to Him I turn." Then ﴿وَأَخَذَتِ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُواْ ٱلصَّيۡحَةُ﴾, "and the blast seized the wrongdoers." Read [I Only Want Reform: 11:88](/blog/in-uridu-illa-al-islah-meaning-english/).

**Three rules in Shu'ayb's words**: a reformer starts with himself, works within his capacity, and knows that success comes from Allah.

### 8. Musa, Pharaoh and the lessons of the stories (96-111)
A brief mention of Musa and Pharaoh: Pharaoh ﴿يَقۡدُمُ قَوۡمَهُۥ يَوۡمَ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِ فَأَوۡرَدَهُمُ ٱلنَّارَۖ﴾, "will lead his people on the Day of Resurrection and bring them down into the Fire": a misguided leader leads his followers to ruin. Then the general comment: ﴿ذَٰلِكَ مِنۡ أَنۢبَآءِ ٱلۡقُرَىٰ نَقُصُّهُۥ عَلَيۡكَۖ مِنۡهَا قَآئِمࣱ وَحَصِيدࣱ﴾, "These are accounts of the towns We relate to you; some still stand and some are mown down", and ﴿وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَخۡذُ رَبِّكَ إِذَآ أَخَذَ ٱلۡقُرَىٰ وَهِيَ ظَٰلِمَةٌۚ﴾, "Such is the seizing of your Lord when He seizes towns while they are doing wrong." In Al-Bukhari and Muslim: "Allah gives the wrongdoer respite, but when He seizes him, He does not let him go", and then he recited this verse. Then a scene of the Day: ﴿يَوۡمَ يَأۡتِ لَا تَكَلَّمُ نَفۡسٌ إِلَّا بِإِذۡنِهِۦۚ فَمِنۡهُمۡ شَقِيࣱّ وَسَعِيدࣱ﴾, "The Day it comes, no soul will speak except by His permission; some will be wretched and some happy."

### 9. A charter of steadfastness (112-123)
After all the stories comes the concentrated command:
- ﴿فَٱسۡتَقِمۡ كَمَآ أُمِرۡتَ وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ وَلَا تَطۡغَوۡاْۚ﴾, "So stand firm as you have been commanded, along with those who have turned back with you, and do not transgress." Steadfastness is the middle between falling short and going too far.
- ﴿وَلَا تَرۡكَنُوٓاْ إِلَى ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُواْ فَتَمَسَّكُمُ ٱلنَّارُ﴾, "Do not incline towards the wrongdoers, lest the Fire touch you." Inclining is a leaning of the heart; how much worse is taking part?
- ﴿وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ طَرَفَيِ ٱلنَّهَارِ وَزُلَفٗا مِّنَ ٱلَّيۡلِۚ إِنَّ ٱلۡحَسَنَٰتِ يُذۡهِبۡنَ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِۚ﴾, "Establish prayer at the two ends of the day and in the early hours of the night. Good deeds remove bad deeds." In Al-Bukhari and Muslim, a man who had kissed a woman came to the Prophet ﷺ and this was revealed; the man asked, "Is this for me alone?" He said: "For all of my nation." Read [Good Deeds Remove Bad Deeds: 11:114](/blog/al-hasanat-yudhhibna-as-sayyiat-meaning-english/).
- ﴿وَٱصۡبِرۡ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُضِيعُ أَجۡرَ ٱلۡمُحۡسِنِينَ﴾, "And be patient, for Allah does not let the reward of those who do good be lost."
- Why nations were destroyed: too few people of virtue forbidding corruption, then ﴿وَمَا كَانَ رَبُّكَ لِيُهۡلِكَ ٱلۡقُرَىٰ بِظُلۡمٖ وَأَهۡلُهَا مُصۡلِحُونَ﴾, "Your Lord would not destroy towns unjustly while their people were reformers." Note "reformers" (*muslihun*), not merely "righteous" (*salihun*): personal goodness alone does not protect a society.
- ﴿وَلَوۡ شَآءَ رَبُّكَ لَجَعَلَ ٱلنَّاسَ أُمَّةٗ وَٰحِدَةٗۖ وَلَا يَزَالُونَ مُخۡتَلِفِينَ﴾, "Had your Lord willed, He would have made mankind one community, but they will not cease to differ."
- Then the purpose of the whole surah: "Each of these stories We relate to you to make your heart firm, and in this has come to you the truth, an admonition and a reminder for the believers."
- And the last verse: ﴿فَٱعۡبُدۡهُ وَتَوَكَّلۡ عَلَيۡهِۚ﴾, "So worship Him and rely upon Him."

For the whole surah see [Surah Hud Meaning in English](/blog/surah-hud-meaning-english/).

## Part two: the first half of Surah Yusuf (1-52)

Surah Yusuf is Makki, with 111 verses, the only surah that tells one prophet's story complete from beginning to end in one place. Many commentators say it was revealed in the Year of Sorrow, after the deaths of Khadijah and Abu Talib, as comfort for the Prophet ﷺ: a brother envied by his brothers, sent away from his family, and then established in the land. This juz contains its first half. For the full surah see [Surah Yusuf Meaning in English](/blog/surah-yusuf-meaning-english/).

### 1. The best of stories and the dream (1-6)
﴿نَحۡنُ نَقُصُّ عَلَيۡكَ أَحۡسَنَ ٱلۡقَصَصِ بِمَآ أَوۡحَيۡنَآ إِلَيۡكَ هَٰذَا ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَ﴾, "We relate to you the best of stories in what We have revealed to you of this Quran." Then the dream: ﴿إِذۡ قَالَ يُوسُفُ لِأَبِيهِ يَـٰٓأَبَتِ إِنِّي رَأَيۡتُ أَحَدَ عَشَرَ كَوۡكَبٗا وَٱلشَّمۡسَ وَٱلۡقَمَرَ رَأَيۡتُهُمۡ لِي سَٰجِدِينَ﴾, "When Yusuf said to his father: O my father, I saw eleven stars and the sun and the moon; I saw them prostrating to me." His father warns him: ﴿يَٰبُنَيَّ لَا تَقۡصُصۡ رُءۡيَاكَ عَلَىٰٓ إِخۡوَتِكَ فَيَكِيدُواْ لَكَ كَيۡدًاۖ﴾, "O my son, do not tell your dream to your brothers, lest they plot against you."

**A parenting lesson**: a wise father protects his child from envy through discretion; not every blessing should be announced.

### 2. The brothers' plot and the well (7-18)
﴿لَّقَدۡ كَانَ فِي يُوسُفَ وَإِخۡوَتِهِۦٓ ءَايَٰتࣱ لِّلسَّآئِلِينَ﴾, "Certainly in Yusuf and his brothers are signs for those who ask." The brothers said: Yusuf and his brother are dearer to our father than we are; then: kill him or cast him into some land. One suggested throwing him into the bottom of a well. They persuaded their father, threw him in, came back at night weeping, and brought his shirt stained with false blood. Ya'qub said: ﴿بَلۡ سَوَّلَتۡ لَكُمۡ أَنفُسُكُمۡ أَمۡرٗاۖ فَصَبۡرࣱ جَمِيلࣱۖ وَٱللَّهُ ٱلۡمُسۡتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ﴾, "Rather, your souls have tempted you into something. So patience is most fitting, and Allah is the one whose help is sought against what you describe."

Beautiful patience is patience without complaint to people. Notice Yusuf's shirt: it appears three times in the surah, each time with a different role.

### 3. From the well to the house of al-'Aziz (19-22)
A caravan came, drew him out, and sold him for a small price, a few coins. Then: ﴿وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِي ٱشۡتَرَىٰهُ مِن مِّصۡرَ لِٱمۡرَأَتِهِۦٓ أَكۡرِمِي مَثۡوَىٰهُ﴾, "The man from Egypt who bought him said to his wife: make his stay honourable", and ﴿وَكَذَٰلِكَ مَكَّنَّا لِيُوسُفَ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ﴾, "Thus We established Yusuf in the land." Then: ﴿وَلَمَّا بَلَغَ أَشُدَّهُۥٓ ءَاتَيۡنَٰهُ حُكۡمٗا وَعِلۡمٗاۚ وَكَذَٰلِكَ نَجۡزِي ٱلۡمُحۡسِنِينَ﴾, "When he reached maturity, We gave him judgement and knowledge; thus We reward those who do good."

**Reflect**: what the brothers saw as an end (the well) was the start of his establishment, and what looked like humiliation (the sale) was the road to the house of al-'Aziz.

### 4. Temptation and chastity (23-29)
﴿وَرَٰوَدَتۡهُ ٱلَّتِي هُوَ فِي بَيۡتِهَا عَن نَّفۡسِهِۦ وَغَلَّقَتِ ٱلۡأَبۡوَٰبَ وَقَالَتۡ هَيۡتَ لَكَۚ قَالَ مَعَاذَ ٱللَّهِۖ إِنَّهُۥ رَبِّيٓ أَحۡسَنَ مَثۡوَايَۖ﴾, "The woman in whose house he was sought to seduce him; she closed the doors and said: come to me. He said: I seek refuge in Allah. He is my master; he has made my stay good." Then: ﴿وَلَقَدۡ هَمَّتۡ بِهِۦۖ وَهَمَّ بِهَا لَوۡلَآ أَن رَّءَا بُرۡهَٰنَ رَبِّهِۦۚ﴾, "She was determined on him, and he would have inclined to her had he not seen the proof of his Lord." Scholars hold two well-known views on Yusuf's "inclination": that it was a passing thought he never resolved on or acted upon, or that the answer to "had he not" comes first, meaning he did not incline at all because he saw his Lord's proof. Either way Yusuf was innocent and protected: ﴿إِنَّهُۥ مِنۡ عِبَادِنَا ٱلۡمُخۡلَصِينَ﴾, "He was one of Our chosen servants." Strange reports about what the "proof" was are not established, so we keep to what the Quran says.

They raced to the door, she tore his shirt from behind, and a witness from her family said: if his shirt is torn from the front she is truthful, and if from behind she has lied. When her husband saw it torn from behind, he said: this is your women's scheming.

**Lesson**: chastity in private is the greatest test, and Yusuf's weapons were three: seeking refuge in Allah, remembering the kindness he had received, and seeing his Lord's proof.

### 5. The women and the prison (30-35)
Women in the city gossiped, so she invited them to a banquet and gave each a knife. When they saw him they cut their hands and said: ﴿حَٰشَ لِلَّهِ مَا هَٰذَا بَشَرًا إِنۡ هَٰذَآ إِلَّا مَلَكࣱ كَرِيمࣱ﴾, "Perfect is Allah! This is no human; this is none but a noble angel." She then threatened him with prison, and Yusuf chose: ﴿رَبِّ ٱلسِّجۡنُ أَحَبُّ إِلَيَّ مِمَّا يَدۡعُونَنِيٓ إِلَيۡهِۖ وَإِلَّا تَصۡرِفۡ عَنِّي كَيۡدَهُنَّ أَصۡبُ إِلَيۡهِنَّ وَأَكُن مِّنَ ٱلۡجَٰهِلِينَ﴾, "My Lord, prison is dearer to me than what they invite me to. Unless You turn their scheming away from me, I may incline to them and be among the ignorant."

**Reflect**: Yusuf did not rely on his own strength; he asked Allah to turn the scheming away. Even one protected by Allah seeks His help.

### 6. Calling to Allah in prison (36-42)
Two young men entered prison with him and each had a dream. Yusuf did not start with the interpretation; he started with the call to Allah: ﴿يَٰصَٰحِبَيِ ٱلسِّجۡنِ ءَأَرۡبَابࣱ مُّتَفَرِّقُونَ خَيۡرٌ أَمِ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡوَٰحِدُ ٱلۡقَهَّارُ﴾, "O my two prison companions, are many separate lords better, or Allah, the One, the Prevailing?" Then he interpreted: one would serve wine to his master, the other would be crucified and birds would eat from his head. He asked the one to be freed to mention him to the king, but Shaytan made him forget, and Yusuf remained in prison several years.

**Lesson**: a caller to Allah uses every situation, even prison, and begins with what matters most: monotheism.

### 7. The king's dream and Yusuf's vindication (43-52)
The king saw seven fat cows eaten by seven lean ones, and seven green ears and others dry. His advisers could not interpret it: muddled dreams. The freed man remembered Yusuf, who explained: seven years of plenty, leave what you harvest in its ears except a little to eat; then seven hard years; then a year in which people will have rain.

**Reflect**: Yusuf did not stop at interpretation; he laid out an economic plan for storage, and he did not make his release a condition.

The king summoned him, but Yusuf refused to leave until his innocence was established: go back to your master and ask him about the women who cut their hands. The women confessed: ﴿حَٰشَ لِلَّهِ مَا عَلِمۡنَا عَلَيۡهِ مِن سُوٓءٖۚ﴾, "Perfect is Allah! We know no evil about him", and the wife of al-'Aziz confessed: ﴿ٱلۡـَٰٔنَ حَصۡحَصَ ٱلۡحَقُّ أَنَا۠ رَٰوَدتُّهُۥ عَن نَّفۡسِهِۦ وَإِنَّهُۥ لَمِنَ ٱلصَّـٰدِقِينَ﴾, "Now the truth has come to light. It was I who sought to seduce him, and he is truthful." The juz ends: ﴿ذَٰلِكَ لِيَعۡلَمَ أَنِّي لَمۡ أَخُنۡهُ بِٱلۡغَيۡبِ وَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَهۡدِي كَيۡدَ ٱلۡخَآئِنِينَ﴾, "That is so he will know I did not betray him in his absence, and that Allah does not guide the scheme of betrayers."

**The great lesson**: dignity before freedom. Yusuf stayed in prison until his innocence was proven, because leaving under suspicion did not befit a prophet or a caller to Allah.

## Tajweed points in Juz 12

### ﴿مَجۡر۪ىٰهَا﴾ in Hud 41
The only major *imalah* (tilting the alif towards an "e" sound) in the narration of Hafs: the ra and the alif after it lean towards kasrah, shown by the small sign under the ra.

### ﴿يَٰبُنَيَّ ٱرۡكَب مَّعَنَا﴾
The sakin ba may merge into the meem for Hafs; merging is the preferred option.

### ﴿لَا تَأۡمَ۫نَّا﴾ in Yusuf 11
Read with *ishmam* (rounding the lips at the merged noon to indicate a dropped dammah) or *rawm*, one of the special cases for Hafs.

### ﴿يَـٰٓأَرۡضُ ٱبۡلَعِي﴾
The connecting hamzah of *ibla'i* drops when joined, and is read with a kasrah when starting from it.

### Rare words: ﴿هَيۡتَ لَكَۚ﴾ and ﴿حَصۡحَصَ﴾
These occur only here; pronounce them exactly as written in the Mushaf. For an overview of these special cases see [A Roadmap for Learning Tajweed](/blog/learn-tajweed-roadmap-english/).

## Similar verses to watch

### 1. *Wa-lamma* and *fa-lamma* in the rescue of prophets
- Hud 58 and 94: ﴿وَلَمَّا جَآءَ أَمۡرُنَا نَجَّيۡنَا﴾, with *wa*, for Hud and Shu'ayb.
- Hud 66 and 82: ﴿فَلَمَّا جَآءَ أَمۡرُنَا﴾, with *fa*, for Salih and Lut.
**Key**: commentators note that the stories of Salih and Lut include a fixed appointment before the punishment (three days, and the morning), so the immediate *fa* follows; the stories of Hud and Shu'ayb have no such appointment, so *wa* is used.

### 2. *Akhadha* and *akhadhat*
- Thamud, 67: ﴿وَأَخَذَ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُواْ ٱلصَّيۡحَةُ﴾, masculine verb.
- Madyan, 94: ﴿وَأَخَذَتِ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُواْ ٱلصَّيۡحَةُ﴾, feminine verb.

### 3. Reaching maturity: Yusuf and Al-Qasas
- Yusuf 22: ﴿وَلَمَّا بَلَغَ أَشُدَّهُۥٓ ءَاتَيۡنَٰهُ حُكۡمٗا وَعِلۡمٗاۚ﴾.
- Al-Qasas 14, about Musa: ﴿وَلَمَّا بَلَغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ وَٱسۡتَوَىٰٓ﴾, adding "and was fully grown".

### 4. The opening of Nuh's story
Hud 25 reads ﴿وَلَقَدۡ أَرۡسَلۡنَا نُوحًا إِلَىٰ قَوۡمِهِۦٓ إِنِّي لَكُمۡ نَذِيرࣱ﴾, without "and he said", unlike Al-A'raf and Al-Mu'minun.

## How to memorize Juz 12 with understanding

1. **Hud has seven prophets**: learn their order: Nuh, Hud, Salih, Ibrahim, Lut, Shu'ayb, Musa.
2. **Make a table for each prophet**: the call, the people's reply, the punishment, and *wa-lamma* or *fa-lamma*.
3. **Memorize the steadfastness verses (112-115)** while acting on them; they are the summary of the surah.
4. **Yusuf is a continuous story**: memorize it as scenes: the dream, the well, the sale, the house, the temptation, the women, the prison, the king's dream, the vindication.
5. **Do not pause long in the middle of Yusuf**: move into Juz 13 soon after.

For technique see [How to Memorize the Quran Step by Step](/blog/how-to-memorize-quran-step-by-step/).

## Practical lessons from Juz 12

- **Your provision is written**; do not destroy yourself with worry.
- **Aim for the best deeds**, not the most.
- **Do not despair in hardship or boast in ease.**
- **Faith is closer than blood.**
- **Seeking forgiveness brings good** in this world before the next.
- **Honour your guests** and show compassion even to sinners.
- **Be a reformer, not only righteous.**
- **Start reform with yourself** and work within your ability.
- **Stand firm without excess**, and do not lean towards wrongdoers.
- **Follow a bad deed with a good one** to erase it.
- **Beautiful patience** means no complaint to people.
- **Chastity in private** is the greatest test.
- **Call to Allah everywhere**, even in prison.
- **Put dignity before a hasty exit.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is Juz 12 called "Wa Ma Min Dabbah"?
Because it begins with ﴿۞وَمَا مِن دَآبَّةٖ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ إِلَّا عَلَى ٱللَّهِ رِزۡقُهَا﴾.

### Where does Juz 12 end?
At Yusuf 12:52. Juz 13 begins with ﴿۞وَمَآ أُبَرِّئُ نَفۡسِيٓۚ إِنَّ ٱلنَّفۡسَ لَأَمَّارَةُۢ بِٱلسُّوٓءِ﴾, "And I do not acquit myself; the soul is ever inclined to evil."

### What is the only *imalah* in Hafs?
In the word ﴿مَجۡر۪ىٰهَا﴾ in Hud 41.

### Did Yusuf commit a sin?
No. The Quran states his innocence through the confession of the women and of al-'Aziz's wife, and Allah describes him as one of His chosen servants.

### What is "beautiful patience"?
Patience without complaint to people, while still complaining to Allah, as Ya'qub later said: ﴿إِنَّمَآ أَشۡكُواْ بَثِّي وَحُزۡنِيٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ﴾, "I only complain of my grief and sorrow to Allah."

## Conclusion

Juz 12 is a school of steadfastness: seven prophets in Surah Hud holding firm before their peoples, a clear command to the Prophet ﷺ and his nation to stand firm, pray and be patient, and a young man who endured his brothers' envy, temptation in the house and the injustice of prison until his innocence shone.
