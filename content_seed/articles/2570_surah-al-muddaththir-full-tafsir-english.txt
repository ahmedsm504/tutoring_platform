Surah Al-Muddaththir marks the moment the Prophet's ﷺ private experience of revelation became a public mission. After the first revelation in the cave of Hira, there was a pause. Then one day he saw the angel again, between heaven and earth, and returned home trembling, asking to be covered. Allah called him: "O you who covers himself, arise and warn." The surah gives six short commands that form the foundation of every caller's character, describes a powerful man of Quraysh who knew the Quran was not human speech yet called it magic, introduces Saqar and its nineteen keepers, and ends with a conversation between the people of Paradise and the people of Hell.

This guide explains Surah Al-Muddaththir section by section in English, with the authentic account of its revelation.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Muddaththir
- **Number of verses**: 56.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 74, in the twenty-ninth juz.
- **Name**: Al-Muddaththir means "the one who covers himself", from the first verse.
- **Main themes**: the start of the public mission, the character of the caller, the arrogance of the deniers, and the causes of entering Hell.

## The Story of Its Revelation
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Jabir ibn 'Abdullah reported that the Prophet ﷺ, speaking about the pause in revelation, said: "While I was walking, I heard a voice from the sky. I raised my eyes, and there was the angel who had come to me in Hira, sitting on a chair between the sky and the earth. I was terrified of him, so I returned and said: Wrap me up, wrap me up. So Allah revealed: *O you who covers himself, arise and warn, and your Lord glorify, and your clothing purify, and uncleanliness avoid*. Then revelation became frequent and continuous."

## Verses 1–7: Six Commands for the Caller
﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلۡمُدَّثِّرُ﴾ ﴿قُمۡ فَأَنذِرۡ﴾ ﴿وَرَبَّكَ فَكَبِّرۡ﴾ ﴿وَثِيَابَكَ فَطَهِّرۡ﴾ ﴿وَٱلرُّجۡزَ فَٱهۡجُرۡ﴾ ﴿وَلَا تَمۡنُن تَسۡتَكۡثِرُ﴾ ﴿وَلِرَبِّكَ فَٱصۡبِرۡ﴾ (Quran 74:1-7)

1. **"Arise and warn"**: get up from your covering and warn people. The time for private worship alone is over; the message must be carried.
2. **"And your Lord glorify"**: say *Allahu akbar*, magnify Him above everything. Whoever magnifies Allah sees everyone else as small, and fears no one.
3. **"And your clothing purify"**: purify your clothes from impurity, and, as many commentators said, purify your deeds and character, for "clothing" is used in Arabic for a person's conduct.
4. **"And uncleanliness avoid"**: abandon idols and every sin.
5. **"And do not confer favour to acquire more"**: do not give expecting more in return, and do not consider your efforts a favour to anyone.
6. **"And for your Lord be patient"**: patience in obedience, patience against sin, and patience with people's harm.

**These six commands** are a complete programme for anyone who calls others to good: action, glorifying Allah, personal purity, avoiding sin, sincerity without expecting return, and patience.

## Verses 8–10: The Trumpet
﴿فَإِذَا نُقِرَ فِي ٱلنَّاقُورِ﴾ ﴿فَذَٰلِكَ يَوۡمَئِذٖ يَوۡمٌ عَسِيرٌ﴾ ﴿عَلَى ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ غَيۡرُ يَسِيرٖ﴾ (Quran 74:8-10)

**"And when the Trumpet is blown, that Day will be a difficult day, for the disbelievers not easy."** Notice that the verse says "not easy" for the disbelievers, implying that for the believers it will be eased.

## Verses 11–25: The Man Who Called the Quran Magic
﴿ذَرۡنِي وَمَنۡ خَلَقۡتُ وَحِيدٗا﴾ ﴿وَجَعَلۡتُ لَهُۥ مَالٗا مَّمۡدُودٗا﴾ ﴿وَبَنِينَ شُهُودٗا﴾ ﴿وَمَهَّدتُّ لَهُۥ تَمۡهِيدٗا﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ يَطۡمَعُ أَنۡ أَزِيدَ﴾ ﴿كَلَّآۖ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ لِأٓيَٰتِنَا عَنِيدٗا﴾ ﴿سَأُرۡهِقُهُۥ صَعُودًا﴾ (Quran 74:11-17)

**"Leave Me with the one I created alone"**: he came into the world alone, without wealth or children, and Allah gave him extensive wealth, sons present with him, and every comfort. **"Then he desires that I should add more. No!"**

Many commentators mention that these verses were revealed about al-Walid ibn al-Mughirah, one of the leaders of Quraysh, who heard the Quran and was moved, then refused to accept it out of pride and pressure from his people.

﴿إِنَّهُۥ فَكَّرَ وَقَدَّرَ﴾ ﴿فَقُتِلَ كَيۡفَ قَدَّرَ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ قُتِلَ كَيۡفَ قَدَّرَ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ نَظَرَ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ عَبَسَ وَبَسَرَ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ أَدۡبَرَ وَٱسۡتَكۡبَرَ﴾ ﴿فَقَالَ إِنۡ هَٰذَآ إِلَّا سِحۡرࣱ يُؤۡثَرُ﴾ ﴿إِنۡ هَٰذَآ إِلَّا قَوۡلُ ٱلۡبَشَرِ﴾ (Quran 74:18-25)

**A vivid portrait of a man fighting the truth**:
- **"He thought and deliberated."**
- **"So may he be destroyed, how he deliberated! Then may he be destroyed, how he deliberated!"**
- **"Then he looked; then he frowned and scowled; then he turned back and was arrogant."**
- **"And said: This is not but magic imitated. This is not but the word of a human being."**

Every facial expression is captured: looking, frowning, scowling, turning away. He was searching for an accusation that would stick, knowing in his heart that the Quran was not ordinary speech.

## Verses 26–31: Saqar and the Nineteen
﴿سَأُصۡلِيهِ سَقَرَ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا سَقَرُ﴾ ﴿لَا تُبۡقِي وَلَا تَذَرُ﴾ ﴿لَوَّاحَةࣱ لِّلۡبَشَرِ﴾ ﴿عَلَيۡهَا تِسۡعَةَ عَشَرَ﴾ ﴿وَمَا جَعَلۡنَآ أَصۡحَٰبَ ٱلنَّارِ إِلَّا مَلَـٰٓئِكَةٗۖ وَمَا جَعَلۡنَا عِدَّتَهُمۡ إِلَّا فِتۡنَةٗ لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ لِيَسۡتَيۡقِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُواْ ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ وَيَزۡدَادَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓاْ إِيمَٰنٗا وَلَا يَرۡتَابَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُواْ ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ وَٱلۡمُؤۡمِنُونَ وَلِيَقُولَ ٱلَّذِينَ فِي قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضࣱ وَٱلۡكَٰفِرُونَ مَاذَآ أَرَادَ ٱللَّهُ بِهَٰذَا مَثَلٗاۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يُضِلُّ ٱللَّهُ مَن يَشَآءُ وَيَهۡدِي مَن يَشَآءُۚ وَمَا يَعۡلَمُ جُنُودَ رَبِّكَ إِلَّا هُوَۚ وَمَا هِيَ إِلَّا ذِكۡرَىٰ لِلۡبَشَرِ﴾ (Quran 74:26-31)

**"I will drive him into Saqar"**, a name of Hell, **"it lets nothing remain and leaves nothing unburned, blackening the skins"**. **"Over it are nineteen."**

**Why nineteen?** The surah explains the wisdom itself: Allah made the keepers of the Fire angels, and made their number **"a trial for those who disbelieve"**, who mocked the number; **"that those who were given the Scripture will be convinced"**, because it matched what they knew; **"and those who have believed will increase in faith"**; and the hypocrites and disbelievers would say: "What does Allah intend by this as an example?" **"And none knows the soldiers of your Lord except Him."**

## Verses 32–37: An Oath by the Moon and the Night
﴿كَلَّا وَٱلۡقَمَرِ﴾ ﴿وَٱلَّيۡلِ إِذۡ أَدۡبَرَ﴾ ﴿وَٱلصُّبۡحِ إِذَآ أَسۡفَرَ﴾ ﴿إِنَّهَا لَإِحۡدَى ٱلۡكُبَرِ﴾ ﴿نَذِيرٗا لِّلۡبَشَرِ﴾ ﴿لِمَن شَآءَ مِنكُمۡ أَن يَتَقَدَّمَ أَوۡ يَتَأَخَّرَ﴾ (Quran 74:32-37)

Allah swears by the moon, the night as it retreats and the morning as it brightens: **"Indeed, it (Saqar) is of the greatest matters, as a warning to humanity, to whoever wills among you to proceed or stay behind."** Every person chooses whether to advance towards good or fall behind.

## Verses 38–48: What Led You Into Saqar?
﴿كُلُّ نَفۡسِۭ بِمَا كَسَبَتۡ رَهِينَةٌ﴾ ﴿إِلَّآ أَصۡحَٰبَ ٱلۡيَمِينِ﴾ ﴿فِي جَنَّـٰتٖ يَتَسَآءَلُونَ﴾ ﴿عَنِ ٱلۡمُجۡرِمِينَ﴾ ﴿مَا سَلَكَكُمۡ فِي سَقَرَ﴾ ﴿قَالُواْ لَمۡ نَكُ مِنَ ٱلۡمُصَلِّينَ﴾ ﴿وَلَمۡ نَكُ نُطۡعِمُ ٱلۡمِسۡكِينَ﴾ ﴿وَكُنَّا نَخُوضُ مَعَ ٱلۡخَآئِضِينَ﴾ ﴿وَكُنَّا نُكَذِّبُ بِيَوۡمِ ٱلدِّينِ﴾ ﴿حَتَّىٰٓ أَتَىٰنَا ٱلۡيَقِينُ﴾ ﴿فَمَا تَنفَعُهُمۡ شَفَٰعَةُ ٱلشَّـٰفِعِينَ﴾ (Quran 74:38-48)

**"Every soul, for what it has earned, will be retained"** (held in pledge), **"except the companions of the right"**, who are freed by their good deeds.

**The conversation**: the people of Paradise ask the criminals: **"What put you into Saqar?"** They give four answers:
1. **"We were not of those who prayed."**
2. **"Nor did we used to feed the poor."**
3. **"And we used to enter into vain discourse with those who engaged in it."**
4. **"And we used to deny the Day of Recompense, until there came to us the certainty"**, death.

**"So there will not benefit them the intercession of the intercessors."**

**Notice the first answer**: abandoning prayer. In Sahih Muslim, Jabir reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Between a man and shirk and disbelief is abandoning the prayer." And notice the second: neglecting the poor. And the third: joining in vain, mocking talk, the gatherings that waste time and harm faith.

## Verses 49–56: Like Fleeing Donkeys
﴿فَمَا لَهُمۡ عَنِ ٱلتَّذۡكِرَةِ مُعۡرِضِينَ﴾ ﴿كَأَنَّهُمۡ حُمُرࣱ مُّسۡتَنفِرَةࣱ﴾ ﴿فَرَّتۡ مِن قَسۡوَرَةِۭ﴾ ﴿بَلۡ يُرِيدُ كُلُّ ٱمۡرِيٕٖ مِّنۡهُمۡ أَن يُؤۡتَىٰ صُحُفٗا مُّنَشَّرَةٗ﴾ ﴿كَلَّاۖ بَل لَّا يَخَافُونَ ٱلۡأٓخِرَةَ﴾ ﴿كَلَّآ إِنَّهُۥ تَذۡكِرَةࣱ﴾ ﴿فَمَن شَآءَ ذَكَرَهُۥ﴾ ﴿وَمَا يَذۡكُرُونَ إِلَّآ أَن يَشَآءَ ٱللَّهُۚ هُوَ أَهۡلُ ٱلتَّقۡوَىٰ وَأَهۡلُ ٱلۡمَغۡفِرَةِ﴾ (Quran 74:49-56)

**"Then what is the matter with them that they are turning away from the reminder, as if they were alarmed donkeys fleeing from a lion?"** A sharp, almost comic image of people running from the Quran in panic.

**"Rather, every person among them desires that he would be given scriptures spread out"**: they wanted personal revelations before they would believe.

**"No! Indeed, it is a reminder. Then whoever wills will remember it. And they will not remember except that Allah wills. He is worthy of being feared, and worthy to forgive."** The surah ends with two descriptions of Allah: *ahl at-taqwa*, the One who deserves to be feared, and *ahl al-maghfirah*, the One who forgives.

## Word Meanings
- **Al-Muddaththir**: the one who covers himself.
- **Andhir**: warn.
- **Kabbir**: glorify, magnify.
- **Ar-Rujz**: uncleanliness, idols.
- **Tamnun**: confer favour expecting more.
- **An-Naqur**: the Trumpet.
- **Wahid**: alone.
- **Mamdud**: extensive.
- **Qaddara**: deliberated, plotted.
- **'Abasa wa basara**: frowned and scowled.
- **Yu'thar**: imitated, handed down.
- **Saqar**: a name of Hell.
- **Lawwahah**: blackening, scorching.
- **Rahinah**: held in pledge.
- **Humur mustanfirah**: alarmed donkeys.
- **Qaswarah**: a lion, or hunters.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **Six short commands**: rhythmic, urgent and complete.
2. **The portrait of the plotter**: every expression captured in a few words.
3. **"May he be destroyed, how he deliberated"**: repeated for emphasis.
4. **The conversation between the two groups**: an unforgettable scene.
5. **Fleeing donkeys**: an image that mocks those who run from guidance.
6. **"Worthy of being feared, and worthy to forgive"**: fear and hope in the final line.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Ikhfa'**: in *fa-andhir* (verse 2), the noon sakinah is hidden before the dhal.
- **Izhar shafawi**: in *wa la tamnun* (verse 6), the meem sakinah before the noon is pronounced clearly.
- **Ghunnah**: in *innahu fakkara* (verse 18), on the noon with shaddah.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *adbara* (verse 23), the dal sakinah bounces lightly.
- **Ikhfa'**: in *mustanfirah* (verse 50), the noon sakinah is hidden before the fa'.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Al-Muzzammil before it said "Arise in the night", for private devotion, and Al-Muddaththir says "Arise and warn", for public mission. Surah Al-Qiyamah after it develops the theme of the Day of Resurrection that the deniers rejected, and the self-reproaching soul.

## The Caller's Character From the First Commands
1. **Do not stay covered**: the truth needs people who stand up.
2. **Glorify Allah**: and people will look small to you.
3. **Purify your appearance and your conduct**: people judge the message by the messenger.
4. **Leave sin**: you cannot call to what you do not live.
5. **Expect nothing in return**: your reward is with Allah.
6. **Be patient**: the road is long.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Pray**: abandoning prayer is the first reason given for entering Saqar.
2. **Feed the poor.**
3. **Avoid vain and mocking conversations.**
4. **Believe in the Day of Recompense and prepare for it.**
5. **Keep your clothes and your character clean.**
6. **Give without reminding people of your favours.**
7. **Do not run from reminders**: listen when advised.

## How to Memorise Surah Al-Muddaththir

## Teaching Children
- **Teach them the six commands** as six rules for being a good Muslim.
- **Explain purifying clothes** and keeping themselves clean for prayer.
- **Ask them the question of the people of Paradise**, and teach them the four answers to avoid.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### When was Surah Al-Muddaththir revealed?
After the pause in revelation following the first revelation in Hira, as described by Jabir in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### What does "Arise and warn" mean?
It is the command to begin the public mission and warn people.

### What does "And your clothing purify" mean?
Purify your clothes from impurity, and, according to many commentators, purify your deeds and character as well.

### Who is the man described in verses 11–25?
Many commentators said he was al-Walid ibn al-Mughirah, a leader of Quraysh who called the Quran magic.

### What is Saqar?
A name of Hell, which leaves nothing unburned.

### What are the four reasons for entering Saqar?
Not praying, not feeding the poor, engaging in vain talk with those who do, and denying the Day of Recompense.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Muddaththir?
56 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Muddaththir is the call that turned private devotion into a public mission: arise, warn, glorify your Lord, purify yourself, expect nothing, and be patient. It warns through the man who knew the truth and called it magic, and through the people of Saqar who admit they did not pray and did not feed the poor. He is worthy of being feared and worthy to forgive, so turn to Him now.
