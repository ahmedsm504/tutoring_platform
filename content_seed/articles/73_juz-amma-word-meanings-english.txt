## Meanings of Difficult Words in Juz Amma for Kids: 30 Words Explained Simply

Children memorize faster, and love the Quran more, when they understand what they're saying. Here are 30 words from the short surahs, explained in simple language based on concise, reliable tafsir. Use one or two words after your child memorizes each surah.

### An-Nas to Al-Ikhlas
| Word | Surah | Simple meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ﴿ٱلۡوَسۡوَاسِ﴾ *al-waswās* | An-Nas | Shaytan, who whispers bad thoughts |
| ﴿ٱلۡخَنَّاسِ﴾ *al-khannās* | An-Nas | The one who hides and retreats when Allah is remembered |
| ﴿ٱلۡفَلَقِ﴾ *al-falaq* | Al-Falaq | Daybreak, when morning splits the darkness |
| ﴿غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ﴾ | Al-Falaq | The night when it falls and grows dark |
| ﴿ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ فِي ٱلۡعُقَدِ﴾ | Al-Falaq | Those who practise magic by blowing on knots |
| ﴿ٱلصَّمَدُ﴾ *aṣ-ṣamad* | Al-Ikhlas | The Master whom all creation needs and turns to |
| ﴿كُفُوًا﴾ *kufuwan* | Al-Ikhlas | Equal or comparable |

| ﴿ٱلۡأَبۡتَرُ﴾ *al-abtar* | Al-Kawthar | Cut off from all good |

### At-Takathur to Ad-Duha
| Word | Surah | Simple meaning |
|---|---|---|

| ﴿ٱلۡفَرَاشِ ٱلۡمَبۡثُوثِ﴾ | Al-Qariah | Scattered moths |
| ﴿كَٱلۡعِهۡنِ ٱلۡمَنفُوشِ﴾ | Al-Qariah | Like fluffed-up wool |
| ﴿وَٱلۡعَٰدِيَٰتِ ضَبۡحًا﴾ | Al-Adiyat | Horses running fast, panting loudly |
| ﴿فَٱلۡمُورِيَٰتِ قَدۡحًا﴾ | Al-Adiyat | Striking sparks with their hooves |

### How to use this glossary
1. **One or two words per surah**, not all at once.
2. **Make it visual:** draw the birds of Al-Fil, or show your child some fluffy wool for ﴿كَٱلۡعِهۡنِ ٱلۡمَنفُوشِ﴾.
3. **Ask again two days later:** "Do you remember what *aṣ-ṣamad* means?"
4. **Check a reliable tafsir** for deeper questions rather than guessing.

## Raising Kids Connected to the Quran in the West: A Practical Guide for Muslim Families

Thousands of Muslim families live in the UK, the US, Canada, Australia and across Europe. One of their biggest concerns is the same: **how do our children grow up loving the Quran and Arabic when they spend all day in another language and culture?**

### The real challenges
- **Language:** children speak English (or another language) all day, so their Arabic weakens and some letters become hard to pronounce.
- **Time:** long school days, after-school activities and homework.
- **Environment:** the nearest mosque or Quran school may be far away.
- **Motivation:** they may not see friends memorizing around them.

### 1. Online classes: the practical solution
With online learning, your child studies with a qualified teacher from home:
- **Times in your own time zone**, after school or at weekends.
- **One-to-one sessions** adapted to your child's Arabic level.
- **No commuting, no waiting.**

### 2. Combine reading Arabic with memorization
A child who can't read Arabic well needs a foundation. The best approach combines:
- **Learning to read** with a step-by-step method such as our [Nour Al-Bayan course](/pricing/#nour-albayan).
- **Memorizing short surahs** by listening and repeating.

### 3. Give extra attention to "new" sounds
Children abroad usually struggle with ح ع ق خ غ ض ظ ص ط, and sometimes ث ذ. See [the hardest Arabic letters for non-Arabs](/blog/arabic-letters-pronunciation-for-non-arabs/#The-10-Arabic-Letters-Non-Arabs-Find-Hardest-in-Quran-Recita).

### 4. Make home an Arabic, Quran-friendly place
- **Speak Arabic at home**, even in dialect: what matters is that they hear it daily.
- **Quran in the background:** in the car, in the morning before school.
- **Stories of the Prophets** at bedtime.
- **A small Quran corner:** their own Mushaf and a star chart.

### 5. Tie the Quran to identity, not just duty
Children abroad often ask, "Why am I different?" Make the Quran part of a **positive identity**:
- Celebrate their achievements in front of family.
- Connect them with other Muslim children who memorize.
- Link Ramadan and Eid to the Quran.

### 6. A realistic weekly routine
| When | What |
|---|---|
| Two weekdays | Online class with the teacher (30–40 min) |
| Other school days | 10 minutes of review and listening |
| Weekend | A longer session or a family Quran quiz |

### 7. Use technology on your side
- **Audio homework:** your child records their recitation when it suits them and the teacher replies.
- **Quran games** in our student portal make revision fun.
- **Monthly reports** keep you informed.

### Don't wait
The younger a child starts, the easier the Arabic sounds and the stronger the bond. And if your child is starting later, it's still not too late.

## Common Mistakes Kids Make Reciting the Short Surahs (and Gentle Ways to Fix Them)

From years in the classroom, we see the same mistakes in children's recitation of the short surahs, whatever country they live in. Knowing them in advance helps you catch them early, before they become habits.

### Surah An-Nas
- ﴿ٱلۡوَسۡوَاسِ﴾: many kids make the Seen heavy, like a Ṣaad. It should be **light**.
- ﴿ٱلۡخَنَّاسِ﴾: Khaa from the top of the throat (like "ch" in *loch*), plus the nasal sound of the doubled Noon.
- ﴿ٱلنَّاسِ﴾: repeated often; keep the **2-count nasal sound** on the doubled Noon.

### Surah Al-Falaq
- ﴿ٱلۡفَلَقِ﴾ and ﴿خَلَقَ﴾: a light **bounce (qalqalah)** on the Qaaf when stopping, without adding a full vowel.
- ﴿مِن شَرِّ﴾: Noon before Sheen is **hidden (Ikhfa)**; kids often pronounce a clear "n".
- ﴿ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ﴾: the Thaa is "th" as in *think*, not "s".

### Surah Al-Ikhlas
- ﴿ٱلصَّمَدُ﴾: the Ṣaad must be **heavy**; many kids say *as-samad* with a light "s".
- ﴿كُفُوًا﴾: in Hafs it's read *kufuwan* **with a Waw**. Some children say *kufu'an* with a hamza because they've heard it that way; that's a valid reading for other reciters, but in Hafs it's with Waw.
- ﴿لَمۡ يَلِدۡ وَلَمۡ يُولَدۡ﴾: a light bounce on the silent Daal.

### Surah Al-Masad
- ﴿تَبَّتۡ يَدَآ أَبِي لَهَبٍ﴾: a **4–5 count Madd** in *yadā abī*; kids often shorten it.
- ﴿حَمَّالَةَ ٱلۡحَطَبِ﴾: Ḥaa in both words, and a heavy Ṭaa.

### Surah Al-Kawthar
- ﴿وَٱنۡحَرۡ﴾: the Noon before Ḥaa is **clear** (Izhar), and the Ḥaa must be a real Ḥaa.
- ﴿إِنَّ شَانِئَكَ﴾: pronounce the hamza in *shāniʾaka* clearly.

### Surah Al-Fil
- ﴿تَرۡمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ﴾: Meem before Baa is **hidden** with a nasal sound (Ikhfa Shafawi).
- ﴿سِجِّيلٍ﴾: doubled Jeem.
- ﴿كَعَصۡفٍ مَّأۡكُولِۭ﴾: the Tanween **merges** into the Meem with nasal sound.

### Surah Al-Asr
- ﴿وَٱلۡعَصۡرِ﴾: clear ʿAyn, heavy Ṣaad, and a heavy Raa when stopping.
- ﴿إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ﴾: the Noon in *al-insān* is **hidden** before the Seen.

### Surah Ad-Duha
- ﴿وَٱلضُّحَىٰ﴾: a real Ḍaad, not a "d".
- ﴿مَا وَدَّعَكَ﴾: doubled Daal.

### Gentle correction tips
1. **Don't interrupt every mistake.** Let them finish the verse, then fix just one thing.
2. **Model it:** say the word clearly and ask them to copy you.
3. **Make it a game:** "Who can say the Ṣaad the heaviest?"
4. **Praise progress:** "Your Ṣaad is so much better than yesterday!"
5. **Listen together** to a clear reciter like Sheikh Al-Husary's teaching recitation.

## Learning to Read the Quran as an Adult From Zero: A Step-by-Step Roadmap

Many adults, born Muslim or new to Islam, carry a quiet regret: "I never learned to read the Quran." Some feel embarrassed to start at 30, 40 or 60. Please don't be. Many of the Companions learned the Quran as adults, and today we teach adults from zero every week.

Here is a realistic roadmap.

### Stage 1: The Arabic letters (2–4 weeks)
- The **28 letters**, their names and sounds.
- How each letter changes shape at the start, middle and end of a word.
- The letters that don't exist in English get extra attention: ح ع ق خ غ ض ظ ص ط. See our guide to [the hardest letters for non-Arabs](/blog/arabic-letters-pronunciation-for-non-arabs/#The-10-Arabic-Letters-Non-Arabs-Find-Hardest-in-Quran-Recita).

### Stage 2: Short vowels and joining (3–6 weeks)
- **Fatha (a), Damma (u), Kasra (i):** the three short vowels.
- **Sukoon:** a letter with no vowel.
- Reading two- and three-letter syllables, then short words.

### Stage 3: Long vowels, Tanween and Shadda (3–6 weeks)
- **Long vowels** with Alif, Waw and Yaa.
- **Tanween** (an, un, in).
- **Shadda** (doubled letters).
- Reading complete words and short phrases from the Quran.

At the end of this stage, most adults can slowly read [Surah Al-Fatiha](/quran/1/) and the short surahs.

### Stage 4: Reading the Mushaf with basic Tajweed (2–3 months)
- Reading whole verses, slowly but correctly.
- The most frequent Tajweed rules: [Noon Sakinah and Tanween](/blog/noon-sakinah-tanween-rules-english/) and the basic [Madd rules](/blog/madd-rules-tajweed-english/).
- [Correcting your Fatiha](/blog/surah-al-fatiha-common-mistakes/) for prayer.

### Stage 5: Fluency (ongoing)
- Daily reading builds speed and confidence.
- Many students start memorizing short surahs at this point.

### How long does it take overall?
| Goal | Typical timeline (2 classes/week + daily practice) |
|---|---|
| Recognise and pronounce all letters | 1 month |
| Read short surahs slowly | 2–4 months |
| Read the Mushaf with basic Tajweed | 6–9 months |

### Tips for adult learners
1. **15 minutes a day beats 2 hours on Sunday.** Consistency is everything.
2. **Don't be embarrassed by mistakes.** The Prophet ﷺ said that the one who recites the Quran with difficulty, stumbling over it, gets a **double reward** (Bukhari and Muslim).
3. **One-to-one classes** remove the fear of reading in front of others.
4. **Listen a lot.** Your ear learns before your tongue. Our [Quran pages](/quran/) let you listen verse by verse and repeat.
5. **Learn the meaning alongside.** Read a translation of what you recite.
