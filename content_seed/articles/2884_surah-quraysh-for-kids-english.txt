Do you have food in your fridge today? Do you sleep at night in a home where you feel safe? If yes, then you have two of the biggest blessings in the world. Many people on earth don't have enough food, and many don't feel safe. Surah Quraysh reminds us of these two blessings, food and safety, and tells us how to thank Allah for them: by worshipping Him.

Surah Quraysh is about the tribe of the Prophet ﷺ, the Quraysh of Makkah. Allah had given them something special: safe, successful trade journeys every winter and every summer, plenty of food, and safety in a land where other tribes were often attacked. So Allah told them: Worship the Lord of this House, the One who fed you against hunger and made you safe from fear. In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Quraysh to children in simple English, with activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Quraysh

- **Position**: surah 106, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: four.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Name**: after the tribe of Quraysh, the tribe of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, who lived in Makkah and looked after the Kaaba.

## The Whole Surah

﴿لِإِيلَٰفِ قُرَيۡشٍ﴾ ﴿إِۦلَٰفِهِمۡ رِحۡلَةَ ٱلشِّتَآءِ وَٱلصَّيۡفِ﴾ ﴿فَلۡيَعۡبُدُواْ رَبَّ هَٰذَا ٱلۡبَيۡتِ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِيٓ أَطۡعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٖ وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنۡ خَوۡفِۭ﴾ (Quran 106:1-4)

## Who Were the Quraysh?

### Explaining it to your child
"Quraysh was a big Arab tribe living in Makkah. The Prophet ﷺ was born into this tribe. They were respected by all the Arabs because they lived next to the Kaaba and looked after the pilgrims who came to visit it. Makkah is in a dry valley with no farms, so the Quraysh couldn't grow much food. Instead, they became traders."

## Verses 1–2: The Winter and Summer Journeys

### Explaining it to your child
"Ilaf means being used to something and feeling comfortable with it. The Quraysh were used to two big trade journeys every year:
- **In winter**, they travelled south to Yemen, where it was warmer.
- **In summer**, they travelled north to Syria, where it was cooler.
They took goods to sell and brought back food, cloth and other things Makkah needed."

### Why were their journeys safe?
"In those days, travelling across the desert was dangerous. Robbers attacked caravans. But people respected the Quraysh because they were 'the people of the Kaaba', so their caravans were usually left in peace. That was a blessing from Allah."

### Activity: the journey map
Draw a simple map of Arabia with your child: Makkah in the middle, an arrow south to Yemen labelled "Winter", and an arrow north to Syria labelled "Summer". Draw camels carrying goods. Say: "Allah made these journeys safe and easy for them."

### Activity: seasons
Talk about winter and summer where you live. What do you wear? What do you eat? Then explain that the Quraysh planned their travel around the seasons, choosing warm Yemen in winter and cooler Syria in summer. Smart planning!

## The Link With Surah Al-Fil

Surah Quraysh comes right after Surah Al-Fil, and many scholars say they are closely connected. In Al-Fil, Allah protected the Kaaba from the elephant army. After that, the Arabs respected the Quraysh even more as "the people of Allah's House", and their journeys became even safer.

### Explaining it to your child
"First Allah protected the Kaaba from the elephant army. Because of that, everyone respected the people of Makkah, and their trade journeys became safe. So Surah Al-Fil and Surah Quraysh are like two parts of one story!"

## Verse 3: So Let Them Worship the Lord of This House

### Explaining it to your child
"After reminding them of these blessings, Allah says: So let them worship the Lord of this House, meaning the Kaaba. The Quraysh lived next to the Kaaba and were proud of it, but many of them worshipped idols. Allah was telling them: the One who gave you all this is the Lord of this House. Worship Him alone!"

### Lesson for kids: blessings mean gratitude
"When someone gives you a gift, you say thank you. Allah gives us food, safety, family and so much more, and our 'thank you' is to worship Him: to pray, to obey Him, and to be kind."

## Verse 4: Who Fed Them Against Hunger and Made Them Safe From Fear

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah reminds the Quraysh of two blessings:
- **Food**: they lived in a dry valley, but Allah brought them food from far away.
- **Safety**: other tribes were often attacked, but the Quraysh were safe.
These are two of the most important things any person needs."

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever wakes up safe in his home, healthy in his body, and has food for his day, it is as if the whole world has been given to him" (at-Tirmidhi, who graded it hasan).

### Explaining it to your child
"If you wake up safe, healthy, and with food for today, the Prophet ﷺ said it's like you own the whole world! Most of us have all three, so we should be very grateful."

## Activities on Gratitude

### Activity: the three treasures
Each morning for a week, ask your child: "Are you safe? Are you healthy? Do you have food for today?" Each "yes" earns a sticker on a chart titled "My Three Treasures". At the end of the week, talk about how lucky they are.

### Activity: where does our food come from?
At dinner, pick one item, like rice, and trace where it might have come from: a farm in another country, a ship, a truck, a shop. Say: "Like the Quraysh, our food travels a long way to reach us. Allah arranged all of that."

### Activity: sharing food
Help your child pack a small food bag for a food bank, a neighbour or a family in need. Explain: "Allah fed us against hunger, so we help feed others."

### Activity: a safety prayer
Teach your child the du'a for leaving the house: "Bismillah, tawakkaltu 'alallah, wa la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah" (Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi, who graded it hasan sahih). Explain that we ask Allah for safety every time we go out.

## Lessons for Kids

### Allah provides
"Makkah had no farms, but Allah made sure the Quraysh had food. Allah is Ar-Razzaq, the Provider. He finds ways to bring us what we need."

### Safety is a gift
"Being safe at home, at school and in the street is a huge blessing. Some children in the world don't have it. We thank Allah for it and pray for those who are suffering."

### Remember the Giver, not just the gift
"The Quraysh enjoyed the gifts but forgot the Giver. We try to remember Allah every time we enjoy something: Alhamdulillah for my food, Alhamdulillah for my home."

## Gratitude Around the World

Talk with your child about children in other parts of the world who don't have enough food or don't feel safe because of war or disasters. Keep it gentle and age-appropriate, and focus on what we can do.

### Explaining it to your child
"Some children in the world go to bed hungry, or have to leave their homes because it isn't safe. When we remember them, two things happen: we feel more grateful for what Allah has given us, and we want to help. We can make du'a for them, give charity, and never waste food."

### Activity: the no-waste week
For one week, challenge the whole family to waste no food: take small portions first, save leftovers for the next meal, and share extra food with neighbours. At the end of the week, talk about how it felt. Remind your child that the Prophet ﷺ taught us to pick up a fallen morsel, clean it and eat it, and not leave it for Shaytan (Muslim).

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Empty Lunchbox"

One day, Bilal opened his lunchbox at school and found it empty. His mother had forgotten to pack his sandwich in the morning rush. His tummy rumbled, and he felt grumpy. "This is the worst day ever," he muttered.

His friend Musa saw him and immediately broke his own sandwich in half. "Here, take this." Another friend gave him an apple. By the end of lunch, Bilal had eaten more than usual!

At home, Bilal told his mother, who apologized and hugged him. Then she said: "You know, today you felt hungry for a few hours. Some children feel like that every day." She opened the mushaf and read Surah Quraysh with him: "Alladhi at'amahum min ju'in wa amanahum min khawf." "Allah fed them against hunger and made them safe from fear."

Bilal thought for a while. "Mum, I always have food. I never really thought about it." His mother said: "And today Allah sent you food through Musa's kindness. Allah provides in many ways."

The next day, Bilal asked his mother to pack an extra sandwich. "In case someone else forgets theirs," he said. And from then on, before every meal, he said a little "Alhamdulillah" a bit more carefully than before.

Ask your child: "How did Bilal feel when his lunchbox was empty? How did Allah send him food? What did he decide to do the next day?"

## What We Learn From Surah Quraysh

- **The Quraysh** were the tribe of the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Allah made their journeys** safe and successful.
- **Surah Al-Fil and Surah Quraysh** are connected.
- **We worship the Lord of the Kaaba**, Allah alone.
- **Food and safety** are huge blessings.
- **Waking up safe, healthy and fed** is like owning the world.
- **Remember the Giver**, not just the gift.
- **Share food** with others.

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Why does the surah begin with "li", meaning "for"?
Scholars explain it connects to the meaning of worship in verse 3, as if to say: because of the blessing of these journeys, let them worship the Lord of this House. Some also connect it with Surah Al-Fil before it.

### Is Surah Quraysh good for young children?
Yes. It's short, has a clear story and links to everyday blessings like food and safety, which children understand well.

### How can I teach gratitude without making my child feel guilty?
Focus on joy rather than guilt: "Look how much Allah has given us!" And give them active ways to share, so gratitude turns into kindness.
