The Quran has **114 surahs**. To make reading and memorization easier, scholars divided it into **30 roughly equal parts**, each called a **juz** (plural **ajza'**). Each juz is split into **two hizbs**, and each hizb into **four quarters**, giving **60 hizbs** and **240 quarters**. In the standard Madinah Mushaf the Quran fills **604 pages**, and a juz usually covers **20 pages**.

The juz division is a **practical arrangement** made for ease of reading. The order of verses within each surah, by contrast, was fixed by the Prophet ﷺ. That is why some juz begin in the middle of a surah, and occasionally in the middle of a story.

This guide is a **complete map** of all thirty: the popular name memorizers use for each juz, its opening verse, its boundaries, its pages in the Madinah Mushaf, and its main themes in a few lines. Under each juz you will find a link to a **full English guide** to that juz, covering its sections, key verses, tajweed points, similar verses and memorization tips.

## Why a map of the juz helps
- **For reading**: reading one juz a day completes the Quran in a month, the most popular khatm in Ramadan. Knowing the themes of a juz before you read it turns reading into reflection. See [How to Finish the Quran: Khatm Schedules for a Year, a Month or a Week](/blog/quran-khatm-schedules-english/).
- **For memorization**: memorizers learn and revise by the juz, and teachers test by asking "recite from the start of Juz such-and-such".
- **For understanding**: once you see the Quran as a sequence of parts with clear themes, the Mushaf becomes a connected map in your mind rather than a run of separate pages.

## Juz 1 to 10: the first long surahs
These ten parts contain **Al-Baqarah, Al Imran, An-Nisa, Al-Ma'idah, Al-An'am, Al-A'raf, Al-Anfal** and the first part of **At-Tawbah**. They lay the foundations of belief and law, build the community, and tell the long stories of earlier prophets.

### Juz 1: Al-Fatiha and Alif Lam Mim
It opens with **Surah Al-Fatiha**: ﴿بِسۡمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ ﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾, "In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate. All praise is for Allah, Lord of the worlds." It then runs through **Al-Baqarah to verse 141**, on **pages 1 to 21**.

Its big idea is **guidance**: asked for in Al-Fatiha, then given in Al-Baqarah. It describes three kinds of people (believers, disbelievers and hypocrites), makes the first call to all humanity to worship Allah, tells the story of **Adam**, addresses the Children of Israel at length, and closes with **Ibrahim** building the Kaaba.

Read the full guide: [Juz 1 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-1-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 2: Sayaqul
It begins with ﴿سَيَقُولُ ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَا وَلَّىٰهُمۡ عَن قِبۡلَتِهِمُ ٱلَّتِي كَانُواْ عَلَيۡهَاۚ﴾, "The foolish among the people will say: what has turned them away from the qibla they used to face?" It lies entirely within **Al-Baqarah, 2:142 to 2:252**, on **pages 22 to 41**.

Its theme is **building the middle nation**: the change of qibla, patience in trials, the verse of righteousness, and a series of laws on retribution and bequests, **fasting**, **Hajj**, marriage, divorce and nursing, ending with the story of **Talut and Jalut**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 2 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-2-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 3: Tilka ar-Rusul
It begins with ﴿تِلۡكَ ٱلرُّسُلُ فَضَّلۡنَا بَعۡضَهُمۡ عَلَىٰ بَعۡضٖۘ﴾, "Those messengers, We preferred some of them over others." It runs from **Al-Baqarah 2:253 to Al Imran 3:92**, on **pages 42 to 61**.

It contains **Ayat al-Kursi**, three scenes of bringing the dead to life, the parables of spending, the prohibition of **riba**, the **debt verse** (the longest verse in the Quran) and the closing verses of Al-Baqarah. Then **Al Imran** opens with the clear and the ambiguous verses and the story of the **family of Imran**: Maryam, Zakariya, Yahya and 'Isa. It ends with ﴿لَن تَنَالُواْ ٱلۡبِرَّ حَتَّىٰ تُنفِقُواْ مِمَّا تُحِبُّونَۚ﴾, "You will never attain righteousness until you spend from what you love."

Read the full guide: [Juz 3 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-3-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 4: Lan Tanalu
It begins with ﴿۞كُلُّ ٱلطَّعَامِ كَانَ حِلّٗا لِّبَنِيٓ إِسۡرَـٰٓءِيلَ﴾, "All food was lawful to the Children of Israel." Many memorizers call it **Juz Lan Tanalu** after the famous verse just before it. It runs from **Al Imran 3:93 to An-Nisa 4:23**, on **pages 62 to 81**.

It covers the Sacred House and Hajj, holding fast to the rope of Allah, the **best nation raised for mankind**, the **Battle of Uhud** and its lessons in about sixty verses, and the people of understanding at the close of Al Imran. Then **An-Nisa** opens with orphans, **inheritance** and the women one may not marry.

Read the full guide: [Juz 4 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-4-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 5: Wal-Muhsanat
It begins with ﴿۞وَٱلۡمُحۡصَنَٰتُ مِنَ ٱلنِّسَآءِ إِلَّا مَا مَلَكَتۡ أَيۡمَٰنُكُمۡۖ﴾, "And forbidden are married women, except those your right hands possess." It lies entirely within **An-Nisa, verses 24 to 147**, on **pages 82 to 101**.

Its theme is **justice in every relationship**: completing the rules of marriage, the family in conflict, the **verse of ten rights**, returning trusts, obeying Allah and His Messenger, fighting to defend the oppressed, migration and the **fear prayer**, standing for justice even against oneself, and the traits of the **hypocrites**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 5 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-5-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 6: La Yuhibbullah
It begins with ﴿۞لَّا يُحِبُّ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡجَهۡرَ بِٱلسُّوٓءِ مِنَ ٱلۡقَوۡلِ إِلَّا مَن ظُلِمَۚ﴾, "Allah does not like the public mention of evil, except by one who has been wronged." It runs from **An-Nisa 4:148 to Al-Ma'idah 5:81**, on **pages 102 to 120**.

Its big idea is **the covenant**: the raising of **'Isa**, the chain of revelation to the prophets, and the verse of kalalah closing An-Nisa. Then **Al-Ma'idah** opens with fulfilling contracts, the perfection of the religion, purification, the wandering in the desert, the story of the **two sons of Adam**, and judging by what Allah revealed.

Read the full guide: [Juz 6 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-6-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 7: Latajidanna
It begins with ﴿۞لَتَجِدَنَّ أَشَدَّ ٱلنَّاسِ عَدَٰوَةٗ لِّلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱلۡيَهُودَ وَٱلَّذِينَ أَشۡرَكُواْۖ﴾, "You will surely find the most intense of people in animosity towards the believers to be the Jews and the polytheists." Some call it **Wa Idha Sami'u**. It runs from **Al-Ma'idah 5:82 to Al-An'am 6:110**, on **pages 121 to 141**.

It describes those whose eyes overflow with tears on hearing the Quran, the final prohibition of wine, the **Table Spread** and 'Isa's last dialogue. Then **Al-An'am**: proofs of Allah's oneness, the **keys of the unseen**, **Ibrahim's** argument with his people, and eighteen prophets named in one passage.

Read the full guide: [Juz 7 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-7-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 8: Wa Law Annana
It begins with ﴿۞وَلَوۡ أَنَّنَا نَزَّلۡنَآ إِلَيۡهِمُ ٱلۡمَلَـٰٓئِكَةَ وَكَلَّمَهُمُ ٱلۡمَوۡتَىٰ﴾, "Even if We had sent down angels to them and the dead had spoken to them." It runs from **Al-An'am 6:111 to Al-A'raf 7:87**, on **pages 142 to 161**.

It covers the enemies of the prophets, the parable of life and light, the refutation of idolaters' superstitions about cattle and crops, the **ten commandments** and the straight path. Then **Al-A'raf** opens with **Adam and Iblis**, the calls "O children of Adam", the **people of the heights**, and five prophets with their nations: Nuh, Hud, Salih, Lut and Shu'ayb.

Read the full guide: [Juz 8 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-8-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 9: Qal al-Mala'
It begins with ﴿۞قَالَ ٱلۡمَلَأُ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسۡتَكۡبَرُواْ مِن قَوۡمِهِۦ لَنُخۡرِجَنَّكَ يَٰشُعَيۡبُ﴾, "The arrogant chiefs of his people said: we will surely drive you out, O Shu'ayb." It runs from **Al-A'raf 7:88 to Al-Anfal 8:40**, on **pages 162 to 181**.

It finishes the story of **Shu'ayb**, sets out Allah's laws for towns, and gives the longest account of **Musa and Pharaoh** in the Quran, then the Sabbath-breakers, the **primordial covenant**, the **Beautiful Names**, and the first prostration of recitation in the Mushaf at the end of Al-A'raf. Then **Al-Anfal** opens with the **Battle of Badr**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 9 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-9-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 10: Wa'lamu
It begins with ﴿۞وَٱعۡلَمُوٓاْ أَنَّمَا غَنِمۡتُم مِّن شَيۡءٖ فَأَنَّ لِلَّهِ خُمُسَهُۥ وَلِلرَّسُولِ﴾, "And know that whatever you take as spoils, one fifth belongs to Allah and the Messenger." It runs from **Al-Anfal 8:41 to At-Tawbah 9:92**, on **pages 182 to 200**.

It covers the division of spoils, the rules of steadfastness, peace and alliances. Then **At-Tawbah**, the only surah that does not begin with the basmalah: the declaration of disavowal, **Hunayn**, the **cave** of the Hijrah, the **Tabuk** expedition, the exposure of the hypocrites, and the **eight recipients of zakah**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 10 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-10-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

## Juz 11 to 20: surahs of stories and the call
Most surahs in these ten parts are **Makki**, and they are rich in the stories of the prophets: Nuh, Hud, Salih, Ibrahim, Lut, Shu'ayb, Yusuf and Musa, with **Al-Kahf**, **Maryam**, **Ta-Ha** and **Al-Anbiya'** at their heart.

### Juz 11: Ya'tadhirun
It begins with ﴿۞إِنَّمَا ٱلسَّبِيلُ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ يَسۡتَـٔۡذِنُونَكَ وَهُمۡ أَغۡنِيَآءُۚ﴾, "The blame is only on those who ask your permission while they are rich", and is named **Ya'tadhirun** after the next verse. It runs from **At-Tawbah 9:93 to Hud 11:5**, on **pages 201 to 221**.

It describes the forerunners, the mosque of harm and the mosque of taqwa, the **profitable bargain**: ﴿۞إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱشۡتَرَىٰ مِنَ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ أَنفُسَهُمۡ وَأَمۡوَٰلَهُم﴾, "Allah has purchased from the believers their lives and their wealth", and the **three who stayed behind**. Then all of **Surah Yunus**: signs in creation, the parable of worldly life, the allies of Allah, Pharaoh's drowning and the people of Yunus.

Read the full guide: [Juz 11 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-11-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 12: Wa Ma Min Dabbah
It begins with ﴿۞وَمَا مِن دَآبَّةٖ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ إِلَّا عَلَى ٱللَّهِ رِزۡقُهَا﴾, "There is no creature on earth but that its provision is from Allah." It runs from **Hud 11:6 to Yusuf 12:52**, on **pages 222 to 241**.

It begins with provision for every creature, then the line of prophets in **Hud**: Nuh and the flood, Hud, Salih, Ibrahim's guests, Lut and Shu'ayb, with the great command ﴿فَٱسۡتَقِمۡ كَمَآ أُمِرۡتَ﴾, "So stand firm as you have been commanded." Then the first half of the story of **Yusuf**, from his dream to his vindication while in prison.

Read the full guide: [Juz 12 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-12-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 13: Wa Ma Ubarri'u
It begins with ﴿۞وَمَآ أُبَرِّئُ نَفۡسِيٓۚ إِنَّ ٱلنَّفۡسَ لَأَمَّارَةُۢ بِٱلسُّوٓءِ إِلَّا مَا رَحِمَ رَبِّيٓۚ﴾, "And I do not acquit myself. The soul is ever inclined to evil, except those upon whom my Lord has mercy." It runs from **Yusuf 12:53 to the end of Ibrahim**, on **pages 242 to 261**.

It completes the story of **Yusuf** up to his reunion with Ya'qub, then **Ar-Ra'd** with its signs, the parable of the foam, and **hearts at rest in the remembrance of Allah**. Then **Surah Ibrahim**: gratitude and the increase of blessings, the **good word**, and Ibrahim's prayer by the House.

Read the full guide: [Juz 13 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-13-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 14: Rubama
It opens with **Surah Al-Hijr**: ﴿الٓرۚ تِلۡكَ ءَايَٰتُ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ وَقُرۡءَانٖ مُّبِينٖ﴾, "Alif Lam Ra. These are the verses of the Book and a clear Quran", and is named **Rubama** after its second verse: ﴿رُّبَمَا يَوَدُّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ لَوۡ كَانُواْ مُسۡلِمِينَ﴾, "Perhaps those who disbelieve will wish they had been Muslims." It contains two complete surahs, **Al-Hijr** and **An-Nahl**, on **pages 262 to 281**.

Al-Hijr speaks of the **preservation of the Reminder**, Adam and Iblis, Ibrahim's guests, the people of al-Hijr and the **Seven Oft-Repeated**. **An-Nahl** is the surah of blessings: cattle, rain, the sea, bees and honey, the **verse of justice and excellence**, the **good life**, and calling to Allah with wisdom.

Read the full guide: [Juz 14 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-14-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 15: Subhan
It begins with ﴿سُبۡحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَسۡرَىٰ بِعَبۡدِهِۦ لَيۡلٗا مِّنَ ٱلۡمَسۡجِدِ ٱلۡحَرَامِ إِلَى ٱلۡمَسۡجِدِ ٱلۡأَقۡصَا ٱلَّذِي بَٰرَكۡنَا حَوۡلَهُۥ﴾, "Glory be to the One who took His servant by night from al-Masjid al-Haram to al-Masjid al-Aqsa, whose surroundings We have blessed." By count of parts, it begins the **second half** of the Quran. It contains all of **Al-Isra'** and **Al-Kahf to verse 74**, on **pages 282 to 301**.

Al-Isra' covers the **Night Journey**, the commandments on honouring parents and good character, the honour of the children of Adam, the prayer times, and the question about the **spirit**. Al-Kahf then tells of the **youths of the cave**, the owner of two gardens, and the beginning of **Musa's journey with al-Khidr**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 15 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-15-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 16: Qal Alam
It begins with ﴿۞قَالَ أَلَمۡ أَقُل لَّكَ إِنَّكَ لَن تَسۡتَطِيعَ مَعِيَ صَبۡرٗا﴾, "He said: did I not tell you that you would never be able to be patient with me?" It runs from **Al-Kahf 18:75 to the end of Ta-Ha**, with all of **Maryam** in between, on **pages 302 to 321**.

It completes Musa's journey with al-Khidr and tells of **Dhul-Qarnayn** and Ya'juj and Ma'juj. Then **Maryam**: Zakariya and Yahya, Maryam and 'Isa, and Ibrahim with his father. Then **Ta-Ha**: the fullest account of Musa, from the sacred valley to the sea, and then the story of Adam.

Read the full guide: [Juz 16 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-16-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 17: Iqtaraba
It begins with ﴿ٱقۡتَرَبَ لِلنَّاسِ حِسَابُهُمۡ وَهُمۡ فِي غَفۡلَةٖ مُّعۡرِضُونَ﴾, "Mankind's reckoning has drawn near, while they turn away in heedlessness." It contains two complete surahs, **Al-Anbiya'** and **Al-Hajj**, on **pages 322 to 341**.

Al-Anbiya' presents a procession of **eighteen prophets** and their answered prayers, among them Ayyub, Yunus and Zakariya. Al-Hajj describes the **quake of the Hour**, the rites of the House, the **permission to fight**, and the **parable of the fly**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 17 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-17-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 18: Qad Aflaha
It begins with ﴿قَدۡ أَفۡلَحَ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنُونَ﴾, "The believers have succeeded." It contains all of **Al-Mu'minun** and **An-Nur**, and **Al-Furqan to verse 20**, on **pages 342 to 361**.

Al-Mu'minun lists the **traits of the successful believers**, the stages of human creation and the line of messengers. An-Nur gives the rulings on chastity, the **slander against 'A'ishah**, the **Verse of Light** and the etiquette of asking permission. The opening of Al-Furqan defends the Quran and the Messenger.

Read the full guide: [Juz 18 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-18-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 19: Wa Qala Alladhina
It begins with ﴿۞وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَرۡجُونَ لِقَآءَنَا﴾, "And those who do not expect to meet Us said." It runs from **Al-Furqan 25:21 to An-Naml 27:55**, with all of **Ash-Shu'ara'** in between, on **pages 362 to 381**.

Al-Furqan describes the **servants of the Most Merciful**. Ash-Shu'ara' tells the stories of seven prophets, each closing with the same refrain, and records **Ibrahim's** prayer and the **sound heart**. An-Naml then opens with **Sulayman**, the ant, the hoopoe and the **Queen of Sheba**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 19 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-19-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 20: Amman Khalaq
It begins with ﴿۞فَمَا كَانَ جَوَابَ قَوۡمِهِۦٓ إِلَّآ أَن قَالُوٓاْ أَخۡرِجُوٓاْ ءَالَ لُوطٖ مِّن قَرۡيَتِكُمۡۖ﴾, "The only answer of his people was to say: expel the family of Lut from your town." It is popularly known as **Amman Khalaq** because its first pages contain ﴿أَمَّنۡ خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضَ﴾, "Or who created the heavens and the earth?" It runs from **An-Naml 27:56 to Al-'Ankabut 29:45**, with all of **Al-Qasas** in between, on **pages 382 to 401**.

It contains the repeated question at the close of An-Naml, ﴿أَءِلَٰهٞ مَّعَ ٱللَّهِۚ﴾, "Is there a god with Allah?", then **Al-Qasas**: the life of **Musa** from infancy to Madyan and back, and the story of **Qarun**. Al-'Ankabut then opens on **trials** and the **spider's house**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 20 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-20-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

## Juz 21 to 30: the Mathani, the Hawamim and the Mufassal
In these final ten parts the surahs grow steadily shorter. They include the seven **Hawamim**, then the **Mufassal**, which on the best-known view begins at **Surah Qaf**, ending with the short surahs of Juz 'Amma.

### Juz 21: Wa La Tujadilu
It begins with ﴿۞وَلَا تُجَٰدِلُوٓاْ أَهۡلَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ إِلَّا بِٱلَّتِي هِيَ أَحۡسَنُ﴾, "Do not argue with the People of the Book except in the best manner." It runs from **Al-'Ankabut 29:46 to Al-Ahzab 33:30**, with **Ar-Rum**, **Luqman** and **As-Sajdah** complete in between, on **pages 402 to 421**.

After the close of Al-'Ankabut comes Ar-Rum with its **prophecy of the Romans' victory**, signs in creation and the **fitrah**, then **Luqman's advice to his son**, then As-Sajdah, then the opening of Al-Ahzab with the **Battle of the Trench** and the **excellent example** of the Prophet ﷺ.

Read the full guide: [Juz 21 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-21-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 22: Wa Man Yaqnut
It begins with ﴿۞وَمَن يَقۡنُتۡ مِنكُنَّ لِلَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِۦ وَتَعۡمَلۡ صَٰلِحٗا نُّؤۡتِهَآ أَجۡرَهَا مَرَّتَيۡنِ﴾, "Whoever of you is devoutly obedient to Allah and His Messenger and does good, We will give her reward twice." It runs from **Al-Ahzab 33:31 to Ya-Sin 36:27**, with **Saba'** and **Fatir** complete in between, on **pages 422 to 441**.

It covers the etiquette of the Prophet's household, **sending blessings on the Prophet ﷺ** and the **Trust**. Saba' tells of the family of **Dawud** and the great flood; Fatir speaks of **mankind's need of Allah** and the heirs of the Book. Then comes the opening of **Ya-Sin**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 22 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-22-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 23: Wa Mali
It begins with ﴿۞وَمَآ أَنزَلۡنَا عَلَىٰ قَوۡمِهِۦ مِنۢ بَعۡدِهِۦ مِن جُندٖ مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَمَا كُنَّا مُنزِلِينَ﴾, "And We did not send down upon his people after him any army from the sky", and is popularly known as **Wa Mali** after a verse just before it: ﴿وَمَالِيَ لَآ أَعۡبُدُ ٱلَّذِي فَطَرَنِي وَإِلَيۡهِ تُرۡجَعُونَ﴾, "Why should I not worship the One who created me, and to whom you will be returned?" It runs from **Ya-Sin 36:28 to Az-Zumar 39:31**, on **pages 442 to 461**.

It contains the rest of Ya-Sin with signs in creation and scenes of resurrection, then **As-Saffat** with the story of the **sacrifice**, then **Sad** with Dawud, Sulayman, Ayyub and Iblis, and finally the opening of Az-Zumar on **devoting religion sincerely to Allah**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 23 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-23-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 24: Fa Man Azlam
It begins with ﴿۞فَمَنۡ أَظۡلَمُ مِمَّن كَذَبَ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ وَكَذَّبَ بِٱلصِّدۡقِ إِذۡ جَآءَهُۥٓۚ﴾, "Who does greater wrong than one who lies about Allah and denies the truth when it comes to him?" It runs from **Az-Zumar 39:32 to Fussilat 41:46**, with all of **Ghafir** in between, on **pages 462 to 481**.

It holds the **verse of hope**: ﴿۞قُلۡ يَٰعِبَادِيَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسۡرَفُواْ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ لَا تَقۡنَطُواْ مِن رَّحۡمَةِ ٱللَّهِۚ﴾, "Say: O My servants who have wronged themselves, do not despair of Allah's mercy", and the scene of people led in groups to Paradise and Hell. Ghafir tells of the **believer from Pharaoh's family** and the prayer of the Throne-bearers. Fussilat opens with creation, the **testimony of the skins**, and **steadfastness**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 24 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-24-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 25: Ilayhi Yuradd
It begins with ﴿۞إِلَيۡهِ يُرَدُّ عِلۡمُ ٱلسَّاعَةِۚ﴾, "To Him is referred knowledge of the Hour." It runs from **Fussilat 41:47 to the end of Al-Jathiyah**, with four complete Hawamim: **Ash-Shura**, **Az-Zukhruf**, **Ad-Dukhan** and **Al-Jathiyah**, on **pages 482 to 501**.

Ash-Shura speaks of the **one religion** and the traits of believers; Az-Zukhruf of worldly adornment and Pharaoh; Ad-Dukhan of the **blessed night** and Pharaoh's end; and Al-Jathiyah of the subjected universe and the one who takes his desire as his god.

Read the full guide: [Juz 25 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-25-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 26: Al-Ahqaf
It opens with **Surah Al-Ahqaf**, the last of the Hawamim: ﴿حمٓ﴾ ﴿تَنزِيلُ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡعَزِيزِ ٱلۡحَكِيمِ﴾, "Ha Mim. The revelation of the Book is from Allah, the Almighty, the All-Wise." It contains **Al-Ahqaf**, **Muhammad**, **Al-Fath**, **Al-Hujurat** and **Qaf** in full, and **Adh-Dhariyat to verse 30**, on **pages 502 to 521**.

It covers **honouring parents** and the jinn listening to the Quran, then Surah Muhammad on fighting and reflection, then Al-Fath with **Hudaybiyah** and the **Pledge of Ridwan**, then Al-Hujurat with the manners of society, then Qaf on death and the ever-present watcher.

Read the full guide: [Juz 26 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-26-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 27: Qala Fama Khatbukum
It begins with ﴿۞قَالَ فَمَا خَطۡبُكُمۡ أَيُّهَا ٱلۡمُرۡسَلُونَ﴾, "He said: then what is your business, O messengers?", and is also called **Juz Adh-Dhariyat**. It runs from **Adh-Dhariyat 51:31 to the end of Al-Hadid**, with **At-Tur**, **An-Najm**, **Al-Qamar**, **Ar-Rahman** and **Al-Waqi'ah** complete in between, on **pages 522 to 541**.

It states the **purpose of creation**: ﴿وَمَا خَلَقۡتُ ٱلۡجِنَّ وَٱلۡإِنسَ إِلَّا لِيَعۡبُدُونِ﴾, "I did not create jinn and mankind except to worship Me." Then At-Tur; An-Najm with the Ascension and its prostration; Al-Qamar with the splitting of the moon and the **Quran made easy to remember**; Ar-Rahman and its favours; Al-Waqi'ah and its three groups; and Al-Hadid on **spending and humility**.

Read the full guide: [Juz 27 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-27-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 28: Qad Sami'
It begins with ﴿قَدۡ سَمِعَ ٱللَّهُ قَوۡلَ ٱلَّتِي تُجَٰدِلُكَ فِي زَوۡجِهَا وَتَشۡتَكِيٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ﴾, "Allah has heard the words of the woman who pleads with you about her husband and complains to Allah." It contains nine complete surahs, all **Madani**: Al-Mujadilah, Al-Hashr, Al-Mumtahanah, As-Saff, Al-Jumu'ah, Al-Munafiqun, At-Taghabun, At-Talaq and At-Tahrim, on **pages 542 to 561**.

Its theme is **building the Muslim community**: the ruling on zihar and the manners of gatherings, the expulsion of Banu an-Nadir and the traits of the Muhajirun and Ansar, loyalty and disavowal, the **Friday prayer**, the traits of the hypocrites, the rules of divorce, and the Prophet's household in At-Tahrim.

Read the full guide: [Juz 28 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-28-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 29: Tabarak
It begins with ﴿تَبَٰرَكَ ٱلَّذِي بِيَدِهِ ٱلۡمُلۡكُ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيۡءٖ قَدِيرٌ﴾, "Blessed is He in whose hand is the dominion, and He has power over all things." It contains eleven complete surahs, from **Al-Mulk** to **Al-Mursalat**, on **pages 562 to 581**.

Its themes are dominion and testing, the **great character** of the Prophet ﷺ, the Inevitable Reality, anxious man, Nuh's long call, the jinn who believed, **night prayer**, the Day of Resurrection, and the reward of the righteous.

Read the full guide: [Juz 29 of the Quran: Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-29-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

### Juz 30: Juz 'Amma
It begins with ﴿عَمَّ يَتَسَآءَلُونَ﴾ ﴿عَنِ ٱلنَّبَإِ ٱلۡعَظِيمِ﴾, "About what are they asking one another? About the great news." It is the last juz and contains **thirty-seven surahs**, from **An-Naba'** to **An-Nas**, more than any other juz, on **pages 582 to 604**.

Its themes are scenes of the Resurrection, faith and trial, the soul and its two paths, Allah's favours on His Prophet ﷺ, deeds and reckoning, and the short comprehensive surahs: Al-'Asr, Al-Kawthar, Al-Ikhlas and the two Mu'awwidhat. It is the first part most Muslims memorize and the most often recited in prayer.

Read the full guide: [Juz 30 of the Quran (Juz Amma): Summary, Themes and Key Verses](/blog/juz-30-of-the-quran-summary-english/).

## Patterns worth noticing
- **Parts that begin with a new surah**: Juz 1 (Al-Fatiha), 14 (Al-Hijr), 15 (Al-Isra'), 17 (Al-Anbiya'), 18 (Al-Mu'minun), 26 (Al-Ahqaf), 28 (Al-Mujadilah), 29 (Al-Mulk) and 30 (An-Naba'). Their starts are the easiest to remember.
- **Parts that lie within a single surah**: Juz 2 (Al-Baqarah) and Juz 5 (An-Nisa).
- **Parts that begin in the middle of a story**: Juz 13 begins in the middle of Yusuf's story, Juz 16 in the middle of Musa's journey with al-Khidr, Juz 20 in the middle of Lut's story, and Juz 23 in the middle of the story of the man in Ya-Sin. Read the last page of the previous juz before you start, so the meaning stays connected.
- **Names of the parts** usually come from their first words, though some are named after a famous verse near the start, as with Lan Tanalu, Ya'tadhirun, Amman Khalaq and Wa Mali.
- **The ۞ sign** at the start of a verse marks the beginning of a quarter-hizb in the Mushaf. You will see it at the start of most parts that begin in the middle of a surah.

## How to use this guide
### For a Ramadan khatm
Before each juz, spend five minutes reading its **guide**: the big idea and the main sections. Then read the juz knowing where you are in its argument. Reading becomes a little slower but far deeper, and the famous verses become landmarks you look out for. For other paces (a week, two weeks, two months), see [How to Finish the Quran: Khatm Schedules for a Year, a Month or a Week](/blog/quran-khatm-schedules-english/).

### For memorization
Start where it suits you. Many memorizers begin with **Juz 'Amma**, then **Tabarak**, then **Qad Sami'**, and then go back to the start of the Mushaf. Before memorizing a juz, read its guide: knowing the meaning binds the verses together and reduces confusion between similar passages. If you are just starting out, read [10 Mistakes Beginner Quran Memorizers Make (and How to Fix Them)](/blog/beginner-memorizer-mistakes-english/), and choose a recitation to follow with [how to choose a reciter for memorization](/blog/choosing-reciter-for-memorization-english/).

### For revision
Make a **card** for every juz with its name, first verse, main sections and the similar verses to watch. Look over the card before reciting the juz from memory. This map divides the juz in your mind into clearly bounded sections, so if you stop while reciting, you know exactly where you are.

### For teaching children
Turn the juz starts into a family game: say a juz number and let your child recite its first verse, or the other way round. Over time the whole Mushaf becomes a map in their mind. With younger children, start with the last three parts.

## Frequently asked questions
### How many juz are there in the Quran?
Thirty, divided into sixty hizbs and 240 quarters. The division is a practical arrangement for ease of reading, while the surahs and the order of their verses were fixed by the Prophet ﷺ.

### Are all the juz the same length?
They are close but not identical. In the Madinah Mushaf most fill twenty pages, Juz 1 fills twenty-one, and Juz 'Amma twenty-three.

### Which juz has the most surahs?
Juz 30, with thirty-seven surahs. Juz 29 has eleven and Juz 28 has nine.

### Where does the second half of the Quran begin?
By count of parts, it begins with Juz 15, at the start of Surah Al-Isra'.

### Do I have to stop exactly at the end of a juz?
No. If a juz ends in the middle of a connected passage, it is better to finish the passage, or to begin your next reading from the start of that passage.

## Conclusion
The thirty parts are like thirty doors in one house. When you know what lies behind each door, you enter the Mushaf knowing your way. Keep this page as your reference: whenever you begin a juz, read its guide first, then recite it with a present heart.
