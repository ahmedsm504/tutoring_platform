Surah At-Tariq begins with a question about a star that comes at night: "And what will make you know what the night-comer is? It is the piercing star." Then it delivers the point of the oath: every single soul has a guardian over it. From there it asks man to look at what he was created from, reminds him that the One who created him can bring him back, and warns him of a Day when every hidden secret will be put on trial. It ends with a promise to the Prophet ﷺ and to everyone who carries the truth: the enemies are plotting, but Allah is planning, so be patient.

This guide explains Surah At-Tariq verse by verse in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah At-Tariq
- **Number of verses**: 17.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 86, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: At-Tariq means "the night-comer", from the first verse.
- **Recited by the Prophet ﷺ**: at-Tirmidhi reported from Jabir ibn Samurah that the Prophet ﷺ used to recite "By the sky with its great stars" and "By the sky and the night-comer" in Zuhr and 'Asr, and at-Tirmidhi graded it *hasan sahih*.
- **Main themes**: Allah's watch over every soul, the creation of man as proof of the resurrection, the exposing of secrets, and the truth of the Quran.

## Verses 1–4: The Night-Comer
﴿وَٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلطَّارِقِ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلطَّارِقُ﴾ ﴿ٱلنَّجۡمُ ٱلثَّاقِبُ﴾ ﴿إِن كُلُّ نَفۡسٖ لَّمَّا عَلَيۡهَا حَافِظࣱ﴾ (Quran 86:1-4)

**"By the sky and the night-comer"**: *at-tariq* originally means someone who comes knocking at night. Then Allah explains it Himself: **"the piercing star"** (*an-najm ath-thaqib*), a star whose light pierces the darkness.

**The point of the oath**: **"There is no soul but that it has a guardian over it."** In the recitation of Hafs the word is *lamma*, with a shaddah, meaning "except": every soul without exception has a guardian. Commentators explain the guardians as the angels who record deeds and protect a person by Allah's command, and ultimately Allah Himself is the Guardian over all.

**The link between the star and the guardian**: just as the star pierces the darkness of the night, nothing you do in the dark is hidden. The light of Allah's knowledge pierces every secret.

## Verses 5–8: Look at What You Were Created From
﴿فَلۡيَنظُرِ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ مِمَّ خُلِقَ﴾ ﴿خُلِقَ مِن مَّآءٖ دَافِقٖ﴾ ﴿يَخۡرُجُ مِنۢ بَيۡنِ ٱلصُّلۡبِ وَٱلتَّرَآئِبِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّهُۥ عَلَىٰ رَجۡعِهِۦ لَقَادِرࣱ﴾ (Quran 86:5-8)

**"So let man look at what he was created from."** A practical command: look at your own origin. **"He was created from a fluid gushing forth, emerging from between the backbone and the ribs."** A small, weak fluid, and from it a human being with hearing, sight and intelligence.

**"Indeed, He is able to bring him back"**: this is the conclusion. The One who made a full human being from a drop of fluid will easily bring him back after death. The resurrection is less difficult than the first creation, and the first creation already happened.

**A note on the wording**: the Quran describes the fluid as coming out "from between the backbone and the ribs". Commentators explained this in several ways, and some modern writers have linked it to embryology. It is wise not to overburden the verse with scientific claims; its purpose is clear: to remind man of his humble beginning and of Allah's power to bring him back.

## Verses 9–10: The Day Secrets Are Tested
﴿يَوۡمَ تُبۡلَى ٱلسَّرَآئِرُ﴾ ﴿فَمَا لَهُۥ مِن قُوَّةٖ وَلَا نَاصِرٖ﴾ (Quran 86:9-10)

**"The Day when secrets will be put on trial"** (*tubla as-sara'ir*): every hidden intention, every secret deed, every private sin and every private act of goodness will be examined and brought into the open. The things you thought nobody knew will be displayed.

**"Then he will have no power and no helper"**: no strength within himself to defend himself, and nobody outside to help him.

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn 'Umar reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "On the Day of Resurrection, a banner will be raised for every betrayer, and it will be said: This is the betrayal of so-and-so son of so-and-so." What was hidden will be announced.

**The hopeful side**: secrets are not only sins. The secret charity, the tears shed alone at night, the patience nobody saw: all of these will also be revealed, and they will be honoured. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, among the seven whom Allah will shade on that Day is "a man who gave charity and concealed it so that his left hand did not know what his right hand gave" and "a man who remembered Allah in private and his eyes overflowed".

## Verses 11–14: A Decisive Word
﴿وَٱلسَّمَآءِ ذَاتِ ٱلرَّجۡعِ﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ ذَاتِ ٱلصَّدۡعِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّهُۥ لَقَوۡلࣱ فَصۡلࣱ﴾ ﴿وَمَا هُوَ بِٱلۡهَزۡلِ﴾ (Quran 86:11-14)

A second set of oaths: **"By the sky which returns"** (*dhat ar-raj'*), the sky that returns rain again and again, and **"the earth which cracks open"** (*dhat as-sad'*), splitting to let the plants come out. The rain comes down, the earth splits, life comes out: a living picture of resurrection.

**"Indeed, it is a decisive word, and it is not a joke."** The Quran is *qawlun fasl*, a word that separates truth from falsehood decisively. It is serious, not entertainment. Whoever treats it lightly has misunderstood it.

## Verses 15–17: Their Plot and Allah's Plan
﴿إِنَّهُمۡ يَكِيدُونَ كَيۡدٗا﴾ ﴿وَأَكِيدُ كَيۡدٗا﴾ ﴿فَمَهِّلِ ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ أَمۡهِلۡهُمۡ رُوَيۡدَۢا﴾ (Quran 86:15-17)

**"Indeed, they are plotting a plot, and I am planning a plan."** The disbelievers of Makkah were scheming against the Prophet ﷺ and his message. Allah's answer is short and certain: whatever they plot, His plan is greater and will prevail.

**"So give respite to the disbelievers; leave them awhile."** The Prophet ﷺ is told not to hurry. The outcome is coming. Within a few years the same Makkah that plotted against him opened its gates to him.

## Word Meanings
- **At-Tariq**: the night-comer, explained as the piercing star.
- **Ath-Thaqib**: piercing, shining through the darkness.
- **Hafiz**: guardian, watcher.
- **Dafiq**: gushing, pouring out.
- **As-Sulb**: the backbone.
- **At-Tara'ib**: the ribs or the upper chest.
- **Raj'ihi**: bringing him back.
- **Tubla**: will be tested, examined.
- **As-Sara'ir**: secrets, hidden things.
- **Ar-Raj'**: the returning, the rain that returns.
- **As-Sad'**: cracking, splitting.
- **Fasl**: decisive, separating truth from falsehood.
- **Ruwayda**: a little while, gently.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"And what will make you know what the night-comer is?"**: a question that builds curiosity before the answer.
2. **The star and the guardian**: light piercing darkness, knowledge piercing secrets.
3. **"Let man look at what he was created from"**: a call to humility.
4. **"Tubla as-sara'ir"**: two words that describe the most frightening exposure.
5. **The returning sky and the splitting earth**: an image of resurrection drawn from nature.
6. **"They plot a plot, and I plan a plan"**: perfect balance with an unequal outcome.
7. **"Give respite... leave them awhile"**: the repetition calms the heart.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd muttasil**: in *as-sama'i* (verse 1).
- **Ghunnah**: in *lamma* (verse 4), on the meem with shaddah.
- **Idgham with ghunnah**: in *ma'in dafiqin yakhruju* (verses 6–7), the tanween merges into the ya' with nasalisation.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *raj'ihi* (verse 8), the jim sakinah bounces lightly.
- **Qalqalah kubra**: when stopping on *at-tariq* (verse 1), the qaf bounces strongly.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Al-Buruj before it told of tyrants who plotted against believers, and ended with the Preserved Tablet. At-Tariq continues: "They plot a plot, and I plan a plan." Surah Al-A'la after it opens with glorifying "your Lord, the Most High", the One whose plan cannot be defeated, and promises the Prophet ﷺ: "We will make you recite, and you will not forget."

## Living With Surah At-Tariq
1. **Remember the guardian**: you are never alone and never unseen.
2. **Make your secret better than your public**: the Day of tested secrets is coming.
3. **Have a secret good deed**: charity, night prayer or du'a that only Allah knows.
4. **Look at your origin**: it cures arrogance.
5. **Take the Quran seriously**: it is a decisive word.
6. **Be patient with those who oppose the truth**: Allah's plan is at work.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Do not do in private what you would be ashamed of in public.**
2. **Repent from hidden sins before they are exposed.**
3. **Keep a secret store of good deeds.**
4. **Reflect on the rain and the plants**: they are a picture of resurrection.
5. **Trust Allah's plan** when you see people plotting against Islam.
6. **Recite this surah in your prayers**, as the Prophet ﷺ did.

## How to Memorise Surah At-Tariq
- **Day 1**: verses 1–10.
- **Day 2**: verses 11–17, then revise the whole surah.

## Teaching Children
- **Look at the stars at night** and read the first three verses.
- **Teach them that Allah sees them** even when nobody else does, in a way that builds love and shyness, not fear.
- **Plant a seed** and watch the earth crack open: "the earth which cracks open".

## The Seriousness of the Quran
"Indeed, it is a decisive word, and it is not a joke" deserves a moment of its own. In an age of entertainment, many people consume religious content the way they consume everything else: a quick clip, a nice quote, a moment of emotion, then on to the next thing. The surah reminds us that the Quran is not one more piece of content. It is *fasl*: it separates truth from falsehood, right from wrong, and the people of Paradise from the people of the Fire.

**How to treat the Quran as a decisive word**:
1. **Read it to act on it**: after each session, choose one thing to apply.
2. **Let it judge your opinions**: not the other way round.
3. **Recite it with presence**: slowly, with reflection, as Allah says in Surah Al-Muzzammil: "and recite the Quran with measured recitation".
4. **Do not joke with its verses**: or use them in mockery or careless humour.
5. **Return to it in disputes**: "If you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger."

## Patience With the Plotters
The final verses teach a principle that every generation needs. When believers see people working hard against Islam, they may feel anxious or rush into reactions that cause harm. The surah calms the heart: "They plot a plot, and I plan a plan. So give respite to the disbelievers; leave them awhile." This is not a call to passivity; the Prophet ﷺ kept calling people, teaching and building. It is a call to work without panic, knowing that the outcome is in Allah's hands.

## Conclusion
Surah At-Tariq is a star piercing the night: it reminds you that a guardian watches every soul, that you came from a humble drop, and that a Day is coming when every secret will be tested. Make your secrets beautiful, take the Quran seriously, and trust that whatever people plot, Allah's plan prevails.
