Surah Al-Hashr brings together history, law, character and creed. It begins with an event in Madinah: the expulsion of the Jewish tribe of Banu an-Nadir after they broke their treaty and plotted to kill the Prophet ﷺ. It then sets out how wealth gained without battle is distributed, and paints one of the most beautiful pictures of a believing society: emigrants who left everything, Ansar who preferred others over themselves even when in need, and later generations who pray for those who came before them. It exposes the hypocrites, then calls the believers: "Let every soul look to what it has sent forth for tomorrow." And it ends with three verses that contain more of Allah's beautiful names than almost any other passage of the Quran.

In Sahih al-Bukhari, Sa'id ibn Jubayr said: "I said to Ibn 'Abbas: Surat al-Hashr? He said: Say Surat an-Nadir."

This guide explains Surah Al-Hashr section by section in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Hashr
- **Number of verses**: 24.
- **Type**: Medinan.
- **Position**: Surah 59, in the twenty-eighth juz.
- **Name**: Al-Hashr means "the gathering" or "the exile", from verse 2: "at the first gathering".
- **Another name**: Surat Bani an-Nadir, as Ibn 'Abbas called it.
- **Main themes**: Allah's might, the distribution of wealth, the model society, hypocrisy, self-accounting and Allah's names.

## Verses 1–5: The Expulsion of Banu an-Nadir
﴿سَبَّحَ لِلَّهِ مَا فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِۖ وَهُوَ ٱلۡعَزِيزُ ٱلۡحَكِيمُ﴾ ﴿هُوَ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَخۡرَجَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ مِنۡ أَهۡلِ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ مِن دِيَٰرِهِمۡ لِأَوَّلِ ٱلۡحَشۡرِۚ مَا ظَنَنتُمۡ أَن يَخۡرُجُواْۖ وَظَنُّوٓاْ أَنَّهُم مَّانِعَتُهُمۡ حُصُونُهُم مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ فَأَتَىٰهُمُ ٱللَّهُ مِنۡ حَيۡثُ لَمۡ يَحۡتَسِبُواْۖ وَقَذَفَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ ٱلرُّعۡبَۚ يُخۡرِبُونَ بُيُوتَهُم بِأَيۡدِيهِمۡ وَأَيۡدِي ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ فَٱعۡتَبِرُواْ يَـٰٓأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَبۡصَٰرِ﴾ ﴿وَلَوۡلَآ أَن كَتَبَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيۡهِمُ ٱلۡجَلَآءَ لَعَذَّبَهُمۡ فِي ٱلدُّنۡيَاۖ وَلَهُمۡ فِي ٱلۡأٓخِرَةِ عَذَابُ ٱلنَّارِ﴾ ﴿ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمۡ شَآقُّواْ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥۖ وَمَن يُشَآقِّ ٱللَّهَ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ شَدِيدُ ٱلۡعِقَابِ﴾ (Quran 59:1-4)

**Glorification at the start**: the surah opens and closes with everything in the heavens and earth glorifying Allah, the Almighty, the Wise. The whole event bears witness to His might and wisdom.

**"You did not think they would leave, and they thought their fortresses would protect them from Allah."** The Muslims thought the strong fortresses would hold; the Jews of Banu an-Nadir thought the fortresses would protect them from Allah. **"But Allah came upon them from where they had not expected"**: from inside their hearts. **"And He cast terror into their hearts."** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Jabir reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "I have been given victory through awe, the distance of a month's journey."

**"They destroyed their houses with their own hands and the hands of the believers"**: they tore their houses down to carry off what they could. **"So take warning, O people of vision."** Do not trust in walls and material power; hearts are in Allah's hand.

﴿مَا قَطَعۡتُم مِّن لِّينَةٍ أَوۡ تَرَكۡتُمُوهَا قَآئِمَةً عَلَىٰٓ أُصُولِهَا فَبِإِذۡنِ ٱللَّهِ وَلِيُخۡزِيَ ٱلۡفَٰسِقِينَ﴾ (Quran 59:5)

**The palm trees**: during the siege, the Muslims cut some palm trees and left others. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn 'Umar reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ burnt and cut down the palms of Banu an-Nadir, and this verse was revealed: "Whatever you have cut down of palm trees or left standing on their trunks, it was by permission of Allah."

## Verses 6–7: The Rulings of Fay'
﴿وَمَآ أَفَآءَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ رَسُولِهِۦ مِنۡهُمۡ فَمَآ أَوۡجَفۡتُمۡ عَلَيۡهِ مِنۡ خَيۡلٖ وَلَا رِكَابٖ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُسَلِّطُ رُسُلَهُۥ عَلَىٰ مَن يَشَآءُۚ وَٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيۡءٖ قَدِيرࣱ﴾ ﴿مَّآ أَفَآءَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ رَسُولِهِۦ مِنۡ أَهۡلِ ٱلۡقُرَىٰ فَلِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ وَلِذِي ٱلۡقُرۡبَىٰ وَٱلۡيَتَٰمَىٰ وَٱلۡمَسَٰكِينِ وَٱبۡنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ كَيۡ لَا يَكُونَ دُولَةَۢ بَيۡنَ ٱلۡأَغۡنِيَآءِ مِنكُمۡۚ وَمَآ ءَاتَىٰكُمُ ٱلرَّسُولُ فَخُذُوهُ وَمَا نَهَىٰكُمۡ عَنۡهُ فَٱنتَهُواْۚ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ شَدِيدُ ٱلۡعِقَابِ﴾ (Quran 59:6-7)

**Fay'** is wealth taken from the enemy without fighting. It belongs to Allah and the Messenger, his near relatives, orphans, the needy and the traveller.

**The economic wisdom**: **"So that it will not be a perpetual distribution among the rich from among you."** Wealth must circulate through the whole society, not only among the wealthy. This is a foundational principle of Islamic economics.

**"And whatever the Messenger has given you, take it; and what he has forbidden you, refrain from it."** A great principle obliging obedience to the Sunnah. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud cursed women who tattoo and pluck their eyebrows to alter Allah's creation. A woman objected: "I have read what is between the two covers of the Mushaf and did not find it." He said: "If you had read it, you would have found it. Have you not read: *Whatever the Messenger has given you, take it, and what he has forbidden you, refrain from it*?" The Sunnah is revelation, and following it is following the Quran.

## Verses 8–10: Three Groups in the Believing Society
**First, the Muhajirun**
﴿لِلۡفُقَرَآءِ ٱلۡمُهَٰجِرِينَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُخۡرِجُواْ مِن دِيَٰرِهِمۡ وَأَمۡوَٰلِهِمۡ يَبۡتَغُونَ فَضۡلٗا مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَرِضۡوَٰنٗا وَيَنصُرُونَ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥٓۚ أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلصَّـٰدِقُونَ﴾ (Quran 59:8)

They left their homes and wealth, seeking Allah's bounty and pleasure, supporting Allah and His Messenger. **"Those are the truthful."**

**Second, the Ansar**
﴿وَٱلَّذِينَ تَبَوَّءُو ٱلدَّارَ وَٱلۡإِيمَٰنَ مِن قَبۡلِهِمۡ يُحِبُّونَ مَنۡ هَاجَرَ إِلَيۡهِمۡ وَلَا يَجِدُونَ فِي صُدُورِهِمۡ حَاجَةٗ مِّمَّآ أُوتُواْ وَيُؤۡثِرُونَ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ وَلَوۡ كَانَ بِهِمۡ خَصَاصَةࣱۚ وَمَن يُوقَ شُحَّ نَفۡسِهِۦ فَأُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُفۡلِحُونَ﴾ (Quran 59:9)

**Their qualities**:
1. **"They settled in the home and adopted the faith"**: Madinah, and faith firmly in their hearts.
2. **"They love those who emigrated to them."**
3. **"And find not any want in their breasts of what the emigrants were given"**: no envy.
4. **"But give them preference over themselves, even though they are in privation."**

**The story behind it**: in Sahih al-Bukhari, Abu Hurayrah reported that a man came to the Prophet ﷺ, and he sent to his wives, who said they had nothing but water. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Who will host this man?" A man of the Ansar said: "I will." He took him to his wife and said: "Honour the guest of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ." She said: "We have nothing but our children's food." He said: "Prepare your food, light your lamp, and put your children to sleep if they want dinner." She did so, then stood as if to fix the lamp and put it out, and they made the guest think they were eating, while they went to bed hungry. In the morning he went to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, who said: "Allah laughed tonight (or wondered) at what you two did." And Allah revealed: "And they give preference over themselves, even though they are in privation. And whoever is protected from the stinginess of his soul, it is those who will be the successful."

**"Whoever is protected from the stinginess of his soul"**: *shuhh* is worse than ordinary miserliness: a greedy grip on what one has, combined with craving what others have. Whoever is saved from it has succeeded.

**Third, those who came after**
﴿وَٱلَّذِينَ جَآءُو مِنۢ بَعۡدِهِمۡ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا ٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَلِإِخۡوَٰنِنَا ٱلَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِٱلۡإِيمَٰنِ وَلَا تَجۡعَلۡ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلّٗا لِّلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ رَبَّنَآ إِنَّكَ رَءُوفࣱ رَّحِيمٌ﴾ (Quran 59:10)

**A great du'a**: "Our Lord, forgive us and our brothers who preceded us in faith, and put not in our hearts any resentment toward those who have believed." This verse is the basis for loving the Companions, praying for them, and keeping one's heart clean towards them.

## Verses 11–17: The Hypocrites
﴿۞أَلَمۡ تَرَ إِلَى ٱلَّذِينَ نَافَقُواْ يَقُولُونَ لِإِخۡوَٰنِهِمُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ مِنۡ أَهۡلِ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ لَئِنۡ أُخۡرِجۡتُمۡ لَنَخۡرُجَنَّ مَعَكُمۡ وَلَا نُطِيعُ فِيكُمۡ أَحَدًا أَبَدٗا وَإِن قُوتِلۡتُمۡ لَنَنصُرَنَّكُمۡ وَٱللَّهُ يَشۡهَدُ إِنَّهُمۡ لَكَٰذِبُونَ﴾ ﴿لَئِنۡ أُخۡرِجُواْ لَا يَخۡرُجُونَ مَعَهُمۡ وَلَئِن قُوتِلُواْ لَا يَنصُرُونَهُمۡ وَلَئِن نَّصَرُوهُمۡ لَيُوَلُّنَّ ٱلۡأَدۡبَٰرَ ثُمَّ لَا يُنصَرُونَ﴾ ﴿لَأَنتُمۡ أَشَدُّ رَهۡبَةٗ فِي صُدُورِهِم مِّنَ ٱللَّهِۚ ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمۡ قَوۡمࣱ لَّا يَفۡقَهُونَ﴾ ﴿لَا يُقَٰتِلُونَكُمۡ جَمِيعًا إِلَّا فِي قُرٗى مُّحَصَّنَةٍ أَوۡ مِن وَرَآءِ جُدُرِۭۚ بَأۡسُهُم بَيۡنَهُمۡ شَدِيدࣱۚ تَحۡسَبُهُمۡ جَمِيعٗا وَقُلُوبُهُمۡ شَتَّىٰۚ ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمۡ قَوۡمࣱ لَّا يَعۡقِلُونَ﴾ (Quran 59:11-14)

**False promises**: the hypocrites promised Banu an-Nadir that they would leave with them if expelled and fight with them if attacked. When the expulsion came, they did neither. **"You (believers) are more fearful in their breasts than Allah."** **"You think they are together, but their hearts are diverse."** True unity is unity of hearts, not appearances.

**The parable of Satan**: **"Like the example of Satan when he says to man: Disbelieve. But when he disbelieves, he says: Indeed, I am disassociated from you."** The hypocrites encouraged Banu an-Nadir and then abandoned them, just as Satan does with man.

## Verses 18–20: Self-Accounting
﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ وَلۡتَنظُرۡ نَفۡسࣱ مَّا قَدَّمَتۡ لِغَدٖۖ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ خَبِيرُۢ بِمَا تَعۡمَلُونَ﴾ ﴿وَلَا تَكُونُواْ كَٱلَّذِينَ نَسُواْ ٱللَّهَ فَأَنسَىٰهُمۡ أَنفُسَهُمۡۚ أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡفَٰسِقُونَ﴾ ﴿لَا يَسۡتَوِيٓ أَصۡحَٰبُ ٱلنَّارِ وَأَصۡحَٰبُ ٱلۡجَنَّةِۚ أَصۡحَٰبُ ٱلۡجَنَّةِ هُمُ ٱلۡفَآئِزُونَ﴾ (Quran 59:18-20)

**"O you who believe, fear Allah. And let every soul look to what it has put forth for tomorrow."** "Tomorrow" is the Day of Resurrection, called so because it is near. This verse is the foundation of *muhasabah*, self-accounting. A well-known saying attributed to 'Umar ibn al-Khattab is: "Hold yourselves to account before you are held to account."

**"Fear Allah" twice**: once before the call to self-accounting and once after it. Taqwa leads to self-accounting, and self-accounting increases taqwa.

**"And do not be like those who forgot Allah, so He made them forget themselves."** Whoever forgets Allah is punished by forgetting his own true interests.

## Verse 21: If This Quran Came Down on a Mountain
﴿لَوۡ أَنزَلۡنَا هَٰذَا ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَ عَلَىٰ جَبَلٖ لَّرَأَيۡتَهُۥ خَٰشِعٗا مُّتَصَدِّعٗا مِّنۡ خَشۡيَةِ ٱللَّهِۚ وَتِلۡكَ ٱلۡأَمۡثَٰلُ نَضۡرِبُهَا لِلنَّاسِ لَعَلَّهُمۡ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ﴾ (Quran 59:21)

**"If We had sent down this Quran upon a mountain, you would have seen it humbled and splitting apart from fear of Allah."** If a mountain understood the Quran, it would crack from awe. What then of a human heart that hears it and feels nothing? **"And these examples We present to the people that perhaps they will give thought."**

## Verses 22–24: The Most Beautiful Names
﴿هُوَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِي لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَۖ عَٰلِمُ ٱلۡغَيۡبِ وَٱلشَّهَٰدَةِۖ هُوَ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ﴾ ﴿هُوَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِي لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ٱلۡمَلِكُ ٱلۡقُدُّوسُ ٱلسَّلَٰمُ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنُ ٱلۡمُهَيۡمِنُ ٱلۡعَزِيزُ ٱلۡجَبَّارُ ٱلۡمُتَكَبِّرُۚ سُبۡحَٰنَ ٱللَّهِ عَمَّا يُشۡرِكُونَ﴾ ﴿هُوَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡخَٰلِقُ ٱلۡبَارِئُ ٱلۡمُصَوِّرُۖ لَهُ ٱلۡأَسۡمَآءُ ٱلۡحُسۡنَىٰۚ يُسَبِّحُ لَهُۥ مَا فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِۖ وَهُوَ ٱلۡعَزِيزُ ٱلۡحَكِيمُ﴾ (Quran 59:22-24)

Three verses, each beginning **"He is Allah"**, containing more than fifteen of His names:
- **Knower of the unseen and the witnessed.**
- **Ar-Rahman, ar-Rahim**: the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful.
- **Al-Malik, al-Quddus, as-Salam**: the Sovereign, the Pure, the Perfection.
- **Al-Mu'min, al-Muhaymin**: the Grantor of Security, the Overseer.
- **Al-'Aziz, al-Jabbar, al-Mutakabbir**: the Almighty, the Compeller, the Supreme.
- **Al-Khaliq, al-Bari', al-Musawwir**: the Creator, the Inventor, the Fashioner.

**Creator, Inventor, Fashioner**: *al-Khaliq* is the One who determines and plans; *al-Bari'* brings into existence; *al-Musawwir* gives each creation its particular form. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah has ninety-nine names, one hundred less one; whoever enumerates them will enter Paradise."

**A note on a popular narration**: a hadith says that whoever recites the last three verses of Al-Hashr in the morning after seeking refuge three times will have seventy thousand angels praying for him. At-Tirmidhi reported it and called it *gharib*, and hadith scholars graded it weak, so this specific reward should not be attributed to the Prophet ﷺ with certainty. Reflecting on these names, however, is among the greatest acts of worship.

## Word Meanings
- **Al-Hashr**: gathering, exile.
- **Husun**: fortresses.
- **Ar-Ru'b**: terror, awe.
- **Lina**: palm tree.
- **Fay'**: wealth gained without fighting.
- **Dulah**: circulation among a group.
- **Yu'thirun**: give preference.
- **Khasasah**: privation, need.
- **Shuhh**: greed, stinginess of the soul.
- **Ghill**: resentment.
- **Shatta**: diverse, divided.
- **Khashi'**: humbled.
- **Mutasaddi'**: splitting apart.
- **Al-Bari'**: the Inventor.
- **Al-Musawwir**: the Fashioner.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **Beginning and ending with glorification.**
2. **"Allah came upon them from where they had not expected"**: defeat came from within the heart, not from outside the walls.
3. **"You think they are together, but their hearts are diverse"**: a sharp contrast.
4. **"Forgot Allah, so He made them forget themselves"**: punishment matching the deed.
5. **The parable of the mountain**: a picture that shakes hard hearts.
6. **"He is Allah" three times**: filling the heart with His greatness.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd muttasil**: in *ja'u* (verse 10) and *al-asma'u* (verse 24).
- **Ghunnah**: in *annahum* and *inna Allaha*, on the noon with shaddah.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *al-absar* (verse 2), the ba' sakinah bounces lightly.
- **Idgham without ghunnah**: in *ghafurun rahim* (verse 10), the tanween merges into the ra'.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Al-Mujadilah before it ended with "the party of Allah, they are the successful", and Al-Hashr shows Allah's support for His party against Banu an-Nadir and the hypocrites. Surah Al-Mumtahanah after it forbids taking Allah's enemies as allies; together the three surahs deal with loyalty and disassociation.

## Building a Society From Surah Al-Hashr
1. **Fair circulation of wealth.**
2. **Following the Messenger ﷺ.**
3. **Preferring others**, even in need.
4. **Clean hearts** towards fellow believers.
5. **Real unity**: of hearts, not appearances.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Do not trust material power alone.**
2. **Take the Sunnah**: it is revelation.
3. **Prefer others over yourself.**
4. **Fight the stinginess of your soul.**
5. **Pray for the believers who came before you**, and cleanse your heart of resentment.
6. **Account for yourself daily**: what have I sent forth for tomorrow?
7. **Do not forget Allah**, lest you forget yourself.
8. **Learn and live by Allah's beautiful names.**

## How to Memorise Surah Al-Hashr
- **Week 1**: verses 1–7.
- **Week 2**: verses 8–14.
- **Week 3**: verses 15–24.

## Teaching Children
- **Tell them the story of the Ansari and his guest**, who put out the lamp so the guest could eat.
- **Teach them the du'a** "Our Lord, forgive us and our brothers".
- **Memorise the names at the end of the surah together**, explaining each one.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Why is it called Surah Al-Hashr?
Because of verse 2, "at the first gathering", referring to the exile of Banu an-Nadir from their homes.

### Who were Banu an-Nadir?
A Jewish tribe of Madinah who broke their treaty with the Prophet ﷺ and were besieged until they agreed to leave.

### What is fay'?
Wealth taken from the enemy without fighting, distributed among the categories mentioned in verse 7.

### What is the story behind "they give preference over themselves"?
The Ansari who fed the Prophet's ﷺ guest with his children's food and put out the lamp, as reported in Sahih al-Bukhari.

### What does "let every soul look to what it has put forth for tomorrow" mean?
Let every person consider what deeds he has prepared for the Day of Resurrection.

### What is the difference between al-Khaliq, al-Bari' and al-Musawwir?
Al-Khaliq determines, al-Bari' brings into existence, and al-Musawwir gives each creation its form.

### Is the hadith about the virtue of the last three verses of Al-Hashr authentic?
At-Tirmidhi reported it and called it gharib, and hadith scholars graded it weak.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Hashr?
24 verses, and it is a Medinan surah.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Hashr teaches that fortresses cannot hold back Allah's decree, that a believing society is built on preferring others, clean hearts and following the Messenger ﷺ, and that every believer must keep looking ahead: what have I sent forth for tomorrow? Then it fills the heart with Allah's beautiful names, reminding us that if this Quran had come down on a mountain, the mountain would have split apart. What, then, of our hearts?
