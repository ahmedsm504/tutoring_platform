Surah Al-Humazah is short, but it treats two diseases that destroy people and communities: **belittling others** and **worshipping money**. It begins with "woe" to everyone who habitually mocks and finds fault with people, by word or by gesture. Then it exposes the root of that behaviour: a man who gathered wealth, counted it again and again, and thought it would make him immortal. Feeling superior because of his money, he looked down on everyone else. Then it shows his end: al-Hutamah, the crushing Fire of Allah that reaches all the way to the hearts.

This guide explains Surah Al-Humazah verse by verse in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons for a world full of mockery and materialism.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Humazah
- **Number of verses**: 9.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 104, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: Al-Humazah means "the scorner", from the first verse.
- **Main themes**: mocking and belittling people, the love of wealth, and the punishment that matches the sin.

## Who Was It Revealed About?
Some commentators named particular men from the idolaters of Quraysh who used to belittle the Prophet ﷺ and the believers, but these reports do not have chains strong enough to be certain. **What matters is the generality of the wording**: the word "every" makes the warning apply to anyone with these qualities in any time and place.

## Verse 1: Woe to Every Scorner and Mocker
﴿وَيۡلࣱ لِّكُلِّ هُمَزَةٖ لُّمَزَةٍ﴾ (Quran 104:1)

**"Woe"** (*wayl*): a word of punishment and ruin; some said it is a valley in Hell.

**Humazah and lumazah**: both words are in an intensive form, showing a person who does this habitually, not someone who slipped once. Scholars gave several distinctions:
- **Humazah**: one who belittles people with actions and gestures, such as winking or raising the eyebrows, or who backbites them in their absence.
- **Lumazah**: one who belittles them with the tongue, or to their face.

**The combined meaning**: someone who tracks people's faults, attacks and mocks them by word or deed, in front of them or behind their backs.

**In the Quran and Sunnah**: Allah says in Surah Al-Hujurat: "Do not insult one another and do not call each other by offensive nicknames," and "O you who believe, let not a people ridicule another people; perhaps they may be better than them." In Sahih Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ asked: "Do you know what backbiting is?" They said: "Allah and His Messenger know best." He said: "Mentioning your brother in a way he dislikes." Someone asked: "What if what I say is true of him?" He said: "If it is true, you have backbitten him; and if it is not true, you have slandered him." At-Tirmidhi reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The believer is not one who taunts, curses, is obscene or vulgar," and al-Albani graded it authentic.

## Verses 2–3: The One Who Gathers Wealth and Counts It
﴿ٱلَّذِي جَمَعَ مَالٗا وَعَدَّدَهُۥ﴾ ﴿يَحۡسَبُ أَنَّ مَالَهُۥٓ أَخۡلَدَهُۥ﴾ (Quran 104:2-3)

**The root of the mockery**: this scorner's main concern is collecting money. **"And counts it"**: he counts it again and again, delighting in it, or stores it up against the future. Money has become his whole world.

**"He thinks that his wealth will make him immortal"**: as if his money will keep him alive forever, or keep his name alive. A great illusion: wealth has never stopped death.

**The link between mockery and love of money**: the arrogant rich man sees himself above others, so he looks down on them, because he measures human worth by wealth. He sees a poor man and despises him; he sees a pious man and mocks him.

**The reality of wealth**: in Sahih Muslim, 'Abdullah ibn ash-Shikhkhir reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The son of Adam says: My wealth, my wealth! But do you own anything of your wealth, O son of Adam, except what you ate and used up, or wore and wore out, or gave in charity and sent ahead?" And in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Anas reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "If the son of Adam had a valley of gold, he would love to have two valleys. Nothing will fill his mouth except dust. And Allah accepts the repentance of whoever repents."

## Verses 4–9: Al-Hutamah
﴿كَلَّاۖ لَيُنۢبَذَنَّ فِي ٱلۡحُطَمَةِ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلۡحُطَمَةُ﴾ ﴿نَارُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡمُوقَدَةُ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّتِي تَطَّلِعُ عَلَى ٱلۡأَفۡـِٔدَةِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّهَا عَلَيۡهِم مُّؤۡصَدَةࣱ﴾ ﴿فِي عَمَدٖ مُّمَدَّدَةِۭ﴾ (Quran 104:4-9)

**"No!"**: a firm rejection. It is not as he thinks; his wealth will not make him immortal.

**"He will surely be thrown into al-Hutamah"**: *nabdh* means to throw something away with contempt, as one throws away rubbish. **The contrast**: he thought he was great because of his money, and he is thrown away like something worthless.

**Al-Hutamah**: one of the names of the Fire, meaning "the crusher", because it crushes and breaks everything thrown into it. **The match**: he used to crush people's honour with his tongue, so he is thrown into the crusher.

**"And what will make you know what al-Hutamah is? It is the Fire of Allah, kindled."** It is attributed to Allah to show how great and terrible it is, and it is kept burning without dying down.

**"Which reaches the hearts"** (*tattali'u 'ala al-af'idah*): it rises up to the hearts. **The match**: the heart is where arrogance, contempt for people and love of money lived, so the Fire reaches it.

**"Indeed, it will be closed over them"** (*mu'sadah*): sealed shut, with no way out. **"In extended columns"**: long columns stretched out, with which its doors are closed over them, or to which they are bound.

**How hot it is**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "This fire of yours that the son of Adam kindles is one part of seventy parts of the heat of Hell."

## The Punishment Matches the Sin
Surah Al-Humazah shows clearly that the recompense is of the same kind as the deed:
1. **He crushed people with his tongue** → he is thrown into **the crusher**.
2. **He thought himself great** → he is **thrown away** like something worthless.
3. **His heart was full of arrogance and love of money** → the Fire **reaches the hearts**.
4. **He gathered his money and locked it away** → the Fire is **closed over him**.
5. **He thought his money would make him immortal** → he remains forever in the Fire.

## Word Meanings
- **Wayl**: woe, ruin.
- **Humazah**: one who habitually belittles people, especially by gesture or behind their backs.
- **Lumazah**: one who habitually finds fault, especially with the tongue or to their face.
- **'Addadah**: counted it, or stored it up.
- **Akhladah**: made him immortal.
- **Yunbadhanna**: will surely be thrown away.
- **Al-Hutamah**: the crusher, a name of the Fire.
- **Al-Muqadah**: kindled.
- **Tattali'u**: reaches, rises up to.
- **Al-Af'idah**: the hearts.
- **Mu'sadah**: closed over, sealed shut.
- **'Amad mumaddadah**: extended columns.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"Humazah lumazah"**: two intensive forms with the same rhythm.
2. **"Gathers wealth and counts it"**: a picture of a man consumed by money.
3. **"He will surely be thrown"**: doubly emphasised, and the verb itself shows contempt.
4. **"Al-Hutamah"**: a name that matches what he did to people.
5. **"The Fire of Allah"**: attributed to Allah to magnify it.
6. **"Reaches the hearts"**: the Fire reaches the source of the disease.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Idgham without ghunnah**: in *humazatin lumazah* (verse 1), the tanween merges into the lam.
- **Iqlab**: in *la-yunbadhanna* (verse 4), the noon sakinah before the ba' becomes a hidden meem, with ghunnah also on the final noon with shaddah.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *wa ma adraka* (verse 5), the dal sakinah bounces lightly.
- **Madd munfasil**: in *wa ma adraka* (verse 5), the long vowel followed by a hamzah in the next word.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Al-'Asr before it ended with urging one another to truth and patience, the way believers use their tongues to benefit people, and Al-Humazah condemns the one who uses his tongue to harm them. Surah Al-Fil after it tells of Abrahah and his army, who trusted in their power and equipment and were destroyed, just as the scorner trusted in his wealth.

## Mockery in the Digital Age
Al-Humazah is strikingly relevant today. Forms of *hamz* and *lamz* we see daily:
1. **Sarcastic comments** on people's posts.
2. **Imitating people's speech or walk** to make others laugh.
3. **Offensive nicknames**.
4. **Winking and gesturing** about someone in a gathering.
5. **Sharing photos of people** to mock them.
6. **Bullying** in schools and workplaces.

All of these fall within the meaning of the verse, and the warning is for those who make it a habit. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "The Muslim is the one from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe."

## Treating the Disease
**For mockery**:
1. **Focus on your own faults**: whoever is busy with his own has no time for others'.
2. **Remember that Allah hears you** and the angels write.
3. **Remember that the person you mock may be better than you.**
4. **If you backbite someone, ask forgiveness for him** and speak well of him.
5. **Leave gatherings full of backbiting.**
6. **Speak good or stay silent**, as in the hadith in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

**For the love of money**:
1. **Remember that wealth makes nobody immortal.**
2. **Give charity**: it is the wealth that remains.
3. **Never measure people by their money.**
4. **Earn halal and spend halal.**
5. **Be content with what Allah gives you.**
6. **Remember death.**

## Repenting From Backbiting
Whoever has fallen into this has an open door of repentance: stop immediately, regret it, resolve not to return, and seek the pardon of the person wronged where that does not cause greater harm; otherwise pray for him and speak well of him where you spoke badly. In Sahih al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever has wronged his brother in his honour or anything else, let him seek his pardon today, before there is no dinar and no dirham. If he has good deeds, they will be taken from him in proportion to the wrong; and if he has no good deeds, the sins of the other will be taken and placed on him."

## Practical Lessons
1. **Guard your tongue** from people's honour.
2. **Never mock anyone**, by word, gesture or comment.
3. **Do not make money the measure of people.**
4. **Spend your wealth in good causes**: that is what remains for you.
5. **Cleanse your heart of arrogance.**
6. **Remember that the recompense matches the deed.**

## How to Memorise Surah Al-Humazah
- **Day 1**: verses 1–4.
- **Day 2**: verses 5–9, then revise the whole surah.

## Teaching Children
- **Teach them never to laugh at anyone's appearance or clothes.**
- **Explain that money does not make anyone better than others.**
- **Teach them to say good or stay silent.**

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does "Woe to every scorner and mocker" mean?
A warning of ruin to everyone who habitually belittles people and finds fault with them by word or gesture.

### What is the difference between humazah and lumazah?
One view is that humazah is by gesture and lumazah by the tongue; another is that humazah is behind people's backs and lumazah to their faces.

### What does "He thinks his wealth will make him immortal" mean?
He imagines his money will keep him alive or preserve his name forever, which is an illusion.

### What is al-Hutamah?
A name of the Fire meaning "the crusher", because it crushes whatever is thrown into it.

### What does "reaches the hearts" mean?
The Fire rises up to the hearts, the place of arrogance and the love of money.

### Is online mockery included in this surah?
Yes. Any comment or post that belittles or mocks people falls within its meaning.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Humazah?
9 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Humazah warns you not to turn your tongue into a weapon that crushes people, and not to turn your money into an idol you think will keep you alive. Guard your tongue, spend your wealth in good, and cleanse your heart of arrogance, before the day of the crusher that reaches the hearts.
