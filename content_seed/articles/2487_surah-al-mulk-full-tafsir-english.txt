Surah Al-Mulk is the surah many Muslims recite every night before they sleep. Its thirty verses carry the reader from the dominion of Allah over the heavens to the smallest detail of a bird spreading its wings, and from the regret of people in Hell to the quiet fear of those who believe in the Unseen. The Prophet ﷺ spoke of a surah of thirty verses that interceded for a man until he was forgiven, and that surah is Al-Mulk.

This guide explains the whole surah in English, passage by passage, with authentic narrations and practical lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Mulk
- **Number of verses**: 30.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 67, the first surah of the twenty-ninth juz.
- **Other names**: Tabarak, and it is also described as "the one that protects" in some reports.
- **Central theme**: Allah's absolute sovereignty, and life as a test that ends in accountability.

## The Virtue of Surah Al-Mulk
**It intercedes for its reader.** Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There is a surah of the Quran of thirty verses that interceded for a man until he was forgiven, and it is *Tabarak alladhi bi-yadihi al-mulk*." This was recorded by Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi, and at-Tirmidhi graded it *hasan*.

**The Prophet ﷺ recited it at night.** Jabir reported that the Prophet ﷺ would not sleep until he had recited *Alif Lam Mim Tanzil* (Surah As-Sajdah) and *Tabarak alladhi bi-yadihi al-mulk*. At-Tirmidhi recorded it, and it was graded authentic by al-Albani.

These two narrations are the basis of the widespread habit of reciting Al-Mulk every night.

## Verses 1–2: Dominion, Death and Life
﴿تَبَٰرَكَ ٱلَّذِي بِيَدِهِ ٱلۡمُلۡكُ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيۡءٖ قَدِيرٌ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ ٱلۡمَوۡتَ وَٱلۡحَيَوٰةَ لِيَبۡلُوَكُمۡ أَيُّكُمۡ أَحۡسَنُ عَمَلٗاۚ وَهُوَ ٱلۡعَزِيزُ ٱلۡغَفُورُ﴾ (Quran 67:1-2)

**Meaning:** Blessed is He in whose hand is the dominion, and He has power over everything. He who created death and life to test you, which of you is best in deed; and He is the Almighty, the Forgiving.

The surah opens with *tabarak*, a word that expresses abundant blessing and exaltation. All dominion, of heaven and earth, of this world and the next, is in His hand.

Then comes one of the most important verses about the purpose of life. Allah created death and life to test you. Notice three things:
1. **Death is mentioned before life.** Some scholars note that this keeps death in front of us: life only makes sense when you remember it ends.
2. **"Best in deed", not "most in deed".** The test is quality, not quantity. The scholar al-Fudayl ibn 'Iyad explained "best in deed" as the most sincere and the most correct: sincere for Allah alone, and correct according to the Sunnah.
3. **The verse closes with two names**: the Almighty, so that you do not take the test lightly, and the Forgiving, so that you do not despair when you fail.

## Verses 3–5: The Flawless Heavens
﴿ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبۡعَ سَمَٰوَٰتٖ طِبَاقٗاۖ مَّا تَرَىٰ فِي خَلۡقِ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ مِن تَفَٰوُتٖۖ فَٱرۡجِعِ ٱلۡبَصَرَ هَلۡ تَرَىٰ مِن فُطُورٖ﴾ ﴿ثُمَّ ٱرۡجِعِ ٱلۡبَصَرَ كَرَّتَيۡنِ يَنقَلِبۡ إِلَيۡكَ ٱلۡبَصَرُ خَاسِئٗا وَهُوَ حَسِيرࣱ﴾ (Quran 67:3-4)

**Meaning:** He who created seven heavens in layers. You will not see any inconsistency in the creation of the Most Merciful. So look again: do you see any flaw? Then look again and again; your sight will return to you humbled and exhausted.

This is a direct challenge. Look at the sky once, then twice, then again and again, searching for a crack or an imperfection. Your eyes will come back tired, having found nothing. The more humanity has learned about the universe, from the precise constants of physics to the order of galaxies, the more this verse has gained in meaning.

﴿وَلَقَدۡ زَيَّنَّا ٱلسَّمَآءَ ٱلدُّنۡيَا بِمَصَٰبِيحَ وَجَعَلۡنَٰهَا رُجُومٗا لِّلشَّيَٰطِينِۖ وَأَعۡتَدۡنَا لَهُمۡ عَذَابَ ٱلسَّعِيرِ﴾ (Quran 67:5)

**Meaning:** We have adorned the nearest heaven with lamps and made them missiles against the devils, and We have prepared for them the punishment of the Blaze.

The stars serve three purposes mentioned in the Quran: beauty, guidance at night, and protection of the heavens from the eavesdropping of devils.

## Verses 6–11: The Regret of the People of Hell
﴿وَلِلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ بِرَبِّهِمۡ عَذَابُ جَهَنَّمَۖ وَبِئۡسَ ٱلۡمَصِيرُ﴾ ﴿إِذَآ أُلۡقُواْ فِيهَا سَمِعُواْ لَهَا شَهِيقٗا وَهِيَ تَفُورُ﴾ ﴿تَكَادُ تَمَيَّزُ مِنَ ٱلۡغَيۡظِۖ كُلَّمَآ أُلۡقِيَ فِيهَا فَوۡجࣱ سَأَلَهُمۡ خَزَنَتُهَآ أَلَمۡ يَأۡتِكُمۡ نَذِيرࣱ﴾ (Quran 67:6-8)

**Meaning:** For those who disbelieve in their Lord is the punishment of Hell, and what a wretched destination. When they are thrown into it, they hear it roaring as it boils up. It almost bursts with rage. Every time a group is thrown in, its keepers ask them: Did no warner come to you?

Hell is described as almost bursting with fury. Then the keepers ask every group the same question: did no one warn you?

﴿وَقَالُواْ لَوۡ كُنَّا نَسۡمَعُ أَوۡ نَعۡقِلُ مَا كُنَّا فِيٓ أَصۡحَٰبِ ٱلسَّعِيرِ﴾ (Quran 67:10)

**Meaning:** They will say: If only we had listened or reasoned, we would not be among the companions of the Blaze.

This is perhaps the most painful confession in the Quran. The people of Hell admit that their problem was not a lack of evidence. They had ears and minds, and they did not use them. Listening and reasoning are the two doors to guidance, and the surah will return to them at the end when it mentions hearing, sight and hearts.

## Verses 12–14: Fearing Allah in Secret
﴿إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَخۡشَوۡنَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلۡغَيۡبِ لَهُم مَّغۡفِرَةࣱ وَأَجۡرࣱ كَبِيرࣱ﴾ (Quran 67:12)

**Meaning:** Those who fear their Lord unseen will have forgiveness and a great reward.

After the scene of regret comes its opposite: people who fear Allah *bil-ghayb*, in the Unseen, without having seen Him and without anyone watching them. This is the highest form of sincerity. In the hadith of the seven whom Allah shades on the Day of Judgement, recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim, one is "a man who remembered Allah in private and his eyes overflowed with tears."

﴿وَأَسِرُّواْ قَوۡلَكُمۡ أَوِ ٱجۡهَرُواْ بِهِۦٓۖ إِنَّهُۥ عَلِيمُۢ بِذَاتِ ٱلصُّدُورِ﴾ ﴿أَلَا يَعۡلَمُ مَنۡ خَلَقَ وَهُوَ ٱللَّطِيفُ ٱلۡخَبِيرُ﴾ (Quran 67:13-14)

**Meaning:** Whether you keep your words secret or say them openly, He knows what is in the hearts. Does He who created not know, when He is the Subtle, the All-Aware?

The argument is beautifully simple. The Creator knows His creation. Your thoughts, intentions and whispers are known to the One who made the heart that holds them.

## Verses 15–22: The Earth Made Easy and the Birds in the Sky
﴿هُوَ ٱلَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلۡأَرۡضَ ذَلُولٗا فَٱمۡشُواْ فِي مَنَاكِبِهَا وَكُلُواْ مِن رِّزۡقِهِۦۖ وَإِلَيۡهِ ٱلنُّشُورُ﴾ (Quran 67:15)

**Meaning:** He is the One who made the earth subservient to you, so walk through its regions and eat of His provision; and to Him is the resurrection.

The earth is described as *dhalul*, tame and easy, like a riding animal that obeys. Its gravity holds us, its surface is walkable, its soil grows food. Allah tells us to walk in it and eat from it. Working and earning are encouraged. But the verse ends with a reminder: to Him is the resurrection. Earn in this world, but do not forget where you are going.

The next verses warn: do you feel safe that He who is above the heaven will not cause the earth to swallow you, or send a storm of stones against you?

﴿أَوَلَمۡ يَرَوۡاْ إِلَى ٱلطَّيۡرِ فَوۡقَهُمۡ صَـٰٓفَّـٰتٖ وَيَقۡبِضۡنَۚ مَا يُمۡسِكُهُنَّ إِلَّا ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنُۚ إِنَّهُۥ بِكُلِّ شَيۡءِۭ بَصِيرٌ﴾ (Quran 67:19)

**Meaning:** Have they not seen the birds above them, spreading and folding their wings? None holds them up except the Most Merciful. He sees everything.

Every bird in the sky is a lesson. It stays aloft because of laws that Allah created and sustains. The verse uses the name *ar-Rahman*, the Most Merciful, reminding us that the order of creation is a mercy.

﴿أَفَمَن يَمۡشِي مُكِبًّا عَلَىٰ وَجۡهِهِۦٓ أَهۡدَىٰٓ أَمَّن يَمۡشِي سَوِيًّا عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٖ مُّسۡتَقِيمٖ﴾ (Quran 67:22)

**Meaning:** Is one who walks fallen on his face better guided, or one who walks upright on a straight path?

A vivid comparison: the one who lives without guidance is like someone crawling on his face, unable to see where he is going. The believer walks upright, seeing the road ahead.

## Verses 23–27: Senses, Gathering and the Promise
﴿قُلۡ هُوَ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَنشَأَكُمۡ وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلسَّمۡعَ وَٱلۡأَبۡصَٰرَ وَٱلۡأَفۡـِٔدَةَۚ قَلِيلٗا مَّا تَشۡكُرُونَ﴾ (Quran 67:23)

**Meaning:** Say: It is He who brought you into being and gave you hearing, sight and hearts. Little do you give thanks.

Hearing, sight and hearts: the very tools the people of Hell admitted they failed to use. The surah reminds us that these are gifts that call for gratitude, and gratitude means using them for what they were made for.

The disbelievers then ask: when is this promise? The answer: knowledge of the Hour is with Allah alone, and the Prophet ﷺ is only a clear warner. When they see it near, their faces will darken.

## Verses 28–30: The Closing Questions
﴿قُلۡ هُوَ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنُ ءَامَنَّا بِهِۦ وَعَلَيۡهِ تَوَكَّلۡنَاۖ فَسَتَعۡلَمُونَ مَنۡ هُوَ فِي ضَلَٰلٖ مُّبِينٖ﴾ ﴿قُلۡ أَرَءَيۡتُمۡ إِنۡ أَصۡبَحَ مَآؤُكُمۡ غَوۡرٗا فَمَن يَأۡتِيكُم بِمَآءٖ مَّعِينِۭ﴾ (Quran 67:29-30)

**Meaning:** Say: He is the Most Merciful; we believe in Him and on Him we rely. You will come to know who is in clear error. Say: Have you considered, if your water were to sink away into the earth, who then could bring you flowing water?

The surah ends with a question that touches daily life. Water is the most basic need. If it sank deep into the ground, who could bring it back? No one but Allah. So the One who gives you water every day is the One you should rely on.

## Key Vocabulary
- **Tabaraka**: blessed and exalted is He.
- **Al-Mulk**: dominion, sovereignty.
- **Liyabluwakum**: to test you.
- **Tibaqan**: in layers, one above another.
- **Tafawut**: inconsistency, imperfection.
- **Futur**: cracks, flaws.
- **Masabih**: lamps, the stars.
- **Shahiq**: the roaring of Hell.
- **Bil-ghayb**: in the Unseen, in secret.
- **Dhalul**: subservient, tame, easy to walk on.
- **Ghawran**: sunk deep into the earth.

## Tajweed Notes for Reciting Al-Mulk
- **Ikhfa** in *min tafawut* (verse 3): the nun sakinah is hidden before *ta* with a nasal sound.
- **Izhar** in *man khalaq* (verse 14): the nun is pronounced clearly before the throat letter *kha*.
- **Idgham shafawi** in *lahum maghfirah* (verse 12): the meem sakinah merges into the following meem with ghunnah.
- **Idgham without ghunnah** in *rujuman lish-shayatin* (verse 5): the tanween merges into the *lam* with no nasal sound.
- **Madd 'arid**: almost every verse ends in *-ir*, *-ur* or *-un*, giving the surah its measured rhythm.

## Practical Lessons from Surah Al-Mulk
1. **Recite it every night.** Follow the practice of the Prophet ﷺ and hope for its intercession.
2. **Measure your deeds by quality.** Aim for "best in deed": sincere and in line with the Sunnah.
3. **Listen and reason.** Do not be among those who regret that they did not.
4. **Fear Allah in private.** The secret moments are the real test.
5. **Work and earn**, while remembering the resurrection.
6. **Look up.** Let the sky and the birds remind you of His care.
7. **Thank Allah for water.** It is a gift you would be helpless without.

## How to Teach Surah Al-Mulk to Children
- Make it part of the bedtime routine, a few verses at a time.
- Watch birds together and read verse 19.
- Ask them what they would do if the tap stopped working, then read the last verse.

## A Memorisation Plan
Al-Mulk is thirty verses, ideal for a one-month plan:
1. Days 1–7: verses 1–5.
2. Days 8–14: verses 6–14.
3. Days 15–22: verses 15–22.
4. Days 23–30: verses 23–30, then recite the full surah every night.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Does Surah Al-Mulk protect from the punishment of the grave?
The authentic hadith graded *hasan* by at-Tirmidhi says it interceded for a man until he was forgiven. Other reports describe it as protecting from the punishment of the grave; scholars differ on their grading. Reciting it regularly is a well-established practice.

### When should I recite Surah Al-Mulk?
At night before sleeping, following the practice of the Prophet ﷺ narrated by Jabir.

### What does "to test you, which of you is best in deed" mean?
Life and death were created as a test, and the measure is the quality of deeds: sincerity and correctness.

### What does "fear their Lord unseen" mean?
To fear Allah without seeing Him and when no one else is watching.

### What is the meaning of "dhalul"?
Tame and easy, like an animal that can be ridden; the earth was made easy for us to live on.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Mulk?
30 verses.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Mulk is a nightly reminder of who truly holds the dominion. It tells you why you are alive, shows you a sky without a flaw, lets you hear the regret of those who did not listen, and closes by asking who would bring you water if it vanished. Recite it before you sleep, think about one verse each night, and hope that it will speak for you on the Day you need it most.
