Words can be like gifts, or they can be like stones. A kind word can make someone's whole day better. A mean word, a nasty look, or making fun of someone behind their back can hurt far more than a scraped knee, and the pain can last for years. Allah takes this very seriously. In Surah Al-Humazah, He warns: "Woe to every slanderer and mocker", the person who uses words, faces and gestures to hurt and belittle others.

The surah also describes a person who is so proud of his money that he counts it again and again, thinking it will make him last forever. He uses his wealth to look down on others. In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Al-Humazah to children in simple English, with practical lessons about bullying, mocking, backbiting and kindness, plus activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Humazah

- **Position**: surah 104, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: nine.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Name**: al-Humazah means "the one who slanders and mocks people".

## The Whole Surah

﴿وَيۡلࣱ لِّكُلِّ هُمَزَةٖ لُّمَزَةٍ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي جَمَعَ مَالٗا وَعَدَّدَهُۥ﴾ ﴿يَحۡسَبُ أَنَّ مَالَهُۥٓ أَخۡلَدَهُۥ﴾ ﴿كَلَّاۖ لَيُنۢبَذَنَّ فِي ٱلۡحُطَمَةِ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلۡحُطَمَةُ﴾ ﴿نَارُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡمُوقَدَةُ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّتِي تَطَّلِعُ عَلَى ٱلۡأَفۡـِٔدَةِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّهَا عَلَيۡهِم مُّؤۡصَدَةࣱ﴾ ﴿فِي عَمَدٖ مُّمَدَّدَةِۭ﴾ (Quran 104:1-9)

## Verse 1: Woe to Every Humazah and Lumazah

### Explaining it to your child
"Wayl is a strong warning, like a big red STOP sign. Allah warns two kinds of people:
- **Humazah**: someone who mocks or insults people, often with looks, gestures or behind their backs.
- **Lumazah**: someone who points out people's faults to their faces, criticising and putting them down.
Scholars explain that both are about hurting people's dignity, whether with the tongue, the eyes, the hands or the face."

### Examples children understand
- Rolling eyes at someone when they speak.
- Imitating how someone walks or talks to make others laugh.
- Calling people names.
- Whispering and laughing about someone.
- Posting or sharing mean comments or pictures.
- Telling someone "You're so stupid!" or "You can't play with us."

### Lesson for kids
"Allah sees every look, every gesture and every word. Hurting someone's feelings is not 'just a joke'; it's something Allah warns us about very strongly."

## Verses 2–3: Gathering Wealth and Counting It

### Explaining it to your child
"This person collects money and counts it again and again, thinking: My money will make me last forever! He feels better than others because he's rich, so he looks down on them and mocks them. But money can't make anyone live forever."

### Lesson for kids: don't look down on anyone
"Having nice things doesn't make you better than anyone. Some of the best people in the world are poor, and some of the richest people are unkind. Allah looks at our hearts and actions, not at our things."

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah does not look at your appearance or your wealth; rather, He looks at your hearts and your deeds" (Muslim).

## Verses 4–9: The Crushing Fire

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah says: No! This person will be thrown into al-Hutamah, the crusher. And what will make you know what the crusher is? It's the fire of Allah, which reaches the hearts. Why the hearts? Because the hearts were full of pride and cruelty. This is a serious warning so that people stop hurting others."

### Tip for parents
With young children, mention this part briefly and calmly, emphasizing that it is a warning, and that Allah forgives those who stop and apologize. The main focus should be on kind behaviour.

## Practical Anti-Bullying Lessons

### If you see someone being mocked
1. **Don't laugh**: laughing encourages the bully.
2. **Speak up kindly**: "That's not nice. Leave them alone."
3. **Stand with the person**: invite them to play or sit with you.
4. **Tell an adult**: a teacher or parent can help.

### If someone mocks you
1. **Stay calm**: take a deep breath and say "A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim."
2. **Use a confident voice**: "Please stop. I don't like that."
3. **Walk away** if it continues.
4. **Tell an adult** you trust. Telling isn't "snitching"; it's getting help.
5. **Remember your worth**: what they say doesn't change how Allah sees you.

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said: "A Muslim is the brother of a Muslim: he does not wrong him, abandon him or belittle him... It is enough evil for a person to belittle his Muslim brother" (Muslim).

## The Tongue: Small but Powerful

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him say something good or stay silent" (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### Activity: the toothpaste lesson
Give your child a small tube of toothpaste and ask them to squeeze some out onto a plate. Then ask them to put it back in the tube. They can't! Explain: "Words are like toothpaste. Once they're out, you can't put them back. So think before you speak."

### Activity: the kind-word jar
Keep a jar and slips of paper. Every day, each family member writes one kind thing they said to someone, or a compliment they want to give. Read them together at the end of the week.

### Activity: the three gates
Teach your child to pass their words through three "gates" before speaking:
1. **Is it true?**
2. **Is it kind?**
3. **Is it needed?**
If it doesn't pass all three, don't say it.

## Money, Pride and Gratitude

### Explaining it to your child
"The person in this surah counted his money and felt proud. A believer thanks Allah for money, uses it well, shares it with others and never uses it to make others feel small."

### Activity: the share box
Keep a box where your child puts toys, books or clothes they no longer need, to give to children who have less. Talk about how sharing makes everyone feel good.

## Online Mocking

Many children now have tablets, games and group chats where mocking can happen through messages, emojis, comments and shared pictures. The surah's warning applies there too, because a hurtful message is still a hurtful word.

### Explaining it to your child
"Sending a mean message, laughing emojis under someone's photo, or sharing a picture to make fun of someone is the same as mocking them to their face. Sometimes it's even worse, because many people see it and it stays there."

### Family rules for online kindness
- **Never post or share** anything about someone that they wouldn't like.
- **Don't join in** group chats that make fun of people; leave or speak up.
- **Screenshot and tell an adult** if someone is being bullied online.
- **Before sending**, ask: would I say this to their face, with Mum or Dad standing next to me?

### Activity: the kind-comment challenge
If your child uses a family-approved app, challenge them to leave three kind comments this week, on a friend's drawing, a cousin's video or a class project. Talk about how kind words online can brighten someone's day just as much as in person.

## Turning the Surah Around

Every warning in the Quran also teaches us its opposite. If Allah dislikes the mocker and the fault-finder, He loves the one who encourages, praises and covers others' faults. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever covers a Muslim's fault, Allah will cover his faults on the Day of Resurrection (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### Activity: be an encourager
For one week, your child tries to give one sincere compliment every day: "Great goal!", "Your handwriting is so neat", "Thanks for helping me". At the end of the week, ask how people responded and how it made them feel.

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Funny Voice"

There was a girl in Sara's class called Mia, who had a stammer. When she read aloud, her words sometimes got stuck. One day, a boy called Jake started imitating her: "M-m-m-my n-n-name is M-M-Mia!" Some children laughed. Mia's face turned red, and she stopped reading.

Sara felt uncomfortable. She didn't laugh, but she didn't say anything either. At home, she told her mother. Her mother opened the mushaf and read Surah Al-Humazah: "Waylul-li-kulli humazatil-lumazah." "Woe to every mocker and slanderer." Then she explained: "Imitating someone to make others laugh at them is exactly what this surah warns about."

"But I didn't do it," said Sara. "That's good," said her mother. "But Mia needed someone to stand with her. What could you do tomorrow?"

The next day, when Jake started imitating Mia again, Sara took a deep breath and said: "Stop it, Jake. That's not funny, it's mean." A few other children nodded. Jake went quiet. At break, Sara sat with Mia and said: "I think you're really brave for reading aloud." Mia smiled for the first time that week.

Later, the teacher spoke to the class about kindness, and Jake apologised to Mia. By the end of term, Mia and Sara were best friends, and Mia read aloud in the class assembly.

Ask your child: "What did Jake do? Why was it wrong? How did Sara help?"

## What We Learn From Surah Al-Humazah

- **Mocking and belittling** people is a serious wrong.
- **Hurtful looks and gestures** count, not just words.
- **Money doesn't make anyone better** or make them last forever.
- **Allah looks** at hearts and deeds.
- **Don't laugh** when someone is being mocked; stand with them.
- **Think before you speak**: is it true, kind and needed?
- **Share** what you have instead of showing off.

## One-Week Plan: Memorize and Practise

- **Day 1**: memorize verses 1–3.
- **Day 2**: memorize verses 4–6.
- **Day 3**: memorize verses 7–9.
- **Day 4**: recite the whole surah and do the toothpaste lesson.
- **Day 5**: learn the three gates.
- **Day 6**: practise what to do if you see or face mocking.
- **Day 7**: recite Al-Humazah in prayer and celebrate!

## Questions to Ask Your Child

1. What does "humazah" mean?
2. Can mocking happen without words? How?
3. What did the man in the surah think his money would do?
4. Does Allah look at our money or our hearts?
5. What should you do if you see someone being mocked?
6. What are the three gates for our words?
7. What did the toothpaste lesson teach you?

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### My child says "it's just a joke". How do I respond?
Explain that a joke is only funny if everyone is laughing, including the person it's about. If someone feels hurt, it isn't a joke. The surah shows that Allah takes hurtful mockery seriously.

### My child is being bullied. How can this surah help?
It reassures them that Allah is on the side of those who are wronged and that mocking is a serious wrong. Combine this with practical steps: talking to the school, building confidence, and making sure your child feels safe telling you.

### How do I explain the fire part without scaring my child?
Keep it brief: "Allah warns very strongly so that people stop hurting others." Then focus on the good news that anyone who stops and says sorry is forgiven.
