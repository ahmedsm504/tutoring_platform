As soon as a Muslim woman learns she is expecting, she often receives long lists from friends, relatives and social media: "Read Surah Yusuf so your baby will be beautiful." "Read Surah Maryam for an easy delivery." "Read Surah Muhammad if you want a boy." "Play the Quran on your belly so the baby memorises it." Some of these lists come with strict instructions and even warnings.

This article answers clearly: what is established in the religion, what is not, what is known scientifically about how an unborn baby hears, and then offers a practical, gentle plan for an expecting mother with the Quran, along with the Sunnahs for welcoming a newborn.

## Assigning Specific Surahs to Pregnancy and Birth
### What is commonly shared
- **Surah Yusuf**: so that the baby will be beautiful like Yusuf, peace be upon him.
- **Surah Maryam**: for an easy pregnancy and birth, because it tells of the birth of 'Isa, peace be upon him.
- **Surah Luqman**: so the child will be wise.
- **Surah Muhammad**: for a boy, or for good character.
- **Surah Al-Waqi'ah and Ar-Rahman**: for various purposes.
- **Surah Al-Inshiqaq** ("When the sky is split open"): for an easy delivery.

### What is the ruling?
**No authentic Sunnah assigns any surah to a pregnant woman** for a specific purpose such as the baby's beauty, gender or an easier birth. There is no authentic hadith and no established report from the Companions about it. These associations are popular ideas based on surface connections between the story of a surah and the hoped-for result.

In detail:
- **Reading these surahs is certainly permitted**: they are part of the Quran, and all of the Quran is good.
- **Believing that reading them affects the baby's beauty or gender** has no evidence and should not be spread as part of the religion.
- **Reflecting on the meanings of Surah Maryam**, the birth of 'Isa and Allah's mercy to Maryam in her distress, gives an expecting mother comfort and hope, and that is a good and true meaning.

﴿فَأَجَآءَهَا ٱلۡمَخَاضُ إِلَىٰ جِذۡعِ ٱلنَّخۡلَةِ قَالَتۡ يَٰلَيۡتَنِي مِتُّ قَبۡلَ هَٰذَا وَكُنتُ نَسۡيٗا مَّنسِيّٗا﴾ ﴿فَنَادَىٰهَا مِن تَحۡتِهَآ أَلَّا تَحۡزَنِي قَدۡ جَعَلَ رَبُّكِ تَحۡتَكِ سَرِيّٗا﴾ ﴿وَهُزِّيٓ إِلَيۡكِ بِجِذۡعِ ٱلنَّخۡلَةِ تُسَٰقِطۡ عَلَيۡكِ رُطَبٗا جَنِيّٗا﴾ ﴿فَكُلِي وَٱشۡرَبِي وَقَرِّي عَيۡنٗاۖ فَإِمَّا تَرَيِنَّ مِنَ ٱلۡبَشَرِ أَحَدٗا فَقُولِيٓ إِنِّي نَذَرۡتُ لِلرَّحۡمَٰنِ صَوۡمٗا فَلَنۡ أُكَلِّمَ ٱلۡيَوۡمَ إِنسِيّٗا﴾ (Quran 19:23-26)

In these verses we see a woman alone in the hardest moments of labour, and relief and reassurance come to her from Allah: "Do not grieve", fresh dates, water, and "be comforted". Some scholars drew from these verses the benefit of dates for a woman in labour, and the meaning of calm reassurance at the time of birth.

### Why these beliefs can cause harm
- **Linking a surah to the baby's gender** can lead to disappointment, or even blame on the mother, if the baby is not what was hoped for. Boys and girls are both gifts from Allah:

﴿لِّلَّهِ مُلۡكُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِۚ يَخۡلُقُ مَا يَشَآءُۚ يَهَبُ لِمَن يَشَآءُ إِنَٰثٗا وَيَهَبُ لِمَن يَشَآءُ ٱلذُّكُورَ﴾ ﴿أَوۡ يُزَوِّجُهُمۡ ذُكۡرَانٗا وَإِنَٰثٗاۖ وَيَجۡعَلُ مَن يَشَآءُ عَقِيمًاۚ إِنَّهُۥ عَلِيمࣱ قَدِيرࣱ﴾ (Quran 42:49-50)

- **Linking a surah to beauty** reinforces superficial standards; true beauty is in character and faith.
- **Being busy with unestablished practices** can distract from what is established, such as du'a and remembrance.

## Can the Baby Hear the Quran?
### What science says
Studies of fetal development indicate that the baby's hearing develops gradually during pregnancy, and that the baby **begins to respond to sounds around the second trimester**, with responses becoming stronger in the **third trimester**. The clearest sound the baby hears is the **mother's own voice**, which travels through her body, while outside sounds are muffled.

### What this means in practice
- **Your own voice reciting** reaches the baby more clearly than a recording.
- **Placing speakers or headphones on your belly** has no proven additional benefit, and some doctors advise against loud sounds close to the abdomen.
- **The idea that a baby memorises the Quran in the womb** has no scientific evidence; memorisation is a cognitive process that develops after birth.
- **The clearest effect is on the mother herself**: reciting the Quran gives her calm and reassurance, and doctors stress the importance of the mother's emotional state during pregnancy.

## Quranic Du'as for Righteous Children
These du'as were made by prophets and righteous people, and they are the best things for an expecting mother and father to repeat.

### The du'a of the wife of 'Imran
﴿إِذۡ قَالَتِ ٱمۡرَأَتُ عِمۡرَٰنَ رَبِّ إِنِّي نَذَرۡتُ لَكَ مَا فِي بَطۡنِي مُحَرَّرٗا فَتَقَبَّلۡ مِنِّيٓۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلۡعَلِيمُ﴾ ﴿فَلَمَّا وَضَعَتۡهَا قَالَتۡ رَبِّ إِنِّي وَضَعۡتُهَآ أُنثَىٰ وَٱللَّهُ أَعۡلَمُ بِمَا وَضَعَتۡ وَلَيۡسَ ٱلذَّكَرُ كَٱلۡأُنثَىٰۖ وَإِنِّي سَمَّيۡتُهَا مَرۡيَمَ وَإِنِّيٓ أُعِيذُهَا بِكَ وَذُرِّيَّتَهَا مِنَ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنِ ٱلرَّجِيمِ﴾ (Quran 3:35-36)

She dedicated what was in her womb to Allah, asked Him to accept it, and then sought refuge with Allah for her daughter and her descendants from Shaytan. Allah answered: "So her Lord accepted her with good acceptance and caused her to grow in a good manner." This is one of the most beautiful du'as an expecting mother can make: to give her child to Allah and seek His protection for the child and their descendants.

### The du'a of Zakariyya
﴿هُنَالِكَ دَعَا زَكَرِيَّا رَبَّهُۥۖ قَالَ رَبِّ هَبۡ لِي مِن لَّدُنكَ ذُرِّيَّةٗ طَيِّبَةًۖ إِنَّكَ سَمِيعُ ٱلدُّعَآءِ﴾ (Quran 3:38)

### The du'a of Ibrahim
"My Lord, grant me a child from among the righteous" (As-Saffat 100).

### The du'a of the servants of the Most Merciful
﴿وَٱلَّذِينَ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا هَبۡ لَنَا مِنۡ أَزۡوَٰجِنَا وَذُرِّيَّـٰتِنَا قُرَّةَ أَعۡيُنٖ وَٱجۡعَلۡنَا لِلۡمُتَّقِينَ إِمَامًا﴾ (Quran 25:74)

### Ibrahim's du'a for his descendants to pray
"My Lord, make me an establisher of prayer, and from my descendants. Our Lord, and accept my supplication" (Ibrahim 40).

## A Gentle Reading Plan for Each Trimester
Pregnancy brings tiredness, nausea and changes, so a realistic plan is better than an ambitious one.

### First trimester (months 1-3)
- Nausea and exhaustion are often at their peak, so keep your daily portion short: a page or two.
- Read lying down if you need to; reading in any position is permitted.
- Listen to the Quran when reading is hard.
- Keep up the morning and evening adhkar; they are a protection for you and your baby.

### Second trimester (months 4-6)
- Energy often improves, so increase your portion.
- Recite aloud; your voice reaches your baby.
- Read surahs with stories of motherhood and mercy, such as Al 'Imran (the wife of 'Imran and Maryam), Maryam, and Al-Qasas (the mother of Musa), for reflection and comfort, not as special prescriptions.
- Begin a slow, unhurried completion of the Quran if you can.

### Third trimester (months 7-9)
- Tiredness returns as the pregnancy becomes heavier, so reduce your portion and focus on quality rather than quantity.
- Make plenty of du'a with the Quranic du'as above.
- Prepare a plan for the postnatal period: listening, dhikr, du'a, and reciting according to the scholars who permit it.
- Write a letter of du'a to your baby that you can read to them after birth.

## Labour and the Postnatal Period
### During labour
- Make plenty of du'a, seek forgiveness and remember Allah. The one in distress is closer to being answered:

﴿أَمَّن يُجِيبُ ٱلۡمُضۡطَرَّ إِذَا دَعَاهُ وَيَكۡشِفُ ٱلسُّوٓءَ وَيَجۡعَلُكُمۡ خُلَفَآءَ ٱلۡأَرۡضِۗ أَءِلَٰهࣱ مَّعَ ٱللَّهِۚ قَلِيلٗا مَّا تَذَكَّرُونَ﴾ (Quran 27:62)

- Say the du'a of distress reported in al-Bukhari and Muslim: "La ilaha illallahul-'Azimul-Halim, la ilaha illallahu Rabbul-'Arshil-'Azim, la ilaha illallahu Rabbus-samawati wa Rabbul-ardi wa Rabbul-'Arshil-Karim."
- Use general ruqyah: Al-Fatihah and the last three surahs.

### During nifas
The ruling for a woman in the postnatal period is the same as for a menstruating woman regarding reciting the Quran and touching the Mushaf, and scholars differ on reciting, as explained in our separate article. In all cases, listening, dhikr and du'a are permitted by agreement.

## Sunnahs for Welcoming a Newborn
- **Tahnik**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, babies were brought to the Prophet ﷺ and he would chew a date and rub it on their palate and pray for blessing for them.
- **Choosing a good name**: the most beloved names to Allah are 'Abdullah and 'Abdur-Rahman, as in Sahih Muslim.
- **'Aqiqah**: on the seventh day, two sheep for a boy and one for a girl, as established in the Sunan with authentic chains.
- **Shaving the baby's head** on the seventh day and giving its weight in silver in charity, as reported in the Sunan.
- **The adhan in the baby's ear**: there is a hadith from Abu Rafi' in Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi, and hadith scholars differ over its authenticity; some graded it hasan and others weak. Many Muslims practise it.
- **Making du'a for the baby** for blessing and righteousness.

## Mothers in the Quran: Stories to Reflect On
### The mother of Musa: trusting Allah in the hardest decision
Allah inspired her to place her baby in the river, and promised to return him to her. She trusted, and Allah fulfilled His promise:

﴿وَأَوۡحَيۡنَآ إِلَىٰٓ أُمِّ مُوسَىٰٓ أَنۡ أَرۡضِعِيهِۖ فَإِذَا خِفۡتِ عَلَيۡهِ فَأَلۡقِيهِ فِي ٱلۡيَمِّ وَلَا تَخَافِي وَلَا تَحۡزَنِيٓۖ إِنَّا رَآدُّوهُ إِلَيۡكِ وَجَاعِلُوهُ مِنَ ٱلۡمُرۡسَلِينَ﴾ (Quran 28:7)

### The wife of 'Imran: dedicating her child to Allah
She dedicated her unborn child to Allah, then gave birth to a girl and said, "My Lord, I have delivered a female." Allah accepted her and honoured Maryam greatly. The lesson: intend from now that your child is for Allah, boy or girl.

### Maryam: patience in loneliness and pain
She faced labour alone, and Allah sent her relief, food and reassurance.

### Sarah: good news after despair
She received the good news of a child in old age and was amazed, and the angels said: "Do you wonder at the decree of Allah?"

## The Husband's Role
1. **Recite the Quran near your wife** sometimes, so she is reassured and your voice also reaches the baby.
2. **Make du'a together** with the Quranic du'as.
3. **Help with the house** so she has time and energy for worship and rest.
4. **Reassure her** when she feels guilty about reading less.
5. **Learn the newborn Sunnahs together**, so you are prepared.

## Practical Ideas to Connect Your Baby with the Quran
- **Record your voice** reciting Al-Fatihah and the last three surahs, so your baby can hear your recitation to calm them.
- **Prepare a Mushaf for the baby**, writing your du'a and their date of birth at the front.
- **Choose a calm reciter** whose voice will accompany your baby in the early months.
- **Intend now** to teach your child the Quran, and look for a suitable teacher early.

## Common Worries
**"I feel guilty because I cannot read as much as before pregnancy."** Do not feel guilty. Allah knows your tiredness, and it is hoped that the good you used to do when well is still written for you. Read what you can, even one verse, listen, and remember Allah lying down.

**"I was told that not reading a certain surah will affect the baby."** This has no basis. Do not let the Quran become a source of fear; it is a source of peace.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Which surah should a pregnant woman read?
All of the Quran is good for her; no surah is established for a specific purpose such as the baby's beauty or gender.

### Does reading Surah Yusuf make the baby beautiful?
There is no evidence for this; reading it is good, but linking it to the baby's beauty is a belief without basis.

### Can the baby hear the Quran?
Studies suggest the baby responds to sounds from around the second trimester, and the mother's voice is the clearest sound they hear.

### Should I play the Quran on my belly?
Reciting in your own voice is better, and there is no proven benefit in placing speakers on your belly.

### What du'a should a pregnant woman make?
The du'a of the wife of 'Imran, Zakariyya's "My Lord, grant me from Yourself good offspring", and "Our Lord, grant us from our spouses and offspring comfort to our eyes".

### Does reading Surah Muhammad give a baby boy?
There is no evidence for this; boys and girls are gifts that Allah gives to whom He wills.

### Does the Quran replace medical care in pregnancy?
No. The Quran brings peace to the heart, and medical care is part of taking the means; a Muslim woman does both.

## Conclusion
No surah is established for a baby's beauty, gender or an easier birth, but all of the Quran is good for an expecting mother, and her own voice reciting is the clearest sound her baby hears. Make your pregnancy a journey with the Quran through a gentle plan that suits each stage, fill your days with the Quranic du'as of the prophets and the righteous, learn the Sunnahs of welcoming your baby, and let the Quran be your comfort, not a source of worry.
