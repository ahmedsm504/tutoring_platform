Six letters come from the **throat** (al-ḥalq). English has only two of them (the glottal stop and *h*), so the other four need practice.

## The three levels
| Level | Letters | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Deepest | ء hamza, هـ haa | Like the catch in "uh-oh" and a soft English h |
| Middle | ع ʿayn, ح ḥaa | Tighten the middle of the throat |
| Closest to the mouth | غ ghayn, خ khaa | Like a gargle; khaa is the breathy one |

## Pairs English speakers mix up
| Pair | Difference | Example |
|---|---|---|
| هـ / ح | ح is tighter, from the middle of the throat | ﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ﴾ |
| ء / ع | ع has a squeezed, voiced sound | ﴿نَعۡبُدُ﴾ |
| غ / خ | غ is voiced, خ is whispered | ﴿ٱلۡمَغۡضُوبِ﴾ |

## Izhar before throat letters
- When **noon sakinah or tanween** comes before any throat letter, the noon is pronounced **clearly**, with no extra ghunnah.
- **Example:** ﴿أَنۡعَمۡتَ﴾.
- Read [Noon Sakinah and Tanween Rules](/blog/noon-sakinah-tanween-rules-english/).

## Exercises
1. Say *aʿ*, *aḥ*, *agh*, *akh* slowly, feeling where each stops.
2. Read Al-Fatihah and underline every throat letter.
3. Record yourself and compare with a teacher.
