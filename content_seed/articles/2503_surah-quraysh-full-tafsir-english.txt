Surah Quraysh is four verses reminding the tribe of Quraysh of two blessings that life cannot do without: food and security. Quraysh lived in a valley with no crops, yet Allah arranged for them two trade journeys each year, to Yemen in winter and to Syria in summer, from which they returned with provisions; and He made them safe, respected by all the Arabs as the people of the Sacred House. Then the surah states what is required in return: "Let them worship the Lord of this House."

This guide explains the surah verse by verse in English and reflects on the blessings of food and security in our own lives.

## Quick Facts About Surah Quraysh
- **Number of verses**: 4.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 106, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: after Quraysh; it is the only surah named after a tribe.
- **Also called**: *Li-ilafi Quraysh*.

## The Full Text of Surah Quraysh
﴿لِإِيلَٰفِ قُرَيۡشٍ﴾ ﴿إِۦلَٰفِهِمۡ رِحۡلَةَ ٱلشِّتَآءِ وَٱلصَّيۡفِ﴾ ﴿فَلۡيَعۡبُدُواْ رَبَّ هَٰذَا ٱلۡبَيۡتِ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِيٓ أَطۡعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٖ وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنۡ خَوۡفِۭ﴾ (Quran 106:1-4)

**Meaning:** For the security of Quraysh, their security in the journey of winter and summer, let them worship the Lord of this House, who fed them against hunger and made them safe from fear.

## Who Were Quraysh?
Quraysh was a tribe of Kinanah, descended from Isma'il son of Ibrahim, and the tribe of the Prophet ﷺ. They lived in Mecca, served the Sacred House and gave water to the pilgrims. In Sahih Muslim, Wathilah ibn al-Asqa' reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah chose Kinanah from the children of Isma'il, chose Quraysh from Kinanah, chose Banu Hashim from Quraysh, and chose me from Banu Hashim."

## Verses 1–2: For the Security of Quraysh
﴿لِإِيلَٰفِ قُرَيۡشٍ﴾ ﴿إِۦلَٰفِهِمۡ رِحۡلَةَ ٱلشِّتَآءِ وَٱلصَّيۡفِ﴾ (Quran 106:1-2)

**"Ilaf"** comes from the root of familiarity and habit: what Quraysh had grown used to, their safe, regular journeys. It is also explained as the pacts and guarantees of safe passage that Quraysh obtained from kings and tribes so their caravans could travel securely. The word is repeated to emphasise and to explain what they were accustomed to:
- **The winter journey**: to Yemen, where it is warmer.
- **The summer journey**: to Syria, where it is cooler.

### What is "for" connected to?
The commentators give several views:
1. **To Surah Al-Fil**: Allah destroyed the people of the elephant for the security of Quraysh, so they would remain safe in Mecca and in their journeys.
2. **To "let them worship"**: let them worship the Lord of this House because of this blessing of security.
3. **An expression of wonder**: marvel at Quraysh, who enjoyed these journeys and yet abandoned the worship of Allah.
All the views meet in one meaning: this blessing calls for worship.

## Verse 3: Let Them Worship the Lord of This House
﴿فَلۡيَعۡبُدُواْ رَبَّ هَٰذَا ٱلۡبَيۡتِ﴾ (Quran 106:3)

**"Let them worship"**: a command to worship Allah alone. The *fa* shows cause: because of these blessings, let them worship. **"The Lord of this House"**: Allah links Himself to the Ka'bah to honour it, and to remind Quraysh that their standing among the Arabs came from this House. How then could they worship idols around it and neglect its Lord?

**A painful irony**: Quraysh honoured the House, yet they had placed three hundred and sixty idols around it. The verse tells them: worship the Lord of the House, not the idols.

## Verse 4: Fed Against Hunger, Safe From Fear
﴿ٱلَّذِيٓ أَطۡعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٖ وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنۡ خَوۡفِۭ﴾ (Quran 106:4)

**"Who fed them against hunger"**: He provided them with food in a valley without crops, through trade and the pilgrims. **"And made them safe from fear"**: secure in their town and on their journeys, while the Arabs around them were raided and robbed, as Surah Al-'Ankabut says: "Have they not seen that We made a safe sanctuary while people are snatched away all around them?"

### The answer to Ibrahim's prayer
These blessings answer the prayer of Ibrahim for Mecca:

﴿وَإِذۡ قَالَ إِبۡرَٰهِـۧمُ رَبِّ ٱجۡعَلۡ هَٰذَا بَلَدًا ءَامِنٗا وَٱرۡزُقۡ أَهۡلَهُۥ مِنَ ٱلثَّمَرَٰتِ مَنۡ ءَامَنَ مِنۡهُم بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡيَوۡمِ ٱلۡأٓخِرِۚ قَالَ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَأُمَتِّعُهُۥ قَلِيلٗا ثُمَّ أَضۡطَرُّهُۥٓ إِلَىٰ عَذَابِ ٱلنَّارِۖ وَبِئۡسَ ٱلۡمَصِيرُ﴾ (Quran 2:126)

**Meaning:** And when Ibrahim said: My Lord, make this a secure city and provide its people with fruits, those of them who believe in Allah and the Last Day...

Ibrahim asked for security and provision, and Allah gave both to the people of Mecca.

## Word Meanings
- **Ilaf**: familiarity, habit; or pacts of safe passage.
- **Quraysh**: the tribe of the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Rihlah**: journey.
- **Ash-Shita'**: winter, the journey to Yemen.
- **As-Sayf**: summer, the journey to Syria.
- **Falya'budu**: let them worship.
- **Al-Bayt**: the House, the Ka'bah.
- **At'amahum min ju'**: fed them against hunger.
- **Amanahum min khawf**: made them safe from fear.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"Ilaf" repeated** to emphasise the blessing.
2. **"Journey" in the singular** for two journeys, as a general noun or because both serve one purpose.
3. **"This House"**: a near demonstrative, pointing to what is right before them.
4. **"Hunger" and "fear" left indefinite**: a severe hunger and a great fear.
5. **Food before security**, because food is the most urgent need, then security without which food cannot be enjoyed.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd badal** in *li-ilafi* and *ilafihim*.
- **Madd muttasil** in *ash-shita'i*: four or five counts.
- **Madd lin** when stopping on *quraysh*, *as-sayf*, *al-bayt* and *khawf*: a *ya* or *waw* with sukun after a fatha.
- **Lam shamsiyyah** in *ash-shita'*, *as-sayf* and *alladhi*; **lam qamariyyah** in *al-bayt*.
- **Idgham shafawi** in *at'amahum min* and *amanahum min*: meem merges into meem with ghunnah.
- **Ikhfa** in *min ju'*: nun hidden before *jim*.
- **Izhar** in *min khawf*: nun clear before the throat letter *kha*.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Quraysh is so closely linked to Al-Fil before it that some scholars described them as one in meaning, though they are two separate surahs in the mushaf with the basmalah between them. Al-Fil shows the protection of the House; Quraysh shows the fruit of that protection for its people. After it comes Al-Ma'un, which condemns those who "do not encourage feeding the poor": having reminded Quraysh that Allah fed them, the next surah condemns those who withhold food from others. Whoever Allah has fed should feed others.

## Security: Among the Greatest Blessings
Security is a blessing whose value is only known by those who lose it. Islam gives it great weight:
- **Ibrahim's prayer** put security before provision: "My Lord, make this a secure city."
- **The hadith**: at-Tirmidhi recorded, grading it *hasan*, from 'Ubaydullah ibn Mihsan that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever wakes up secure in his home, healthy in his body, with his food for the day, it is as if the whole world has been gathered for him."
- **Security is the fruit of faith**: "Those who believe and do not mix their faith with wrongdoing, they will have security, and they are rightly guided" (6:82).
- **Security is the reward of worship**: "He will surely replace their fear with security; they worship Me, not associating anything with Me" (24:55).

**How is security preserved?** Through faith, obedience and gratitude. Ingratitude replaces it with fear, as Surah An-Nahl describes a town that was secure and at peace, its provision coming abundantly from everywhere, "but it denied the favours of Allah, so Allah made it taste the garment of hunger and fear for what they used to do" (16:112).

**Reflect**: Surah Quraysh says "fed them against hunger and made them safe from fear"; Surah An-Nahl speaks of "the garment of hunger and fear". Gratitude preserves both blessings, and ingratitude replaces both.

## Trade and Travel in Islam
The surah mentions trade journeys as a blessing, showing that trade and earning a living are honourable. The Prophet ﷺ himself was a merchant before prophethood and travelled to Syria with Khadijah's goods. "When the prayer is finished, disperse in the land and seek the bounty of Allah" (62:10). A Muslim works and trades, but never forgets that provision is from Allah and that gratitude means worship.

## Practical Lessons
1. Do not let familiarity make you forget a blessing.
2. Thank Allah through worship, the highest form of gratitude.
3. Praise Allah for your food and your safety every day.
4. Feed the hungry: whoever Allah fed should feed others.
5. Preserve security through obedience; sins remove blessings.
6. Work for your provision while relying on Allah.
7. Pray for places that have lost their security and food.

## Teaching Children
- Draw a map: Mecca, Yemen and Syria, and the winter and summer journeys.
- Before meals, say together: praise be to Allah who fed us against hunger.
- Tell them about children who have no food or safety, so they learn gratitude and charity.

## Quraysh After Islam
After years of opposing the Prophet ﷺ, Mecca was conquered and most of Quraysh entered Islam. From them came the four rightly guided caliphs and many of the greatest companions, such as Abu 'Ubaydah, Talhah, az-Zubayr, 'Abd ar-Rahman ibn 'Awf and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "People follow Quraysh in this matter: their Muslims follow their Muslims, and their disbelievers follow their disbelievers."

**The lesson**: lineage alone is not enough. Quraysh produced Abu Jahl and Abu Lahab, and it produced Abu Bakr and 'Umar. In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever is slowed down by his deeds will not be hastened by his lineage."

## Gratitude in Action
The surah does not say "let them give thanks" but "let them worship", because the highest gratitude is worship. Gratitude has four parts: acknowledging in the heart that the blessing is from Allah; praising Him with the tongue; using the blessing in obedience; and sharing it with others. In Sahih al-Bukhari, Abu Umamah reported that when his meal was cleared away the Prophet ﷺ would say: "Praise be to Allah, abundant, good and blessed praise, not insufficient, not abandoned, and not dispensed with, our Lord."

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does "li-ilafi Quraysh" mean?
For the security and familiar safe journeys of Quraysh, or for the pacts that protected their trade.

### What were the journeys of winter and summer?
Two trade journeys of Quraysh: to Yemen in winter and to Syria in summer.

### Who is "the Lord of this House"?
Allah, the Lord of the Ka'bah.

### What does "fed them against hunger and made them safe from fear" mean?
He gave them food in a valley without crops, and security while people around them were raided.

### Are Surah Quraysh and Surah Al-Fil one surah?
They are two separate surahs in the mushaf with the basmalah between them, though closely linked in meaning.

### Why is the surah named after Quraysh?
Because it speaks of Allah's blessings on Quraysh; it is the only surah named after a tribe.

### How many verses are in Surah Quraysh?
4 verses.

### Why was food mentioned before security?
Food is the most urgent human need, and security is what allows food to be enjoyed; Ibrahim put security first in his prayer because it is the basis of stability. Both are great blessings.

### Is there a special virtue for reciting Surah Quraysh?
No specific authentic hadith on its virtue is established; it is recited in prayer like the other short surahs.

### What does "the Lord of this House" teach us?
That the honour of Quraysh came from the House, so the One who deserves their worship is its Lord, not the idols placed around it.

## Conclusion
Surah Quraysh tells you that the food on your table and the safety in which you sleep are not ordinary things; they are two great blessings from the Lord of the House. Do not let familiarity make you forget the Giver. Thank Him through worship, feed those around you, and pray for those deprived of safety and food, for gratitude preserves blessings and ingratitude replaces them with fear and hunger.
