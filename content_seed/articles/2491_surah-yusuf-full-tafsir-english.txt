Surah Yusuf is unique in the Quran. Almost the entire surah tells one story from beginning to end, and Allah Himself calls it *ahsan al-qasas*, the best of stories. It moves from a child's dream to a dark well, from a palace to a prison, from a prison to the treasury of Egypt, and from a grieving father to a joyful reunion. Along the way it teaches patience, chastity, forgiveness and trust in Allah's hidden plan.

This guide explains the surah in English, scene by scene, staying with what the Quran and authentic hadith tell us rather than the many unverified additions found in storybooks.

## Quick Facts About Surah Yusuf
- **Number of verses**: 111.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 12, in the twelfth and thirteenth juz.
- **Name**: after Prophet Yusuf son of Ya'qub, peace be upon them.
- **Central theme**: Allah's plan unfolds through trials, and patience and righteousness lead to honour.

## Who Was Yusuf?
In Sahih al-Bukhari, Ibn 'Umar reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The noble, son of the noble, son of the noble, son of the noble: Yusuf son of Ya'qub son of Ishaq son of Ibrahim." Four generations of prophets. And in the account of the Night Journey in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said that he met Yusuf in the third heaven and "he had been given half of beauty".

## Verses 1–6: The Best of Stories and the Dream
﴿نَحۡنُ نَقُصُّ عَلَيۡكَ أَحۡسَنَ ٱلۡقَصَصِ بِمَآ أَوۡحَيۡنَآ إِلَيۡكَ هَٰذَا ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَ وَإِن كُنتَ مِن قَبۡلِهِۦ لَمِنَ ٱلۡغَٰفِلِينَ﴾ (Quran 12:3)

**Meaning:** We relate to you the best of stories through Our revealing this Quran to you, though before it you were among the unaware.

﴿إِذۡ قَالَ يُوسُفُ لِأَبِيهِ يَـٰٓأَبَتِ إِنِّي رَأَيۡتُ أَحَدَ عَشَرَ كَوۡكَبٗا وَٱلشَّمۡسَ وَٱلۡقَمَرَ رَأَيۡتُهُمۡ لِي سَٰجِدِينَ﴾ ﴿قَالَ يَٰبُنَيَّ لَا تَقۡصُصۡ رُءۡيَاكَ عَلَىٰٓ إِخۡوَتِكَ فَيَكِيدُواْ لَكَ كَيۡدًاۖ إِنَّ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنَ لِلۡإِنسَٰنِ عَدُوࣱّ مُّبِينࣱ﴾ (Quran 12:4-5)

**Meaning:** When Yusuf said to his father: O my father, I saw eleven stars and the sun and the moon; I saw them prostrating to me. He said: O my son, do not relate your dream to your brothers, lest they plot against you; Satan is to man a clear enemy.

The whole story is contained in this dream: the eleven stars are his brothers, and the sun and moon his parents. Ya'qub understood its meaning and its danger. His advice teaches a lesson about blessings: not every blessing should be announced to everyone, especially where envy is feared.

## Verses 7–18: The Brothers and the Well
The brothers felt their father loved Yusuf and his brother more. Their jealousy grew into a plan: kill him or throw him into a distant land, then become righteous afterwards. Notice that line: they planned sin with a promise of later repentance. This is one of Satan's oldest tricks.

They took Yusuf, threw him into the bottom of a well, and returned in the evening weeping, with his shirt stained with false blood.

﴿وَجَآءُو عَلَىٰ قَمِيصِهِۦ بِدَمٖ كَذِبٖۚ قَالَ بَلۡ سَوَّلَتۡ لَكُمۡ أَنفُسُكُمۡ أَمۡرٗاۖ فَصَبۡرࣱ جَمِيلࣱۖ وَٱللَّهُ ٱلۡمُسۡتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ﴾ (Quran 12:18)

**Meaning:** They brought his shirt with false blood. He said: Rather, your souls have tempted you into something; so patience is most fitting, and Allah is the One whose help is sought against what you describe.

*Sabrun jamil*, beautiful patience: patience without complaint to people. Ya'qub did not explode, did not curse, did not give up. He turned to Allah.

## Verses 19–22: The Caravan and Egypt
A caravan drew water from the well and found the boy. They sold him for a few coins. In Egypt, a man of authority bought him and said to his wife: "Make his stay honourable; perhaps he will benefit us or we will adopt him as a son."

﴿وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِي ٱشۡتَرَىٰهُ مِن مِّصۡرَ لِٱمۡرَأَتِهِۦٓ أَكۡرِمِي مَثۡوَىٰهُ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَنفَعَنَآ أَوۡ نَتَّخِذَهُۥ وَلَدٗاۚ وَكَذَٰلِكَ مَكَّنَّا لِيُوسُفَ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَلِنُعَلِّمَهُۥ مِن تَأۡوِيلِ ٱلۡأَحَادِيثِۚ وَٱللَّهُ غَالِبٌ عَلَىٰٓ أَمۡرِهِۦ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكۡثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَعۡلَمُونَ﴾ (Quran 12:21)

**Meaning:** Thus We established Yusuf in the land, to teach him the interpretation of events. Allah has full control over His affair, but most people do not know.

The boy thrown into a well to be lost is now in the house of a powerful man in Egypt. What looked like loss was the first step of honour.

## Verses 23–34: The Test of Chastity
﴿وَرَٰوَدَتۡهُ ٱلَّتِي هُوَ فِي بَيۡتِهَا عَن نَّفۡسِهِۦ وَغَلَّقَتِ ٱلۡأَبۡوَٰبَ وَقَالَتۡ هَيۡتَ لَكَۚ قَالَ مَعَاذَ ٱللَّهِۖ إِنَّهُۥ رَبِّيٓ أَحۡسَنَ مَثۡوَايَۖ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يُفۡلِحُ ٱلظَّـٰلِمُونَ﴾ (Quran 12:23)

**Meaning:** And she in whose house he was sought to seduce him. She closed the doors and said: Come. He said: I seek refuge in Allah. He is my master, who has treated me well. The wrongdoers do not succeed.

Every condition for sin was present: he was young and handsome, a stranger far from home, she was a woman of status who invited him, the doors were closed, and refusing could cost him dearly. Yet his first words were "I seek refuge in Allah." In al-Bukhari and Muslim, among the seven whom Allah will shade on the Day when there is no shade but His is "a man invited by a woman of status and beauty, and he said: I fear Allah."

When the truth of what happened spread in the city, the women gossiped. She invited them, gave each a knife, and called Yusuf in. Overwhelmed, they cut their hands and said: "This is no human; this is a noble angel." She then threatened him with prison.

﴿قَالَ رَبِّ ٱلسِّجۡنُ أَحَبُّ إِلَيَّ مِمَّا يَدۡعُونَنِيٓ إِلَيۡهِۖ وَإِلَّا تَصۡرِفۡ عَنِّي كَيۡدَهُنَّ أَصۡبُ إِلَيۡهِنَّ وَأَكُن مِّنَ ٱلۡجَٰهِلِينَ﴾ (Quran 12:33)

**Meaning:** He said: My Lord, prison is dearer to me than what they invite me to. And unless You turn their plot away from me, I will incline towards them and be among the ignorant.

Yusuf did not rely on his own strength. He asked Allah to protect him. Real chastity is not confidence in oneself; it is reliance on Allah.

## Verses 35–42: The Prison and the Call to Tawhid
Though innocent, Yusuf was imprisoned. Two young men entered with him, each with a dream. Before interpreting their dreams, he did something remarkable:

﴿يَٰصَٰحِبَيِ ٱلسِّجۡنِ ءَأَرۡبَابࣱ مُّتَفَرِّقُونَ خَيۡرٌ أَمِ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡوَٰحِدُ ٱلۡقَهَّارُ﴾ ﴿مَا تَعۡبُدُونَ مِن دُونِهِۦٓ إِلَّآ أَسۡمَآءٗ سَمَّيۡتُمُوهَآ أَنتُمۡ وَءَابَآؤُكُم مَّآ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ بِهَا مِن سُلۡطَٰنٍۚ إِنِ ٱلۡحُكۡمُ إِلَّا لِلَّهِ أَمَرَ أَلَّا تَعۡبُدُوٓاْ إِلَّآ إِيَّاهُۚ ذَٰلِكَ ٱلدِّينُ ٱلۡقَيِّمُ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكۡثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَعۡلَمُونَ﴾ (Quran 12:39-40)

**Meaning:** O my two companions of the prison, are many different lords better, or Allah, the One, the Prevailing? You do not worship besides Him except names you have named, you and your fathers, for which Allah has sent down no authority. Judgement belongs only to Allah. He has commanded that you worship none but Him. That is the upright religion, but most people do not know.

Yusuf used the moment when they needed him to call them to Allah. Even in prison, he was a caller to tawhid. Then he interpreted their dreams: one would serve wine to his master, the other would be crucified. He asked the one who would be freed to mention him to the king, but Satan made him forget, and Yusuf remained in prison for several years.

## Verses 43–57: The King's Dream and the Storehouses
The king dreamed of seven fat cows eaten by seven lean ones, and seven green ears of grain and seven dry ones. No one could interpret it until the freed prisoner remembered Yusuf.

Yusuf interpreted it as seven years of plenty followed by seven years of drought, and he offered a plan: store the grain in its ears during the good years, eat only a little, and save for the hard years.

When the king summoned him, Yusuf refused to leave prison until his innocence was declared. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ praised his patience: "Had I stayed in prison as long as Yusuf stayed, I would have answered the caller." The women admitted the truth, and the wife of al-'Aziz confessed: "I sought to seduce him, and he is among the truthful."

﴿قَالَ ٱجۡعَلۡنِي عَلَىٰ خَزَآئِنِ ٱلۡأَرۡضِۖ إِنِّي حَفِيظٌ عَلِيمࣱ﴾ (Quran 12:55)

**Meaning:** He said: Put me in charge of the storehouses of the land; I am a knowledgeable guardian.

He asked for the responsibility he was qualified for, citing two qualities: trustworthiness and knowledge. These remain the two conditions for any responsibility.

## Verses 58–82: The Brothers Come to Egypt
The drought reached Canaan, and the brothers came to Egypt for grain. Yusuf recognised them; they did not recognise him. He asked them to bring their younger brother next time. When they returned with him, Yusuf revealed himself privately to his full brother and then, by a plan, kept him in Egypt by placing the king's measuring cup in his bag.

Ya'qub received the news of a second lost son.

## Verses 83–98: The Grief of Ya'qub and the Shirt
﴿قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أَشۡكُواْ بَثِّي وَحُزۡنِيٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَأَعۡلَمُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ﴾ ﴿يَٰبَنِيَّ ٱذۡهَبُواْ فَتَحَسَّسُواْ مِن يُوسُفَ وَأَخِيهِ وَلَا تَاْيۡـَٔسُواْ مِن رَّوۡحِ ٱللَّهِۖ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يَاْيۡـَٔسُ مِن رَّوۡحِ ٱللَّهِ إِلَّا ٱلۡقَوۡمُ ٱلۡكَٰفِرُونَ﴾ (Quran 12:86-87)

**Meaning:** He said: I only complain of my grief and sorrow to Allah, and I know from Allah what you do not know. O my sons, go and search for Yusuf and his brother, and do not despair of the mercy of Allah; none despairs of the mercy of Allah except the disbelieving people.

Ya'qub wept until his eyes turned white with grief, yet he never complained to people, only to Allah. And he never lost hope. Grief is human; despair is not permitted.

When the brothers returned in humility, Yusuf revealed himself: "I am Yusuf, and this is my brother." They confessed their wrong.

﴿قَالَ لَا تَثۡرِيبَ عَلَيۡكُمُ ٱلۡيَوۡمَۖ يَغۡفِرُ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمۡۖ وَهُوَ أَرۡحَمُ ٱلرَّـٰحِمِينَ﴾ (Quran 12:92)

**Meaning:** He said: No blame upon you today. May Allah forgive you, and He is the Most Merciful of the merciful.

After everything they did, he forgave them completely, without reproach. He sent his shirt to his father, and when it was cast over his face, his sight returned.

## Verses 99–101: The Dream Fulfilled
﴿وَرَفَعَ أَبَوَيۡهِ عَلَى ٱلۡعَرۡشِ وَخَرُّواْ لَهُۥ سُجَّدٗاۖ وَقَالَ يَـٰٓأَبَتِ هَٰذَا تَأۡوِيلُ رُءۡيَٰيَ مِن قَبۡلُ قَدۡ جَعَلَهَا رَبِّي حَقّٗاۖ وَقَدۡ أَحۡسَنَ بِيٓ إِذۡ أَخۡرَجَنِي مِنَ ٱلسِّجۡنِ وَجَآءَ بِكُم مِّنَ ٱلۡبَدۡوِ مِنۢ بَعۡدِ أَن نَّزَغَ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنُ بَيۡنِي وَبَيۡنَ إِخۡوَتِيٓۚ إِنَّ رَبِّي لَطِيفࣱ لِّمَا يَشَآءُۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلۡعَلِيمُ ٱلۡحَكِيمُ﴾ (Quran 12:100)

**Meaning:** He raised his parents on the throne, and they fell down before him in prostration. He said: O my father, this is the interpretation of my dream of before; my Lord has made it come true. He was good to me when He brought me out of prison and brought you from the desert after Satan had caused discord between me and my brothers. My Lord is subtle in what He wills. He is the All-Knowing, the All-Wise.

Notice his courtesy: he mentions being brought out of *prison*, not the well, so as not to embarrass his brothers. And he blames Satan, not them. Then he makes a beautiful du'a: "Cause me to die a Muslim and join me with the righteous."

## Verses 102–111: The Lesson
﴿لَقَدۡ كَانَ فِي قَصَصِهِمۡ عِبۡرَةࣱ لِّأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَلۡبَٰبِۗ مَا كَانَ حَدِيثٗا يُفۡتَرَىٰ وَلَٰكِن تَصۡدِيقَ ٱلَّذِي بَيۡنَ يَدَيۡهِ وَتَفۡصِيلَ كُلِّ شَيۡءٖ وَهُدٗى وَرَحۡمَةٗ لِّقَوۡمٖ يُؤۡمِنُونَ﴾ (Quran 12:111)

**Meaning:** There is truly a lesson in their stories for people of understanding. It is not a fabricated narration but a confirmation of what came before it, a detailed explanation of everything, and guidance and mercy for a people who believe.

## Key Vocabulary
- **Ahsan al-qasas**: the best of stories.
- **Ghayabat al-jubb**: the depths of the well.
- **Sabrun jamil**: beautiful patience.
- **Ma'adh Allah**: I seek refuge in Allah.
- **Ta'wil al-ahadith**: the interpretation of events and dreams.
- **Hafizun 'alim**: a knowledgeable guardian.
- **La tathriba**: no blame.
- **Latif**: subtle, gentle in His plan.

## Practical Lessons from Surah Yusuf
1. **Trust the hidden plan.** The well led to the palace and the prison led to the treasury.
2. **Practise beautiful patience.** Complain to Allah, not to people.
3. **Guard your chastity by relying on Allah.** "I seek refuge in Allah" and "unless You turn their plot away".
4. **Call to Allah wherever you are.** Yusuf gave da'wah in prison.
5. **Seek responsibility only with trustworthiness and knowledge.**
6. **Protect your reputation.** Yusuf insisted on being cleared before leaving prison.
7. **Forgive fully.** "No blame upon you today."
8. **Never despair of Allah's mercy.**
9. **Plan for hard times.** Save in the years of plenty.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Why is Surah Yusuf called the best of stories?
Allah Himself describes it so in verse 3; it is complete, full of lessons, and shows how His plan works through trials.

### What did Yusuf's dream mean?
The eleven stars were his brothers and the sun and moon his parents, who would one day honour him.

### What is "beautiful patience"?
Patience without complaining to people, turning only to Allah.

### Is the wife of al-'Aziz named in the Quran?
No. Names found in some books come from unverified sources; the Quran calls her the wife of al-'Aziz.

### What did Yusuf say when he forgave his brothers?
"No blame upon you today. May Allah forgive you, and He is the Most Merciful of the merciful."

### How many verses are in Surah Yusuf?
111 verses.

## Conclusion
Surah Yusuf shows you a life in which every closed door opened onto a better one. A boy in a well became the guardian of Egypt's storehouses; a man in prison became the means of saving a nation from famine; a grieving father regained his son and his sight. The thread running through all of it is patience, chastity and trust that Allah is subtle in what He wills. When your own life feels like a well or a prison, read Surah Yusuf and remember how the story ends.
