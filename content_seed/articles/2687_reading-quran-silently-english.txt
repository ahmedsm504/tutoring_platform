Many people read the Quran on the train, at work, or in a room where others are sleeping, and they read silently, their eyes moving over the lines without their lips moving. Others finish their daily portion "in their head" because they are shy of being heard, or because it is faster. Then a question comes: Is this counted as recitation? Do I get the reward of ten good deeds for each letter? And what about my prayer: is it enough to "say" Al-Fatihah in my heart in the silent prayers?

This article explains what the jurists say about the minimum of recitation, the difference between reciting, looking and reflecting, the ruling on silent recitation in prayer and remembrance, and offers practical solutions that give you the full reward without disturbing anyone.

## The Virtue of Reciting with the Tongue
At-Tirmidhi reported from 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever recites a letter from the Book of Allah will have a good deed for it, and a good deed is multiplied by ten. I do not say that Alif Lam Mim is a letter, but Alif is a letter, Lam is a letter and Mim is a letter." At-Tirmidhi graded it hasan sahih.

The hadith connects the reward to **reciting**, and to the number of **letters**. That raises the question: what kind of reading achieves this reward? Is it the eyes passing over the letters, or pronouncing them?

## What Counts as Recitation According to the Jurists?
The jurists agree that **recitation in the legal sense is done with the tongue**, and that merely looking with the eyes or passing the words through the heart without moving the tongue is not called recitation. They then differ on the minimum of recitation, in two well-known views:

### View one: you must hear yourself
This is the position of the Shafi'is and Hanbalis, and one view among the Hanafis. Recitation is pronouncing the letters so that you would hear yourself if there were no obstacle such as noise or deafness. If you move your tongue and lips without producing any sound that you could hear, you have not recited, according to them.

### View two: correct pronunciation of the letters, even if you do not hear yourself
This is a view among the Hanafis and the Malikis: it is enough to move the tongue and lips correctly with the letters, even if the sound does not reach your ears. The Malikis say that moving the tongue is sufficient in quiet recitation.

**Both views agree on something important**: recitation requires **the action of the tongue**. Someone who reads only with the eyes or the heart, without any movement of the tongue, is not reciting, by agreement. They are looking or reflecting.

## Reciting, Looking and Reflecting: Three Different Acts of Worship
### 1. Recitation
Pronouncing the letters of the Quran with the tongue. It carries the reward of the letters mentioned in the hadith, and it has its own rulings in prayer and remembrance.

### 2. Looking at the Mushaf
The eyes passing over the words. Some scholars mention that looking at the Mushaf is a recommended act of worship, and reports from the early generations encourage it, but it is not recitation, and the one who looks does not take the ruling of the reciter in matters of fiqh.

### 3. Reflection
Engaging the heart with the meanings of the verses, which is one of the greatest acts of worship:

﴿كِتَٰبٌ أَنزَلۡنَٰهُ إِلَيۡكَ مُبَٰرَكࣱ لِّيَدَّبَّرُوٓاْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ وَلِيَتَذَكَّرَ أُوْلُواْ ٱلۡأَلۡبَٰبِ﴾ (Quran 38:29)

Reflection is a goal in its own right. Someone who reads with their eyes while reflecting is in a great act of worship, but it is worship of the heart, not of the tongue.

**In summary**: someone who looks with their eyes and reflects with their heart is rewarded, by Allah's permission, for looking and reflecting, but is not counted as reciting in the legal sense, and the reward of "ten good deeds per letter" is connected to reciting with the tongue according to the majority of scholars.

## Silent Recitation in Prayer
This is the most important application, because a mistake here affects the validity of the prayer:
- **Reciting Al-Fatihah is a pillar** of the prayer according to the majority of scholars, and it is only achieved with the tongue. Someone who prays the quiet prayers while passing Al-Fatihah through their heart without moving their tongue has left out a pillar, according to the majority.
- **The quiet prayers**, such as Zuhr and 'Asr, do not mean reciting in the heart. They mean reciting with the tongue without raising the voice. The person praying moves their tongue and lips, and hears themselves, according to the majority.
- **Following an imam in the loud prayers**: whether the follower recites Al-Fatihah behind the imam is a well-known difference of opinion, but whoever does recite it does so with their tongue quietly, not in their heart.

This is a common mistake among some people who pray, especially young people, who think that quiet recitation means complete silence.

## Remembrance (Dhikr) in the Heart
The same applies to adhkar: glorification, declarations of oneness, morning and evening remembrances and the remembrances after prayer obtain their specific reward when pronounced. Imam an-Nawawi said in *al-Adhkar*, in meaning, that the prescribed remembrances in prayer and elsewhere are not counted until they are pronounced so that the person hears themselves, if their hearing is sound.

As for remembrance of the heart, being conscious of Allah's greatness and His watchfulness, it is a great act of worship in itself. And the best remembrance is that in which the heart and tongue are joined.

## Looking at the Mushaf During Menstruation or Janabah
This issue is related, because scholars who prohibit a menstruating woman or a person in janabah from reciting with the tongue generally permit **looking** at the Mushaf without pronouncing, and **reflecting** on the verses in the heart, because that is not recitation. This is a concession that those who hold the prohibiting view can use to stay connected to the Quran through looking, reflecting and listening.

## Silent Reading and Memorisation
In practice, the experience of Quran teachers shows that **reciting aloud is stronger for memorisation** than silent reading, for several reasons:
1. **More senses are involved**: the eyes read, the tongue pronounces, and the ears hear, so the memory stores the verse in several ways.
2. **It reveals mistakes**: silent reading hides errors of pronunciation and tajweed, because the mind automatically "corrects" what it sees.
3. **It sets the rhythm**: a memoriser who revises aloud knows where to breathe and pause, and so recites smoothly.

Silent revision does have some value, such as quickly recalling the order of verses, or revising in a place where you cannot raise your voice. But it does not replace revising aloud.

## The Practical Solution: Whispered Recitation
Many people who ask about silent reading do not want to abandon their tongue; they are worried about disturbing others or attracting attention. The solution is **whispered recitation**:
- **Move your tongue and lips** correctly with the letters.
- **Produce a soft sound** that you can hear but the person next to you barely can.
- **Pronounce the letters from their proper places**, even in a low voice; tajweed does not disappear when you recite quietly.

This fulfils the definition of recitation according to the majority of scholars, earns the full reward of the letters, and disturbs no one.

## Tips for Reciting on Public Transport and at Work
1. **Use a small printed Mushaf or an app without notifications**, so your recitation is not interrupted by messages.
2. **Recite in a whisper**, as described above.
3. **Choose a fixed time**, such as your commute to work, so it becomes a habit.
4. **Never read from a Mushaf or phone while driving**: revise from memory or listen, because your safety and others' comes first.
5. **Use headphones to listen** in crowded places; listening is a separate act of worship with its own reward.
6. **Prefer a short, careful portion** over a long, rushed one.

## Common Mistakes
- **Thinking quiet recitation in prayer means silence**: the correct practice is to move the tongue.
- **Counting completions "with the eyes" as completions of recitation**: it is better to recite in a whisper to obtain the full reward.
- **Abandoning recitation aloud in revision**, which weakens memorisation and hides errors.
- **The opposite extreme**: raising your voice in a public place, disturbing people and attracting attention. The goal is balance.

## Why Is the Reward Linked to the Tongue?
Someone may ask: Isn't the purpose of the Quran understanding and action? What is the difference between reading with my eyes and understanding, and reading with my tongue? The answer is that the Shari'ah established several acts of worship connected to the Quran, each with its own purpose:
- **Recitation with the tongue** is a verbal act of worship that honours Allah's words by pronouncing them as they were revealed, and preserves the text on people's tongues generation after generation. This is why one of the distinctions of this Ummah is that its Book is preserved in hearts before it is preserved on paper.
- **Reflection with the heart** is an act of the heart whose purpose is understanding and action.
- **Listening** is an act of the ears that Allah commanded: "When the Quran is recited, listen to it and be silent."

Each has its reward, and a successful Muslim combines them: recites with the tongue, reflects with the heart, and listens to others. The Prophet ﷺ loved to hear the Quran from others, as in al-Bukhari and Muslim when he asked Ibn Mas'ud to recite to him.

## Fast Silent Reading to Complete the Quran
Some people use fast silent reading to complete the Quran quickly, especially in Ramadan, thinking that the number of completions is the goal. It is worth reconsidering this, because:
1. **It is not counted as recitation** according to the majority of scholars.
2. **It usually sacrifices reflection**, since fast-moving eyes skip over words.
3. **A slow completion with the tongue has a greater effect** than several fast completions with the eyes.

In Sahih al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ told 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr: "Recite the Quran in a month." When he asked for more, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Then recite it in seven, and do not do more than that." The aim is regular, careful recitation, not speed for its own sake.

## Special Cases
**Someone unable to speak**: Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity. Someone who cannot move their tongue because of illness or a condition from birth reads with their heart and looks with their eyes, and is rewarded for their intention and effort as far as they are able.

**Someone who is ill and tires from speaking**: recite as much as you can, even in a soft whisper, or listen. In Sahih al-Bukhari: "When a servant falls ill or travels, there is written for him the like of what he used to do when resident and healthy."

**A mother whose baby sleeps beside her**: recite in a whisper. A soft recitation rarely wakes a baby, and may even calm them.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Do I get the reward of recitation if I read the Quran silently in my head?
According to the majority of scholars, the letter-by-letter reward is connected to reciting with the tongue; looking and reflecting are rewarded as separate acts of worship.

### Do I have to hear myself when reciting?
The Shafi'is and Hanbalis say yes; some Hanafis and the Malikis say correct movement of the tongue is enough.

### Can I recite Al-Fatihah in my heart in Zuhr and 'Asr?
No. Quiet prayers mean reciting with the tongue without raising the voice, not reciting in the heart.

### Does dhikr in the heart count?
Remembrance of the heart is a great act of worship, but the specific rewards of adhkar are connected to pronouncing them.

### How can I recite on the train without disturbing anyone?
Recite in a whisper: move your tongue and lips with a soft sound that only you can hear.

### Is silent reading good for memorisation?
Reciting aloud is stronger for memorisation; silent revision can help in limited situations.

## Conclusion
Reading the Quran with your eyes and reflecting with your heart are good acts of worship, but recitation in the legal sense requires the tongue, and the reward of ten good deeds per letter is connected to it according to the majority of scholars. In quiet prayers, move your tongue; in public places, recite in a whisper; in revision, recite aloud. Combine the tongue, the heart and the ears, and you will have the full share of every act of worship connected to the Book of Allah.
