Open a Madinah Mushaf and look at words such as «ٱهۡدِنَا», «ٱلۡحَمۡدُ» or «ٱدۡخُلُواْ». The first alif has a small mark above it that looks like the head of the letter saad, and no vowel. This is **hamzat al-wasl**, the connecting hamza. It is pronounced when you start with it and **dropped** when you join the word to what comes before.

The question that confuses many learners is simple: when I start with it, **which vowel do I use?** Is it "a", "u" or "i"? This guide explains the complete rule step by step, with examples and exceptions, so you never start a word incorrectly again.

## Hamzat al-Wasl vs Hamzat al-Qat'

### Hamzat al-qat' (the cutting hamza)
A hamza that is **always pronounced**, whether you start with it or join it to the previous word. In the Mushaf it is written as a small hamza (ء) on or under the alif, with its vowel. Examples: «أَنۡعَمۡتَ», «إِيَّاكَ», «أَحَدٌ». When you recite «صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنۡعَمۡتَ», you pronounce the hamza of «أَنۡعَمۡتَ» clearly.

### Hamzat al-wasl (the connecting hamza)
An **extra** hamza at the start of a word, added only so that we can pronounce the sukoon letter after it, because Arabic does not begin a word with a sukoon. When you join the word to the previous one, it **disappears from pronunciation**. When you start with it, it **is pronounced** with a vowel.

Example from Al-Fatihah: if you join «نَسۡتَعِينُ» to «ٱهۡدِنَا», you say "nasta'eenu-hdinaa", and the hamza disappears. If you start a new breath with «ٱهۡدِنَا», you say "ihdinaa", with a kasra.

## Where Does Hamzat al-Wasl Appear?

It appears in three kinds of words:
1. **The definite article "al-"** (and words built on it).
2. **Certain nouns**: seven specific nouns, plus the verbal nouns (masdars) of certain verb forms.
3. **Certain verbs**: the past tense of five- and six-letter verbs, their commands, and the command of three-letter verbs.

Each kind has its own rule for the starting vowel.

## Rule 1: "Al-" Always Starts With Fatha

When you start with a word containing the definite article, you always open hamzat al-wasl with a **fatha**, whether the laam is "moon" (pronounced) or "sun" (assimilated):
- «ٱلۡحَمۡدُ» → "**a**l-hamdu".
- «ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ» → "**a**r-rahmaan" (the laam merges into the raa).
- «ٱللَّهُ» → "**A**llaahu", as at the start of Ayat al-Kursi.
- «ٱلَّذِينَ» → "**a**lladheena".

Be careful: «أَلَمۡ تَرَ» is **not** this case. Its hamza is a question hamza (hamzat al-qat') and is always pronounced.

## Rule 2: Nouns Start With Kasra

When hamzat al-wasl is in a noun (other than "al-"), you start with a **kasra**. These nouns are of two kinds.

### The seven specific nouns
These seven nouns occur in the Quran with hamzat al-wasl:
- «ٱبۡن» (son): starting at «ٱبۡنُ مَرۡيَمَ» → "**i**bnu maryam".
- «ٱبۡنَت» (daughter): «ٱبۡنَتَ عِمۡرَٰنَ» → "**i**bnata 'imraan".
- «ٱمۡرُؤٌ» (a man): «ٱمۡرُؤٌاْ هَلَكَ» → "**i**mru'un halak".
- «ٱمۡرَأَت» (woman, wife): «ٱمۡرَأَتُ ٱلۡعَزِيزِ» → "**i**mra'atul-'azeez".
- «ٱسۡم» (name): «ٱسۡمَ رَبِّكَ» → "**i**sma rabbik".
- «ٱثۡنَيۡنِ» (two): → "**i**thnayn".
- «ٱثۡنَتَيۡنِ» (two, feminine): → "**i**thnatayn".

### Verbal nouns (masdars) of five- and six-letter verbs
- «ٱسۡتِكۡبَارٗا» → "**i**stikbaaran".
- «ٱفۡتِرَآءً» → "**i**ftiraa'an".
- «ٱبۡتِغَآءَ» → "**i**btighaa'a".

## Rule 3: Verbs Depend on the Third Letter

This is the most important rule, and the one people get wrong most often. When hamzat al-wasl is in a **verb**, count the hamza as the first letter and look at the **third letter**:

### If the third letter has an original damma → start with damma
- «ٱدۡخُلُواْ» → third letter خ has damma → "**u**dkhuloo".
- «ٱنظُرۡ» → third letter ظ has damma → "**u**ndhur".
- «ٱقۡتُلُواْ يُوسُفَ» → third letter ت has damma → "**u**qtuloo".
- «ٱدۡعُ إِلَىٰ سَبِيلِ رَبِّكَ» → third letter ع has damma → "**u**d'u".

### If the third letter has fatha → start with kasra
- «ٱذۡهَبۡ إِلَىٰ فِرۡعَوۡنَ» → third letter ه has fatha → "**i**dhhab".
- «ٱقۡرَأۡ بِٱسۡمِ رَبِّكَ» → third letter ر has fatha → "**i**qra'".
- «ٱعۡلَمُوٓاْ» → third letter ل has fatha → "**i**'lamoo".

### If the third letter has kasra → start with kasra
- «ٱضۡرِب بِّعَصَاكَ» → third letter ر has kasra → "**i**drib".
- «ٱرۡجِعُوٓاْ» → third letter ج has kasra → "**i**rji'oo".
- «ٱهۡدِنَا» → third letter د has kasra → "**i**hdinaa".

### The rule in one line
**If the third letter of the verb has an original damma, start with damma; otherwise, start with kasra.**

Imam Ibn al-Jazari summarised all of this in his famous poem on Tajweed, al-Muqaddimah al-Jazariyyah: start the connecting hamza of a verb with damma if its third letter has damma, and with kasra when it has kasra or fatha; and in nouns other than "al-", use kasra, as in ibn, ibnah, imru', ithnayn, imra'ah, ism and ithnatayn.

## An Example From the First Revelation

The very first words revealed contain hamzat al-wasl:

﴿ٱقۡرَأۡ بِٱسۡمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ﴾ (Quran 96:1)

Meaning: "Read in the name of your Lord who created." If you start the verse, you say "**i**qra'" with kasra, because the third letter (raa) has fatha. The word «بِٱسۡمِ» also contains hamzat al-wasl, in the noun "ism", but because it comes after the letter baa in the middle of speech, it is not pronounced: "bismi rabbika".

## The Important Exceptions in Verbs

Some verbs have a damma on their third letter that is **not original**. In these verbs you start with **kasra**, not damma. The five best-known examples in the Quran are:

| Verb | Where it appears (examples) | Correct start | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| ٱمۡشُواْ (walk) | Surah Sad, Surah Al-Mulk | **i**mshoo | Originally "imshiyoo": the shin originally had kasra |
| ٱقۡضُوٓاْ (carry out) | Surah Yunus | **i**qdoo | Originally "iqdiyoo" |
| ٱبۡنُواْ (build) | Surah Al-Kahf, Surah As-Saffat | **i**bnoo | Originally "ibniyoo" |
| ٱئۡتُواْ (bring / come) | Many places, e.g. «ٱئۡتُونِي» | **i**ytoo | Originally "i'tiyoo"; the second hamza becomes a long "ee" when starting |
| ٱمۡضُواْ (proceed) | Surah Al-Hijr | **i**mdoo | Originally "imdiyoo" |

In all these verbs a ya was dropped and the damma of the following waw moved back onto the third letter. Since the original vowel of that letter was a kasra, we start with kasra.

### Starting with «ٱئۡتُونِي» and «ٱؤۡتُمِنَ»
When hamzat al-wasl is followed by a hamza with sukoon, that second hamza turns into a long vowel matching the starting vowel:
- «ٱئۡتُونِي» → start: "**ee**toonee" (kasra + long ee).
- «ٱلَّذِي ٱؤۡتُمِنَ أَمَٰنَتَهُۥ», starting at «ٱؤۡتُمِنَ» → "**oo**tumina" (damma, because the third letter ت has damma, + long oo).

## The Special Case of «ٱلِٱسۡمُ» in Surah Al-Hujurat

In Surah Al-Hujurat (49:11) you find the phrase «بِئۡسَ ٱلِٱسۡمُ ٱلۡفُسُوقُ», "wretched is the name of disobedience". Here two connecting hamzas meet: the hamza of "al-" and the hamza of "ism". When joined, you read "bi'sa-**lismu**l-fusooq", with a kasra on the laam.

If you **start** at «ٱلِٱسۡمُ» (for example, after an emergency stop or in a test), Hafs has two ways:
1. "**a-lismu**": start with fatha on the hamza of "al-" and kasra on the laam. This is the preferred way.
2. "**lismu**": start directly with the laam with kasra.

This word is a favourite question in recitation tests and ijazah readings, so practise it with your teacher.

## Hamzat al-Wasl After a Question Hamza

In three words in the Quran, a question hamza comes before "al-": «ءَآلذَّكَرَيۡنِ» (two places in Al-An'am), «ءَآلۡـَٰٔنَ» (two places in Yunus) and «ءَآللَّهُ» (in Yunus and An-Naml). Here the connecting hamza is not simply dropped, so that the question is not confused with a statement. Hafs has two ways:
1. **Ibdaal**: change it into a long alif and stretch it **six counts** (madd laazim). This is the preferred and most common way.
2. **Tas-heel**: pronounce it softly between a hamza and an alif, without stretching.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Always starting command verbs with damma**: "udhhab", "u'lamoo". Correct: "idhhab", "i'lamoo".
2. **Starting the five exceptions with damma**: "umshoo", "ubnoo". Correct: "imshoo", "ibnoo".
3. **Starting "al-" with kasra**: "il-hamdu". Correct: "al-hamdu".
4. **Pronouncing hamzat al-wasl in the middle of joined speech**: "nasta'eenu ihdinaa". Correct: "nasta'eenu-hdinaa".
5. **Confusing a question hamza with hamzat al-wasl**: «أَلَمۡ» always keeps its hamza.
6. **Forgetting to change the second hamza**: "i'toonee" with two hamzas. Correct: "eetoonee".

## When Will You Actually Need This?

It might seem theoretical, but you will meet hamzat al-wasl on almost every page:
- **Starting your recitation** at a verse that begins with it, such as Ayat al-Kursi («ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ») or the first verse of Surah Al-'Alaq.
- **After stopping for breath**, when the next word begins with a connecting hamza.
- **In prayer**, when you begin the second rak'ah from a passage that starts with one.
- **When reciting to a teacher**, who may ask you to continue from the middle of a verse.
- **In tests and ijazah readings**, where the exceptions and «ٱلِٱسۡمُ» are classic questions.

## How to Practise

- **Mark them on your page**: each day, put your finger on every connecting hamza on the page you read and ask: if I started here, which vowel would I use?
- **Stop-and-start drills**: ask your teacher to stop you at random words and have you start again from the next word.
- **Memorise the five exceptions**: imshoo, iqdoo, ibnoo, eetoo, imdoo. They come up again and again.
- **Watch the sign**: the small saad-like mark on the alif is your cue that this is a connecting hamza.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I recognise hamzat al-wasl in the Mushaf?
In the Madinah Mushaf it is an alif with a small saad-like mark on top (ٱ) and no vowel or hamza sign. Hamzat al-qat' has a visible hamza (ء) with its vowel.

### Can I start a word with a sukoon?
No. Arabic does not begin with a sukoon, which is exactly why hamzat al-wasl exists. It must be given a vowel according to the rules above.

### Is starting with the wrong vowel a serious mistake?
It is a recitation error that should be corrected through learning and practice with a teacher, since it can change the form of the word.

### Do these rules differ between the qira'at?
The core rules for the starting vowel are broadly agreed upon. This guide follows the narration of Hafs from 'Asim, which is the most widely used today.

### How do I start with «ٱللَّذَانِ» or «ٱلَّٰٓـِٔي»?
They begin with "al-", so you start with fatha: "alladhaani", "allaa'ee".
