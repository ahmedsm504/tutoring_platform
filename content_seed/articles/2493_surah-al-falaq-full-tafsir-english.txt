Surah Al-Falaq is the first of the *mu'awwidhatayn*, the two surahs of seeking refuge. The Prophet ﷺ said of them, in Sahih Muslim: "Have you not seen verses revealed tonight, the like of which has never been seen? *Qul a'udhu bi-rabbil-falaq* and *Qul a'udhu bi-rabbin-nas*."

Al-Falaq teaches the believer to seek protection from external harms: the evil in created things, the dangers of the night, the harm of magic, and the harm of envy. It turns the believer's fear into a prayer, and the prayer into a fortress. This guide explains the surah in English, verse by verse, and shows how the Prophet ﷺ used it every day.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Falaq
- **Number of verses**: 5.
- **Type**: Meccan in the standard mushaf; scholars have discussed the point.
- **Position**: Surah 113, the second-to-last surah of the Quran.
- **Name**: from *al-falaq*, daybreak, in its first verse.
- **Paired with**: Surah An-Nas; together they are *al-mu'awwidhatayn*.

## The Full Text of Surah Al-Falaq
﴿قُلۡ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلۡفَلَقِ﴾ ﴿مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ﴾ ﴿وَمِن شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ﴾ ﴿وَمِن شَرِّ ٱلنَّفَّـٰثَٰتِ فِي ٱلۡعُقَدِ﴾ ﴿وَمِن شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ﴾ (Quran 113:1-5)

**Meaning:** Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of daybreak, from the evil of what He has created, and from the evil of darkness when it settles, and from the evil of those who blow on knots, and from the evil of an envier when he envies.

## The Virtues of the Mu'awwidhatayn
1. **Unmatched verses.** In Sahih Muslim, 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Have you not seen verses revealed tonight, the like of which has never been seen? *Qul a'udhu bi-rabbil-falaq* and *Qul a'udhu bi-rabbin-nas*."
2. **No better refuge.** Abu Dawud and an-Nasa'i recorded that the Prophet ﷺ told 'Uqbah: "Seek refuge with them, for no one seeks refuge with anything like them." Al-Albani graded it authentic.
3. **The Prophet's nightly protection.** In Sahih al-Bukhari, 'A'ishah reported that every night he blew into his palms, recited Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, and wiped over his body three times.
4. **His remedy in illness.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah reported that when he fell ill, he would recite the mu'awwidhat over himself and blow; when his pain grew severe, she recited them and wiped with his own hand, hoping for its blessing.
5. **They replaced other formulas.** At-Tirmidhi recorded, grading it *hasan gharib*, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri that the Prophet ﷺ used to seek refuge from the jinn and from the evil eye of people until the mu'awwidhatayn were revealed; then he took them and left everything else.

## Verse 1: The Lord of Daybreak
﴿قُلۡ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلۡفَلَقِ﴾ (Quran 113:1)

**"Say"**: the refuge is spoken aloud with the tongue, not only felt in the heart.

**"I seek refuge"** (*a'udhu*): I take shelter, I hold fast, I run for protection.

**"The Lord of al-falaq"**: most commentators explain *al-falaq* as daybreak, because Allah splits the darkness of the night to bring out the morning, as He says in Surah Al-An'am: "Splitter of the dawn."

Why seek refuge in the Lord of daybreak specifically? Because the One who splits the darkest night with light every single morning can split the darkness of any evil that surrounds you. There is hope in the name itself: after every night, a dawn.

## Verse 2: The Evil of What He Created
﴿مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ﴾ (Quran 113:2)

A general request for protection from every created thing that carries harm: people, jinn, animals, insects, illnesses and disasters. Notice that evil is attributed to the *created things*, not to the Creator. Allah created everything with wisdom; harm occurs in some of the actions and effects of creatures.

## Verse 3: Darkness When It Settles
﴿وَمِن شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ﴾ (Quran 113:3)

*Ghasiq* is the night when its darkness deepens; *idha waqab* means when it enters and settles. The night is singled out because harms spread in it: predators and insects come out, criminals move about, and the devils spread.

At-Tirmidhi recorded from 'A'ishah, grading it *hasan sahih*, that the Prophet ﷺ looked at the moon and said: "O 'A'ishah, seek refuge with Allah from the evil of this, for this is the *ghasiq* when it *waqab*." The two explanations meet: darkness when light disappears.

From the same theme, in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Jabir reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "When night falls, keep your children in, for the devils spread out at that time."

## Verse 4: Those Who Blow on Knots
﴿وَمِن شَرِّ ٱلنَّفَّـٰثَٰتِ فِي ٱلۡعُقَدِ﴾ (Quran 113:4)

*An-naffathat fil-'uqad* are the souls that practise magic by blowing, with a little breath and spittle, onto knots they tie. The surah confirms that magic has a real effect by Allah's permission, and it teaches the believer how to be protected from it.

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah reported that the Prophet ﷺ was affected by magic done by a man named Labid ibn al-A'sam, to the point that he imagined he had done something he had not done. Then Allah cured him and showed him where the magic was, and it was removed. This affected only his body, like an illness; it never touched his conveying of revelation.

## Verse 5: An Envier When He Envies
﴿وَمِن شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ﴾ (Quran 113:5)

*Hasad* is wishing that a blessing be removed from someone else. The condition *when he envies* matters: the harm comes when the envy is acted upon, through words, deeds or the evil eye. Envy that a person struggles against in his heart harms mainly himself.

The surah closes with envy because it is the root of many evils. The first sin in the heavens was Iblis envying Adam, and the first sin on earth was one son of Adam envying his brother.

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The evil eye is real."

## Word Meanings
- **A'udhu**: I seek refuge.
- **Al-Falaq**: daybreak.
- **Ma khalaq**: what He created.
- **Ghasiq**: deep darkness, or the moon when it is eclipsed or sets.
- **Waqab**: entered and settled.
- **An-Naffathat**: those who blow, the practitioners of magic.
- **Al-'Uqad**: knots.
- **Hasid**: an envier.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **General, then specific**: refuge from all created evil, then three specific hidden evils.
2. **All three specific evils are hidden**: night hides, magic is secret, envy lives in the heart. So refuge is sought in the One from whom nothing is hidden.
3. **"From the evil of" repeated four times**, so each evil receives its own refuge.
4. **One name for many evils**: "Lord of daybreak" alone covers four evils, while An-Nas uses three names against one evil, as we will see.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Qalqalah** when stopping on *al-falaq* and *khalaq* (qaf), *waqab* (ba), and *al-'uqad* and *hasad* (dal).
- **Ikhfa** in *min sharri*: the nun is hidden before *shin*.
- **Izhar** in *ghasiqin idha* and *hasidin idha*: the tanween is clear before hamza.
- **Lam shamsiyyah** in *an-naffathat*; **lam qamariyyah** in *al-falaq* and *al-'uqad*.

## A Daily Protection Routine from the Sunnah
1. **Morning and evening**: Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas three times, from the hadith of 'Abdullah ibn Khubayb recorded by Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi, who graded it *hasan sahih gharib*: "They will suffice you against everything."
2. **After every obligatory prayer**: the mu'awwidhat once, from the hadith of 'Uqbah.
3. **Before sleep**: blow into the palms, recite the three surahs, and wipe the body, three times.
4. **When ill**: recite and blow over yourself, and over family members.
5. **When stopping at a place**: say "I seek refuge in the perfect words of Allah from the evil of what He created," which, as reported in Sahih Muslim from Khawlah bint Hakim, protects you until you leave that place.

## Envy: Protecting Yourself and Not Becoming an Envier
**To protect yourself:** keep the adhkar, avoid needless display of your blessings, and rely on Allah rather than living in fear of envious people.

**To avoid envying others:** when you see something that impresses you, say *ma sha' Allah, barak Allahu lak*. The Prophet ﷺ told a man whose glance had harmed his brother: "Why did you not invoke blessing?" (recorded by Malik, Ahmad and Ibn Majah with an authentic chain). And in Sahih Muslim: "Do not envy one another... and be, O servants of Allah, brothers."

## Magic in Islam
- It is real, as the surah and the authentic hadith show.
- It is among the major sins. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ listed it among the seven destroyers.
- It harms only by Allah's permission (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:102).
- The cure is the Quran and authentic supplications, never visiting magicians or using amulets.

## Practical Lessons
1. Keep the mu'awwidhatayn morning, evening, after prayers and before sleep.
2. Tie your heart to Allah, not to amulets or fortune-tellers.
3. Do not live in obsessive fear of magic or envy; Allah is greater.
4. Cleanse your own heart of envy.
5. Recite over your children and family.
6. Be optimistic: after every night comes daybreak.

## Teaching Children
- Make the bedtime routine of blowing and wiping a nightly habit.
- Watch a sunrise together and explain *al-falaq*.
- Teach them to say *ma sha' Allah, barak Allah* when they like something someone else has.

## Seeking Refuge Only in Allah
Seeking refuge (*isti'adhah*) is an act of worship, and it must be directed to Allah alone in matters that only He controls. The Quran criticises those who sought refuge in the jinn, in Surah Al-Jinn:

﴿وَأَنَّهُۥ كَانَ رِجَالࣱ مِّنَ ٱلۡإِنسِ يَعُوذُونَ بِرِجَالٖ مِّنَ ٱلۡجِنِّ فَزَادُوهُمۡ رَهَقٗا﴾ (Quran 72:6)

**Meaning:** There were men from mankind who sought refuge in men from the jinn, and they only increased them in burden.

The Sunnah teaches many forms of refuge in Allah: before reciting the Quran, on entering the toilet ("O Allah, I seek refuge in You from male and female devils", in al-Bukhari and Muslim), when angry, and before ending the prayer, when the Prophet ﷺ sought refuge from the punishment of Hell, the punishment of the grave, the trials of life and death, and the trial of the False Messiah, as reported in Sahih Muslim. Each of these is a small application of Surah Al-Falaq in daily life.

## Amulets Are Not a Substitute
Some people abandon the adhkar and rely instead on amulets, beads and charms hung on children, cars or doors. Imam Ahmad recorded with a strong chain from 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever hangs an amulet has committed shirk." True protection is what the Prophet ﷺ taught: recitation, remembrance and reliance on Allah.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does "al-falaq" mean?
Daybreak, the splitting of the night by the dawn, according to most commentators.

### What does "ghasiqin idha waqab" mean?
Darkness when it deepens and settles; in a hadith graded *hasan sahih* by at-Tirmidhi, the Prophet ﷺ applied it to the moon.

### Who are "those who blow on knots"?
Practitioners of magic who blow on knots when doing their magic.

### Is the evil eye real?
Yes. The Prophet ﷺ said: "The evil eye is real," in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### When should I recite Surah Al-Falaq?
Morning and evening three times, after every prayer, before sleep, and when ill.

### Can I do ruqyah on myself with Al-Falaq?
Yes; the Prophet ﷺ recited it over himself and blew, as in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Falaq?
5 verses.

### Is it allowed to go to a magician to undo magic?
No. Magic is a major sin, and the legitimate cure is ruqyah with the Quran and authentic supplications.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Falaq tells you that you are never alone in facing harm. The Lord who splits the night open every morning is the One you run to: from the evil of every creature, from the dangers of darkness, from magic and from envy. Keep these five verses on your tongue morning and evening, cleanse your heart of envy, and remember that after every night there is a dawn.
