Surah Al-Balad tells the truth about human life in five words: "We have certainly created man in toil." Nobody is exempt. Rich or poor, young or old, every person struggles. The surah then shows two ways to deal with that struggle. One man boasts, "I have spent wealth in heaps," and thinks nobody sees him. The other takes "the steep path": he frees a slave, feeds an orphan relative on a day of hunger, feeds a poor man lying in the dust, and joins the believers who urge one another to patience and mercy. The surah calls that path steep because it is hard on the self, and it calls those who climb it "the companions of the right".

This guide explains Surah Al-Balad verse by verse in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Balad
- **Number of verses**: 20.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 90, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: Al-Balad means "the city", referring to Makkah.
- **Main themes**: the toil of human life, the gifts Allah gives every person, and the steep path of compassion and faith.

## Verses 1–4: The Oath by the City
﴿لَآ أُقۡسِمُ بِهَٰذَا ٱلۡبَلَدِ﴾ ﴿وَأَنتَ حِلُّۢ بِهَٰذَا ٱلۡبَلَدِ﴾ ﴿وَوَالِدٖ وَمَا وَلَدَ﴾ ﴿لَقَدۡ خَلَقۡنَا ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ فِي كَبَدٍ﴾ (Quran 90:1-4)

**"I swear by this city"**: Makkah, the sacred city. **"And you are a resident (or: made free) in this city"**: commentators gave two meanings to *hillun*. One: you, O Prophet, live in this city, which adds to its honour. Two: this city, which is sacred, will be made lawful for you for a time, which happened on the day of the Conquest. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said on the day of the Conquest: "Allah made this city sacred... and it was made lawful for me only for an hour of a day, and its sanctity has returned today as it was yesterday."

**"And by the father and what he fathered"**: commentators explained it as Adam and his descendants, or every father and his children.

**The answer to the oath**: **"We have certainly created man in toil"** (*kabad*): hardship and struggle. From the pains of birth to the pains of death, life is effort. This is not a complaint; it is a realistic description that frees you from expecting a life without difficulty. The real question is what you do with your toil.

## Verses 5–7: The Arrogant Man
﴿أَيَحۡسَبُ أَن لَّن يَقۡدِرَ عَلَيۡهِ أَحَدࣱ﴾ ﴿يَقُولُ أَهۡلَكۡتُ مَالٗا لُّبَدًا﴾ ﴿أَيَحۡسَبُ أَن لَّمۡ يَرَهُۥٓ أَحَدٌ﴾ (Quran 90:5-7)

**"Does he think that no one has power over him?"** Strength and wealth can make a person feel untouchable. **"He says: I have spent wealth in heaps"** (*malan lubada*), boasting about how much he wasted on showing off and on opposing the truth. **"Does he think that no one sees him?"** Allah saw where every coin came from and where it went.

## Verses 8–10: The Gifts and the Two Highways
﴿أَلَمۡ نَجۡعَل لَّهُۥ عَيۡنَيۡنِ﴾ ﴿وَلِسَانٗا وَشَفَتَيۡنِ﴾ ﴿وَهَدَيۡنَٰهُ ٱلنَّجۡدَيۡنِ﴾ (Quran 90:8-10)

**"Did We not make for him two eyes, and a tongue and two lips?"** The eyes to see the signs; the tongue and lips to speak, to eat, to express himself. Each is a blessing that should be used in gratitude. Think of what life would be without any one of them.

**"And guided him to the two highways"** (*an-najdayn*): the way of good and the way of evil. Allah showed man both and gave him the ability to choose. This links closely with Surah Ash-Shams: "and inspired it with its wickedness and its righteousness".

## Verses 11–16: The Steep Path
﴿فَلَا ٱقۡتَحَمَ ٱلۡعَقَبَةَ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلۡعَقَبَةُ﴾ ﴿فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ﴾ ﴿أَوۡ إِطۡعَٰمࣱ فِي يَوۡمٖ ذِي مَسۡغَبَةٖ﴾ ﴿يَتِيمٗا ذَا مَقۡرَبَةٍ﴾ ﴿أَوۡ مِسۡكِينٗا ذَا مَتۡرَبَةٖ﴾ (Quran 90:11-16)

**"But he has not attempted the steep path"** (*al-'aqabah*): the mountain road that is hard to climb. **"And what will make you know what the steep path is?"** The surah answers with concrete acts:

1. **"Freeing a slave"** (*fakku raqabah*): in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever frees a Muslim slave, Allah will free from the Fire, for every limb of the slave, a limb of his." Today, the meaning extends to helping people out of bondage of every kind: paying off a debtor's debt, ransoming a prisoner, freeing someone from crushing hardship.
2. **"Or feeding on a day of severe hunger"**: giving food is always good, but giving it when food is scarce and you need it yourself is the steep part.
3. **"An orphan of near relationship"**: an orphan who is a relative, so the act combines charity and keeping family ties. At-Tirmidhi reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Charity to a poor person is charity, and to a relative it is two things: charity and keeping ties of kinship," and at-Tirmidhi graded it *hasan*. In Sahih al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ said: "I and the one who looks after an orphan will be in Paradise like this," and he held up his index and middle fingers.
4. **"Or a poor person in the dust"** (*dha matrabah*): so poor he is clinging to the ground, with nothing between him and the dust.

**Why "steep"?** Because each of these acts goes against the self's love of wealth. The arrogant man spent "wealth in heaps" on showing off; the steep path spends it on those whom nobody sees.

## Verses 17–20: Those Who Believe and Urge Mercy
﴿ثُمَّ كَانَ مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلصَّبۡرِ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلۡمَرۡحَمَةِ﴾ ﴿أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ أَصۡحَٰبُ ٱلۡمَيۡمَنَةِ﴾ ﴿وَٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا هُمۡ أَصۡحَٰبُ ٱلۡمَشۡـَٔمَةِ﴾ ﴿عَلَيۡهِمۡ نَارࣱ مُّؤۡصَدَةُۢ﴾ (Quran 90:17-20)

**"Then he is among those who believe"**: the good deeds are built on faith. **"And urge one another to patience, and urge one another to mercy."** Two qualities that hold a community together: *sabr*, patience in obedience and hardship, and *marhamah*, compassion for people. At-Tirmidhi reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The merciful are shown mercy by the Most Merciful. Be merciful to those on earth, and the One in the heaven will be merciful to you." At-Tirmidhi graded it *hasan sahih*.

**"Those are the companions of the right."** And **"those who disbelieve in Our signs, they are the companions of the left. Over them is a Fire closed in."**

## Word Meanings
- **Al-Balad**: the city, Makkah.
- **Hill**: resident, or one for whom it is made lawful.
- **Kabad**: toil, hardship, struggle.
- **Lubad**: in heaps, a great amount.
- **An-Najdayn**: the two highways, of good and evil.
- **Iqtahama**: attempted, broke through.
- **Al-'Aqabah**: the steep mountain path.
- **Fakk raqabah**: freeing a neck, freeing a slave.
- **Masghabah**: severe hunger.
- **Maqrabah**: near relationship.
- **Matrabah**: lying in the dust from poverty.
- **Al-Marhamah**: mercy, compassion.
- **Mu'sadah**: closed in, shut over them.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"Created man in toil"**: a whole philosophy of life in one sentence.
2. **"I have spent wealth in heaps"**: the boast exposes the emptiness of spending for show.
3. **"Two eyes, and a tongue and two lips"**: concrete gifts we take for granted.
4. **"The steep path"**: a metaphor that makes goodness a climb worth taking.
5. **"A poor person in the dust"**: an image of extreme poverty that moves the heart.
6. **"Patience and mercy"**: the two pillars of a healthy community.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd munfasil**: in *la uqsimu* (verse 1), the long vowel is followed by a hamzah in the next word.
- **Idgham without ghunnah**: in *malan lubada* (verse 6), the tanween merges into the lam.
- **Ikhfa'**: in *yatiman dha* (verse 15), the tanween is hidden before the dhal.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *an-najdayn* (verse 10), the jim sakinah bounces lightly, and in *maqrabah* (verse 15), the qaf sakinah.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Al-Fajr before it criticised those who "do not honour the orphan and do not urge feeding the poor", and Al-Balad shows the opposite path: feeding the orphan and the poor. Surah Ash-Shams after it develops the idea of the two highways: "and inspired it with its wickedness and its righteousness. He has succeeded who purifies it."

## Climbing the Steep Path Today
1. **Sponsor an orphan**, especially one from your own family.
2. **Help someone pay off a debt** that is crushing him.
3. **Feed people in times of hardship**: in famines, crises and wars.
4. **Look for the hidden poor**: the ones "in the dust" who do not ask.
5. **Give from what you need**, not only from what you do not need.
6. **Spread patience and mercy** in your family and community.

## Accepting Life's Toil
"We have certainly created man in toil" is a verse of realism and comfort. If you expect a life without difficulty, every hardship feels like an injustice. If you know that toil is part of being human, you stop asking "why me?" and start asking "what now?" The believer turns toil into worship: patience in hardship, gratitude in ease, and effort in the way of Allah.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Respect the sanctity of Makkah** and long to visit it.
2. **Accept that life involves struggle** and do not despair.
3. **Do not boast about what you spend**: Allah sees.
4. **Thank Allah for your eyes, tongue and lips** by using them well.
5. **Choose the highway of good** every day.
6. **Climb the steep path**: free, feed and support.
7. **Urge others to patience and mercy.**

## How to Memorise Surah Al-Balad
- **Day 1**: verses 1–10.
- **Day 2**: verses 11–20, then revise the whole surah.

## Teaching Children
- **Point to their eyes, tongue and lips** while reading verses 8–9, and talk about using them well.
- **Explain "the steep path"** as climbing a mountain: hard, but worth it.
- **Do a feeding project together**: prepare food for a poor family.

## Mercy as a Social Duty
The surah does not just say "be merciful"; it says "urge one another to mercy". Mercy is presented as something a community promotes together, not only a private feeling. That means speaking up for the weak, encouraging others to give, teaching children to be gentle with animals and people, and creating a culture where compassion is normal. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever does not show mercy will not be shown mercy."

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What city is meant in Surah Al-Balad?
Makkah, the sacred city.

### What does "We have created man in toil" mean?
That human life is full of hardship and struggle from birth to death.

### What are "the two highways"?
The way of good and the way of evil, which Allah showed to every person.

### What is "the steep path" (al-'aqabah)?
A metaphor for difficult good deeds: freeing slaves, feeding the hungry in times of famine, caring for orphan relatives and the extremely poor, together with faith and urging patience and mercy.

### What is the reward for freeing a slave?
Allah frees a limb of the freer from the Fire for every limb of the slave, as in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### What does "a poor person in the dust" mean?
Someone so poor that he is clinging to the ground with nothing to protect him.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Balad?
20 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

### What does "you are a resident in this city" mean?
Either that the Prophet ﷺ lived in Makkah, adding to its honour, or that it would be made lawful for him for a time, which happened on the day of the Conquest.

### Why is it called "the steep path"?
Because these good deeds go against the self's love of wealth and comfort, so they are hard like climbing a mountain, but they lead to safety.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Balad does not promise you an easy life; it tells you plainly that you were created in toil. What it offers is a way to make that toil meaningful: use the eyes, tongue and lips Allah gave you, choose the highway of good, and climb the steep path of freeing, feeding and caring, among those who urge one another to patience and mercy.
