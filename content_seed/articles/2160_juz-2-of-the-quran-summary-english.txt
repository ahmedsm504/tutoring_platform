Juz 2 begins with ﴿سَيَقُولُ ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَا وَلَّىٰهُمۡ عَن قِبۡلَتِهِمُ ٱلَّتِي كَانُواْ عَلَيۡهَاۚ﴾, "The foolish among the people will say: what has turned them away from the qibla they used to face?" For this reason memorizers call it **Juz Sayaqul**. It covers **Al-Baqarah 2:142 to 2:252** and ends with ﴿تِلۡكَ ءَايَٰتُ ٱللَّهِ نَتۡلُوهَا عَلَيۡكَ بِٱلۡحَقِّۚ وَإِنَّكَ لَمِنَ ٱلۡمُرۡسَلِينَ﴾, "These are the verses of Allah which We recite to you in truth, and you are indeed one of the messengers." In the Madinah Mushaf it fills **pages 22 to 41**.

If Juz 1 presented the experience of an earlier nation that carried scripture, Juz 2 announces the **birth of a new nation** and lays its foundations: its own qibla, a clear identity, acts of worship that unite it, laws that protect its families and society, and a story that teaches how a small patient group can prevail.

## The big idea: building the middle nation

Juz 2 can be summarized as **building the middle nation**. The first thing it declares after the change of qibla is: ﴿وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلۡنَٰكُمۡ أُمَّةٗ وَسَطٗا لِّتَكُونُواْ شُهَدَآءَ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ وَيَكُونَ ٱلرَّسُولُ عَلَيۡكُمۡ شَهِيدٗاۗ﴾, "And thus We have made you a middle nation, so that you may be witnesses over mankind and the Messenger a witness over you." "Middle" here means the best and most balanced. A witnessing nation needs:
- **an independent identity**: its own qibla;
- **spiritual strength**: remembrance, patience and prayer;
- **a true understanding of righteousness**: faith, giving and keeping promises, not empty forms;
- **shared worship**: fasting and Hajj;
- **a stable society**: laws of retribution, bequest, wealth and family;
- **readiness to sacrifice**: lawful fighting to end oppression.

## Section 1: The change of qibla (2:142-152)

After the Hijrah, the Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims prayed towards Jerusalem for sixteen or seventeen months, as reported in Sahih Al-Bukhari, and he ﷺ longed to be turned towards the Kaaba. Then came: ﴿قَدۡ نَرَىٰ تَقَلُّبَ وَجۡهِكَ فِي ٱلسَّمَآءِۖ فَلَنُوَلِّيَنَّكَ قِبۡلَةٗ تَرۡضَىٰهَاۚ فَوَلِّ وَجۡهَكَ شَطۡرَ ٱلۡمَسۡجِدِ ٱلۡحَرَامِۚ﴾, "We have seen the turning of your face towards the heaven, so We will surely turn you to a qibla that will please you. So turn your face towards al-Masjid al-Haram."

The Quran announced the objection of the "foolish" before it happened and answered it in advance: ﴿قُل لِّلَّهِ ٱلۡمَشۡرِقُ وَٱلۡمَغۡرِبُۚ يَهۡدِي مَن يَشَآءُ إِلَىٰ صِرَٰطٖ مُّسۡتَقِيمٖ﴾, "Say: to Allah belong the east and the west; He guides whom He wills to a straight path." All directions belong to Allah; what matters is obedience.

### The wisdom of the change
Allah Himself explains it: ﴿وَمَا جَعَلۡنَا ٱلۡقِبۡلَةَ ٱلَّتِي كُنتَ عَلَيۡهَآ إِلَّا لِنَعۡلَمَ مَن يَتَّبِعُ ٱلرَّسُولَ مِمَّن يَنقَلِبُ عَلَىٰ عَقِبَيۡهِۚ﴾, "We only made the qibla you used to face in order to make evident who would follow the Messenger from who would turn back on his heels." It was a test of obedience. When some Companions worried about the prayers of those who had died before the change, Allah revealed: ﴿وَمَا كَانَ ٱللَّهُ لِيُضِيعَ إِيمَٰنَكُمۡۚ﴾, "Allah would never let your faith go to waste", meaning their prayers.

### A repeated command
The command to face al-Masjid al-Haram is repeated three times in this section (verses 144, 149 and 150). Repetition in a major new ruling removes all doubt. The section closes with the greatest blessing, the sending of the Messenger, and then: ﴿فَٱذۡكُرُونِيٓ أَذۡكُرۡكُمۡ وَٱشۡكُرُواْ لِي وَلَا تَكۡفُرُونِ﴾, "So remember Me, I will remember you; be grateful to Me and do not be ungrateful." Read more in [Remember Me, I Will Remember You](/blog/fadhkuruni-adhkurkum-meaning-english/).

## Section 2: Patience and trials (2:153-157)

Once the nation's identity is set, it receives what every carrier of a message needs: ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱسۡتَعِينُواْ بِٱلصَّبۡرِ وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ مَعَ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ﴾, "O you who believe, seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed Allah is with the patient." Allah then tells us that the martyrs are alive, and announces the law of trial: ﴿وَلَنَبۡلُوَنَّكُم بِشَيۡءٖ مِّنَ ٱلۡخَوۡفِ وَٱلۡجُوعِ وَنَقۡصٖ مِّنَ ٱلۡأَمۡوَٰلِ وَٱلۡأَنفُسِ وَٱلثَّمَرَٰتِۗ وَبَشِّرِ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ﴾, "We will surely test you with something of fear and hunger and loss of wealth, lives and fruits; and give good news to the patient."

Who are the patient? ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ إِذَآ أَصَٰبَتۡهُم مُّصِيبَةࣱ قَالُوٓاْ إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّآ إِلَيۡهِ رَٰجِعُونَ﴾, "Those who, when a disaster strikes them, say: indeed we belong to Allah and to Him we will return." They receive three gifts: blessings from their Lord, mercy, and guidance.

**Why patience here?** Because the new nation would face mockery, then the weight of new laws, then battle. Allah gave the provisions before the journey. See also [Seek Help Through Patience and Prayer](/blog/istainu-bis-sabri-was-salah-meaning-english/).

## Section 3: Safa and Marwa, hidden knowledge and the signs of creation (2:158-167)

Allah declares that ﴿ٱلصَّفَا وَٱلۡمَرۡوَةَ مِن شَعَآئِرِ ٱللَّهِۖ﴾, "Safa and Marwa are among the symbols of Allah", so there is no blame in walking between them; some Companions had hesitated because of pre-Islamic practices there. Then comes a warning to those who conceal the clear proofs and guidance Allah revealed, except those who repent, reform and make it clear.

Next comes the declaration of oneness: ﴿وَإِلَٰهُكُمۡ إِلَٰهࣱ وَٰحِدࣱۖ لَّآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ﴾, followed by a magnificent verse listing signs in the universe: the creation of the heavens and earth, the alternation of night and day, ships sailing the sea, rain reviving dead land, creatures spread across it, the shifting winds and the clouds held between sky and earth, ending: ﴿لَأٓيَٰتٖ لِّقَوۡمٖ يَعۡقِلُونَ﴾, "are signs for people who use reason."

Then a painful scene of the Hereafter: leaders disown their followers, and followers wish they could return to the world to disown them.

## Section 4: Good food and blind imitation (2:168-176)

Allah addresses all people: eat what is lawful and good on earth, and do not follow the footsteps of Shaytan. He then condemns blind imitation: ﴿وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمُ ٱتَّبِعُواْ مَآ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ قَالُواْ بَلۡ نَتَّبِعُ مَآ أَلۡفَيۡنَا عَلَيۡهِ ءَابَآءَنَآۚ﴾, "When it is said to them, follow what Allah has revealed, they say: rather we follow what we found our fathers doing." Then the forbidden foods: ﴿إِنَّمَا حَرَّمَ عَلَيۡكُمُ ٱلۡمَيۡتَةَ وَٱلدَّمَ وَلَحۡمَ ٱلۡخِنزِيرِ وَمَآ أُهِلَّ بِهِۦ لِغَيۡرِ ٱللَّهِۖ﴾, with no sin on someone forced by necessity, neither desiring it nor exceeding the limit.

**The link**: this section continues building identity. The middle nation has its own good food and takes its authority from revelation, not ancestral habit.

## Section 5: The verse of righteousness (2:177)

After all the argument about the qibla comes the great correction: ﴿لَّيۡسَ ٱلۡبِرَّ أَن تُوَلُّواْ وُجُوهَكُمۡ قِبَلَ ٱلۡمَشۡرِقِ وَٱلۡمَغۡرِبِ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلۡبِرَّ مَنۡ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡيَوۡمِ ٱلۡأٓخِرِ وَٱلۡمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ وَٱلۡكِتَٰبِ وَٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَ﴾, "Righteousness is not that you turn your faces towards the east or the west; rather, righteousness is in one who believes in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Book and the prophets."

The verse gathers the religion in three circles:
1. **Belief**: in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Book and the prophets.
2. **Giving and worship**: giving wealth despite loving it to relatives, orphans, the needy, the traveller, those who ask, and to free captives; establishing prayer; paying zakah.
3. **Character**: keeping promises, and patience in poverty, illness and battle.

And the verdict: ﴿أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ صَدَقُواْۖ وَأُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُتَّقُونَ﴾, "Those are the ones who are true, and it is they who are the God-conscious." Juz 1 opened with the qualities of the God-conscious, and here they return in fuller detail. Read [Quran 2:177 Explained](/blog/laysa-al-birr-meaning-english/).

## Section 6: Retribution and bequests (2:178-182)

A series of commands beginning "prescribed for you" (*kutiba 'alaykum*) starts here: retribution, then bequest, then fasting, then fighting. On retribution there is a great principle: ﴿وَلَكُمۡ فِي ٱلۡقِصَاصِ حَيَوٰةࣱ يَـٰٓأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَلۡبَٰبِ﴾, "In retribution there is life for you, O people of understanding." Justice in punishment protects life, while the door of pardon and compensation stays open: ﴿فَمَنۡ عُفِيَ لَهُۥ مِنۡ أَخِيهِ شَيۡءࣱ فَٱتِّبَاعُۢ بِٱلۡمَعۡرُوفِ﴾.

## Section 7: Fasting, with prayer at its heart (2:183-187)

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ كُتِبَ عَلَيۡكُمُ ٱلصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبۡلِكُمۡ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تَتَّقُونَ﴾, "O you who believe, fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may become God-conscious." Ramadan and the Quran are then named together: ﴿شَهۡرُ رَمَضَانَ ٱلَّذِيٓ أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانُ هُدٗى لِّلنَّاسِ﴾, with the concession for the sick and the traveller, and the great principle: ﴿يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ بِكُمُ ٱلۡيُسۡرَ وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ ٱلۡعُسۡرَ﴾, "Allah intends ease for you, not hardship."

In the middle of the fasting verses sits the verse of supplication: ﴿وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِي عَنِّي فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌۖ أُجِيبُ دَعۡوَةَ ٱلدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِۖ﴾, "And when My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls upon Me." Most questions in the Quran are answered with "Say...", but here Allah answers directly, "I am near", showing His closeness. Its place among the fasting verses points to the fasting person as one whose prayer is hoped to be accepted. Read [Quran 2:186 Explained](/blog/wa-idha-saalaka-ibadi-meaning-english/).

The section then permits eating, drinking and marital relations during the nights of Ramadan until the white thread of dawn is distinct from the black, and gives rulings on i'tikaf.

## Section 8: Wealth, new moons and fighting (2:188-195)

- **Forbidding consuming wealth unjustly** and bribing judges to take people's property.
- **The question about new moons**: ﴿قُلۡ هِيَ مَوَٰقِيتُ لِلنَّاسِ وَٱلۡحَجِّۗ﴾, "Say: they are measures of time for people and for Hajj", followed by correcting a pre-Islamic habit of entering houses from the back during ihram: ﴿وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلۡبِرَّ مَنِ ٱتَّقَىٰۗ﴾, "rather, righteousness is in one who is God-conscious."
- **Lawful fighting**: fight those who fight you, but do not transgress: ﴿وَلَا تَعۡتَدُوٓاْۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلۡمُعۡتَدِينَ﴾.
- **Spending in Allah's cause**: ﴿وَلَا تُلۡقُواْ بِأَيۡدِيكُمۡ إِلَى ٱلتَّهۡلُكَةِ وَأَحۡسِنُوٓاْۚ﴾, "and do not throw yourselves into destruction, and do good." Abu Ayyub al-Ansari explained it as abandoning striving and becoming absorbed in wealth, as reported by Abu Dawud and At-Tirmidhi.

## Section 9: Hajj (2:196-203)

Allah sets out the rulings of Hajj and Umrah: completing them, being prevented, the sacrificial animal, and tamattu'. Then: ﴿ٱلۡحَجُّ أَشۡهُرࣱ مَّعۡلُومَٰتࣱۚ فَمَن فَرَضَ فِيهِنَّ ٱلۡحَجَّ فَلَا رَفَثَ وَلَا فُسُوقَ وَلَا جِدَالَ فِي ٱلۡحَجِّۗ﴾, "Hajj is in well-known months; whoever undertakes it, let there be no indecency, no wrongdoing and no quarrelling during Hajj." And a beautiful piece of advice: ﴿وَتَزَوَّدُواْ فَإِنَّ خَيۡرَ ٱلزَّادِ ٱلتَّقۡوَىٰۖ﴾, "Take provisions, and the best provision is God-consciousness."

After the departure from Arafah and al-Mash'ar al-Haram, people are divided by their prayers: some ask only for this world, and others say: ﴿رَبَّنَآ ءَاتِنَا فِي ٱلدُّنۡيَا حَسَنَةٗ وَفِي ٱلۡأٓخِرَةِ حَسَنَةٗ وَقِنَا عَذَابَ ٱلنَّارِ﴾, "Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire." Anas reported, in Al-Bukhari and Muslim, that this was the supplication the Prophet ﷺ made most often. Read ["Our Lord, Give Us Good in This World and the Next": Quran 2:201 Explained](/blog/rabbana-atina-fid-dunya-meaning-english/).

## Section 10: Two kinds of people and entering Islam fully (2:204-214)

- **One whose speech impresses you**, who calls Allah to witness what is in his heart while being the fiercest of opponents; when he leaves, he spreads corruption.
- **One who sells himself** seeking the pleasure of Allah.

Then the comprehensive call: ﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱدۡخُلُواْ فِي ٱلسِّلۡمِ كَآفَّةٗ وَلَا تَتَّبِعُواْ خُطُوَٰتِ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنِۚ﴾, "O you who believe, enter into Islam completely and do not follow the footsteps of Shaytan": take the whole religion, not only the parts that suit your desires. Read [Enter Islam Completely: 2:208](/blog/enter-islam-completely-2-208-meaning-english/).

Allah reminds the believers that the road to Paradise has trials, as it did for those before them, until the Messenger and the believers said: when is the help of Allah? ﴿أَلَآ إِنَّ نَصۡرَ ٱللَّهِ قَرِيبࣱ﴾, "Indeed, the help of Allah is near."

## Section 11: The Companions' questions and social law (2:215-232)

"They ask you" (*yas'alunaka*) repeats through this section, a lesson that the Companions asked in order to act:
- **What should they spend?** On parents, relatives, orphans, the needy and travellers.
- **Fighting in the sacred month**, with a comprehensive verse: ﴿وَعَسَىٰٓ أَن تَكۡرَهُواْ شَيۡـٔٗا وَهُوَ خَيۡرࣱ لَّكُمۡۖ وَعَسَىٰٓ أَن تُحِبُّواْ شَيۡـٔٗا وَهُوَ شَرࣱّ لَّكُمۡۚ﴾, "Perhaps you dislike something while it is good for you, and perhaps you love something while it is bad for you."
- **Wine and gambling**: ﴿قُلۡ فِيهِمَآ إِثۡمࣱ كَبِيرࣱ وَمَنَٰفِعُ لِلنَّاسِ وَإِثۡمُهُمَآ أَكۡبَرُ مِن نَّفۡعِهِمَاۗ﴾, "Say: in them is great sin and some benefit for people, but their sin is greater than their benefit." This was a stage in the prohibition that was completed in Surah Al-Ma'idah.
- **Orphans**: improving their affairs is best, and mixing your affairs with theirs is allowed.
- **Marrying polytheists**: forbidden.
- **Menstruation**: avoiding relations during it.
- **Oaths and *ila'*** (a vow to stay away from one's wife).
- **Divorce**: ﴿ٱلطَّلَٰقُ مَرَّتَانِۖ فَإِمۡسَاكُۢ بِمَعۡرُوفٍ أَوۡ تَسۡرِيحُۢ بِإِحۡسَٰنٖۗ﴾, "Divorce is twice; then either keep her in an acceptable manner or release her with kindness", with the repeated warning: ﴿تِلۡكَ حُدُودُ ٱللَّهِ فَلَا تَعۡتَدُوهَاۚ﴾, "These are the limits of Allah, so do not transgress them."

Notice that divorce rulings in the Quran are always surrounded by words of fairness, kindness and God-consciousness: even separation has its ethics.

## Section 12: Nursing, waiting periods and guarding the prayer (2:233-242)

- **Nursing**: ﴿وَٱلۡوَٰلِدَٰتُ يُرۡضِعۡنَ أَوۡلَٰدَهُنَّ حَوۡلَيۡنِ كَامِلَيۡنِۖ﴾, "Mothers may nurse their children two complete years", with the principle ﴿لَا تُكَلَّفُ نَفۡسٌ إِلَّا وُسۡعَهَاۚ﴾, "no soul is burdened beyond its capacity", and the principle of mutual consultation between parents.
- **The waiting period of a widow**: four months and ten days.
- **Dowry and divorce before consummation**, with the advice: ﴿وَلَا تَنسَوُاْ ٱلۡفَضۡلَ بَيۡنَكُمۡۚ﴾, "and do not forget graciousness between you."

In the middle of these family rulings comes, suddenly, the verse of prayer: ﴿حَٰفِظُواْ عَلَى ٱلصَّلَوَٰتِ وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِ ٱلۡوُسۡطَىٰ وَقُومُواْ لِلَّهِ قَٰنِتِينَ﴾, "Guard the prayers, and the middle prayer, and stand before Allah in devotion." Its position reminds spouses that their bond with Allah is what keeps them fair in times of conflict. Read [Guard the Prayers: 2:238](/blog/guard-the-prayers-2-238-meaning-english/).

## Section 13: Life after death and the story of Talut (2:243-252)

It opens with those who left their homes in their thousands fearing death, whom Allah caused to die and then revived, followed by a call to fight and to spend: ﴿مَّن ذَا ٱلَّذِي يُقۡرِضُ ٱللَّهَ قَرۡضًا حَسَنٗا﴾, "Who will lend Allah a goodly loan?"

Then the **story of Talut (Saul) and Jalut (Goliath)**, full of lessons:
1. **The chiefs ask for a king** to fight under, and most turn away once fighting is prescribed.
2. **Talut is chosen**, and they object that he has no wealth. The answer: ﴿إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱصۡطَفَىٰهُ عَلَيۡكُمۡ وَزَادَهُۥ بَسۡطَةࣰ فِي ٱلۡعِلۡمِ وَٱلۡجِسۡمِۖ﴾, "Allah has chosen him over you and increased him abundantly in knowledge and stature." Leadership is measured by knowledge and strength, not money.
3. **The test of the river**: ﴿فَمَن شَرِبَ مِنۡهُ فَلَيۡسَ مِنِّي﴾, "whoever drinks from it is not with me", and they drank, except a few.
4. **Few, but certain**: ﴿كَم مِّن فِئَةٖ قَلِيلَةٍ غَلَبَتۡ فِئَةٗ كَثِيرَةَۢ بِإِذۡنِ ٱللَّهِۗ وَٱللَّهُ مَعَ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ﴾, "How many a small company has overcome a large company by the permission of Allah, and Allah is with the patient."
5. **Prayer before the battle**: ﴿رَبَّنَآ أَفۡرِغۡ عَلَيۡنَا صَبۡرٗا وَثَبِّتۡ أَقۡدَامَنَا وَٱنصُرۡنَا عَلَى ٱلۡقَوۡمِ ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ﴾, "Our Lord, pour patience upon us, make our feet firm and give us victory over the disbelieving people."
6. **Victory**: Dawud kills Jalut and is given kingship and wisdom.

Al-Bukhari reports from al-Bara' ibn 'Azib that the Companions used to say the number of the people of Badr matched the number of Talut's companions who crossed the river with him: three hundred and ten-odd.

**Notice the thread**: section 2 said ﴿إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ مَعَ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ﴾, and the story of Talut ends with almost the same words. Patience is the thread running through the whole juz.

The juz closes with ﴿تِلۡكَ ءَايَٰتُ ٱللَّهِ نَتۡلُوهَا عَلَيۡكَ بِٱلۡحَقِّۚ وَإِنَّكَ لَمِنَ ٱلۡمُرۡسَلِينَ﴾, reassuring the Prophet ﷺ that these accounts of the unseen are proof of his message.

## Tajweed points in Juz 2

### Qalqalah in ﴿قَدۡ نَرَىٰ﴾
The sakin dal in the middle of speech has a light echo (qalqalah sughra): do not swallow it and do not give it a vowel.

### Iqlab in ﴿فِئَةٗ كَثِيرَةَۢ بِإِذۡنِ﴾ and ﴿غُرۡفَةَۢ بِيَدِهِۦۚ﴾
Tanween before ba turns into a hidden meem with ghunnah, shown by the small meem in the Mushaf. The same happens in ﴿وَٰلِدَةُۢ بِوَلَدِهَا﴾.

### Doubled noon in ﴿إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّآ إِلَيۡهِ﴾
The doubled noon carries a ghunnah of two counts, and the second "inna" is followed by a separated madd.

### The connecting hamzah in ﴿ءَامَنُواْ ٱسۡتَعِينُواْ﴾
When reading continuously, the connecting hamzah drops, so the waw joins the seen. The waw is not stretched because a sakin letter follows.

### Madd 'arid at verse endings
As in ﴿يَرۡشُدُونَ﴾ and ﴿ٱلۡمُتَّقُونَ﴾: two, four or six counts are allowed, but keep one length consistently. For the full rules see [Madd Rules in Tajweed](/blog/madd-rules-tajweed-english/).

## Similar verses to watch

### 1. The command to face al-Masjid al-Haram
- 2:144: "...turn your face towards al-Masjid al-Haram, and wherever you are, turn your faces towards it", ending with ﴿يَعۡمَلُونَ﴾ (*ya'malun*, "they do").
- 2:149: begins ﴿وَمِنۡ حَيۡثُ خَرَجۡتَ فَوَلِّ وَجۡهَكَ﴾, "and from wherever you go out, turn your face", ending with ﴿تَعۡمَلُونَ﴾ (*ta'malun*, "you do").
- 2:150: begins like 149, then adds "and wherever you are, turn your faces towards it, so that people will have no argument against you."

### 2. Imitating forefathers in three surahs
- Al-Baqarah 170: *alfayna* ("we found") ... *la ya'qilun shay'an* ("understood nothing").
- Al-Ma'idah 104: *hasbuna ma wajadna* ... *la ya'lamun shay'an* ("knew nothing").
- Luqman 21: *bal nattabi'u ma wajadna* ... "even if Shaytan was calling them".
**Key**: *alfayna* appears only in Al-Baqarah, together with *ya'qilun*.

### 3. Forbidden foods
- Al-Baqarah 173: ﴿وَمَآ أُهِلَّ بِهِۦ لِغَيۡرِ ٱللَّهِۖ﴾ ... ﴿فَلَآ إِثۡمَ عَلَيۡهِۚ﴾.
- An-Nahl 115: ﴿وَمَآ أُهِلَّ لِغَيۡرِ ٱللَّهِ بِهِۦۖ﴾ ... ﴿فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَفُورࣱ رَّحِيمࣱ﴾.
**Key**: in Al-Baqarah "bihi" comes first and it has "no sin upon him".

### 4. "Allah is with the patient"
Verse 153 has ﴿إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ﴾ (*inna Allaha*), while verse 249 has ﴿وَٱللَّهُ﴾ (*wa-Allahu*). The start of the juz has *inna*, the end has *wa*.

## How to memorize Juz 2 with understanding

1. **Learn the map.** Thirteen sections sound like many, but they gather into four blocks: identity (142-177), worship law (178-203), society (204-242) and the story (243-252).
2. **Watch the openers.** "O you who believe", "They ask you" and "Prescribed for you" mark new sections.
3. **Give legal verses extra repetition**, because they lack the flow of stories.
4. **Link each ruling to its question**, for example "They ask you about the new moons" with "righteousness is not entering houses from the back".
5. **Treat the Talut story as the reward at the end**, and memorize it in one sequence.
6. **Revise with Juz 1**, since the start of Juz 2 completes the end of Juz 1 on Ibrahim and the qibla.

For general technique see [How to Memorize the Quran Step by Step](/blog/how-to-memorize-quran-step-by-step/).

## Practical lessons from Juz 2

- **Obedience is a test**: who follows the Messenger ﷺ through every change.
- **Remembrance is answered with remembrance**: remember Allah and He remembers you.
- **When calamity strikes, say "inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un"** and hope for reward.
- **Righteousness is substance, not appearance**: faith, giving, keeping promises, patience.
- **Supplication is close to answer**, especially while fasting.
- **Take the whole religion**, not a selection.
- **In family disputes, do not forget graciousness between you.**
- **A small, patient group can prevail by Allah's permission.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is Juz 2 called "Sayaqul"?
Because it begins with ﴿سَيَقُولُ ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾, and memorizers traditionally name each juz by its first word.

### Where does Juz 2 end?
At Al-Baqarah 2:252. Juz 3 begins with ﴿تِلۡكَ ٱلرُّسُلُ فَضَّلۡنَا بَعۡضَهُمۡ عَلَىٰ بَعۡضٖۘ﴾, "Those messengers, We preferred some of them over others."

### Is Juz 2 hard to memorize?
It contains long legal verses, especially on divorce and waiting periods, which are harder than stories. Understand the ruling first, then memorize, with extra repetition.

### What are the best-known verses of Juz 2?
Among them: the verse of righteousness (177), the fasting verses (183-187), the verse of supplication (186), and "Our Lord, give us good in this world" (201).

### How does Juz 2 connect to Juz 1?
Juz 1 told the experience of the Children of Israel and ended with Ibrahim building the House; Juz 2 begins by turning towards that House and builds the nation that inherited the message.

## Conclusion

Juz 2 is the constitution of the middle nation: a qibla that unites it, patience that strengthens it, true righteousness that purifies it, worship that gathers it, laws that protect its society and families, and a story that teaches that victory belongs to the patient, not the numerous. Read it with that lens and the whole juz becomes one connected message.
