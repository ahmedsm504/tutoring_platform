For many Muslims today, the closest copy of the Quran is not on a shelf but in their pocket. We read it on the train, in a lunch break, waiting at the doctor's, and before sleep. With this convenience come real questions: Do I need wudu to touch my phone when the Quran is open? Is the reward the same as reading from a printed Mushaf? Can I read from my phone in Taraweeh? And what about taking my phone into the bathroom?

This guide answers these questions based on what scholars have said, explains where they agree and where they differ, and offers practical advice to make your phone a tool that brings you closer to the Quran rather than one that distracts you from it.

## Is Reading from a Phone Legitimate?
Yes. Reading the Quran from a phone or tablet is legitimate, and contemporary scholars do not dispute this. What matters is recitation, and recitation can be done from any medium that displays the Quranic text correctly. Muslims have read the Quran from parchment, bone, palm leaves, handwritten manuscripts and printed books. A screen is another medium carrying the same words of Allah.

## Is the Reward the Same?
The reward for recitation is tied to **reciting**, not to the medium. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever recites a letter from the Book of Allah will have a good deed for it, and a good deed is multiplied by ten. I do not say that Alif Lam Mim is a letter, but Alif is a letter, Lam is a letter and Mim is a letter." This is reported by at-Tirmidhi, who graded it hasan sahih. The hadith does not limit the reward to a particular medium.

Some scholars mention an **additional virtue** in reading from a printed Mushaf: looking at the Mushaf itself, handling it in a state of purity and honouring it. This is an extra merit, not a reduction in the reward of reciting from a phone. If you have a printed Mushaf available, reading from it is more complete; if your phone is what you have, the reward of recitation is yours.

## Do You Need Wudu to Touch Your Phone?
### The background: touching the Mushaf
The majority of scholars, including the four schools of law, hold that a person without wudu should not touch a **printed Mushaf**. Their main evidence is the letter the Prophet ﷺ sent with 'Amr ibn Hazm, which says: "None should touch the Quran except one who is pure," reported by Malik, an-Nasa'i and others and accepted by the scholars; al-Albani graded it authentic through its various chains. A minority, including the Zahiri school, allowed it.

Reading the Quran **from memory** without wudu is permitted by consensus for someone in minor impurity.

### What about phones?
Many contemporary scholars hold that **a phone does not take the ruling of a Mushaf**, so it may be touched and read from without wudu. Their reasons:
1. The letters on a screen are not permanently written; they are an image of light that appears and disappears when the app is closed.
2. A phone contains countless other files and apps; it is closer to a general book that contains some verses than to a Mushaf.
3. The glass of the screen is a barrier between the hand and the text.

So if you are in a state of minor impurity, you may read from your phone. **Being in wudu is still better**, because purity is part of the etiquette of reciting the Quran, but you are not sinful for reading without it.

### An important distinction
This concerns **minor impurity** (after using the bathroom, sleeping, and so on). A person in **major impurity** (janabah) should not recite the Quran at all, from memory or from a phone, according to the majority, until they perform ghusl. Women during menstruation are the subject of a well-known scholarly difference of opinion, covered in a separate article.

## Reading from a Phone During Prayer
### Voluntary prayers
Many scholars permit reading from a Mushaf in **voluntary prayers** such as Taraweeh and night prayer. Al-Bukhari mentions, without a full chain, that 'A'ishah was led in prayer by her servant Dhakwan, who read from the Mushaf. This is the position of the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools; the Malikis disliked it in obligatory prayers, and the Hanafis held that it invalidates the prayer. A phone takes the same ruling as a printed Mushaf here, according to those who permit it.

### Obligatory prayers
It is better to recite from memory in obligatory prayers, since the required recitation is usually short, and holding a phone involves unnecessary movement.

### If you do read from a phone in prayer
1. **Turn on airplane mode** so no call or notification interrupts you.
2. **Use a stand** in front of you rather than holding the phone throughout.
3. **Set the screen to stay on** so it does not lock mid-recitation.
4. **Open the page before you begin**.
5. **Minimise movement**, and put the phone down during bowing and prostration.

## Taking Your Phone into the Bathroom
Scholars have long discussed taking a Mushaf into the bathroom, and agree that it is **not permitted** without necessity, such as fear of it being stolen. They also discouraged taking in objects bearing the name of Allah if they are exposed.

Applied to phones, many contemporary scholars say:
- **A phone with a Quran app that is closed** may be taken into the bathroom without any problem. The phone is not a Mushaf, and the Quran is not displayed. It is like a person who has memorised the Quran entering the bathroom: the Quran is in his memory, not exposed.
- **If the Quran is open on the screen**, close the app or lock the screen before entering.
- **If a recitation is playing**, stop it before entering. It is not appropriate for the Quran to be recited in a place where people relieve themselves.
- **Avoid Quranic verses as ringtones**, since the phone may ring in inappropriate places, and verses get cut off mid-sentence when answering. Many scholars disliked this practice.

## Memorising from a Phone: Does It Work?
Experienced Quran teachers generally recommend that **primary memorisation be done from a fixed printed Mushaf**, for several reasons:
1. **Visual memory**: memorisers remember where a verse sits on the page, top or bottom, right or left. A fixed Mushaf, such as the 15-line Madinah Mushaf, reinforces this mental image. On a phone, pages can shift with font size and app settings.
2. **Fewer distractions**: a printed Mushaf sends no notifications.
3. **Deeper focus**: many people find they concentrate better on paper than on a screen.

**A good balance**: memorise from a printed Mushaf, and revise from your phone during commutes and spare moments, using an app that displays **the same pages** as your printed Mushaf.

## The Phone's Great Advantage: Understanding
One clear benefit of Quran apps is instant access to **translation, word meanings and tafsir**. When you reach a word you do not understand, a tap shows its meaning. Use this to build a daily habit of reading a few verses with their explanation. For non-Arabic speakers, word-by-word features are especially valuable.

## Etiquette When Reading from Your Phone
1. **Renew your intention**: you are reciting the words of Allah, even if the device is ordinary.
2. **Silence notifications** with "Do Not Disturb" or focus mode.
3. **Do not interrupt a verse** to reply to a message unless necessary.
4. **Choose a reliable app** from a well-known source that displays the Uthmani text correctly.
5. **Report errors**: if you notice a mistake in an app's text, stop using it and report it.
6. **Do not place the phone in a disrespectful place** while the Quran is open.
7. **Use headphones** when listening in public places.

## Beating Digital Distraction
The biggest challenge of reading from a phone is not legal but psychological: **distraction**. You open the Quran to read a page, a message arrives, you reply, you see a post, and half an hour later you have read two verses. Practical solutions:
- **Turn on focus mode** as soon as you open the Quran app.
- **Create a "Quran" focus profile** that allows only the Quran app.
- **Move distracting apps off your home screen** and place the Quran app there.
- **Set a goal**: "Four pages in fifteen minutes."
- **If you get distracted twice**, switch to a printed Mushaf.

## Phones and Children
Many parents give children a phone or tablet with a Quran app for memorisation. Some guidance:
- **Printed Mushaf for learning**: let children learn to read and memorise from a printed copy, so they develop respect for the Mushaf and a stable visual memory.
- **Phone for listening**: audio recitation by a skilled reciter is excellent for children, especially before sleep or in the car.
- **No ads, no links**: if a child uses an app, choose one without advertisements or links to other content.
- **Limit screen time**: so that the Quran is not associated with screen addiction.
- **Teach etiquette**: close the app before going to the bathroom, do not leave the phone on the floor with the Quran open, and handle it calmly.

## Listening on Your Phone
One of the greatest blessings of the phone is that it puts the world's best reciters in your pocket. Listening is an act of worship in itself; Allah says: "So when the Quran is recited, listen to it and pay attention, that you may receive mercy" (Al-A'raf 204). Use your phone to listen during your commute, before sleep and while doing chores. Listening to a teacher-style recitation, slow and clear, is especially helpful for beginners correcting their pronunciation. But remember that **listening is not reciting**: listening has its own reward, while the reward of letters belongs to recitation with the tongue.

## A Simple Daily Routine with Your Phone
- **Morning commute**: listen to the page you will memorise tonight, three times.
- **Lunch break**: read one page from the app with its translation or tafsir.
- **Evening**: memorise from your printed Mushaf; then revise it once from the app before sleeping.
- **Before sleep**: recite the bedtime verses (Ayat al-Kursi, the last two verses of Al-Baqarah, the three Quls) from memory, not just by playing them.

This routine uses the phone where it is strongest, listening and understanding, and the printed Mushaf where it is strongest, memorisation.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Can I read the Quran on my phone without wudu?
Yes, according to many contemporary scholars, since a phone does not take the ruling of a Mushaf; being in wudu is still better.

### Is the reward the same as reading from a printed Mushaf?
The reward of recitation is the same, as it is tied to reciting; some scholars mention an extra virtue in reading from a printed Mushaf.

### Can someone in janabah read Quran from a phone?
No, according to the majority, until they perform ghusl, because the restriction concerns recitation itself.

### Can I read from my phone in Taraweeh?
Many scholars permit reading from a Mushaf in voluntary prayers, and a phone takes the same ruling; use airplane mode and minimise movement.

### Can I take my phone into the bathroom if it has a Quran app?
Yes, if the app is closed; close any open Quran page and stop any recitation before entering.

### Is it better to memorise from a phone or a printed Mushaf?
A fixed printed Mushaf is better for memorisation; use your phone for revision with an app showing the same pages.

### Is listening to Quran on my phone the same as reciting?
No. Listening is an act of worship with its own reward, but the reward of letters belongs to reciting with the tongue.

## Conclusion
Reading the Quran from your phone is legitimate, the reward of recitation is yours, and many contemporary scholars do not require wudu for touching a phone, though wudu remains better. It is permitted in voluntary prayers according to many scholars, and the phone may be taken into the bathroom as long as no Quran is displayed or playing. But the phone is a double-edged tool: it can bring the Quran with you everywhere, or bury it under notifications. Memorise from paper, revise and reflect from your screen, and silence everything else when you are in the presence of Allah's words.
