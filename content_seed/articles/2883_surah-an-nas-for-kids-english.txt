Have you ever had a little voice inside your head saying: "Don't pray now, pray later," or "Take that sweet, nobody will see," or "Say something mean back to him"? That voice is called waswasah, whispering. Shaytan whispers bad ideas into our hearts, quietly, hoping we will listen. But Allah gave us a powerful surah to push him away: Surah An-Nas, the very last surah of the Quran.

The amazing thing is what the surah calls Shaytan: "al-khannas", the one who hides and runs away. The moment we remember Allah, he slinks back and hides! In this guide for parents, we explain Surah An-Nas to children in simple English, verse by verse, with practical ways to help kids recognise and push away bad thoughts, the Sunnah bedtime routine, activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah An-Nas

- **Position**: surah 114, the last surah of the Quran.
- **Number of verses**: six.
- **Type**: Meccan according to many scholars, and Madinan according to others.
- **Name**: an-Nas means "mankind", "people", a word repeated five times in this short surah.
- **With Al-Falaq**: together they are called al-Mu'awwidhatayn, the two surahs of seeking refuge.

### Its virtue
The Prophet ﷺ said to 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir: "No one has ever sought refuge with anything like these two" (an-Nasa'i; graded authentic by al-Albani), meaning Al-Falaq and An-Nas.

## The Whole Surah

﴿قُلۡ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿إِلَٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿مِن شَرِّ ٱلۡوَسۡوَاسِ ٱلۡخَنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي يُوَسۡوِسُ فِي صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿مِنَ ٱلۡجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ﴾ (Quran 114:1-6)

## Three Names of Allah in Three Verses

The surah begins by describing Allah in three ways, one after another:

### Verse 1: The Lord of mankind
"Rabb means the One who created us, looks after us and helps us grow. He feeds us, protects us and teaches us. Allah is the Rabb of all people."

### Verse 2: The King of mankind
"Malik means the King who owns everything and whose command is final. Every king on earth is small compared to Allah, the true King of all people."

### Verse 3: The God of mankind
"Ilah means the One we worship, the One we love most and obey. Allah is the only One who deserves our worship."

### Explaining it to your child
"Before telling us what to seek protection from, Allah tells us who we are running to: the Lord who cares for us, the King who rules everything, and the God we worship. With a protector like that, nothing can beat us!"

### Activity: three names, three hands
Draw three big circles. In the first, write "Rabb: He looks after me" and let your child draw things Allah gives them: food, family, home. In the second, "Malik: He rules everything", and draw a crown. In the third, "Ilah: I worship Him", and draw a prayer mat.

## Verse 4: From the Evil of the Whisperer Who Withdraws

### Explaining it to your child
"Al-waswas is the whisperer, Shaytan, who puts bad ideas into people's hearts. Al-khannas means the one who hides and runs back. Scholars explain that when a person forgets Allah, Shaytan comes closer and whispers, and when the person remembers Allah, Shaytan backs away and hides. So remembering Allah is like turning on a light that makes him run!"

### Lesson for kids: Shaytan is weak
"Shaytan can't force you to do anything. He can only whisper. You are the one who decides whether to listen. And when you say 'A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim', he runs away."

## Verse 5: Who Whispers in the Hearts of People

### Explaining it to your child
"Shaytan's whispers go into our chests, our hearts. That's why bad ideas sometimes feel like they're our own. A good way to know: if an idea makes you want to disobey Allah, hurt someone, lie or be lazy about prayer, it's probably a whisper. Push it away!"

## Verse 6: From Among the Jinn and Mankind

### Explaining it to your child
"Whisperers aren't only from the jinn. Some people also 'whisper' bad things: a friend who says 'let's cheat', or someone who says 'don't listen to your parents'. We ask Allah to protect us from both kinds."

### Lesson for kids: choosing good friends
"A good friend reminds you of Allah and encourages you to do good. A bad friend whispers like Shaytan. The Prophet ﷺ said that a good friend is like a perfume seller: you either get some perfume or at least enjoy the nice smell, and a bad friend is like a blacksmith's bellows: either your clothes get burnt or you smell something bad (al-Bukhari and Muslim)."

### Activity: perfume or smoke?
Read out different things a friend might say, and your child decides: "perfume" or "smoke"?
- "Let's go and pray together." (perfume)
- "Copy my homework, the teacher won't know." (smoke)
- "Say sorry to your sister, it's the right thing." (perfume)
- "Don't tell your mum where we went." (smoke)

## How Children Can Push Away Bad Thoughts

Here is a simple four-step plan you can teach your child:

1. **Notice it**: "That's a whisper, not a good idea."
2. **Say it**: "A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim."
3. **Move**: change what you're doing; stand up, get a drink, or go to a parent.
4. **Do the opposite**: if the whisper says "pray later", pray now; if it says "say something mean", say something kind.

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ taught that when a person is angry, saying "A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim" makes the anger go away (al-Bukhari and Muslim). This is the same weapon against whispering.

### Tip for parents: don't turn worries into fear
Some children, especially sensitive ones, may become anxious about "bad thoughts". Reassure them: "Having a bad thought pop into your head isn't a sin. Everyone gets them. What matters is what you do next." The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah has forgiven his nation for what crosses their minds, as long as they don't act on it or speak it (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

## The Bedtime Three

'A'ishah reported that every night the Prophet ﷺ cupped his hands, blew lightly into them, recited Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, and wiped over as much of his body as he could, three times (al-Bukhari).

### Activity: the bedtime routine
Make this part of your child's bedtime every night: cup hands, recite the three Quls, blow gently, wipe over the head, face and body, three times. End with a hug and "Allah is watching over you."

## Why Does the Quran End With An-Nas?

Scholars have reflected that the Quran begins with Al-Fatiha, asking Allah for guidance, and ends with An-Nas, asking Allah to protect us from whatever could pull us away from that guidance. It's like Allah gives us a treasure, then gives us a lock to keep it safe.

### Explaining it to your child
"The Quran starts by asking Allah to guide us and ends by asking Allah to protect us from Shaytan, who wants to make us lose our way. First the treasure, then the lock!"

## Shaytan's Favourite Tricks With Children

It can help children to know the most common whispers, so they can spot them quickly:

- **"Later"**: "Pray later, read Quran later, do your homework later." The answer: do it now.
- **"Nobody will see"**: "Take it, nobody's looking." The answer: Allah sees everything.
- **"Everyone does it"**: "All your friends say bad words." The answer: I follow what Allah loves, not what everyone does.
- **"You're better than them"**: "You're smarter, so you don't need to listen." The answer: humility, like the Prophet ﷺ.
- **"Get them back"**: "He hit you, hit him harder." The answer: tell an adult and stay calm.

### Activity: catch the trick
When your child tells you about their day, help them spot moments when one of these tricks appeared, and praise them for every trick they caught and beat. Over time, they'll start catching them on their own.

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Two Voices"

Zakariya loved football. One afternoon, the ball was right there in the garden, his friends were calling him to play, and the adhan for Asr had just finished. A little voice in his head said: "Play first. Pray later. There's plenty of time."

But another thought came: "Remember what Mum taught you about the whisperer." Zakariya stopped. "Is this a whisper?" he asked himself. It was telling him to delay prayer, so it probably was. He took a deep breath and said quietly: "A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim."

Then he did something brave. He shouted to his friends: "Give me five minutes, I'm going to pray Asr first!" One of his friends, Adam, said: "Wait, I'll come too!" They prayed together quickly but properly, then ran back out to play.

That evening, Zakariya told his mother: "Mum, the whisperer told me to play first, but I chased him away!" His mother smiled and recited: "Minal-waswasil-khannas." "You see? The moment you remembered Allah, he hid. That's why he's called al-khannas." Zakariya laughed: "He's a coward!"

Ask your child: "What did the whisper tell Zakariya? What did he do to push it away? And who did he help do the right thing too?"

## What We Learn From Surah An-Nas

- **Allah is our Lord, King and God**.
- **Shaytan whispers** but cannot force us.
- **Remembering Allah** makes Shaytan hide.
- **We decide** whether to listen to a whisper.
- **Some people whisper** bad ideas too, so we choose good friends.
- **Bad thoughts** aren't sins if we don't act on them.
- **The bedtime three** protect us every night.
- **The Quran ends** by protecting the guidance it began with.

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### My child is scared of Shaytan. How do I talk about him?
Emphasize that Shaytan is weak, that he can only whisper, and that he runs away when we remember Allah. Children feel stronger, not scared, when they know they have a simple "weapon".

### Are bad thoughts a sin?
No. Passing thoughts aren't counted as sins unless a person acts on them or says them, as the authentic hadith teaches. This is reassuring for sensitive children.

### How do I make the three Quls a habit?
Tie them to fixed moments: after Fajr and Maghrib, and at bedtime. Do them together until your child can do them alone.
