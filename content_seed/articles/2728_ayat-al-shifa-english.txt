When someone at home falls ill, many Muslims look for the "verses of healing" (*ayat al-shifa*) to recite over them, write for them, or read over water. Booklets and social media share a list of six verses in which the word "healing" (*shifa'*) appears. What are these verses? Where does the name come from? Did the Prophet ﷺ single them out for ruqyah? And how should we use them?

This guide presents the six verses with their meanings and contexts, explains the origin of the grouping, and then describes ruqyah with the Quran in the correct way.

## The Six Verses
### 1. At-Tawbah 14
﴿قَٰتِلُوهُمۡ يُعَذِّبۡهُمُ ٱللَّهُ بِأَيۡدِيكُمۡ وَيُخۡزِهِمۡ وَيَنصُرۡكُمۡ عَلَيۡهِمۡ وَيَشۡفِ صُدُورَ قَوۡمٖ مُّؤۡمِنِينَ﴾ (Quran 9:14)

Revealed about fighting the polytheists who broke their treaties. The healing here is **the healing of the believers' hearts** from the anger and pain caused by persecution.

### 2. Yunus 57
﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ قَدۡ جَآءَتۡكُم مَّوۡعِظَةࣱ مِّن رَّبِّكُمۡ وَشِفَآءࣱ لِّمَا فِي ٱلصُّدُورِ وَهُدٗى وَرَحۡمَةࣱ لِّلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ﴾ (Quran 10:57)

The Quran is **a healing for what is in the hearts**: doubt, ignorance, and diseases of the heart such as envy, arrogance and hypocrisy.

### 3. An-Nahl 69
﴿ثُمَّ كُلِي مِن كُلِّ ٱلثَّمَرَٰتِ فَٱسۡلُكِي سُبُلَ رَبِّكِ ذُلُلٗاۚ يَخۡرُجُ مِنۢ بُطُونِهَا شَرَابࣱ مُّخۡتَلِفٌ أَلۡوَٰنُهُۥ فِيهِ شِفَآءࣱ لِّلنَّاسِۚ إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَأٓيَةٗ لِّقَوۡمٖ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ﴾ (Quran 16:69)

This verse is about **honey**, and that in it there is healing for people: a physical healing for the body.

### 4. Al-Isra' 82
﴿وَنُنَزِّلُ مِنَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانِ مَا هُوَ شِفَآءࣱ وَرَحۡمَةࣱ لِّلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ وَلَا يَزِيدُ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ إِلَّا خَسَارٗا﴾ (Quran 17:82)

The Quran is **healing and mercy for the believers**. Many commentators understood this to include the healing of both hearts and bodies.

### 5. Ash-Shu'ara' 80
﴿وَإِذَا مَرِضۡتُ فَهُوَ يَشۡفِينِ﴾ (Quran 26:80)

The words of Ibrahim, peace be upon him, attributing **healing to Allah alone**, while attributing illness to himself out of courtesy towards Allah.

### 6. Fussilat 44
﴿وَلَوۡ جَعَلۡنَٰهُ قُرۡءَانًا أَعۡجَمِيّٗا لَّقَالُواْ لَوۡلَا فُصِّلَتۡ ءَايَٰتُهُۥٓۖ ءَا۬عۡجَمِيࣱّ وَعَرَبِيࣱّۗ قُلۡ هُوَ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ هُدٗى وَشِفَآءࣱۚ وَٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤۡمِنُونَ فِيٓ ءَاذَانِهِمۡ وَقۡرࣱ وَهُوَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ عَمًىۚ أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ يُنَادَوۡنَ مِن مَّكَانِۭ بَعِيدٖ﴾ (Quran 41:44)

The Quran is **guidance and healing for those who believe**, and blindness for those who do not.

## Two Kinds of Healing
Looking at these verses, healing is of two kinds:
1. **Healing of hearts**: from doubt, ignorance, anger and spiritual diseases. This is the main sense in most verses, as in Yunus, Fussilat and At-Tawbah.
2. **Healing of bodies**: explicit in the verse about honey, and implied in Ibrahim's words, and in Al-Isra' according to many commentators who said the Quran heals hearts and bodies.

Ibn al-Qayyim wrote in *Zad al-Ma'ad*, in meaning, that the Quran is the complete cure for all diseases of the heart and body, of this world and the next, but not everyone is made fit or granted success to seek cure through it.

## Where Does the Name Come From?
There is **no authentic hadith** that groups these six verses, calls them "the verses of healing", or commands reciting them together over the sick. They were **grouped by some scholars and righteous people** because they share the word "healing", and they used them in ruqyah as a means of seeking blessing through Allah's words.

A story is told in biographical literature about the scholar Abu al-Qasim al-Qushayri: his son fell seriously ill, and he saw in a dream someone directing him to the verses of healing; he recited and wrote them, gave them to his son, and he recovered. This is a dream, and no ruling is built on dreams, but it explains how the grouping became popular.

## Is It Permitted to Use Them?
Ruqyah with the Quran is **permitted by consensus** when it is with Allah's words, names and attributes, in understood language, with the belief that the Healer is Allah. The Prophet ﷺ said, as in Sahih Muslim: "There is no harm in ruqyah as long as it contains no shirk."

So reciting these verses over the sick is **permitted**, because they are part of the Quran, and the whole Quran is healing. But:
- **Do not believe** they have an established special status above other verses.
- **It is better** to begin with what is authentic in the Sunnah: Al-Fatihah, the Mu'awwidhat, Ayat al-Kursi and the prophetic supplications.
- **Do not abandon** medical treatment because of them.

## What the Sunnah Teaches
### Al-Fatihah
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, a Companion recited Al-Fatihah over a man stung by a scorpion and he recovered. The Prophet ﷺ said: "How did you know it is a ruqyah?"

### The Mu'awwidhat
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah reported that when the Prophet ﷺ was unwell, he recited the Mu'awwidhat over himself and blew.

### Prophetic supplications
- "Allahumma Rabban-nas, adhhibil-ba's, ishfi antash-Shafi, la shifa'a illa shifa'uk, shifa'an la yughadiru saqama." Al-Bukhari and Muslim.
- Placing the hand on the place of pain, saying "Bismillah" three times, then seven times "A'udhu billahi wa qudratihi min sharri ma ajidu wa uhadhir." Muslim.
- The ruqyah of Jibril: "Bismillahi arqika, min kulli shay'in yu'dhika, min sharri kulli nafsin aw 'aynin hasid, Allahu yashfik, bismillahi arqik." Muslim.

## How to Recite over a Sick Person
1. **Make your intention sincere**, knowing the Healer is Allah.
2. **Recite Al-Fatihah** seven times or as you can.
3. **Recite Ayat al-Kursi and the Mu'awwidhat**.
4. **Recite whatever you wish from the Quran**, including the verses of healing if you like.
5. **Blow lightly**, or wipe with your hand over the place of pain.
6. **Supplicate** with the prophetic supplications.
7. **Repeat** as needed.

## Writing Verses and Drinking the Water
Scholars differed about writing verses in a bowl, washing them and drinking the water:
- **Permitted** by some of the early scholars; it is reported from Ibn 'Abbas, narrated from Imam Ahmad, and permitted by Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim.
- **Disliked** by others because it is not authentically reported from the Prophet ﷺ.

There is room in the matter, but it is better to keep to recitation and blowing, which are established in the Sunnah.

## The Quran and Medicine
It is a serious mistake to leave medical treatment and rely only on ruqyah. The Prophet ﷺ said, as in Sahih Muslim: "For every disease there is a cure; when the cure matches the disease, it is cured by Allah's permission." And at-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud reported: "Seek treatment, for Allah has not placed a disease without placing a cure for it, except one disease: old age."

A Muslim combines both: **ruqyah**, seeking cure from Allah, and **medical treatment**, taking the means that the Shari'ah commands. There is no conflict between them; in both, the Healer is Allah.

## A Closer Look at the Verses
### "And heal the breasts of a believing people"
A psychological and social healing: believers who were wronged find in Allah's support and the establishment of justice a healing for their hearts. A lesson for the oppressed: Allah heals the heart, even after a while.

### "A healing for what is in the breasts"
The Quran heals doubt with evidence, ignorance with knowledge, forbidden desires with reminders of the Hereafter, sadness and anxiety with remembrance of Allah, and envy with contentment.

### "In it is healing for people"
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, a man complained that his brother had stomach trouble, and the Prophet ﷺ told him to give him honey. He returned saying it had not helped, several times, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah has spoken the truth and your brother's stomach has lied." He gave him honey again and he recovered. Healing may need patience and repetition.

### "We send down of the Quran that which is healing"
Some commentators said that "of" here explains the kind rather than indicating a part: the whole Quran is healing and mercy, not only some verses.

### "And when I am ill, it is He who cures me"
Ibrahim attributed illness to himself and cure to Allah, though everything is by Allah's decree; a lesson in courtesy towards Allah.

### "Guidance and healing for those who believe"
Healing is tied to faith: the stronger the reciter's faith, the greater the benefit.

## Certainty in Ruqyah
Scholars mentioned that benefit from ruqyah depends on two things: **the certainty and faith of the one reciting**, and **the receptiveness of the patient**. Ibn al-Qayyim said, in meaning, that ruqyah with Al-Fatihah and similar is among the greatest remedies, but it requires a receptive heart and a strong, sincere reciter.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Relying on ruqyah and leaving the doctor**.
2. **Believing these six verses have a fixed special status** above the rest of the Quran.
3. **Hanging the verses as amulets**; scholars differ on Quranic amulets, and it is safer to avoid them.
4. **Going to charlatans** who mix Quran with talismans.
5. **Reciting without reflection**, as if they were magic words.
6. **Losing hope** if recovery is slow.

## Self-Ruqyah Is the Sunnah
Many people look for someone else to recite over them, but the Sunnah is to perform ruqyah for yourself and your family. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ recited the Mu'awwidhat over himself. And in describing the seventy thousand who will enter Paradise without reckoning, he said they are those who "do not ask for ruqyah", meaning they do not seek it from others. So it is best to recite over yourself and your family, though there is no harm in asking a trustworthy person when needed.

## Beware of Charlatans
Some fraudsters exploit the verses of healing to deceive the sick, mixing them with talismans, charging large sums and claiming knowledge of the unseen. Warning signs include asking for the mother's name, requesting items belonging to the patient, writing strings of letters and numbers, muttering unintelligible words, and claiming to know who caused an illness. Run from anyone like this. The Prophet ﷺ said, as reported by Ahmad and the authors of the Sunan: "Whoever goes to a soothsayer or fortune-teller and believes what he says has disbelieved in what was revealed to Muhammad."

## A Simple Home Routine
- **Every night**: recite the Mu'awwidhat over yourself and your family, blow and wipe.
- **When someone is ill**: recite Al-Fatihah seven times, Ayat al-Kursi, the Mu'awwidhat and whatever else you wish, including the verses of healing, and make the prophetic supplications.
- **Alongside this**: see the doctor and follow the treatment.
- **Always**: be certain that the Healer is Allah, be patient, and be content with His decree.

## Visiting the Sick
In Sahih al-Bukhari, when the Prophet ﷺ visited a sick person he would say: "No harm; it is a purification, if Allah wills." Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi reported that he said: "Whoever visits a sick person whose time has not come and says seven times in his presence, 'I ask Allah the Mighty, Lord of the Mighty Throne, to cure you,' Allah will cure him of that illness." At-Tirmidhi graded it hasan.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What are the six verses of healing?
At-Tawbah 14, Yunus 57, An-Nahl 69, Al-Isra' 82, Ash-Shu'ara' 80 and Fussilat 44.

### Did the Prophet ﷺ single them out?
No. They were grouped by some scholars because they share the word "healing".

### Can I recite them over a sick person?
Yes; they are Quran. It is better to begin with Al-Fatihah, the Mu'awwidhat and the authentic supplications.

### Is the Quran a healing for the body?
Many commentators said it heals hearts and bodies, together with medical treatment.

### Should I stop medicine and use only ruqyah?
No. The Prophet ﷺ commanded seeking treatment.

### Is writing verses and drinking the water allowed?
Some early scholars permitted it and others disliked it; reciting and blowing is better.

### What do I say when visiting a sick person?
"No harm; it is a purification, if Allah wills," and "As'alullahal-'Azim, Rabbal-'Arshil-'Azim, an yashfiyak" seven times.

### Should I recite over myself or ask someone else?
The Sunnah is to recite over yourself and your family; asking a trustworthy person when needed is permitted.

## Conclusion
The "verses of healing" are six Quranic verses that mention healing, grouped by some scholars; the Sunnah does not single them out, but reciting them is permitted because they are Allah's words. Begin with what is authentic, Al-Fatihah, the Mu'awwidhat, Ayat al-Kursi and the prophetic supplications, with certainty that the Healer is Allah, and take medical treatment too. The Quran is a healing for what is in the hearts and a mercy for the believers, and medicine is a means Allah has permitted. They do not conflict.
