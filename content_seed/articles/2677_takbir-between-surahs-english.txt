If you have ever listened to a Quran completion (*khatm*) recited in the Haram in Makkah, or to a recording by certain teachers of the recitations, you may have noticed something unusual. When the reciter finishes Surah Ad-Duha, he says "Allahu akbar", then the Basmalah, then begins Surah Ash-Sharh. After Ash-Sharh he says "Allahu akbar" again, and so on, until he reaches Surah An-Nas. Listeners naturally ask: what is this takbir? Is it a Sunnah? Why do we not hear it in every recitation? And may I say it if I recite in the narration of Hafs?

This takbir is a topic within the science of the recitations (*qira'at*). It has an origin, a set wording and detailed rules, and the people of Makkah cherished it so much that it became one of the distinctive features of completing the Quran there. This article explains it clearly, separating what is established from what is not.

## What Is the Takbir Between the Surahs?
In the terminology of the reciters, the takbir is **saying "Allahu akbar" between the surahs at the end of the Quran**, starting at the end of Surah Ad-Duha or the beginning of Surah Ash-Sharh, and ending at the beginning or the end of Surah An-Nas, according to the positions described below.

It is connected originally to the recitation of **'Abdullah ibn Kathir al-Makki**, one of the seven famous reciters, and in particular to the narration of his student **al-Bazzi**. From there it spread to other transmission routes.

The takbir is **not part of the Quran**, by consensus. It is not written in the Mushaf, and it does not take the rulings of the Quran. It is a form of remembrance said when moving between surahs in specific places, transmitted by the reciters.

## The Origin and Its Chain
The takbir is narrated through al-Bazzi, from 'Ikrimah ibn Sulayman, from Isma'il ibn Qustantin and Shibl ibn 'Abbad, from Ibn Kathir, from Mujahid, from Ibn 'Abbas, from Ubayy ibn Ka'b, from the Prophet ﷺ, who instructed him to say it. Al-Hakim reported a hadith with this chain in al-Mustadrak and graded it authentic.

### How the hadith scholars graded it
The hadith scholars criticised this report, because it depends entirely on **al-Bazzi**. Al-Bazzi was a trustworthy master of recitation, but in hadith narration he was considered weak: Abu Hatim ar-Razi weakened him, al-'Uqayli described his hadith as *munkar*, and adh-Dhahabi objected to al-Hakim's grading. So the hadith is **weak according to the hadith scholars** as a narration from the Prophet ﷺ.

### Why reciters still use it
Here we must distinguish two things:
- **The hadith as a hadith**, used as evidence for rulings: it is weak according to the hadith scholars.
- **The takbir as a way of reciting**, handed down among the reciters of Makkah generation after generation: established among the reciters and adopted by the imams of recitation.

This is why scholars of the recitations describe the takbir as a Sunnah among the reciters in al-Bazzi's narration, to be practised in recitation, and not a blameworthy innovation, even though the hadith attributed to the Prophet ﷺ does not meet the hadith scholars' conditions. A statement is also attributed to al-Shafi'i telling al-Bazzi that abandoning the takbir means abandoning a Sunnah, but the authenticity of that report is also disputed.

## The Popular Story About Its Cause
Many people mention a story: revelation paused for some days, the polytheists said that the Prophet's Lord had abandoned him, Surah Ad-Duha was revealed, and the Prophet ﷺ said "Allahu akbar" out of joy, so the takbir became a practice from Ad-Duha onward.

The pause in revelation and the revelation of Ad-Duha are **established** in al-Bukhari and Muslim from the hadith of Jundub ibn Sufyan. But **linking the takbir to this event has no authentic chain**. So we should not state as fact that this was the reason for the takbir.

﴿وَٱلضُّحَىٰ﴾ ﴿وَٱلَّيۡلِ إِذَا سَجَىٰ﴾ ﴿مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ وَمَا قَلَىٰ﴾ (Quran 93:1-3)

## The Wording of the Takbir
Reciters transmit two well-known forms:
1. **"Allahu akbar"** alone. This is the original and most famous form.
2. **"La ilaha illallahu wallahu akbar"**, adding the declaration of oneness before it. This is also transmitted through sound routes from al-Bazzi.

Some routes add **"wa lillahil-hamd"** after it, making it "La ilaha illallahu wallahu akbar wa lillahil-hamd", but the more common practice is to stop before that addition.

A reciter keeps to the form he received from his teacher and does not mix forms from different routes in one recitation.

## Where Does It Start and End?
### The start
There are two main positions among the masters of recitation:
1. **From the end of Surah Ad-Duha**: the reciter says the takbir after "wa amma bi ni'mati rabbika fa haddith", then says the Basmalah and begins Ash-Sharh.
2. **From the beginning of Surah Ash-Sharh**: in practice it sounds similar, but the takbir is considered to belong to the opening of the next surah rather than the close of the previous one.

Some also mention starting from the end of Surah Al-Layl, that is, before Ad-Duha, but the first two are the best known.

﴿وَأَمَّا بِنِعۡمَةِ رَبِّكَ فَحَدِّثۡ﴾ (Quran 93:11)

### The end
1. **The beginning of Surah An-Nas**, for those who attach the takbir to the start of each surah.
2. **The end of Surah An-Nas**, for those who attach the takbir to the end of each surah, so that one says the takbir after finishing the whole Quran.

The rule among the masters is consistency: **whoever attaches the takbir to the end of a surah** starts after Ad-Duha and ends after An-Nas; **whoever attaches it to the beginning of a surah** starts before Ash-Sharh and ends before An-Nas.

## How the Takbir Joins the Basmalah and the Surahs
Between two surahs in this section there are four parts: **the end of the surah, the takbir, the Basmalah, and the start of the next surah**. A reciter may stop or join between them in several established ways. The best known are:
1. **Stopping after each**: stop at the end of the surah, say the takbir and stop, say the Basmalah and stop, then begin the surah.
2. **Stopping after the surah, then joining the rest**: stop at the end of the surah, then join the takbir to the Basmalah to the start of the next surah.
3. **Stopping after the surah and after the takbir, then joining the Basmalah to the surah.**
4. **Joining everything**: the end of the surah, the takbir, the Basmalah and the start of the next surah in one breath.
5. **Joining the end of the surah to the takbir**, then stopping, then the Basmalah joined to or separated from the next surah.

**The forbidden way**: joining the end of the surah to the takbir and the Basmalah, then stopping on the Basmalah. This gives the impression that the Basmalah belongs to the previous surah, when it actually belongs to the next one.

### Sound changes when joining
When the end of a surah is joined to "Allahu akbar":
- If the surah ends in a letter with sukun or tanwin, that sound takes a **kasrah** to avoid two consecutive silent letters, as in *fa haddith-illahu akbar*.
- If the surah ends with a vowel, the connecting hamzah of "Allah" drops and the vowel flows into it.
- If the surah ends with a pronoun Ha, as in *khayran yarah* and *sharran yarah* at the end of Az-Zalzalah, the lengthening of that Ha is dropped when joining to the takbir.

These are fine details that are learned by hearing them from a teacher.

## A Worked Example: From Ad-Duha to Ash-Sharh
Using the form "Allahu akbar" only:
- **Stopping after each**: "...fa haddith" (stop), "Allahu akbar" (stop), "Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahim" (stop), "Alam nashrah laka sadrak".
- **Joining everything**: "...fa haddith-illahu akbaru bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahimi alam nashrah laka sadrak". Notice the kasrah on the *tha* of *haddith* and the dropped hamzah of "Allah".
- **Stopping after the surah, joining the rest**: "...fa haddith" (stop), "Allahu akbaru bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahimi alam nashrah".

Notice also the **Lam in the name "Allah"**. When you join after *haddith-i*, the Lam follows a kasrah, so it is pronounced **light**. When you start "Allahu akbar" fresh after a stop, the connecting hamzah is pronounced with a fathah, so the Lam is pronounced **heavy**. Careful reciters pay attention to this subtle change.

## Another Example: From Az-Zalzalah to Al-'Adiyat
"Fa man ya'mal mithqala dharratin sharran yarah", then the takbir:
- **Joining**: "sharran yarahu-llahu akbar". The lengthening of the Ha is dropped because a silent Lam follows it.
- **Stopping**: "yarah" with sukun on the Ha, then "Allahu akbar".

This shows that the takbir changes some sound rules between surahs, which is why it must be learned from a teacher.

﴿فَمَن يَعۡمَلۡ مِثۡقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيۡرٗا يَرَهُۥ﴾ ﴿وَمَن يَعۡمَلۡ مِثۡقَالَ ذَرَّةٖ شَرّٗا يَرَهُۥ﴾ (Quran 99:7-8)

## Should a Hafs Reciter Say the Takbir?
This is the most practical question, and the answer has detail:
- **Through the Shatibiyyah route**, which is how Hafs is recited in most of the Muslim world: the takbir belongs to al-Bazzi's narration of Ibn Kathir, and Hafs does **not** say it.
- **Through the Tayyibah route**: Imam Ibn al-Jazari mentioned in *an-Nashr fi al-Qira'at al-'Ashr* that the takbir was also narrated from other reciters through some routes, and that it became the practice of many people in different lands when completing the Quran. So some teachers teach the takbir for Hafs through the Tayyibah.
- **When completing the Quran**: many people, especially in the two Holy Mosques, say the takbir from Ad-Duha when completing the Quran even if they recite in another narration, following what Ibn al-Jazari mentioned about its recommendation at the khatm.

**Practical conclusion**: if you recite Hafs through the Shatibiyyah in your normal recitation, do not add the takbir. If you wish to say it when completing the Quran, there is no harm, in sha' Allah, but it is best to learn it from a teacher who teaches it, so you know its wording and how to join it.

## Takbir in Prayer
The takbir between surahs belongs originally to recitation outside prayer. Inside prayer, it was not known from the imams to say the takbir between surahs in Taraweeh at the completion, except among some of the people of Makkah. It is better to leave it in prayer, because the form of prayer is fixed by revelation, and so that the takbir of recitation is not confused with the takbir of moving into bowing. Otherwise the people behind the imam may think he is about to bow.

## Who Was al-Bazzi?
He was **Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn 'Abdillah ibn Abi Bazzah al-Makki**, the master reciter of Makkah and the caller to prayer in the Sacred Mosque in his time, and one of the two narrators of Ibn Kathir's reading; the other is **Qunbul**. He lived in the third century after the Hijrah and was a precise authority in recitation, and leadership in teaching recitation in Makkah came to him. Because of his standing in recitation, reciters relied on his transmission of the takbir, even though hadith scholars criticised his hadith narration. Mastery in one science does not require mastery in another.

## The Difference Between the Takbir and the Basmalah
The Basmalah between surahs is **part of the narration**: Hafs recites it between every two surahs except between Al-Anfal and At-Tawbah, and it is written in the Mushaf. The takbir is an **additional remembrance** not written in the Mushaf, and by consensus it is not part of the Quran. That is why it does not appear in printed copies and does not take the rulings of the Quran.

## The Wisdom Behind It
Scholars have suggested several meanings for the takbir in this place:
1. **Glorifying Allah as His Book draws to a close**: the reciter approaches the end of his journey with the Quran and magnifies Allah in gratitude.
2. **Joy at completing the Quran**: the takbir is a sign of celebration, as on the days of 'Eid.
3. **Drawing attention to the short surahs**, which people recite most often.

These are reflections the scholars drew out; acts of worship are based on following, not on reasons we derive.

## Advice for Those Who Want to Practise It
1. **Learn it from a teacher**: the ways of joining and stopping need to be heard.
2. **Keep to one form**: do not mix wordings from different routes in one completion.
3. **Do not criticise those who leave it**: the takbir is a Sunnah among reciters through particular routes, and leaving it is not wrong.
4. **Do not criticise those who say it**: it is transmitted from the imams of recitation and is not an innovation as some people assume.
5. **In family completions**: reciting the last surahs with the takbir can be a beautiful moment in which children learn to glorify Allah and feel the joy of completing His Book.

## The Takbir in Completions Today
In many mosques the Quran is completed in Ramadan, and on the night of the completion the last surahs are recited. Some imams say the takbir from Ad-Duha if they follow that practice, then make the du'a of completion. Some parents complete the Quran at home with their children using the takbir, creating a lasting memory. What matters is to understand the takbir as it really is: a Sunnah of the reciters, not an obligation whose absence is criticised, and not an innovation whose presence is condemned.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What is the takbir from Ad-Duha?
Saying "Allahu akbar" between the surahs from the end of Ad-Duha or the start of Ash-Sharh to the end of An-Nas, as transmitted in Ibn Kathir's reading through al-Bazzi.

### Is the hadith about the takbir authentic?
Al-Hakim graded it authentic, but most hadith scholars consider it weak because it depends on al-Bazzi; the takbir is nevertheless established among the reciters as a way of reciting.

### What is the wording?
"Allahu akbar", or "La ilaha illallahu wallahu akbar"; some add "wa lillahil-hamd".

### Does a Hafs reciter say the takbir?
Not through the Shatibiyyah route; some teachers teach it for Hafs through the Tayyibah, and many say it when completing the Quran.

### Is the story of the pause in revelation the reason for the takbir?
The pause and the revelation of Ad-Duha are authentic, but linking the takbir to it has no authentic chain.

### Should the imam say it between surahs in Taraweeh?
It is better to leave it in prayer, so it is not confused with the takbir of bowing.

### What is the forbidden way of joining?
Joining the end of the surah with the takbir and the Basmalah, then stopping on the Basmalah.

### Is the takbir part of the Quran?
No. It is a remembrance said between surahs and is not part of the Quran by consensus.

### Is it sinful to leave the takbir at a completion?
No. It is a Sunnah among reciters through specific routes, and there is no sin in leaving it.

## Conclusion
The takbir from Ad-Duha to An-Nas is a Sunnah among the masters of recitation in Ibn Kathir's reading through al-Bazzi, received by the people of Makkah generation after generation, even though the hadith scholars criticised the hadith attributed to the Prophet ﷺ about it. Its wording is "Allahu akbar", or with the declaration of oneness, and it has established ways of joining and stopping that are learned from teachers. Hafs through the Shatibiyyah does not include it, while many say it at the completion of the Quran. If you adopt it, learn it from a teacher; if you leave it, you have left nothing obligatory.
