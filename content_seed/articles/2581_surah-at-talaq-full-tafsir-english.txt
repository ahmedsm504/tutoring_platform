Surah At-Talaq is a surah of law about one of the hardest moments in family life: separation. Yet it does not read like a dry legal code. Its twelve verses are filled with taqwa, trust in Allah and promises of relief. Three times it repeats the promise of taqwa: "Whoever fears Allah, He will make for him a way out", "Whoever fears Allah, He will make his matter easy for him", and "Whoever fears Allah, He will remove his misdeeds". The surah teaches that if separation happens, it must happen with fear of Allah, and that whoever fears Allah in the hardest situations will find the doors of relief opened.

This guide explains Surah At-Talaq verse by verse in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons. It is a general explanation; specific cases of divorce should be referred to qualified scholars.

## Quick Facts About Surah At-Talaq
- **Number of verses**: 12.
- **Type**: Medinan.
- **Position**: Surah 65, in the twenty-eighth juz.
- **Name**: At-Talaq means "divorce".
- **Another name**: some early scholars called it "the shorter Surah An-Nisa'".
- **Main themes**: the rulings of divorce and the waiting period ('iddah), housing and maintenance, and the promises of taqwa and tawakkul.

## The Context of Revelation
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar divorced his wife while she was menstruating. 'Umar asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about it, and he said: "Tell him to take her back, then keep her until she becomes pure, then menstruates, then becomes pure. Then, if he wishes, he may keep her, or if he wishes, divorce her before touching her. That is the waiting period for which Allah has commanded that women be divorced." In one narration in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ recited: "O Prophet, when you divorce women, divorce them at the start of their waiting period."

## Verse 1: Divorce at the Right Time
﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّبِيُّ إِذَا طَلَّقۡتُمُ ٱلنِّسَآءَ فَطَلِّقُوهُنَّ لِعِدَّتِهِنَّ وَأَحۡصُواْ ٱلۡعِدَّةَۖ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ رَبَّكُمۡۖ لَا تُخۡرِجُوهُنَّ مِنۢ بُيُوتِهِنَّ وَلَا يَخۡرُجۡنَ إِلَّآ أَن يَأۡتِينَ بِفَٰحِشَةٖ مُّبَيِّنَةٖۚ وَتِلۡكَ حُدُودُ ٱللَّهِۚ وَمَن يَتَعَدَّ حُدُودَ ٱللَّهِ فَقَدۡ ظَلَمَ نَفۡسَهُۥۚ لَا تَدۡرِي لَعَلَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحۡدِثُ بَعۡدَ ذَٰلِكَ أَمۡرٗا﴾ (Quran 65:1)

**Addressed to the Prophet ﷺ, meant for all**: "O Prophet, when you (plural) divorce women".

**"Divorce them for their waiting period"**: divorce them at a time when they can begin the waiting period directly, which is a state of purity in which there has been no intercourse. This is the divorce according to the Sunnah. Divorce during menstruation, or in a state of purity in which intercourse has taken place, is the innovated form that is forbidden.

**"And keep count of the waiting period"**: because many rulings depend on it: the right to take back, maintenance, housing and inheritance.

**"Do not turn them out of their houses, nor should they leave"**: a woman in a revocable divorce stays in the marital home during the waiting period, unless she commits a clear immorality. **Notice "their houses"**: the homes are attributed to the women, though owned by the husbands, to affirm their right to stay.

**"You do not know; perhaps Allah will bring about after that a matter"**: the wisdom of her staying: hearts may change, the husband may regret and take her back. The Shari'ah keeps the door of return open.

## Verses 2–3: Keep or Part on Good Terms
﴿فَإِذَا بَلَغۡنَ أَجَلَهُنَّ فَأَمۡسِكُوهُنَّ بِمَعۡرُوفٍ أَوۡ فَارِقُوهُنَّ بِمَعۡرُوفٖ وَأَشۡهِدُواْ ذَوَيۡ عَدۡلٖ مِّنكُمۡ وَأَقِيمُواْ ٱلشَّهَٰدَةَ لِلَّهِۚ ذَٰلِكُمۡ يُوعَظُ بِهِۦ مَن كَانَ يُؤۡمِنُ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡيَوۡمِ ٱلۡأٓخِرِۚ وَمَن يَتَّقِ ٱللَّهَ يَجۡعَل لَّهُۥ مَخۡرَجٗا﴾ ﴿وَيَرۡزُقۡهُ مِنۡ حَيۡثُ لَا يَحۡتَسِبُۚ وَمَن يَتَوَكَّلۡ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ فَهُوَ حَسۡبُهُۥٓۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ بَٰلِغُ أَمۡرِهِۦۚ قَدۡ جَعَلَ ٱللَّهُ لِكُلِّ شَيۡءٖ قَدۡرٗا﴾ (Quran 65:2-3)

**"When they have nearly fulfilled their term"**, the husband has two options: **keep them in kindness or part with them in kindness**. No keeping to harm, and no parting with insults.

**"And bring to witness two just men from among you"**: witnessing protects rights and prevents disputes.

**"And whoever fears Allah, He will make for him a way out, and will provide for him from where he does not expect."** Revealed in the context of divorce, yet its meaning is general: anyone who fears Allah in any difficulty will find a way out and provision from unexpected places.

**"And whoever relies upon Allah, then He is sufficient for him."** At-Tirmidhi reported from 'Umar that the Prophet ﷺ said: "If you were to rely on Allah as He should be relied upon, He would provide for you as He provides for the birds: they go out hungry in the morning and return full in the evening." At-Tirmidhi graded it *hasan sahih*.

**"Indeed, Allah will accomplish His purpose. Allah has already set for everything a measure."**

**Why here?** Divorce is a moment of fear for both parties. The woman fears poverty and loneliness; the man fears the costs and the future. So the promise came: fear Allah in this moment, and the way out and provision are on Him.

## Verse 4: The Waiting Periods
﴿وَٱلَّـٰٓـِٔي يَئِسۡنَ مِنَ ٱلۡمَحِيضِ مِن نِّسَآئِكُمۡ إِنِ ٱرۡتَبۡتُمۡ فَعِدَّتُهُنَّ ثَلَٰثَةُ أَشۡهُرٖ وَٱلَّـٰٓـِٔي لَمۡ يَحِضۡنَۚ وَأُوْلَٰتُ ٱلۡأَحۡمَالِ أَجَلُهُنَّ أَن يَضَعۡنَ حَمۡلَهُنَّۚ وَمَن يَتَّقِ ٱللَّهَ يَجۡعَل لَّهُۥ مِنۡ أَمۡرِهِۦ يُسۡرٗا﴾ (Quran 65:4)

**Three cases**:
1. **Women past menstruation due to age**: three months.
2. **Those who have not menstruated**: three months as well.
3. **Pregnant women**: until they deliver, whether the period is long or short.

**The pregnant widow**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Subay'ah al-Aslamiyyah's husband died while she was pregnant, and she gave birth a few nights later. The Prophet ﷺ ruled that she had completed her waiting period when she delivered. The words "and those who are pregnant, their term is until they give birth" include both the divorced and the widowed.

**"And whoever fears Allah, He will make his matter easy for him."** The second promise.

## Verse 5: The Third Promise
﴿ذَٰلِكَ أَمۡرُ ٱللَّهِ أَنزَلَهُۥٓ إِلَيۡكُمۡۚ وَمَن يَتَّقِ ٱللَّهَ يُكَفِّرۡ عَنۡهُ سَيِّـَٔاتِهِۦ وَيُعۡظِمۡ لَهُۥٓ أَجۡرًا﴾ (Quran 65:5)

**"Whoever fears Allah, He will remove for him his misdeeds and make great for him his reward."** Three promises of taqwa in one short legal surah: a way out and provision, ease, and forgiveness with great reward.

## Verses 6–7: Housing, Maintenance and Nursing
﴿أَسۡكِنُوهُنَّ مِنۡ حَيۡثُ سَكَنتُم مِّن وُجۡدِكُمۡ وَلَا تُضَآرُّوهُنَّ لِتُضَيِّقُواْ عَلَيۡهِنَّۚ وَإِن كُنَّ أُوْلَٰتِ حَمۡلٖ فَأَنفِقُواْ عَلَيۡهِنَّ حَتَّىٰ يَضَعۡنَ حَمۡلَهُنَّۚ فَإِنۡ أَرۡضَعۡنَ لَكُمۡ فَـَٔاتُوهُنَّ أُجُورَهُنَّ وَأۡتَمِرُواْ بَيۡنَكُم بِمَعۡرُوفٖۖ وَإِن تَعَاسَرۡتُمۡ فَسَتُرۡضِعُ لَهُۥٓ أُخۡرَىٰ﴾ ﴿لِيُنفِقۡ ذُو سَعَةٖ مِّن سَعَتِهِۦۖ وَمَن قُدِرَ عَلَيۡهِ رِزۡقُهُۥ فَلۡيُنفِقۡ مِمَّآ ءَاتَىٰهُ ٱللَّهُۚ لَا يُكَلِّفُ ٱللَّهُ نَفۡسًا إِلَّا مَآ ءَاتَىٰهَاۚ سَيَجۡعَلُ ٱللَّهُ بَعۡدَ عُسۡرٖ يُسۡرٗا﴾ (Quran 65:6-7)

**Housing**: **"Lodge them where you dwell, according to your means, and do not harm them in order to oppress them."** Do not make life so difficult that she is forced out.

**The pregnant woman**: **"If they are pregnant, spend on them until they give birth."**

**Nursing**: if she breastfeeds the child, she is entitled to payment. **"And confer among yourselves in the acceptable way"**: discuss the child's affairs kindly. **"But if you are in discord, then another woman may breastfeed for him."**

**"Let a man of wealth spend from his wealth, and he whose provision is restricted, let him spend from what Allah has given him."** **"Allah does not charge a soul except according to what He has given it."** **"Allah will bring about, after hardship, ease."**

## Verses 8–11: Nations That Rebelled
﴿وَكَأَيِّن مِّن قَرۡيَةٍ عَتَتۡ عَنۡ أَمۡرِ رَبِّهَا وَرُسُلِهِۦ فَحَاسَبۡنَٰهَا حِسَابٗا شَدِيدٗا وَعَذَّبۡنَٰهَا عَذَابٗا نُّكۡرٗا﴾ ﴿فَذَاقَتۡ وَبَالَ أَمۡرِهَا وَكَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ أَمۡرِهَا خُسۡرًا﴾ ﴿أَعَدَّ ٱللَّهُ لَهُمۡ عَذَابٗا شَدِيدٗاۖ فَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ يَـٰٓأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَلۡبَٰبِ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْۚ قَدۡ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ إِلَيۡكُمۡ ذِكۡرٗا﴾ ﴿رَّسُولٗا يَتۡلُواْ عَلَيۡكُمۡ ءَايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ مُبَيِّنَٰتٖ لِّيُخۡرِجَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ مِنَ ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ إِلَى ٱلنُّورِۚ وَمَن يُؤۡمِنۢ بِٱللَّهِ وَيَعۡمَلۡ صَٰلِحٗا يُدۡخِلۡهُ جَنَّـٰتٖ تَجۡرِي مِن تَحۡتِهَا ٱلۡأَنۡهَٰرُ خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَآ أَبَدٗاۖ قَدۡ أَحۡسَنَ ٱللَّهُ لَهُۥ رِزۡقًا﴾ (Quran 65:8-11)

**After the laws, a warning**: how many towns rebelled against the command of their Lord and His messengers, and were held to severe account. **The connection**: whoever transgresses Allah's limits in divorce, waiting periods and maintenance should fear what befell them.

**"A Messenger reciting to you the distinct verses of Allah, that he may bring out those who believe and do righteous deeds from darknesses into the light."**

## Verse 12: Seven Heavens and of the Earth the Like
﴿ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبۡعَ سَمَٰوَٰتٖ وَمِنَ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ مِثۡلَهُنَّۖ يَتَنَزَّلُ ٱلۡأَمۡرُ بَيۡنَهُنَّ لِتَعۡلَمُوٓاْ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيۡءٖ قَدِيرࣱ وَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ قَدۡ أَحَاطَ بِكُلِّ شَيۡءٍ عِلۡمَۢا﴾ (Quran 65:12)

**"It is Allah who has created seven heavens and of the earth the like of them. The command descends among them."** **The purpose**: **"that you may know that Allah is over all things competent and that Allah has encompassed all things in knowledge."** Whoever knows this will fear Allah in His rulings.

In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Sa'id ibn Zayd reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever wrongfully takes a hand-span of land, it will be hung around his neck from seven earths."

## Word Meanings

- **'Iddah**: waiting period.
- **Ahsu**: keep count.
- **Fahishah mubayyinah**: clear immorality.
- **Makhraj**: way out.
- **Hasbuh**: sufficient for him.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"O Prophet, when you divorce"**: honouring the Prophet ﷺ, then addressing all.
2. **"Their houses"**: attributing homes to the women to affirm their right.
3. **"You do not know; perhaps Allah will bring about a matter"**: a gentle psychological insight.
4. **Three promises of taqwa** in a legal surah.
5. **"Allah will bring about, after hardship, ease"**: certainty in a few words.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd muttasil**: in *an-nisa'a* (verse 1).
- **Idgham with ghunnah**: in *makhrajan wa yarzuqhu* (verses 2–3, when joined), the tanween merges into the waw.
- **Ghunnah**: in *inna Allaha balighu amrih* (verse 3), on the noon with shaddah.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *yaj'al* (verses 2 and 4), the jim sakinah bounces lightly.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah At-Taghabun before it ended with family trials and "fear Allah as much as you are able", and At-Talaq regulates separation with taqwa. Surah At-Tahrim after it deals with an incident inside the Prophet's ﷺ own household. The three surahs together address the family.

## Islam's Balanced Approach to Divorce
1. **Divorce is permitted, but a last resort** after attempts at reconciliation.
2. **It has a specific time**: a state of purity without intercourse.
3. **The waiting period is a chance to reconcile.**
4. **The divorced woman has rights**: housing and maintenance where applicable.
5. **Parting must be in kindness**: no humiliation or harm.
6. **The child's rights are protected**: nursing and maintenance.
7. **Taqwa is present in every ruling.**

## Etiquette of Marital Disputes
1. **Preserve dignity**: no insults or exposure.
2. **Seek reconciliation until the last moment.**
3. **Do no harm**: "do not harm them in order to oppress them".
4. **Consult kindly about the children.**
5. **Be fair financially.**
6. **Keep family secrets**, even after separation.
7. **Remember the good**: "And do not forget graciousness between you" (Surah Al-Baqarah).

## Practical Lessons
1. **Do not rush into divorce.**
2. **If it happens, follow the Sunnah way.**
3. **Do not expel a woman in revocable divorce from her home.**
4. **Part with kindness.**
5. **Spend according to your means.**
6. **Protect the children's rights.**
7. **Fear Allah in every affair**: the way out will come.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What is the context of Surah At-Talaq's revelation?
Ibn 'Umar divorced his wife during menstruation, and the Prophet ﷺ told him to take her back and explained the correct time for divorce, as reported in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### What does "divorce them for their waiting period" mean?
Divorce them in a state of purity without intercourse, so they can begin the waiting period directly.

### Should a woman in revocable divorce leave her husband's house?
No. She stays in the marital home during the waiting period, unless she commits clear immorality.

### What is the waiting period of a pregnant woman?
Until she gives birth, whether divorced or widowed, as in the case of Subay'ah al-Aslamiyyah in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### What does "He will provide for him from where he does not expect" mean?
Allah will send provision through routes the person never imagined.

### What is the general lesson of verses 2–3?
Whoever fears Allah in any difficulty will find a way out, and whoever relies on Allah will find Him sufficient.

### How many verses are in Surah At-Talaq?
12 verses, and it is a Medinan surah.

## Conclusion
Surah At-Talaq teaches that taqwa does not disappear even at the moment of separation. Its laws protect rights and keep the door of return open, and its promises reach every believer in every hardship: whoever fears Allah, He will make for him a way out and provide for him from where he does not expect, and whoever relies on Allah, He is sufficient for him.
