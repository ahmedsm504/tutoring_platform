Surah Al-Fajr opens with the dawn and ends with the tranquil soul invited into Paradise. Between them it walks through the ruins of three great powers, 'Ad with its lofty pillars, Thamud who carved the rocks, and Pharaoh of the stakes, all brought down because "your Lord is ever watchful". Then it turns to the human heart and exposes a common error: thinking that wealth means Allah has honoured you and poverty means He has humiliated you. It names the real failures: not honouring the orphan, not urging the feeding of the poor, devouring inheritance and loving wealth with immense love. And it ends with one of the most beautiful calls in the Quran: "O tranquil soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing."

This guide explains Surah Al-Fajr verse by verse in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Fajr
- **Number of verses**: 30.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 89, in the thirtieth juz (Juz 'Amma).
- **Name**: Al-Fajr means "the dawn".
- **Main themes**: Allah's watchfulness, the end of tyrants, the true measure of honour, care for the weak, the Day of Judgement and the tranquil soul.

## Verses 1–5: The Oaths
﴿وَٱلۡفَجۡرِ﴾ (Quran 89:1)

**"By the dawn."** The moment light breaks through darkness.

﴿وَلَيَالٍ عَشۡرٖ﴾ (Quran 89:2)

**"And by the ten nights."** Many commentators, including Ibn 'Abbas, said these are the first ten nights of Dhul-Hijjah. In Sahih al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ said: "There are no days in which righteous deeds are more beloved to Allah than these days," meaning the ten days of Dhul-Hijjah. They asked: "Not even jihad in the way of Allah?" He said: "Not even jihad, except a man who goes out with his life and wealth and returns with nothing."

﴿وَٱلشَّفۡعِ وَٱلۡوَتۡرِ﴾ (Quran 89:3)

**"And by the even and the odd."** Commentators gave several explanations: all of creation, which is in pairs, and the Creator, who is One; or the Day of Sacrifice and the Day of 'Arafah.

﴿وَٱلَّيۡلِ إِذَا يَسۡرِ﴾ (Quran 89:4)

**"And by the night when it passes."**

﴿هَلۡ فِي ذَٰلِكَ قَسَمࣱ لِّذِي حِجۡرٍ﴾ (Quran 89:5)

**"Is there in that an oath sufficient for one of intellect?"** The Arabic word for intellect here is *hijr*, from the root meaning to restrain: true intelligence restrains its owner from what harms him.

## Verses 6–14: Three Fallen Powers
﴿أَلَمۡ تَرَ كَيۡفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِعَادٍ﴾ ﴿إِرَمَ ذَاتِ ٱلۡعِمَادِ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّتِي لَمۡ يُخۡلَقۡ مِثۡلُهَا فِي ٱلۡبِلَٰدِ﴾ (Quran 89:6-8)

**"Have you not considered how your Lord dealt with 'Ad, Iram, possessors of lofty pillars, the likes of whom had never been created in the lands?"** 'Ad were a people of great strength and towering buildings.

﴿وَثَمُودَ ٱلَّذِينَ جَابُواْ ٱلصَّخۡرَ بِٱلۡوَادِ﴾ (Quran 89:9)

**"And Thamud, who carved out the rocks in the valley."** They cut homes into the mountains.

﴿وَفِرۡعَوۡنَ ذِي ٱلۡأَوۡتَادِ﴾ (Quran 89:10)

**"And Pharaoh, owner of the stakes."** Commentators explained "the stakes" as his great structures or the stakes with which he tortured people.

﴿ٱلَّذِينَ طَغَوۡاْ فِي ٱلۡبِلَٰدِ﴾ ﴿فَأَكۡثَرُواْ فِيهَا ٱلۡفَسَادَ﴾ ﴿فَصَبَّ عَلَيۡهِمۡ رَبُّكَ سَوۡطَ عَذَابٍ﴾ (Quran 89:11-13)

**"Who transgressed within the lands and increased therein the corruption. So your Lord poured upon them a scourge of punishment."**

﴿إِنَّ رَبَّكَ لَبِٱلۡمِرۡصَادِ﴾ (Quran 89:14)

**"Indeed, your Lord is ever watchful."** *Bil-mirsad*: in the place of observation. Nothing escapes Him, and no tyrant is beyond His reach.

## Verses 15–16: The False Measure of Honour
﴿فَأَمَّا ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ إِذَا مَا ٱبۡتَلَىٰهُ رَبُّهُۥ فَأَكۡرَمَهُۥ وَنَعَّمَهُۥ فَيَقُولُ رَبِّيٓ أَكۡرَمَنِ﴾ (Quran 89:15)

**"And as for man, when his Lord tries him and is generous to him and favours him, he says: My Lord has honoured me."**

﴿وَأَمَّآ إِذَا مَا ٱبۡتَلَىٰهُ فَقَدَرَ عَلَيۡهِ رِزۡقَهُۥ فَيَقُولُ رَبِّيٓ أَهَٰنَنِ﴾ (Quran 89:16)

**"But when He tries him and restricts his provision, he says: My Lord has humiliated me."** Both are tests. Wealth is not proof of Allah's love, and poverty is not proof of His anger. In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah does not look at your appearance or your wealth, but He looks at your hearts and your deeds."

## Verses 17–20: The Real Failures
﴿كَلَّاۖ بَل لَّا تُكۡرِمُونَ ٱلۡيَتِيمَ﴾ (Quran 89:17)

**"No! But you do not honour the orphan."** The word "No!" (*kalla*) rejects their measure. The real question is not how much you have but how you treat the weak. In Sahih al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ said: "I and the one who sponsors an orphan will be in Paradise like this," and he gestured with his index and middle fingers.

﴿وَلَا تَحَـٰٓضُّونَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ ٱلۡمِسۡكِينِ﴾ (Quran 89:18)

**"And you do not encourage one another to feed the poor."** Not only failing to feed them, but failing to urge others.

﴿وَتَأۡكُلُونَ ٱلتُّرَاثَ أَكۡلٗا لَّمّٗا﴾ (Quran 89:19)

**"And you consume inheritance, devouring it altogether."** Taking the shares of women and orphans.

﴿وَتُحِبُّونَ ٱلۡمَالَ حُبّٗا جَمّٗا﴾ (Quran 89:20)

**"And you love wealth with immense love."** The root of all the above.

## Verses 21–26: The Day of Regret
﴿كَلَّآۖ إِذَا دُكَّتِ ٱلۡأَرۡضُ دَكّٗا دَكّٗا﴾ (Quran 89:21)

**"No! When the earth has been levelled, pounded and crushed."**

﴿وَجَآءَ رَبُّكَ وَٱلۡمَلَكُ صَفّٗا صَفّٗا﴾ (Quran 89:22)

**"And your Lord has come and the angels, rank upon rank."**

﴿وَجِاْيٓءَ يَوۡمَئِذِۭ بِجَهَنَّمَۚ يَوۡمَئِذٖ يَتَذَكَّرُ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ وَأَنَّىٰ لَهُ ٱلذِّكۡرَىٰ﴾ (Quran 89:23)

**"And brought, that Day, is Hell."** In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Hell will be brought on that Day with seventy thousand reins, each rein held by seventy thousand angels pulling it." **"That Day, man will remember, but what good to him will be the remembrance?"**

﴿يَقُولُ يَٰلَيۡتَنِي قَدَّمۡتُ لِحَيَاتِي﴾ (Quran 89:24)

**"He will say: Oh, I wish I had sent ahead for my life."** Notice: "for my life". The real life is the hereafter; this world was only the preparation.

﴿فَيَوۡمَئِذٖ لَّا يُعَذِّبُ عَذَابَهُۥٓ أَحَدࣱ﴾ ﴿وَلَا يُوثِقُ وَثَاقَهُۥٓ أَحَدࣱ﴾ (Quran 89:25-26)

**"So on that Day, none will punish as He punishes, and none will bind as He binds."**

## Verses 27–30: O Tranquil Soul
﴿يَـٰٓأَيَّتُهَا ٱلنَّفۡسُ ٱلۡمُطۡمَئِنَّةُ﴾ ﴿ٱرۡجِعِيٓ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكِ رَاضِيَةٗ مَّرۡضِيَّةٗ﴾ (Quran 89:27-28)

**"O tranquil soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing."** The soul at peace with Allah's decree, certain of His promise, content in remembrance. Well-pleased with Allah, and Allah pleased with it.

﴿فَٱدۡخُلِي فِي عِبَٰدِي﴾ ﴿وَٱدۡخُلِي جَنَّتِي﴾ (Quran 89:29-30)

**"And enter among My servants, and enter My Paradise."** Two honours: joining the company of Allah's servants, and entering *His* Paradise, attributed to Himself.

## How to Reach the Tranquil Soul
- **Remember Allah**: "Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured," as in Surah Ar-Ra'd.
- **Accept Allah's decree** in wealth and poverty.
- **Care for orphans and the poor.**
- **Loosen the heart's grip on wealth.**
- **Send ahead good deeds** for your real life.

- **Al-Watr**: the odd.
- **Yasr**: passes.

- **Al-Awtad**: the stakes.

- **Al-Mutma'innah**: tranquil.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **From dawn to the tranquil soul**: the surah begins and ends with light and peace.
2. **Three powers, one fate**: 'Ad, Thamud and Pharaoh.
3. **"Kalla" twice**: rejecting the false measure, then warning of the Day.
4. **"For my life"**: the hereafter is the true life.
5. **"My servants... My Paradise"**: honour through attribution to Allah.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *wal-fajr* (verse 1), the jim sakinah bounces lightly.
- **Izhar**: in *wa layalin 'ashr* (verse 2), the tanween before the 'ayn is pronounced clearly.
- **Ikhfa'**: in *hubban jamma* (verse 20), the tanween is hidden before the jim.
- **Madd muttasil**: in *wa ja'a rabbuka* (verse 22) and *wa ji'a* (verse 23).
- **Idgham with ghunnah**: in *yawma'idhin yatadhakkaru* (verse 23), the tanween merges into the ya'.

## Connection With the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Al-Ghashiyah before it ends with "Indeed, to Us is their return; then indeed, upon Us is their account", and Al-Fajr shows that account through the fall of tyrants. Al-Fajr ends with the tranquil soul, and Surah Al-Balad after it describes the steep path that leads to it: freeing a slave and feeding the orphan and the poor.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Make the most of the ten days of Dhul-Hijjah.**
2. **Remember that your Lord is ever watchful.**
3. **Do not judge Allah's love by your bank balance.**
4. **Honour orphans** and sponsor one if you can.
5. **Feed the poor and urge others to.**
6. **Give inheritance shares fairly.**
7. **Love wealth less** and send more ahead.
8. **Work toward the tranquil soul** through remembrance and contentment.

## How to Memorise Surah Al-Fajr
- **Day 1**: verses 1–14.
- **Day 2**: verses 15–30, then the whole surah.

## Teaching Children
- **Wake them for Fajr once** and read the first verse as the sky lightens.
- **Discuss**: does having more toys mean Allah loves you more?
- **Plan a family project** to help an orphan or feed someone in need.

## The Story of Pharaoh, 'Ad and Thamud in Brief
The three nations mentioned in verses 6–10 appear across the Quran, and Al-Fajr gathers them in a few lines.

**'Ad** lived in the region of al-Ahqaf in southern Arabia. Their prophet was Hud. They were tall and strong and built great structures, and they said: "Who is greater than us in strength?" A furious, cold wind destroyed them over seven nights and eight days, as Surah Al-Haqqah describes.

**Thamud** lived in al-Hijr in northwest Arabia, where homes carved in rock can still be seen. Their prophet was Salih, and their sign was the she-camel. They killed it and were destroyed by the shout.

**Pharaoh** ruled Egypt, claimed divinity, enslaved the Children of Israel and pursued Musa until Allah drowned him in the sea.

Three civilisations of strength, architecture and political power, each different, each ending the same way. The surah does not repeat their stories in detail; it simply lists them and adds one sentence: "Indeed, your Lord is ever watchful." The reader is meant to draw the line to every tyrant of every age.

## The Tranquil Soul in the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ described the believer's death in terms that echo verses 27–28. In a long hadith collected by Ahmad and Abu Dawud from al-Bara' ibn 'Azib, graded authentic by al-Albani, the angel of death says to the believing soul: "O good soul, come out to the forgiveness of Allah and His pleasure," and it flows out as easily as a drop from the mouth of a water skin.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What are the ten nights in Surah Al-Fajr?
Many commentators, including Ibn 'Abbas, said they are the first ten nights of Dhul-Hijjah.

### What does "your Lord is ever watchful" mean?
Allah observes everything and no oppressor escapes Him.

### Is wealth a sign of Allah's honour?
No; both wealth and poverty are tests, as verses 15–16 explain.

### What failures does the surah name?
Not honouring orphans, not urging feeding the poor, devouring inheritance and loving wealth excessively.

### What is the tranquil soul?
The soul at peace with Allah, content with His decree and certain of His promise.

### What does "I wish I had sent ahead for my life" mean?
Regret for not preparing for the hereafter, the real life.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Fajr?
30 verses.

### Where did 'Ad and Thamud live?
'Ad lived in al-Ahqaf in southern Arabia, and Thamud in al-Hijr in northwest Arabia.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Fajr takes you from the first light of dawn to the gates of Paradise. On the way it shows you that empires fall, that wealth is a test, and that the measure of a person is how they treat the orphan and the poor. Let it change how you see what you have and what you lack, so that one day you might hear: "O tranquil soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing."
