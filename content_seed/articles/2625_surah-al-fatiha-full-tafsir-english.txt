Surah Al-Fatihah is the surah every Muslim recites at least seventeen times a day. It opens the Quran and opens every unit of prayer, and the Prophet ﷺ said that no prayer is valid without it. In seven short verses it contains the whole message of the Quran: praise of Allah, His mercy, the Day of Judgement, worship of Him alone, seeking His help, and a request for guidance to the straight path. Allah Himself described it as a conversation: half for Him and half for His servant, "and My servant shall have what he asked for". Understanding Al-Fatihah is the single fastest way to transform your prayer.

This guide explains Surah Al-Fatihah verse by verse in English, with authentic hadith and practical lessons for praying with presence.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Fatihah
- **Number of verses**: 7, counting the Basmalah as the first verse in the narration of Hafs.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 1, the opening of the Mushaf.
- **Names**: Al-Fatihah (the Opening), Umm al-Kitab and Umm al-Quran (the Mother of the Book), as-Sab' al-Mathani (the Seven Oft-Repeated), al-Hamd (the Praise), ash-Shifa' (the Cure), ar-Ruqyah (the Healing Recitation) and as-Salah (the Prayer).

## The Virtues of Al-Fatihah
1. **The greatest surah**: in Sahih al-Bukhari, Abu Sa'id ibn al-Mu'alla reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Shall I not teach you the greatest surah in the Quran?" Then he said: "*Al-hamdu lillahi rabbil-'alamin*. It is the seven oft-repeated and the great Quran which I was given."
2. **No prayer without it**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'Ubadah ibn as-Samit reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book."
3. **A light never given before**: in Sahih Muslim, Ibn 'Abbas reported that an angel came down and said to the Prophet ﷺ: "Rejoice in two lights given to you that were not given to any prophet before you: the Opening of the Book and the closing verses of Surah Al-Baqarah. You will not recite a letter of them except that you will be given it."
4. **A healing recitation**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Sa'id al-Khudri reported that a Companion recited Al-Fatihah over a tribal chief stung by a scorpion and he recovered. The Prophet ﷺ asked: "How did you know that it is a ruqyah?"

## The Prayer Divided: A Conversation With Allah
In Sahih Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that Allah said: "I have divided the prayer between Myself and My servant into two halves, and My servant shall have what he asks for. When the servant says *Al-hamdu lillahi rabbil-'alamin*, Allah says: My servant has praised Me. When he says *Ar-Rahman ar-Rahim*, Allah says: My servant has extolled Me. When he says *Maliki yawm id-din*, Allah says: My servant has glorified Me. When he says *Iyyaka na'budu wa iyyaka nasta'in*, Allah says: This is between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asked for. When he says *Ihdina as-sirat al-mustaqim...*, Allah says: This is for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asked for."

**Imagine praying with this in mind**: after every verse, Allah is answering you.

## Verse 1: In the Name of Allah
﴿بِسۡمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ (Quran 1:1)

**"In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful."** Every good action begins with Allah's name, seeking His help and blessing. The two names of mercy come first: *Ar-Rahman*, whose mercy is vast and covers all creation, and *Ar-Rahim*, whose mercy reaches the believers in particular.

## Verse 2: All Praise Is Due to Allah
﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ﴾ (Quran 1:2)

**"All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds."** *Al-hamd* is praise combined with love and gratitude. "All" praise: every praise anywhere belongs ultimately to Him. **"Lord of the worlds"** (*Rabb al-'alamin*): the Creator, Owner, Sustainer and Nurturer of every world, humans, jinn, angels, animals and everything else.

## Verse 3: The Most Merciful
﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ (Quran 1:3)

**"The Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful."** Mercy is repeated after "Lord of the worlds" so that the heart, overwhelmed by His lordship, is comforted by His mercy.

## Verse 4: Master of the Day of Recompense
﴿مَٰلِكِ يَوۡمِ ٱلدِّينِ﴾ (Quran 1:4)

**"Sovereign of the Day of Recompense."** After mercy comes accountability. On that Day no one will claim any power; the dominion will be His alone. Mercy and justice together keep the heart between hope and fear.

## Verse 5: You Alone We Worship
﴿إِيَّاكَ نَعۡبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسۡتَعِينُ﴾ (Quran 1:5)

**"It is You we worship and You we ask for help."** This is the heart of the surah. Putting "You" first in Arabic (*iyyaka*) means "You alone". Worship (*'ibadah*) is everything Allah loves, done with love, humility and obedience. Seeking help (*isti'anah*) is admitting that even worship is impossible without Him.

**Why "we" and not "I"?** Because the believer prays as part of a community, and because it is more humble to join one's worship with that of all believers.

## Verse 6: Guide Us to the Straight Path
﴿ٱهۡدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلۡمُسۡتَقِيمَ﴾ (Quran 1:6)

**"Guide us to the straight path."** The greatest request a human being can make. We ask for it at least seventeen times a day, because guidance is needed every moment: to know the truth, to be given the will to follow it, and to remain firm on it.

## Verse 7: The Path of Those You Have Favoured
﴿صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ غَيۡرِ ٱلۡمَغۡضُوبِ عَلَيۡهِمۡ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ﴾ (Quran 1:7)

**"The path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour"**: Surah An-Nisa' names them: the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous. **"Not of those who have earned anger, nor of those who are astray."** At-Tirmidhi reported from 'Adi ibn Hatim that the Prophet ﷺ explained those who earned anger as the Jews and those astray as the Christians; al-Albani graded it authentic. Scholars add the general lesson: those who know the truth and do not act on it earn anger, and those who act without knowledge go astray. The straight path combines knowledge and action.

## Saying Amin
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "When the imam says *Amin*, say *Amin*, for whoever's *Amin* coincides with the *Amin* of the angels, his previous sins will be forgiven." *Amin* means "O Allah, answer."

## Word Meanings
- **Bismillah**: in the name of Allah.
- **Ar-Rahman**: the Entirely Merciful.
- **Ar-Rahim**: the Especially Merciful.
- **Al-Hamd**: praise with love and gratitude.
- **Rabb**: Lord, Creator, Sustainer.
- **Al-'Alamin**: the worlds.
- **Malik**: Sovereign, Owner.
- **Yawm ad-Din**: the Day of Recompense.
- **Na'budu**: we worship.
- **Nasta'in**: we ask for help.
- **Ihdina**: guide us.
- **As-Sirat al-Mustaqim**: the straight path.
- **Al-Maghdub 'alayhim**: those who earned anger.
- **Ad-Dallin**: those who are astray.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **The move from third person to second**: the first verses speak *about* Allah; at verse 5 the servant speaks *to* Him directly, as if praise brought him into His presence.
2. **"You alone" placed first**: emphasis on exclusivity.
3. **Mercy before judgement**: hope before fear.
4. **Praise before request**: the etiquette of du'a.
5. **"Us" throughout**: individual worship within a community.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd lazim kalimi muthaqqal**: in *wa la ad-dallin* (verse 7), the alif followed by the doubled lam is stretched six counts.
- **Lam shamsiyyah**: in *ar-Rahman* and *ar-Rahim*, the lam of *al* is not pronounced and the ra' is doubled.
- **Izhar**: in *an'amta* (verse 7), the noon sakinah before the throat letter 'ayn is pronounced clearly.
- **Shaddah**: in *iyyaka* (verse 5), the ya' must be doubled; reading it lightly changes the meaning, so care is essential.
- **Maliki**: in the narration of Hafs, *Maliki* is read with a long alif (*Maaliki*).

## Connection With the Rest of the Quran
Al-Fatihah is a summary; the rest of the Quran is its explanation. It ends with a request for guidance, and Surah Al-Baqarah begins with the answer: "This is the Book about which there is no doubt, a guidance for those conscious of Allah."

## Praying Al-Fatihah With Presence
1. **Pause briefly after each verse**, remembering that Allah is answering.
2. **Feel gratitude** at "All praise is due to Allah".
3. **Feel hope** at "the Entirely Merciful".
4. **Feel awe** at "Sovereign of the Day of Recompense".
5. **Renew sincerity** at "You alone we worship".
6. **Ask sincerely** at "Guide us".
7. **Say Amin** with the imam.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Learn its correct recitation** with a qualified teacher.
2. **Begin your affairs with Bismillah.**
3. **Make praise your first word in du'a.**
4. **Worship Allah alone and rely on Him alone.**
5. **Ask for guidance daily**, however long you have been Muslim.
6. **Combine knowledge and action** to stay on the straight path.

## How to Memorise Al-Fatihah
- **Day 1**: verses 1–4.
- **Day 2**: verses 5–7, then the whole surah, checked with a teacher.

## Teaching Children
- **Teach them its meaning** in simple words, verse by verse.
- **Tell them the hadith of the prayer divided**, so they know Allah answers them.
- **Practise saying Amin** together after the imam.

## Common Mistakes When Reciting Al-Fatihah
Because Al-Fatihah is a pillar of the prayer, scholars paid special attention to errors that change its meaning. Some of the most common are:

1. **Reading *al-hamdu* with a light ha'**: the ha' in *al-hamdu* is the deep, breathy throat letter, not the light "h" of English. Turning it into a different letter changes the word.
2. **Dropping the shaddah of *iyyaka***: as noted above, *iyyaka* must be read with a doubled ya'. Scholars warned that reading it lightly gives a completely different word.
3. **Confusing *an'amta* with *an'amtu***: the ta' carries a fathah ("You favoured"), not a dammah ("I favoured"). The change turns praise of Allah into a claim about oneself.
4. **Weakening the dad in *ad-dallin***: the dad is a heavy letter produced from the side of the tongue. It should not become a light dal or a za'.
5. **Rushing through the verses**: the Prophet ﷺ recited Al-Fatihah verse by verse, pausing at the end of each, as Umm Salamah described in a report collected by Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi, graded authentic by al-Albani.

Correcting these points with a qualified teacher is one of the most valuable things a Muslim can do, because the benefit returns in every prayer for the rest of one's life.

## Al-Fatihah as a Map of the Whole Religion
Scholars noted that the seven verses contain the three kinds of tawhid: lordship in "Lord of the worlds", the names and attributes in "the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful" and "Sovereign", and worship in "You alone we worship". They contain belief in the hereafter in "the Day of Recompense", the need for prophets in "the path of those You have favoured", and the warning against the two ways of deviation. Ibn al-Qayyim wrote a long section of his book Madarij as-Salikin built entirely around "You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help", calling it the meaning of the whole religion.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Why is Al-Fatihah called the Mother of the Book?
Because it contains the essence of the Quran's message: praise, tawhid, the hereafter, worship and guidance.

### Is reciting Al-Fatihah required in every prayer?
Yes. The Prophet ﷺ said: "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book," as in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### What is the hadith of the prayer divided?
A hadith qudsi in Sahih Muslim in which Allah answers each verse of Al-Fatihah recited by His servant.

### Who are "those who earned anger" and "those astray"?
The Prophet ﷺ explained them as the Jews and the Christians, as at-Tirmidhi reported; scholars also apply them to those who know without acting and those who act without knowledge.

### Can Al-Fatihah be used as ruqyah?
Yes, as in the hadith of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### What is the reward of saying Amin?
Whoever's Amin coincides with that of the angels has his previous sins forgiven.

### How many verses are in Al-Fatihah?
7 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

### Should I pause after every verse of Al-Fatihah?
It is recommended, following the description of the Prophet's recitation reported by Umm Salamah.

## Conclusion
Al-Fatihah is the conversation you have with Allah in every prayer. You praise Him, He answers; you glorify Him, He answers; you ask Him for guidance, and He says: My servant shall have what he asked for. Recite it slowly, understand every word, and let it make every prayer a meeting with your Lord.
