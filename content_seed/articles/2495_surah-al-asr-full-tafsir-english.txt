Surah Al-Asr is only three verses long, but it contains a complete way of life. Imam ash-Shafi'i is reported to have said: "If people reflected on this surah, it would be enough for them." Ibn Kathir mentions this saying in his tafsir. The surah makes a shocking claim, that every human being is in a state of loss, and then gives the only exceptions: those who combine four qualities.

This guide explains the surah in English, verse by verse, and turns its four conditions into a practical daily programme.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Asr
- **Number of verses**: 3.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 103, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: from its first word, *wal-'asr*, "by time".
- **Distinction**: one of the shortest surahs, and one of the most comprehensive in meaning.

## The Full Text of Surah Al-Asr
﴿وَٱلۡعَصۡرِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لَفِي خُسۡرٍ﴾ ﴿إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلۡحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلصَّبۡرِ﴾ (Quran 103:1-3)

**Meaning:** By time, mankind is surely in loss, except those who believe, do righteous deeds, urge one another to the truth, and urge one another to patience.

## Verse 1: By Time
﴿وَٱلۡعَصۡرِ﴾ (Quran 103:1)

Allah swears by *al-'asr*. Commentators give three main explanations:
1. **Time itself, the passing of the ages.** This is the view of many commentators including Ibn 'Abbas. Time is the container of all human action; in it people gain or lose.
2. **The late afternoon**, the last part of the day, a reminder that life passes as the day passes, and the afternoon is near its end.
3. **The 'Asr prayer**, which many scholars identify as "the middle prayer". In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'Ali reported that on the day of the Battle of the Trench the Prophet ﷺ said: "They kept us from the middle prayer, the 'Asr prayer."

**Why swear by time?** Because time is a person's real capital. Every moment that passes is gone forever. When Allah swears by something, it is great, so how can a person waste it?

## Verse 2: Mankind Is in Loss
﴿إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لَفِي خُسۡرٍ﴾ (Quran 103:2)

This is the answer to the oath, and it is stressed three times: by the oath itself, by *inna* (surely), and by the *la* in *la-fi* (truly in). The word *al-insan* covers every human being. *Fi khusr*, "in loss", pictures loss as something that surrounds a person, like a container he is inside. The indefinite *khusr* suggests a great loss.

**How is every person in loss?** Because life is constantly decreasing. Every day spent is a day subtracted. If it is not invested in what benefits a person in the Hereafter, it is lost. Al-Hasan al-Basri said: "O son of Adam, you are only a collection of days; whenever a day passes, part of you passes."

The greatest loss is in the Hereafter, as Surah Az-Zumar says: "The real losers are those who lose themselves and their families on the Day of Resurrection."

## Verse 3: Except Those Who...
﴿إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلۡحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلصَّبۡرِ﴾ (Quran 103:3)

Allah excludes from loss those who combine four qualities:
1. **Iman (they believe)**: faith in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day and divine decree. It is the foundation of everything.
2. **'Amal salih (they do righteous deeds)**: obligatory and voluntary worship, good character and honest dealings. The plural *as-salihat* covers every kind of good.
3. **Tawasi bil-haqq (they urge one another to the truth)**: they advise each other to believe, to do good, to enjoin right and forbid wrong.
4. **Tawasi bis-sabr (they urge one another to patience)**: patience in obeying Allah, patience in avoiding sin, and patience with painful decrees.

**A remarkable order**: the first two qualities reform the self; the last two reform others. It is not enough to be personally good; a believer also works for the good of those around him.

**Why end with patience?** Because calling to the truth brings hardship, and whoever enjoins right will face resistance. Luqman told his son in Surah Luqman:

﴿يَٰبُنَيَّ أَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَأۡمُرۡ بِٱلۡمَعۡرُوفِ وَٱنۡهَ عَنِ ٱلۡمُنكَرِ وَٱصۡبِرۡ عَلَىٰ مَآ أَصَابَكَۖ إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ مِنۡ عَزۡمِ ٱلۡأُمُورِ﴾ (Quran 31:17)

**Meaning:** O my son, establish prayer, enjoin what is right, forbid what is wrong, and be patient over what befalls you; that is among the matters requiring determination.

## Ibn al-Qayyim's Four Levels
Ibn al-Qayyim explained that the surah contains four levels by which a person reaches completeness:
1. **Knowing the truth**: faith.
2. **Acting on it**: righteous deeds.
3. **Teaching it to those who do not know it**: urging one another to the truth.
4. **Being patient in learning it, acting on it and teaching it**: urging one another to patience.
Whoever completes these four is saved from loss.

## Word Meanings
- **Al-'Asr**: time, the late afternoon, or the 'Asr prayer.
- **Al-Insan**: mankind, every human.
- **Khusr**: loss, ruin.
- **Amanu**: they believed.
- **As-Salihat**: righteous deeds.
- **Tawasaw**: they urged and advised one another.
- **Al-Haqq**: the truth.
- **As-Sabr**: patience.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **Extraordinary brevity**: three verses, a complete method.
2. **Triple emphasis in verse 2**, because people are heedless and need to be shaken.
3. **"In" loss**: loss surrounds a person like a container.
4. **The form *tawasaw***: mutual action. Everyone advises and everyone accepts advice.
5. **Repeating *wa tawasaw***: giving patience its own emphasis.
6. **From singular to plural**: "mankind" is singular, while the saved are "those who", plural. Salvation is found in a community that supports one another.

## The Companions and Surah Al-Asr
It is reported that when two companions of the Prophet ﷺ met, they would not part until one had recited Surah Al-Asr to the other, then they would give salams. At-Tabarani recorded this in *al-Mu'jam al-Awsat*, and al-Albani graded it authentic in *as-Silsilah as-Sahihah*. They made the surah a greeting and farewell, reminding each other of the formula for success every time they met.

## The Value of Time in Islam
- **You will be asked about your life.** At-Tirmidhi recorded, grading it *hasan sahih*, from Abu Barzah that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The feet of a servant will not move on the Day of Resurrection until he is asked about his life and how he spent it..."
- **The two neglected blessings.** In Sahih al-Bukhari: "Two blessings that many people lose out on: health and free time."
- **Be like a traveller.** In Sahih al-Bukhari, Ibn 'Umar reported that the Prophet ﷺ told him: "Be in this world as if you were a stranger or a traveller." Ibn 'Umar used to say: "When evening comes, do not wait for morning; when morning comes, do not wait for evening. Take from your health for your sickness, and from your life for your death."

## Urging One Another to the Truth in Daily Life
This is not only sermons. It has many everyday forms:
- **At home**: spouses reminding each other of prayer and Quran, and raising children on good.
- **Among friends**: sincere, gentle advice. In Sahih Muslim, Tamim ad-Dari reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Religion is sincere advice."
- **At work**: encouraging honesty and excellence.
- **In society**: enjoining good and forbidding evil with wisdom.
- **Online**: sharing beneficial knowledge and good words.

**Etiquette of advice**: sincerity, gentleness, giving personal advice privately, and starting with yourself.

## Urging One Another to Patience: Three Types
1. **Patience in obedience**: praying on time, night prayer, honouring parents.
2. **Patience away from sin**: lowering the gaze, leaving the forbidden when it is within reach.
3. **Patience with painful decrees**: illness, loss of loved ones, financial hardship.

In Sahih Muslim, Suhayb reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "How wonderful is the affair of the believer; all of it is good for him... If something good happens to him he is grateful, and that is good for him; if something harmful happens to him he is patient, and that is good for him." And in al-Bukhari and Muslim: "No one has been given a gift better and more expansive than patience."

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before Al-Asr comes At-Takathur, which condemns those distracted by competing for more until they reach the graves, people who wasted their lives. Al-Asr then swears by time and shows how to invest it. After it comes Al-Humazah, describing a model of the losers: the slanderer who gathers wealth and counts it.

## A Daily Programme From Surah Al-Asr
Give each of the four pillars a place in your day:
- **Faith**: a portion of Quran, the morning and evening adhkar, and reflection on Allah's signs.
- **Righteous deeds**: the prayers on time, and at least one act of kindness to someone.
- **Truth**: one piece of advice, reminder or beneficial knowledge shared.
- **Patience**: encouraging someone going through difficulty, or holding yourself to a hard act of obedience.

At the end of the day ask: today, was I among the winners or the losers?

## Tajweed Notes
- **Lam qamariyyah** in *al-'asr*, *al-insan* and *al-haqq*.
- **Lam shamsiyyah** in *alladhina*, *as-salihat* and *as-sabr*.
- **Ghunnah** on the doubled nun in *inna*.
- **Madd badal** in *amanu*.
- **Qalqalah kubra** when stopping on *bil-haqq* (qaf).
- **Qalqalah sughra** on the *ba* with sukun in *bis-sabr*.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Value your time**: it is your life and your capital.
2. **Faith without deeds is not enough**, and deeds without faith are not accepted.
3. **Do not settle for your own goodness**: work for the good of others.
4. **Accept advice**: urging is mutual.
5. **Be patient and help others be patient.**
6. **Choose friends who remind you.**
7. **Review your day** by the standard of the surah.

## Teaching Children
- Use an hourglass to show that time passes and does not return.
- Make the surah a family greeting and farewell, as the companions did.
- Teach the four conditions on four fingers: we believe, we do good, we advise, we are patient.

## Surah Al-Asr and the 'Asr Prayer
If *al-'asr* refers to the afternoon prayer, the surah points to its great importance, which the Sunnah confirms:
- **Missing it is like losing family and wealth.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn 'Umar reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The one who misses the 'Asr prayer is as if he has lost his family and his wealth."
- **Abandoning it nullifies deeds.** In Sahih al-Bukhari, Buraydah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever leaves the 'Asr prayer, his deeds are nullified."
- **The angels witness it.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim: "Angels take turns among you by night and by day, and they gather at the Fajr prayer and the 'Asr prayer."
So when you recite "By time, mankind is in loss", remember that neglecting this prayer is one of the clearest forms of loss.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does "al-'asr" mean?
Time, the late afternoon, or the 'Asr prayer; many commentators favour "time".

### What does "mankind is in loss" mean?
Every person is losing as life passes, except the one who invests it in faith and good deeds.

### What are the four conditions in Surah Al-Asr?
Faith, righteous deeds, urging one another to the truth, and urging one another to patience.

### What did Imam ash-Shafi'i say about Surah Al-Asr?
He is reported to have said that if people reflected on it, it would be enough for them.

### Did the companions recite Al-Asr when they met?
Yes, according to a report by at-Tabarani graded authentic by al-Albani.

### What are the types of patience?
Patience in obedience, patience away from sin, and patience with painful decrees.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Asr?
3 verses.

### Is it enough to be good myself without advising others?
The surah shows that complete success comes from reforming yourself and also working, as far as you are able, for the good of others through mutual advice.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Asr puts a simple equation in front of you: time is passing, and you are either gaining or losing. There is one route to gain: believe, act, advise and be patient. Make these four the measure of your day, and recite the surah the way the companions did when they met and parted, as a constant reminder that your life is too precious to waste.
