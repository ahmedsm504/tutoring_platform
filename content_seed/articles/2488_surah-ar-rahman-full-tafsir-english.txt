Surah Ar-Rahman is one of the most recognisable surahs of the Quran. Even people who do not understand Arabic notice its rhythm and the verse that returns again and again: *fa-bi-ayyi ala'i rabbikuma tukadhdhiban*, "So which of the favours of your Lord will you both deny?" It is a surah of blessings, addressed to both humans and jinn, that lays Allah's favours before them one by one and asks them to acknowledge them.

This guide explains the whole surah in English, section by section, with the meaning of its famous refrain and the lessons it teaches.

## Quick Facts About Surah Ar-Rahman
- **Number of verses**: 78.
- **Type**: Medinan in the standard mushaf; scholars have discussed whether it is Meccan or Medinan.
- **Position**: Surah 55, in the twenty-seventh juz.
- **Name**: from its first word, *Ar-Rahman*, the Most Merciful.
- **The refrain**: "So which of the favours of your Lord will you both deny?" appears 31 times.

## The Refrain and the Jinn's Answer
At-Tirmidhi recorded from Jabir that the Prophet ﷺ recited Surah Ar-Rahman to his companions from beginning to end, and they were silent. He said: "I recited it to the jinn on the night of the jinn, and they responded better than you. Every time I reached *fa-bi-ayyi ala'i rabbikuma tukadhdhiban*, they said: *We deny none of Your favours, our Lord; to You belongs all praise.*" Al-Albani graded this narration *hasan*.

This shows the right response to the refrain. When you hear the question, answer it in your heart or under your breath: none of Your favours do we deny.

You may also hear that Ar-Rahman is "the bride of the Quran". That narration is weak according to hadith scholars, so it is better not to attribute it to the Prophet ﷺ.

## Verses 1–4: The Most Merciful Taught the Quran
﴿ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنُ﴾ ﴿عَلَّمَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَ﴾ ﴿خَلَقَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ﴾ ﴿عَلَّمَهُ ٱلۡبَيَانَ﴾ (Quran 55:1-4)

**Meaning:** The Most Merciful. He taught the Quran. He created man. He taught him clear expression.

The surah begins with a name of Allah, not with an oath or a letter. And the first favour mentioned is not food or water or the sky: it is the Quran. Teaching the Quran is placed even before creating man, which tells you its rank among all blessings. Then He gave man *al-bayan*, the ability to express himself clearly, which is what allows him to understand and convey revelation.

## Verses 5–13: Sun, Moon, Stars, Trees and the Balance
﴿ٱلشَّمۡسُ وَٱلۡقَمَرُ بِحُسۡبَانٖ﴾ ﴿وَٱلنَّجۡمُ وَٱلشَّجَرُ يَسۡجُدَانِ﴾ ﴿وَٱلسَّمَآءَ رَفَعَهَا وَوَضَعَ ٱلۡمِيزَانَ﴾ ﴿أَلَّا تَطۡغَوۡاْ فِي ٱلۡمِيزَانِ﴾ ﴿وَأَقِيمُواْ ٱلۡوَزۡنَ بِٱلۡقِسۡطِ وَلَا تُخۡسِرُواْ ٱلۡمِيزَانَ﴾ (Quran 55:5-9)

**Meaning:** The sun and the moon move by precise calculation. The stars and the trees bow down. He raised the heaven and set up the balance, so that you do not transgress in the balance. Establish weight with justice and do not make the balance deficient.

The sun and moon follow calculations precise enough for us to build calendars on them. The stars, or the low plants without stems according to another view of *an-najm*, and the trees prostrate in their own way. Then the word *mizan*, balance, is repeated three times in three verses. The heavens were built on balance, and humans are commanded to keep balance in their trade, their judgements and their lives. Cheating in a scale is a crime against the order on which the universe stands.

﴿وَٱلۡأَرۡضَ وَضَعَهَا لِلۡأَنَامِ﴾ ﴿فِيهَا فَٰكِهَةࣱ وَٱلنَّخۡلُ ذَاتُ ٱلۡأَكۡمَامِ﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡحَبُّ ذُو ٱلۡعَصۡفِ وَٱلرَّيۡحَانُ﴾ ﴿فَبِأَيِّ ءَالَآءِ رَبِّكُمَا تُكَذِّبَانِ﴾ (Quran 55:10-13)

**Meaning:** He laid out the earth for all creatures. In it are fruit and date palms with sheathed clusters, grain with husks, and fragrant plants. So which of the favours of your Lord will you both deny?

Here the refrain appears for the first time, after the list of the earth's provisions. "Both" refers to humans and jinn, the two accountable kinds of creation.

## Verses 14–25: Clay and Fire, Two Easts and Two Seas
﴿خَلَقَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ مِن صَلۡصَٰلٖ كَٱلۡفَخَّارِ﴾ ﴿وَخَلَقَ ٱلۡجَآنَّ مِن مَّارِجٖ مِّن نَّارٖ﴾ (Quran 55:14-15)

**Meaning:** He created man from dry clay like pottery, and He created the jinn from a smokeless flame of fire.

﴿رَبُّ ٱلۡمَشۡرِقَيۡنِ وَرَبُّ ٱلۡمَغۡرِبَيۡنِ﴾ (Quran 55:17)

**Meaning:** Lord of the two easts and Lord of the two wests.

The two easts and two wests are understood as the points where the sun rises and sets at the height of summer and the depth of winter, the two extremes of its yearly journey. Between them is every sunrise and sunset of the year.

﴿مَرَجَ ٱلۡبَحۡرَيۡنِ يَلۡتَقِيَانِ﴾ ﴿بَيۡنَهُمَا بَرۡزَخࣱ لَّا يَبۡغِيَانِ﴾ ﴿فَبِأَيِّ ءَالَآءِ رَبِّكُمَا تُكَذِّبَانِ﴾ ﴿يَخۡرُجُ مِنۡهُمَا ٱللُّؤۡلُؤُ وَٱلۡمَرۡجَانُ﴾ (Quran 55:19-22)

**Meaning:** He released the two seas, meeting each other, with a barrier between them that they do not overstep. So which of the favours of your Lord will you both deny? From both of them come pearls and coral.

Two bodies of water meet, yet each keeps its own character because of a barrier, *barzakh*, that Allah placed between them. From the seas come pearls and coral, beauty pulled out of the depths. The section closes with ships "like mountains" sailing on the sea.

## Verses 26–30: Everything Perishes Except His Face
﴿كُلُّ مَنۡ عَلَيۡهَا فَانٖ﴾ ﴿وَيَبۡقَىٰ وَجۡهُ رَبِّكَ ذُو ٱلۡجَلَٰلِ وَٱلۡإِكۡرَامِ﴾ (Quran 55:26-27)

**Meaning:** Everyone on it will perish, and there remains the Face of your Lord, Owner of Majesty and Honour.

In the middle of a surah about blessings comes this verse about death. Why is mortality listed among the favours? Because death moves people from the world of action to the world of reward; because it ends the oppression of tyrants; and because it reminds the believer not to cling to what will pass. Only Allah remains, *Dhul-Jalali wal-Ikram*, the Owner of Majesty and Honour. The Prophet ﷺ used this name after prayer, saying, as recorded by Muslim from Thawban: "O Allah, You are Peace and from You is peace; blessed are You, O Owner of Majesty and Honour."

﴿يَسۡـَٔلُهُۥ مَن فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِۚ كُلَّ يَوۡمٍ هُوَ فِي شَأۡنٖ﴾ (Quran 55:29)

**Meaning:** Everyone in the heavens and the earth asks of Him. Every day He is engaged in some matter.

Every creature depends on Him, and every day He forgives a sin, relieves a distress, raises one people and lowers another, answers a prayer and gives life and death.

## Verses 31–45: The Reckoning
﴿سَنَفۡرُغُ لَكُمۡ أَيُّهَ ٱلثَّقَلَانِ﴾ (Quran 55:31)

**Meaning:** We will soon attend to you, O you two burdened kinds.

The verse is a warning to humans and jinn: the reckoning is coming. Then they are challenged: if you can escape beyond the regions of the heavens and the earth, then escape, but you will not escape except with authority, and no one has that authority except by Allah's permission. On that Day the sky will split and become rose-red like oil. The criminals will be known by their marks and seized by their forelocks and feet, and Hell will be shown to them: "This is the Hell the criminals used to deny."

## Verses 46–61: The Two Gardens for Those Who Fear
﴿وَلِمَنۡ خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِۦ جَنَّتَانِ﴾ (Quran 55:46)

**Meaning:** For the one who fears standing before his Lord there are two gardens.

This is the turning point of the surah. Fear of standing before Allah is not a paralysing fear; it is the awareness that stops a person before sin. Its reward is two gardens, described with flowing springs, every kind of fruit in pairs, couches lined with brocade, and fruit within easy reach.

﴿هَلۡ جَزَآءُ ٱلۡإِحۡسَٰنِ إِلَّا ٱلۡإِحۡسَٰنُ﴾ (Quran 55:60)

**Meaning:** Is the reward for excellence anything but excellence?

This short verse is one of the most quoted in the Quran. *Ihsan*, excellence, is defined in the hadith of Jibril recorded by Muslim: "That you worship Allah as though you see Him, and though you do not see Him, He sees you." Whoever brings that excellence in this life will be met with excellence from Allah.

## Verses 62–78: Two More Gardens
﴿وَمِن دُونِهِمَا جَنَّتَانِ﴾ (Quran 55:62)

**Meaning:** And below these two are two other gardens.

Many scholars understand this as a second level of gardens for those below the first group. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Musa al-Ash'ari reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Two gardens of silver, their vessels and everything in them, and two gardens of gold, their vessels and everything in them. Nothing stands between the people and their seeing their Lord except the cloak of majesty over His Face in the Garden of Eden."

The surah closes as it opened, with His name:

﴿تَبَٰرَكَ ٱسۡمُ رَبِّكَ ذِي ٱلۡجَلَٰلِ وَٱلۡإِكۡرَامِ﴾ (Quran 55:78)

**Meaning:** Blessed is the name of your Lord, Owner of Majesty and Honour.

## Why Is the Refrain Repeated 31 Times?
Scholars have offered several reflections:
1. **Each favour deserves its own acknowledgement.** The question follows each blessing so that none is passed over.
2. **Repetition is a way the Arabs emphasised meaning.** It makes the listener stop and answer every time.
3. **Even warnings are favours.** The refrain follows verses about Hell too, because warning people before it is too late is itself a mercy.

## Key Vocabulary
- **Al-Bayan**: clear expression and speech.
- **Bi-husban**: by precise calculation.
- **Al-Mizan**: the balance.
- **Al-Akmam**: the sheaths of date clusters.
- **Al-'Asf**: husks and straw.
- **Ar-Rayhan**: fragrant plants.
- **Salsal**: dry, ringing clay.
- **Marij min nar**: smokeless flame.
- **Barzakh**: barrier.
- **Al-Lu'lu' wal-Marjan**: pearls and coral.
- **Ala'**: favours, blessings.
- **Ihsan**: excellence.

## Tajweed Notes for Reciting Ar-Rahman
- **Lam shamsiyyah** in *ar-Rahman*, *ash-shams* and *an-najm*: the lam is not pronounced, and the next letter is doubled.
- **Lam qamariyyah** in *al-qamar* and *al-insan*: the lam is pronounced clearly.
- **Madd muttasil** in *as-sama'* and *ala'i*: the alif before the hamza in the same word is stretched four or five counts.
- **Madd 'arid**: the endings *-an* and *-ar* give the surah its rhythm when you pause.

## Practical Lessons from Surah Ar-Rahman
1. **Count your favours.** Pause after each refrain and name a blessing in your life.
2. **Answer the question.** Say, as the jinn did: none of Your favours do we deny.
3. **Value the Quran as the first blessing.** It is mentioned before the creation of man.
4. **Keep the balance.** Be fair in trade, speech and judgement.
5. **Remember that everything perishes.** Attach your heart to the One who remains.
6. **Fear standing before Allah.** That awareness is the key to the gardens.
7. **Pursue ihsan.** Excellence in worship and dealings is rewarded with excellence.

## How to Teach Surah Ar-Rahman to Children
- Make a "favours list": every time the refrain comes, each child names one blessing.
- Show pictures of pearls, coral and the sea to illustrate verse 22.
- Teach them the jinn's response so they can say it when they hear the refrain.

## A Memorisation Plan
The refrain makes Ar-Rahman easier to memorise than its length suggests:
1. Week 1: verses 1–25.
2. Week 2: verses 26–45.
3. Week 3: verses 46–61.
4. Week 4: verses 62–78, then full revision.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### How many times is "fa-bi-ayyi ala'i rabbikuma tukadhdhiban" repeated?
31 times.

### Who is addressed by "your Lord... you both"?
Humans and jinn, the two kinds of creation who are accountable.

### What did the jinn say when they heard Surah Ar-Rahman?
"We deny none of Your favours, our Lord; to You belongs all praise," in a narration recorded by at-Tirmidhi and graded *hasan* by al-Albani.

### Is Surah Ar-Rahman "the bride of the Quran"?
That narration is weak, so it should not be attributed to the Prophet ﷺ.

### What are the two easts and two wests?
The points where the sun rises and sets at the summer and winter extremes.

### What does "Is the reward for excellence anything but excellence?" mean?
Whoever worships Allah with ihsan and treats people with excellence will be rewarded with excellence from Allah.

### How many verses are in Surah Ar-Rahman?
78 verses.

## Conclusion
Surah Ar-Rahman lays the universe before you as a gift: the Quran, speech, the sun and moon, the balance, fruit, seas, pearls, and finally gardens beyond imagination. Then it asks you, again and again, which of these you will deny. The best response is the one the jinn gave. Recite the surah slowly, answer every refrain with gratitude, and let it turn your attention from the gifts to the Most Merciful who gave them.
