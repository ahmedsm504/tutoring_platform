- **We Will Surely Test You**: An English explanation of Quran 2:155-157: the five tests of fear, hunger, loss of wealth, lives and fruits, good news for the patient, the words "inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi rajiun", and…
- **Safa and Marwah Are Among the Symbols of Allah**: An English explanation of Quran 2:158: why the verse says "no blame" as Aishah explained, the origin of the saʿy in Hajar's search for water, its ruling, and the lesson of effort with trust.
- **Those Who Believe Are Stronger in Love for Allah**: An English explanation of Quran 2:165: taking rivals equal to Allah in love, love as worship, why the believers' love is stronger, signs of loving Allah, and the scene of the Day of…
- **Eat of What Is on Earth, Lawful and Good**: An English explanation of Quran 2:168: the address to all humanity, the difference between halal and tayyib, permissibility as the default for food, the effect of lawful food on duʿa, and…
- **Righteousness Is Not Turning Your Faces**: An English explanation of Quran 2:177, the verse of righteousness (al-birr), which defines true piety through belief, generous giving, prayer, keeping promises and patience in hardship.
- **When My Servants Ask You About Me, I Am Near**: An English explanation of Quran 2:186, the verse placed among the fasting verses that promises Allah is near and answers the caller, with its conditions, lessons and how to make dua with…
- **They Ask You About the New Moons**: An English explanation of Quran 2:189: the new moons as timekeepers for people and Hajj, the pre-Islamic custom of entering homes from the back during ihram, and righteousness being taqwa.
- **Do Not Throw Yourselves into Destruction**: An English explanation of Quran 2:195: spending in Allah's cause, Abu Ayyub's explanation that abandoning spending is the destruction meant, and the command of ihsan.
- **Take Provision, and the Best Provision Is Taqwa**: An English explanation of Quran 2:197 from the verses of Hajj: no obscenity, sin or argument during Hajj, taking provision for the journey, and taqwa as the best provision for the journey…

## "Seek Help Through Patience and Prayer": Quran 2:153 Explained

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱسۡتَعِينُواْ بِٱلصَّبۡرِ وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ مَعَ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ﴾ [Al-Baqarah 2:153]

### Two tools
| Tool | Role |
|---|---|
| Patience | Holding the heart steady |
| Prayer | Connecting the heart to Allah |

- Patience is inward strength; prayer is the source that refills it.

### The Prophet's habit
- Hudhayfah reported that when something distressed the Prophet ﷺ, **he would pray** (narrated by Abu Dawud).

### Allah is with the patient
- The verse ends with a promise of Allah's special company: support, help and protection.

### The context
- The verse comes right before the verses of tests (2:155-157): first the tools, then the trials.

### Applying it
- Before a difficult conversation, pray two rakʿahs.
- When anxious, make wudu and pray.
- When waiting for results, be patient and keep praying.

### Lessons
- **Run to prayer**, not away from it, when life is hard.
- **Patience is a skill** that grows with practice.

## "We Will Surely Test You": Quran 2:155-157 Explained

﴿وَلَنَبۡلُوَنَّكُم بِشَيۡءٖ مِّنَ ٱلۡخَوۡفِ وَٱلۡجُوعِ وَنَقۡصٖ مِّنَ ٱلۡأَمۡوَٰلِ وَٱلۡأَنفُسِ وَٱلثَّمَرَٰتِۗ وَبَشِّرِ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ﴾ [Al-Baqarah 2:155]

### "Something of"
- Allah says *with something of*: the test is a **portion**, not total loss. Even the trial is measured with mercy.

### Five tests
| Test | Example |
|---|---|
| Fear | Insecurity, worry about the future |
| Hunger | Poverty, hardship |
| Loss of wealth | Business loss, theft |
| Loss of lives | Death of loved ones, illness |
| Loss of fruits | Crops, and some scholars include children |

### What the patient say
﴿ٱلَّذِينَ إِذَآ أَصَٰبَتۡهُم مُّصِيبَةࣱ قَالُوٓاْ إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّآ إِلَيۡهِ رَٰجِعُونَ﴾
- *We belong to Allah* means what was taken was His. *To Him we return* means the separation is temporary.

### The reward
﴿أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ صَلَوَٰتࣱ مِّن رَّبِّهِمۡ وَرَحۡمَةࣱۖ وَأُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُهۡتَدُونَ﴾
- Blessings, mercy and guidance: three gifts for one sincere sentence of patience.

### A duʿa from the Sunnah
- Umm Salamah reported that the Prophet ﷺ taught saying the words of the verse and then: *O Allah, reward me for my affliction and replace it with something better* (narrated by Muslim).

### Lessons
- **Expect tests**; they are part of the plan.
- **Say the words**, even through tears.

## "Safa and Marwah Are Among the Symbols of Allah": Quran 2:158 Explained

﴿۞إِنَّ ٱلصَّفَا وَٱلۡمَرۡوَةَ مِن شَعَآئِرِ ٱللَّهِۖ فَمَنۡ حَجَّ ٱلۡبَيۡتَ أَوِ ٱعۡتَمَرَ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيۡهِ أَن يَطَّوَّفَ بِهِمَاۚ وَمَن تَطَوَّعَ خَيۡرٗا فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ شَاكِرٌ عَلِيمٌ﴾ [Al-Baqarah 2:158]

### Why "no blame"?
- ʿUrwah asked his aunt ʿAishah about it. She explained that some of the Ansar felt uneasy about going between them because of a pre-Islamic practice, so the verse removed that unease (narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim). It does not mean saʿy is merely allowed.

### The origin
- Hajar, mother of Ismaʿil, ran between Safa and Marwah seven times searching for water until Zamzam appeared. The Prophet ﷺ said: *That is the saʿy of the people between them* (narrated by al-Bukhari).

### Its ruling
- Most scholars consider it a pillar of Hajj and ʿUmrah.

### "Appreciative, All-Knowing"
- Allah rewards whoever volunteers good.

### Lessons
- **Take the means** like Hajar, and trust Allah.
- **Honour the symbols** without hesitation.

## "Those Who Believe Are Stronger in Love for Allah": Quran 2:165 Explained

﴿وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَتَّخِذُ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ أَندَادٗا يُحِبُّونَهُمۡ كَحُبِّ ٱللَّهِۖ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓاْ أَشَدُّ حُبّٗا لِّلَّهِۗ وَلَوۡ يَرَى ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوٓاْ إِذۡ يَرَوۡنَ ٱلۡعَذَابَ أَنَّ ٱلۡقُوَّةَ لِلَّهِ جَمِيعٗا وَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ شَدِيدُ ٱلۡعَذَابِ﴾ [Al-Baqarah 2:165]

### *Andad*
- Rivals and equals set up beside Allah in love and reverence.

### Love as worship
- Love with humility, submission and reverence belongs to Allah alone.

### Why "stronger"?
| Believer | Polytheist |
|---|---|
| Love purely for Allah | Love split among rivals |
| Firm in ease and hardship | Abandons him in hardship |

### Signs of loving Allah
- Following the Prophet ﷺ (Al Imran 3:31), loving His words, and putting His command before desire.

### The Day of Judgment
- When they see the punishment, they will know all power belongs to Allah, and the followed will disown the followers.

### Lessons
- **Check your heart**: whose love comes first?
- **Put Allah's love** above all.

## "Eat of What Is on Earth, Lawful and Good": Quran 2:168 Explained

﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ كُلُواْ مِمَّا فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ حَلَٰلٗا طَيِّبٗا وَلَا تَتَّبِعُواْ خُطُوَٰتِ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنِۚ إِنَّهُۥ لَكُمۡ عَدُوࣱّ مُّبِينٌ﴾ [Al-Baqarah 2:168]

### To all people
- *O mankind*: food is a blessing for every human.

### Halal and tayyib
| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Halal* | Permitted in itself and in how it was earned |
| *Tayyib* | Wholesome, pleasant and not harmful |

### The default is permissibility
- *Of what is on earth*: food is permitted unless Allah forbade it.

### Lawful food and duʿa
- The Prophet ﷺ mentioned a man on a long journey, dishevelled, raising his hands: *O Lord, O Lord*, while his food, drink and clothing were unlawful. *How can he be answered?* (narrated by Muslim).

### Satan's footsteps
- Forbidding what Allah permitted without evidence, permitting what He forbade, or unlawful earning.

### Lessons
- **Seek the lawful** in income and food.
- **Do not forbid** what Allah permitted without knowledge.

## "Righteousness Is Not Turning Your Faces": Quran 2:177 Explained

﴿۞لَّيۡسَ ٱلۡبِرَّ أَن تُوَلُّواْ وُجُوهَكُمۡ قِبَلَ ٱلۡمَشۡرِقِ وَٱلۡمَغۡرِبِ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلۡبِرَّ مَنۡ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡيَوۡمِ ٱلۡأٓخِرِ وَٱلۡمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ وَٱلۡكِتَٰبِ وَٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَ وَءَاتَى ٱلۡمَالَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِۦ ذَوِي ٱلۡقُرۡبَىٰ وَٱلۡيَتَٰمَىٰ وَٱلۡمَسَٰكِينَ وَٱبۡنَ ٱلسَّبِيلِ وَٱلسَّآئِلِينَ وَفِي ٱلرِّقَابِ وَأَقَامَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتَى ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَٱلۡمُوفُونَ بِعَهۡدِهِمۡ إِذَا عَٰهَدُواْۖ وَٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ فِي ٱلۡبَأۡسَآءِ وَٱلضَّرَّآءِ وَحِينَ ٱلۡبَأۡسِۗ أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ صَدَقُواْۖ وَأُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُتَّقُونَ﴾ [Al-Baqarah 2:177]

### The context
- After the change of the qiblah, some argued about the direction of prayer. This verse shifts the focus: **direction alone is not righteousness**.

### The pillars of righteousness
| Area | What the verse lists |
|---|---|
| Belief | Allah, the Last Day, angels, the Book, the prophets |
| Giving | Wealth given *despite loving it* to relatives, orphans, the poor, travellers, those who ask, and freeing slaves |
| Worship | Prayer and zakah |
| Character | Keeping promises |
| Endurance | Patience in poverty, illness and battle |

### Charity beyond zakah
- Giving wealth is mentioned **before** zakah, showing that generosity is wider than the obligatory amount.

### The two titles
- *Those are the ones who are true, and those are the pious.*

### Lessons
- **Faith must show in action.**
- **Give what you love**, not only what you no longer need.

## "When My Servants Ask You About Me, I Am Near": Quran 2:186 Explained

﴿وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِي عَنِّي فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌۖ أُجِيبُ دَعۡوَةَ ٱلدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِۖ فَلۡيَسۡتَجِيبُواْ لِي وَلۡيُؤۡمِنُواْ بِي لَعَلَّهُمۡ يَرۡشُدُونَ﴾ [Al-Baqarah 2:186]

### Meaning
*And when My servants ask you about Me, indeed I am near. I respond to the call of the caller when he calls upon Me. So let them respond to Me and believe in Me, that they may be rightly guided.*

### No intermediary
- Elsewhere when people ask a question, Allah says *Say* (to the Prophet ﷺ). Here the answer comes **directly**: *I am near.* Scholars note this shows there is no barrier between the servant and his Lord in duʿa.

### Why among the fasting verses?
- The verse sits between the rulings of fasting, a hint that the fasting person's duʿa is especially hoped to be answered.

### Two conditions in the verse
| Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Let them respond to Me* | Obey Allah's commands |
| *Let them believe in Me* | Trust that He hears and answers |

### How Allah answers
- The Prophet ﷺ taught that every duʿa is answered in one of three ways: granting it, averting an equal harm, or saving its reward for the Hereafter (narrated by Ahmad).

### Lessons
- **Call on Allah yourself**, in any language and at any time.
- **Do not lose patience**: a delayed answer is not a rejected one.

## "They Ask You About the New Moons": Quran 2:189 Explained

﴿۞يَسۡـَٔلُونَكَ عَنِ ٱلۡأَهِلَّةِۖ قُلۡ هِيَ مَوَٰقِيتُ لِلنَّاسِ وَٱلۡحَجِّۗ وَلَيۡسَ ٱلۡبِرُّ بِأَن تَأۡتُواْ ٱلۡبُيُوتَ مِن ظُهُورِهَا وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلۡبِرَّ مَنِ ٱتَّقَىٰۗ وَأۡتُواْ ٱلۡبُيُوتَ مِنۡ أَبۡوَٰبِهَاۚ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تُفۡلِحُونَ﴾ [Al-Baqarah 2:189]

### Timekeepers
| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Worship | Fasting, Hajj, zakah, waiting periods |
| Dealings | Debt terms and contracts |

### A useful answer
- The Quran directs the question to what benefits people: the function of the moon in their lives.

### Entering from the back
- Some of the Ansar, when in ihram, would not enter houses through the door but from the back, considering it righteousness, and the verse was revealed (narrated by al-Bukhari from al-Baraʾ).

### True righteousness
- *Righteousness is in the one who fears Allah*, not in customs Allah did not legislate.

### A general lesson
- *Enter houses through their doors*: approach matters through their proper ways.

### Lessons
- **Do not invent** acts of worship.
- **Learn the Hijri calendar**: worship is tied to it.

## "Do Not Throw Yourselves into Destruction": Quran 2:195 Explained

﴿وَأَنفِقُواْ فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ وَلَا تُلۡقُواْ بِأَيۡدِيكُمۡ إِلَى ٱلتَّهۡلُكَةِ وَأَحۡسِنُوٓاْۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحِبُّ ٱلۡمُحۡسِنِينَ﴾ [Al-Baqarah 2:195]

### Three commands
| Command | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Spend in Allah's cause | Support the community and the religion |
| Do not throw yourselves into destruction | Avoid what ruins you |
| Do good (*ihsan*) | Excellence in everything |

### Abu Ayyub's explanation
- During a battle at Constantinople, a Muslim charged into the enemy lines and people said he threw himself into destruction. Abu Ayyub al-Ansari said the verse was revealed about the Ansar when they considered staying with their wealth and leaving spending and striving; **that** was the destruction (narrated by Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi).

### Wider meanings
- Scholars also include: despairing of forgiveness, harming one's health, and reckless risk.

### *Ihsan*
- The verse ends: *Allah loves those who do good.* Spending is best done with excellence and sincerity.

### Lessons
- **Holding back good** can be its own destruction.
- **Protect your life and faith**, and spend for good causes.

## "Take Provision, and the Best Provision Is Taqwa": Quran 2:197 Explained

﴿ٱلۡحَجُّ أَشۡهُرࣱ مَّعۡلُومَٰتࣱۚ فَمَن فَرَضَ فِيهِنَّ ٱلۡحَجَّ فَلَا رَفَثَ وَلَا فُسُوقَ وَلَا جِدَالَ فِي ٱلۡحَجِّۗ وَمَا تَفۡعَلُواْ مِنۡ خَيۡرٖ يَعۡلَمۡهُ ٱللَّهُۗ وَتَزَوَّدُواْ فَإِنَّ خَيۡرَ ٱلزَّادِ ٱلتَّقۡوَىٰۖ وَٱتَّقُونِ يَـٰٓأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَلۡبَٰبِ﴾ [Al-Baqarah 2:197]

### Three prohibitions in Hajj
| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Rafath* | Marital relations and indecent talk |
| *Fusuq* | Sins and disobedience |
| *Jidal* | Arguing and disputes |

### Take provision
- Some people used to travel for Hajj without supplies, saying they relied on Allah, and then begged from others. The verse commanded them to **take provision**, as reported by al-Bukhari from Ibn ʿAbbas.

### The best provision
- Then the verse lifts the meaning: food is for the journey of Hajj, but **taqwa** is for the journey to Allah.

### Hajj as a model of life
- Life is a journey with a destination. The traveller who carries taqwa arrives safely.

### "O people of understanding"
- Taqwa is addressed to the wise, because the intelligent person prepares for what comes next.

### Lessons
- **Control your tongue** in crowded and stressful places.
- **Prepare for the Hereafter** as you prepare for travel.
