Scroll through social media and you will find it: a beautiful voice reciting moving verses, with soft piano music in the background, or the sound of rain and thunder. In another clip, a verse opens the video, then it cuts suddenly to a song, then back to a verse. Others place recitations behind commercial adverts, or over images that are not appropriate.

Many content creators do this with good intentions: to spread the Quran and reach young people. But is it allowed? And what guidelines preserve the sanctity of the Quran in the digital world?

## The Status of the Quran
The Quran is the speech of Allah and has a great sanctity. Allah says:

﴿وَإِذَا قُرِئَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانُ فَٱسۡتَمِعُواْ لَهُۥ وَأَنصِتُواْ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تُرۡحَمُونَ﴾ (Quran 7:204)

The basic etiquette when the Quran is recited is **to listen and be silent**, not to make it a background to something else or to mix it with what distracts from it.

Allah also says:

﴿ذَٰلِكَۖ وَمَن يُعَظِّمۡ شَعَـٰٓئِرَ ٱللَّهِ فَإِنَّهَا مِن تَقۡوَى ٱلۡقُلُوبِ﴾ (Quran 22:32)

Honouring the Quran is among the greatest of Allah's symbols.

## Mixing the Quran with Music
### Musical instruments according to the majority
The **majority of scholars** from the four schools hold that **musical instruments are prohibited** in general, citing hadiths such as the one al-Bukhari reported in a suspended but definitive form from Abu Malik al-Ash'ari, that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There will be people from my nation who will consider lawful fornication, silk, wine and musical instruments." Some scholars differed on certain forms of listening.

### Quran with music
As for **mixing the Quran with music**, scholars and fatwa councils have held it to be **prohibited**, even among those who are lenient about some forms of listening, because:
1. **It belittles the Quran** by pairing it with what is used for entertainment.
2. **It contradicts the command to listen**, since music distracts from reflection.
3. **It treats the Quran like a song**, heard in the same way songs are heard.
4. **It adds what is not Quran to the Quran**, confusing ordinary listeners about what is recitation and what is addition.

This is not something to be taken lightly, even with a good intention.

## Sound Effects: Rain and Nature
More common today are **sound effects**: rain, waves, birdsong, wind. These are not musical instruments, but contemporary scholars differ:
- **Many scholars** consider them **disliked or not permitted** with recitation, because they:
  - Distract the listener from attentive listening.
  - Turn the Quran into a background for an audio experience, rather than something listened to for its own sake.
  - Open the door to further additions.
  - Were not the practice of the early generations in listening to the Quran.
- **Some are lenient** about very light natural sounds that do not overpower the recitation.

**Better and safer**: keep the Quran pure. The beauty of the reciter's voice and the greatness of the words are enough to move hearts; the Quran does not need effects.

## Cutting Verses
Forms of unintended disrespect include:
- **Cutting a verse in the middle** in a way that changes the meaning or implies something false, such as stopping at "do not approach prayer".
- **Mixing verses with song clips** or entertainment in one video.
- **Using verses in a mocking context** or as a joke.
- **Repeating part of a verse** in a musical "remix" style.

The rule: **choose a passage complete in meaning**, beginning where it is good to start and ending where it is good to stop.

## Images with Recitation
- **Avoid unlawful images**, such as immodest pictures or inappropriate scenes.
- **Avoid exaggerated images of living beings** in the context of the Quran, according to those who prohibit or dislike them.
- **Avoid mismatches**: such as verses of punishment over comic scenes.
- **Suitable images**: nature, the sky, mosques, Arabic calligraphy and the text of the verses.

## The Quran in Adverts
Using verses in **commercial adverts** to promote products is blameworthy, because it makes Allah's words a tool of trade and may place verses in unsuitable contexts. Advertising **Quran-related products**, such as Mushafs or memorisation courses, respectfully and without music, is fine.

## Ringtones
Many scholars **disliked using verses as phone ringtones**, because:
- They are cut off when answering.
- The phone may ring in unsuitable places such as bathrooms.
- They turn the Quran into an alert signal rather than words recited and reflected upon.

## The Difference Between Beautifying and Singing
People sometimes confuse **beautifying the voice** with the Quran and **musical tunes**:
- **Beautifying the voice** is encouraged. In Sahih al-Bukhari: "He is not one of us who does not beautify his voice with the Quran." And Abu Dawud reported: "Adorn the Quran with your voices." A reciter beautifies his voice and recites with emotion, while keeping the rules of tajweed.
- **Blameworthy tunes** are reciting the Quran to **the melodies of songs** or with music, or stretching letters and vowels for the sake of melody until the rules of tajweed are broken. Scholars condemned this.

As for **vocal modes** (*maqamat*) some reciters learn to improve their delivery, scholars differ: some disliked them, others permitted them provided recitation does not leave its rules, the reciter does not affect, and melody does not distract from meaning.

## Mockery of the Verses
One of the most dangerous forms is using verses **in a mocking or humorous context**, quoting them in a comic clip or distorting their words as a joke. Allah says:

﴿وَلَئِن سَأَلۡتَهُمۡ لَيَقُولُنَّ إِنَّمَا كُنَّا نَخُوضُ وَنَلۡعَبُۚ قُلۡ أَبِٱللَّهِ وَءَايَٰتِهِۦ وَرَسُولِهِۦ كُنتُمۡ تَسۡتَهۡزِءُونَ﴾ ﴿لَا تَعۡتَذِرُواْ قَدۡ كَفَرۡتُم بَعۡدَ إِيمَٰنِكُمۡۚ إِن نَّعۡفُ عَن طَآئِفَةٖ مِّنكُمۡ نُعَذِّبۡ طَآئِفَةَۢ بِأَنَّهُمۡ كَانُواْ مُجۡرِمِينَ﴾ (Quran 9:65-66)

Mocking Allah's verses is among the most serious sins, and done deliberately it can take a person out of Islam. Be extremely careful with humorous content involving the Quran.

## Guidelines for Sharing Recitation Online
1. **Pure audio**: recitation without music or effects.
2. **A complete passage**: beginning and ending at correct places.
3. **A skilled reciter**: who observes tajweed; do not share recitations with mistakes.
4. **Correct text**: if verses appear on screen, use the Uthmani script from a reliable source and check it.
5. **A reliable translation**: if you add one, choose an approved translation.
6. **Suitable images**.
7. **No mixing** of Quran with songs or entertainment in the same clip.
8. **Cite the surah and verse number**.
9. **A sincere intention**: spreading good, not only seeking fame.

## Better Alternatives That Still Move Hearts
The Quran moves hearts by itself and needs no music. Ideas that are effective and permissible:
- **Pure, moving recitation** by a skilled reciter.
- **Verses with translation and brief tafsir** on screen.
- **Reflection videos**: someone speaks about a verse and its effect on their life, then recites it.
- **Beautiful Arabic calligraphy** with the recitation.
- **Silent nature footage** with the recitation, without sound effects.
- **Educational clips**: tajweed rules or Quranic stories.
- **"Verse and reflection" series**, **memorisation challenges**, and **stories of the Quran for children**.

## If You Receive Such a Clip
- **Do not reshare it**.
- **Advise the creator gently** if you know them.
- **Find the same recitation** in pure audio from a reliable source.

## The Quran in Public Places
Another form of not listening is playing the Quran loudly in markets, shops and restaurants amid noise, talk and trade. Many scholars disliked this, because the Quran is recited while no one listens. It is better to play the Quran where people can listen, and to lower or stop it when the noise is heavy, out of respect.

## The Responsibility of Large Platforms
Large Islamic channels and platforms carry greater responsibility, because millions imitate them. If a large channel publishes recitation with effects, people assume it is acceptable. Such platforms should commit to the guidelines in everything they publish, raise awareness about respecting the Quran, and have Quran content reviewed by specialists before publication.

## A Note for Reciters
Reciters whose recordings are shared widely also have a responsibility: to follow what is published with their voices, to remind followers not to add music, and to permit sharing on condition the recitation remains pure. Many reciters' recordings are spread with music without their knowledge, and a clear statement from them would stop much of it.

## Three Questions Before You Post
1. Is the recitation free of music and effects?
2. Is the passage complete in meaning, with correct pronunciation and text?
3. Are the images and context fitting for Allah's words?

If the answer to all three is yes, post with peace of mind, and you may be rewarded for everyone who benefits. If not, edit it or do not post it: the sanctity of the Quran comes before view counts.

## The Power of a Beautiful Voice
Remember how the Companions would gather to listen to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari's recitation, and the Prophet ﷺ told him, as in al-Bukhari and Muslim: "You have been given one of the flutes of the family of Dawud," meaning a beautiful voice. The beauty of his voice with the Quran alone was enough to gather people, without any addition.

## The Role of Parents
Children and teenagers today spend hours on platforms and see clips mixing Quran with music, assuming it is normal. Parents can teach the difference between pure recitation and inappropriate clips, choose reliable Quran apps and channels for their children, encourage those with talent to create permissible Quran content, and set an example by listening to the Quran attentively.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Is it allowed to put music behind Quran recitation?
No. Scholars and fatwa councils hold it is prohibited, as it belittles the Quran and contradicts the command to listen.

### Can I add rain sounds to recitation?
Many scholars dislike or prohibit it, and some are lenient about very light sounds; it is safer to keep the Quran pure.

### Can I use a verse as my ringtone?
Many scholars disliked it, because it gets cut off and may ring in unsuitable places.

### How should I share Quran on social media?
Pure audio, a complete passage, a skilled reciter, correct text and suitable images.

### Is cutting verses in a video allowed?
Choosing a complete passage is fine; cutting in a way that changes the meaning, or mixing with songs, is not.

### Does the Quran need effects to move people?
No. The Quran moves hearts by itself.

### What three questions should I ask before posting a Quran clip?
Is the recitation pure? Is the passage complete and correct? Are the images and context fitting?

### Is it fine to play Quran in shops and markets?
Many scholars disliked it when no one listens amid noise and trade.

### How can parents help their children with Quran content online?
Teach the difference, choose reliable apps and channels, encourage permissible creativity, and set an example of attentive listening.

## Conclusion
The Quran is the speech of Allah, recited pure and listened to attentively. It must not be mixed with music; it is safer to leave sound effects aside; it should not be cut in a way that changes meaning or blended with songs; and it should not be used in commercial adverts. Content creators have powerful, permissible alternatives: pure recitation, translation and tafsir, calligraphy and reflection. The Quran needs nothing from outside to adorn it; its own beauty is enough.
