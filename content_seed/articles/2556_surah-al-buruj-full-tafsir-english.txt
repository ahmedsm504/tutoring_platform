Surah Al-Buruj was revealed in Makkah when the early Muslims were being tortured for their faith. To strengthen them, Allah told them the story of believers in an earlier nation who faced something even worse: they were thrown into a ditch of fire because they believed in Allah, and they did not turn back. The surah does not hide the cost of faith, but it shows the outcome: "That is the great success." It also reminds the oppressors that Allah's grip is severe, and in the same breath tells them that He is the Forgiving, the Loving, if they repent.

This guide explains Surah Al-Buruj verse by verse in English, with the authentic story of the boy, the king and the monk from Sahih Muslim.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Buruj
- **Number of verses**: 22.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 85, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: Al-Buruj means "the great stars" or "the constellations", from the first verse.
- **Recited by the Prophet ﷺ**: at-Tirmidhi reported from Jabir ibn Samurah that the Prophet ﷺ used to recite in Zuhr and 'Asr "By the sky with its great stars" and "By the sky and the night-comer", and at-Tirmidhi graded it *hasan sahih*.

## Verses 1–3: The Oaths
﴿وَٱلسَّمَآءِ ذَاتِ ٱلۡبُرُوجِ﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡيَوۡمِ ٱلۡمَوۡعُودِ﴾ ﴿وَشَاهِدٖ وَمَشۡهُودٖ﴾ (Quran 85:1-3)

1. **"By the sky with its great stars"** (*al-buruj*): the great stars and their positions.
2. **"And the promised Day"**: the Day of Resurrection, which Allah has promised.
3. **"And the witness and the witnessed"**: commentators gave several explanations: the witness is Friday and the witnessed is the Day of 'Arafah; or the witness is Allah's messengers and the witnessed is their nations; or the witness is the believers who saw the burning and the witnessed is what was done to them. All are within the meaning.

**The connection**: the oaths prepare for a story about something that was witnessed, and a Day on which it will be judged.

## Verses 4–9: The People of the Ditch
﴿قُتِلَ أَصۡحَٰبُ ٱلۡأُخۡدُودِ﴾ ﴿ٱلنَّارِ ذَاتِ ٱلۡوَقُودِ﴾ ﴿إِذۡ هُمۡ عَلَيۡهَا قُعُودࣱ﴾ ﴿وَهُمۡ عَلَىٰ مَا يَفۡعَلُونَ بِٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ شُهُودࣱ﴾ ﴿وَمَا نَقَمُواْ مِنۡهُمۡ إِلَّآ أَن يُؤۡمِنُواْ بِٱللَّهِ ٱلۡعَزِيزِ ٱلۡحَمِيدِ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي لَهُۥ مُلۡكُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِۚ وَٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيۡءٖ شَهِيدٌ﴾ (Quran 85:4-9)

**"Cursed were the People of the Ditch"** (*ashab al-ukhdud*): those who dug a long trench, filled it with fuel and lit a great fire, then threw the believers into it. **"While they sat by it"**, watching. **"And they were witnesses to what they did to the believers"**: they watched people burn without mercy.

**"And they resented them for nothing except that they believed in Allah, the Almighty, the Praiseworthy."** The believers committed no crime. Their only "offence" was their faith. And notice the two names: *al-'Aziz*, the Almighty who is never overcome, and *al-Hamid*, the Praiseworthy who is praised in all His decrees. The oppressors seemed to win, but the One the believers worshipped is the Almighty.

## The Story of the Boy, the King and the Monk
The story of the People of the Ditch is reported in detail in Sahih Muslim from Suhayb, from the Prophet ﷺ. In summary:

1. **A king had a magician**, who, growing old, asked the king to send him a boy to teach magic.
2. **On his way to the magician**, the boy passed a monk, sat with him and was impressed by his words. He began to visit both.
3. **One day a huge beast blocked the people's path.** The boy took a stone and said: "O Allah, if the monk's way is more beloved to You than the magician's, kill this beast so the people can pass." He threw it and the beast was killed. The monk told him: "My son, today you are better than me. You will be tested; if you are, do not reveal me."
4. **The boy began to heal the blind and the leper** by Allah's permission. A courtier of the king who had gone blind came to him; the boy said: "I do not heal anyone; Allah heals. If you believe in Allah, I will pray to Him and He will heal you." The man believed and was healed.
5. **The king found out** and tortured the courtier until he revealed the boy, then tortured the boy until he revealed the monk. The monk and the courtier were killed for refusing to leave their religion.
6. **The king tried to kill the boy**: he sent him up a mountain to be thrown off, and the mountain shook and the soldiers fell; he sent him out to sea to be drowned, and the boat capsized and the soldiers drowned. Each time the boy returned unharmed.
7. **The boy told the king**: "You will not be able to kill me until you do what I tell you. Gather the people in one place, crucify me on a tree trunk, take an arrow from my quiver, place it in the bow and say: *In the name of Allah, the Lord of the boy*, then shoot me." The king did so, and the boy died.
8. **The people all said**: "We believe in the Lord of the boy." The king's plan had turned the whole crowd into believers.
9. **So the king had ditches dug** at the entrances of the roads, fires lit in them, and ordered that whoever did not abandon his religion be thrown in. A woman came with her baby and hesitated; the baby said: "O mother, be patient, for you are upon the truth."

**Lessons from the story**:
- **Faith may be planted in a young heart** and outgrow its teachers.
- **Healing and every blessing come from Allah**, and the believer never claims them for himself.
- **The boy gave his life to show the truth**, and his death brought a whole people to faith.
- **Victory is not always survival**: sometimes victory is holding firm until the end.

## Verses 10–11: Repentance and the Great Success
﴿إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ فَتَنُواْ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ وَٱلۡمُؤۡمِنَٰتِ ثُمَّ لَمۡ يَتُوبُواْ فَلَهُمۡ عَذَابُ جَهَنَّمَ وَلَهُمۡ عَذَابُ ٱلۡحَرِيقِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ لَهُمۡ جَنَّـٰتࣱ تَجۡرِي مِن تَحۡتِهَا ٱلۡأَنۡهَٰرُۚ ذَٰلِكَ ٱلۡفَوۡزُ ٱلۡكَبِيرُ﴾ (Quran 85:10-11)

**"Indeed, those who persecuted the believing men and women and then did not repent"**: notice the words "then did not repent". Even after torturing and burning believers, the door of repentance was still open to them. Al-Hasan al-Basri reportedly said: "Look at this generosity: they killed His friends, and He invites them to repentance." If they do not repent, they will have **the punishment of Hell and the punishment of burning**.

**"Indeed, those who believe and do righteous deeds will have gardens beneath which rivers flow. That is the great success."** The believers burnt in the ditch were not the losers. Allah calls their outcome *al-fawz al-kabir*, the great success.

## Verses 12–16: The Names of Allah
﴿إِنَّ بَطۡشَ رَبِّكَ لَشَدِيدٌ﴾ ﴿إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ يُبۡدِئُ وَيُعِيدُ﴾ ﴿وَهُوَ ٱلۡغَفُورُ ٱلۡوَدُودُ﴾ ﴿ذُو ٱلۡعَرۡشِ ٱلۡمَجِيدُ﴾ ﴿فَعَّالࣱ لِّمَا يُرِيدُ﴾ (Quran 85:12-16)

- **"The grip of your Lord is severe"**: when He seizes the oppressors, no one can escape.
- **"He originates and repeats"**: He created the first time and will bring back.
- **"He is the Forgiving, the Loving"** (*al-Ghafur al-Wadud*): He forgives the sins of those who repent and loves His believing servants and is loved by them. Placing these names right after "severe grip" shows the balance between fear and hope.
- **"Owner of the Throne, the Glorious"**.
- **"Doer of whatever He wills"**: nothing restrains His will.

## Verses 17–20: Pharaoh and Thamud
﴿هَلۡ أَتَىٰكَ حَدِيثُ ٱلۡجُنُودِ﴾ ﴿فِرۡعَوۡنَ وَثَمُودَ﴾ ﴿بَلِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ فِي تَكۡذِيبٖ﴾ ﴿وَٱللَّهُ مِن وَرَآئِهِم مُّحِيطُۢ﴾ (Quran 85:17-20)

**"Has the story of the armies reached you, Pharaoh and Thamud?"** Two powerful nations who denied, and both were destroyed: Pharaoh by drowning and Thamud by the blast. **"And Allah encompasses them from behind"**: they cannot escape Him.

## Verses 21–22: The Glorious Quran
﴿بَلۡ هُوَ قُرۡءَانࣱ مَّجِيدࣱ﴾ ﴿فِي لَوۡحٖ مَّحۡفُوظِۭ﴾ (Quran 85:21-22)

**"Rather, it is a glorious Quran, in a Preserved Tablet."** The surah ends with the Quran itself: glorious and noble, preserved in *al-Lawh al-Mahfuz*, where nothing can change it. The stories of tyrants pass; the Quran remains.

## Word Meanings
- **Al-Buruj**: the great stars or their positions.
- **Al-Yawm al-Maw'ud**: the promised Day.
- **Shahid wa mashhud**: the witness and the witnessed.
- **Al-Ukhdud**: a long ditch or trench dug in the ground.
- **Dhat al-waqud**: full of fuel.
- **Naqamu**: they resented, they held against them.
- **Fatanu**: they persecuted, tortured.
- **Al-Fawz al-Kabir**: the great success.
- **Batsh**: grip, violent seizure.
- **Al-Wadud**: the Loving.
- **Al-Lawh al-Mahfuz**: the Preserved Tablet.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"Cursed were the People of the Ditch"**: the story begins with its verdict.
2. **"For nothing except that they believed"**: irony that exposes the oppressors.
3. **"Then did not repent"**: a small phrase that reveals enormous mercy.
4. **"Severe grip" beside "the Loving"**: fear and hope side by side.
5. **"The great success"**: redefining victory for the persecuted.
6. **Ending with the Preserved Tablet**: what Allah preserves cannot be burnt.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Madd muttasil**: in *as-sama'i* (verse 1).
- **Qalqalah kubra**: when stopping on *al-buruj* (verse 1), the jim at the end bounces strongly.
- **Qalqalah sughra**: in *batsha* (verse 12), the heavy ṭa' sakinah bounces lightly.
- **Izhar**: in *minhum* (verse 8), the noon sakinah before ha' is pronounced clearly.
- **Ghunnah**: in *inna* (verses 10–12), on the noon with shaddah.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Surah Al-Inshiqaq before it ended with those who do not prostrate when the Quran is recited, and Al-Buruj shows believers who would rather burn than leave their faith. Surah At-Tariq after it continues with an oath by the sky and promises: "Indeed, they are plotting a plot, and I am plotting a plan."

## Surah Al-Buruj for People Facing Pressure Today
1. **Your faith is your most precious possession**: the believers in the story gave their lives rather than lose it.
2. **Allah sees what is done to you**: He is "a witness over all things".
3. **Victory has a wider meaning**: holding firm is success.
4. **Oppressors are not beyond repentance**: pray for their guidance as well as for justice.
5. **Allah is the Loving**: feel His love in your difficulties.

## Practical Lessons
1. **Hold firm to your faith**: whatever pressure you face.
2. **Teach children the story of the boy**: faith can be strong at any age.
3. **Never attribute blessings to yourself**: say, as the boy did, "Allah heals."
4. **Do not despair of anyone's repentance**.
5. **Balance fear and hope**: His grip is severe and He is the Loving.
6. **Recite this surah in your prayers**: the Prophet ﷺ did.

## How to Memorise Surah Al-Buruj
- **Day 1**: verses 1–9.
- **Day 2**: verses 10–16.
- **Day 3**: verses 17–22, then revise the whole surah.

## Teaching Children
- **Tell them the story of the boy and the king** from Sahih Muslim, focusing on his courage and his trust in Allah.
- **Teach them the name al-Wadud**: Allah loves the believers.
- **Point out the stars** and read "By the sky with its great stars".

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Who were the People of the Ditch?
People in an earlier nation who dug ditches of fire and threw believers into them for refusing to abandon their faith.

### Where is the story of the boy and the king reported?
In Sahih Muslim from Suhayb, from the Prophet ﷺ.

### What does "the witness and the witnessed" mean?
Several explanations are given, including Friday and the Day of 'Arafah, or the messengers and their nations.

### Why does the surah say "then did not repent"?
To show that even those who tortured believers could still repent and be forgiven.

### What does al-Wadud mean?
The Loving: Allah loves His believing servants and is loved by them.

### What is the Preserved Tablet?
*Al-Lawh al-Mahfuz*, where Allah has preserved the Quran and all decrees.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Buruj?
22 verses, and it is a Meccan surah.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Buruj tells the story of people who lost their lives and won "the great success". It shows that oppressors may seem to win for a while, but Allah's grip is severe, and that even they could have found forgiveness from the Forgiving, the Loving. Hold firm to your faith, because what Allah preserves no fire can burn.
