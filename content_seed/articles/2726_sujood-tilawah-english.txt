You are reading the Quran at home and reach a small symbol in the margin marking a prostration. Questions follow immediately: Should I prostrate now? I am not in wudu; is that allowed? What do I say? Do I say "Allahu akbar"? Do I end with salam? And if a recording in the car reaches a prostration verse, do I need to do anything?

The **prostration of recitation** (*sujood al-tilawah*) is one of the most beautiful acts of humility in Islam, yet many Muslims are unsure of its rules and so skip it. This guide gathers its rulings in a practical way.

## Its Virtue
In Sahih Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "When the son of Adam recites a verse of prostration and prostrates, Shaytan withdraws, weeping, and says: Woe to me! The son of Adam was commanded to prostrate and he prostrated, so Paradise is his; I was commanded to prostrate and I refused, so the Fire is mine."

So this prostration is an expression of servitude, obedience to Allah's command, and a direct contrast with Iblis, who refused to prostrate.

## Is It Obligatory?
- **The majority** (Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali schools): it is a **confirmed Sunnah**, not obligatory.
- **The Hanafi school**: it is **obligatory** (*wajib*) for the reciter and the listener.

Among the majority's evidence is the report in Sahih al-Bukhari that 'Umar ibn al-Khattab recited Surah An-Nahl from the pulpit on a Friday, came down at the prostration verse and prostrated, and the people prostrated with him. The following Friday he recited it again, reached the prostration and said: "O people, we pass by verses of prostration. Whoever prostrates has done well, and whoever does not, there is no sin on him." And he did not prostrate. This happened in the presence of the Companions, and no one objected.

Also in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Zayd ibn Thabit recited Surah An-Najm to the Prophet ﷺ and he did not prostrate in it.

### Places scholars differ on
- **Surah Sad**: Ibn 'Abbas said, as reported by al-Bukhari, "Sad is not one of the emphasised prostrations, but I saw the Prophet ﷺ prostrate in it." Some scholars consider it a prostration of gratitude rather than of recitation.
- **The second prostration in Al-Hajj**: counted by the Shafi'is and Hanbalis, but not by the Hanafis and, in the well-known view, the Malikis, because its context is the command to bow and prostrate in prayer.
- **The three in the short surahs** (An-Najm, Al-Inshiqaq, Al-'Alaq): the well-known Maliki view is not to prostrate in them, but the majority do, and the Prophet ﷺ is authentically reported to have prostrated in An-Najm and Al-Inshiqaq (al-Bukhari and Muslim) and in Al-'Alaq (Muslim).

Two examples of prostration verses:

﴿إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ عِندَ رَبِّكَ لَا يَسۡتَكۡبِرُونَ عَنۡ عِبَادَتِهِۦ وَيُسَبِّحُونَهُۥ وَلَهُۥ يَسۡجُدُونَۤ۩﴾ (Quran 7:206)

﴿كَلَّا لَا تُطِعۡهُ وَٱسۡجُدۡۤ وَٱقۡتَرِب۩﴾ (Quran 96:19)

## How to Perform It Outside Prayer
1. **Intend** the prostration of recitation.
2. **Say "Allahu akbar"** as you go down, according to the majority.
3. **Make one prostration**, like the prostration of prayer.
4. **Say what is said in prostration**: "Subhana Rabbiyal-A'la", along with the supplications reported for this prostration.
5. **Rise**, saying "Allahu akbar" according to some scholars.
6. **Salam**: scholars differ on whether to end with salam; the matter is flexible.

You may prostrate from standing or sitting; some scholars preferred going down from a standing position.

## How to Perform It Inside Prayer
1. When the imam or a person praying alone recites a prostration verse, he **says "Allahu akbar" and prostrates**.
2. He says the remembrances of prostration.
3. He **says "Allahu akbar" and stands**, continuing his recitation, or bowing after a few verses.
4. **The follower follows the imam**: if the imam prostrates, he prostrates; if not, he does not.

### In silent prayers
Scholars differed about the imam prostrating for recitation in a silent prayer such as Zuhr. Some disliked it because it may confuse the congregation, who may think he forgot to bow; others permitted it. The imam should avoid what confuses his followers.

### Fajr on Friday
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ used to recite Surah As-Sajdah in the first rak'ah of Fajr on Friday, and it contains a prostration, so he prostrated in it.

## What to Say
- **"Subhana Rabbiyal-A'la"** (Glory be to my Lord, the Most High), as in the prayer's prostration.
- **"Sajada wajhi lilladhi khalaqahu, wa shaqqa sam'ahu wa basarahu, bi hawlihi wa quwwatih"** (My face has prostrated to the One who created it and brought forth its hearing and sight by His might and power). Reported by Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi from 'A'ishah, and at-Tirmidhi graded it authentic. Some narrations add "fa tabarak Allahu ahsanul-khaliqin".
- **"Allahummaktub li biha 'indaka ajran, wa da' 'anni biha wizran, waj'alha li 'indaka dhukhran, wa taqabbalha minni kama taqabbaltaha min 'abdika Dawud"** (O Allah, record for me a reward for it with You, remove from me a sin by it, store it for me with You, and accept it from me as You accepted it from Your servant Dawud). Reported by at-Tirmidhi from Ibn 'Abbas, and graded hasan by some scholars.
- **Any supplication** you wish.

## Do You Need Wudu and to Face the Qiblah?
### The majority view
The **majority of scholars** hold that the prostration of recitation requires **what prayer requires**: purity, covering the 'awrah and facing the qiblah, because it is a prostration and takes the ruling of prayer's prostration.

### The other view
Some scholars, including **Ibn 'Umar**, whom al-Bukhari mentions without a chain used to prostrate without wudu, and **Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn al-Qayyim** and **ash-Shawkani**, held that **wudu is not required**, because it is not a prayer but an act of humility, and there is no authentic evidence requiring purity for it.

### In practice
- **Better**: be in wudu and face the qiblah.
- **If you are not in wudu**, you may follow those who do not require it, or you may leave the prostration, since it is Sunnah according to the majority.

## Who Should Prostrate?
### The reciter
It is Sunnah for the reciter according to the majority.

### The listener
The **listener** (*mustami'*) is the one who intentionally listens to the reciter. It is Sunnah for him to prostrate if the reciter prostrates, as indicated by the hadith of Zayd ibn Thabit: when he recited An-Najm and did not prostrate, the Prophet ﷺ did not prostrate either. And in al-Bukhari, Ibn 'Umar said: "The Prophet ﷺ would recite to us a surah with a prostration, and he would prostrate and we would prostrate."

### The passer-by
The **hearer** (*sami'*) who hears recitation incidentally, without intending to listen, such as someone walking past, is not emphatically required to prostrate according to most scholars.

## Recordings
If you hear a prostration verse from a recording, radio or television:
- **Many scholars** hold that prostration is **not prescribed**, because a recording is not a live recitation by a present reciter.
- **Some** permit it for those who wish.

The matter is flexible: if you prostrate, there is no harm; if you do not, there is no harm.

## Repeating the Verse
If you repeat a prostration verse several times in one sitting, for example while memorising it, **one prostration is enough** according to many scholars. You may prostrate after the first or the last repetition.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Never prostrating** outside prayer.
2. **Condemning those who do not prostrate**, although it is Sunnah for the majority.
3. **Prostrating in places that are not prostration verses**.
4. **Believing that full prayer-style takbir and salam are obligatory**.
5. **The follower not following the imam** in prostration.

## Reflections on the Prostration Verses
Each prostration verse carries its own meaning of humility. Reflecting on them turns the prostration from a movement into worship of the heart:
- **Al-A'raf**: the angels near their Lord, who are not too proud to worship Him; you prostrate in imitation of them.
- **Ar-Ra'd, An-Nahl and Al-Hajj**: the whole universe prostrating to Allah, the shadows, the sun, moon, stars, mountains, trees and animals; you join a prostrating creation.
- **Al-Isra' and Maryam**: the knowledgeable and the prophets falling in prostration and weeping when they hear Allah's verses.

﴿أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنۡعَمَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيۡهِم مِّنَ ٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَ مِن ذُرِّيَّةِ ءَادَمَ وَمِمَّنۡ حَمَلۡنَا مَعَ نُوحٖ وَمِن ذُرِّيَّةِ إِبۡرَٰهِيمَ وَإِسۡرَـٰٓءِيلَ وَمِمَّنۡ هَدَيۡنَا وَٱجۡتَبَيۡنَآۚ إِذَا تُتۡلَىٰ عَلَيۡهِمۡ ءَايَٰتُ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ خَرُّواْۤ سُجَّدٗاۤ وَبُكِيّٗا۩﴾ (Quran 19:58)

- **Al-Furqan**: the disbelievers who said "What is the Most Merciful?" when told to prostrate; you prostrate in contrast to them.
- **An-Naml**: the hoopoe who objected to the people of Saba' prostrating to the sun instead of Allah.
- **As-Sajdah**: the believers who fall in prostration when reminded of Allah's verses.
- **Sad**: the repentance of Dawud, who fell down bowing and turned back to Allah.
- **Fussilat**: "Do not prostrate to the sun or the moon, but prostrate to Allah who created them."
- **An-Najm**: "So prostrate to Allah and worship Him." The Prophet ﷺ prostrated at the end of it, and so did the Muslims and even the polytheists present, as in al-Bukhari.
- **Al-Inshiqaq**: "When the Quran is recited to them, they do not prostrate"; you do.
- **Al-'Alaq**: "Prostrate and draw near"; prostration is nearness.

## The Prostration of Gratitude
A related act is the **prostration of gratitude** (*sujood ash-shukr*) when a blessing comes or a harm is averted. Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi reported from Abu Bakrah that when something pleasing came to the Prophet ﷺ, he would fall down in prostration in gratitude to Allah. And Ka'b ibn Malik prostrated when he was given the good news that Allah had accepted his repentance, as in al-Bukhari and Muslim. Many scholars apply the same conditions and manner to it as to the prostration of recitation.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Is sujood al-tilawah obligatory?
A confirmed Sunnah according to the majority; obligatory according to the Hanafis.

### How many prostrations are in the Quran?
Fifteen in the Madinah Mushaf, with scholarly difference about some.

### Do I need wudu for sujood al-tilawah?
The majority require it; some scholars such as Ibn Taymiyyah do not. Wudu is better.

### What do I say in it?
"Subhana Rabbiyal-A'la" and "Sajada wajhi lilladhi khalaqahu wa shaqqa sam'ahu wa basarahu bi hawlihi wa quwwatih."

### Do I prostrate when I hear a recording?
Many scholars say it is not prescribed; the matter is flexible.

### Do I prostrate each time I repeat the verse?
One prostration is enough for the sitting.

### What is sujood ash-shukr?
A single prostration of gratitude when a blessing comes or a harm is averted, as the Prophet ﷺ did.

## Conclusion
The prostration of recitation is a moment at which Shaytan weeps and the believer draws near to Allah. It is a confirmed Sunnah according to the majority, with fifteen places in the Mushaf. You say "Allahu akbar", make one prostration, glorify Allah and say "My face has prostrated to the One who created it", then rise. Being in wudu is better, and scholars differ on whether it is required. The reciter and the listener prostrate, and one prostration covers a repeated verse. Do not pass a prostration mark without stopping; it is a moment of closeness you will not want to miss.
