At a reconciliation between two families, a dispute between business partners, or an argument between spouses, one person says to the other: "Swear on the Quran." A Mushaf is brought, the other person places a hand on it and swears. Many people believe that such an oath is greater than others, and that whoever swears falsely on the Mushaf will be struck by a swift calamity. Others ask: is this practice actually from Islam, or just custom?

This guide answers: Is swearing by the Quran a valid oath? What is the difference between swearing by the Quran and by the Mushaf? Must you place your hand on it? What is the expiation for breaking an oath? And how serious is a false oath?

## What Is an Islamic Oath?
An oath (*yamin*) is **confirming a statement by mentioning something revered in a specific way**. The basic rule is that an oath is **by Allah or by one of His attributes**: "Wallahi", "Billahi", "Tallahi", or "by the might of Allah", "by the power of Allah".

### Swearing by other than Allah
Swearing by other than Allah is **prohibited**. At-Tirmidhi reported, and graded hasan, from Ibn 'Umar that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever swears by other than Allah has disbelieved or associated partners." And in al-Bukhari and Muslim: "Allah forbids you to swear by your fathers; whoever swears, let him swear by Allah or remain silent."

So one may not swear by the Prophet ﷺ, the Ka'bah, one's honour, one's children or one's life, nor by "trust" (*amanah*) according to many scholars.

## Swearing by the Quran
**The Quran is the speech of Allah**, and His speech is one of His attributes. So swearing by the Quran is **swearing by an attribute of Allah**, and it is **a valid, binding oath** according to the majority of scholars from the Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali schools, and the later Hanafis gave fatwa accordingly because it became customary.

So whoever says "By the Mighty Quran" or "By the speech of Allah" has sworn a valid oath, with all its consequences.

## Swearing by the Mushaf
The word **Mushaf** refers to the paper, binding and ink on which the Quran is written, and also to the Quran written in it. So scholars distinguished:
- **If the person intends the Quran** in the Mushaf, Allah's written speech, it is **a valid oath**, and this is usually what people intend.
- **If he intends the paper, binding and ink**, it is not an Islamic oath, because these are created things.

Since Muslims generally mean the Quran when they swear by the Mushaf, many scholars considered it a binding oath.

## Placing Your Hand on the Mushaf
### Is there a basis for it?
**It is not reported from the Prophet ﷺ** that he asked anyone to place a hand on the Mushaf to swear. It was not known in his time, as the Quran had not yet been compiled into a single book during his life.

### Emphasising oaths in court
Some jurists, including the Shafi'is, mentioned that a judge may **emphasise an oath** in serious cases, by time (such as after 'Asr), by place (such as near the pulpit), and by wording (mentioning Allah's attributes). Some included **bringing the Mushaf** and placing it in the person's lap. This was reported from some early judges, while others objected that it has no basis in the Sunnah.

### In summary
- **Placing your hand on the Mushaf is not required** for an oath to be valid; an oath is established by saying "Wallahi" or "By the Quran".
- **People should not be pressured** into it in everyday disputes, nor should one believe that an oath without it is worthless.
- **In court**, this is left to the judge's assessment of recognised means of emphasis.

## Is an Oath on the Mushaf Greater?
Every oath by Allah or His attributes is **a weighty oath**, and lying in it is among the major sins. An oath on the Mushaf has no special status that guarantees a particular worldly punishment for the liar, as some believe. Rather, the false swearer by Allah, by the Quran or on the Mushaf faces the severe threat, whether the punishment comes quickly or is delayed.

## The False Oath (al-Yamin al-Ghamus)
**Al-yamin al-ghamus** is swearing **falsely and deliberately** about a past matter, to take someone's property, clear oneself, or accuse another. It is called *ghamus* ("immersing") because it immerses its owner in sin and then in the Fire.

### Its seriousness
- Al-Bukhari reported from 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr that the Prophet ﷺ said: "The major sins are: associating partners with Allah, disobeying parents, killing a soul, and the false oath."
- In al-Bukhari and Muslim: "Whoever swears a binding oath to take the property of a Muslim while lying, will meet Allah while He is angry with him."

﴿إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَشۡتَرُونَ بِعَهۡدِ ٱللَّهِ وَأَيۡمَٰنِهِمۡ ثَمَنٗا قَلِيلًا أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ لَا خَلَٰقَ لَهُمۡ فِي ٱلۡأٓخِرَةِ وَلَا يُكَلِّمُهُمُ ٱللَّهُ وَلَا يَنظُرُ إِلَيۡهِمۡ يَوۡمَ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِ وَلَا يُزَكِّيهِمۡ وَلَهُمۡ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمࣱ﴾ (Quran 3:77)

### Its expiation
The **majority of scholars** hold that a false oath **has no expiation**, because it is too grave to be expiated; what is required is **sincere repentance** and **returning rights** to their owners. The Shafi'is held that expiation is required along with repentance.

## Expiation for a Broken Oath
If a Muslim swears about something in the future and then breaks it, he must give **the expiation of an oath** (*kaffarat al-yamin*), as Allah says:

﴿لَا يُؤَاخِذُكُمُ ٱللَّهُ بِٱللَّغۡوِ فِيٓ أَيۡمَٰنِكُمۡ وَلَٰكِن يُؤَاخِذُكُم بِمَا عَقَّدتُّمُ ٱلۡأَيۡمَٰنَۖ فَكَفَّـٰرَتُهُۥٓ إِطۡعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَٰكِينَ مِنۡ أَوۡسَطِ مَا تُطۡعِمُونَ أَهۡلِيكُمۡ أَوۡ كِسۡوَتُهُمۡ أَوۡ تَحۡرِيرُ رَقَبَةٖۖ فَمَن لَّمۡ يَجِدۡ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَٰثَةِ أَيَّامٖۚ ذَٰلِكَ كَفَّـٰرَةُ أَيۡمَٰنِكُمۡ إِذَا حَلَفۡتُمۡۚ وَٱحۡفَظُوٓاْ أَيۡمَٰنَكُمۡۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمۡ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تَشۡكُرُونَ﴾ (Quran 5:89)

**In order**:
1. **A choice of three**: feeding ten poor people, clothing them, or freeing a slave.
2. **If unable** to do any of these: fasting three days.

Fasting is not permitted while one is able to feed or clothe.

### Does swearing on the Mushaf require multiple expiations?
It is reported from Ibn Mas'ud that whoever swears by the Quran and breaks it owes an expiation for each verse, but this is a weaker view; the majority hold that **one expiation** is due.

## When Something Else Is Better
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "If you swear an oath and then see something better than it, expiate for your oath and do what is better." So if someone swore not to speak to his brother or not to visit his relatives, it is better to expiate and do the good.

## Types of Oaths
1. **A binding oath**: swearing about a possible future matter with intention. If broken, expiation is due.
2. **A false oath**: swearing falsely and deliberately about the past. A major sin; the remedy is repentance and returning rights.
3. **An idle oath** (*laghw*): words that slip out without intention, such as "No, by Allah" or "Yes, by Allah" in conversation. No expiation is due: "Allah will not hold you accountable for what is unintentional in your oaths." In Sahih al-Bukhari, 'A'ishah said this was revealed about a man saying "No, by Allah" and "Yes, by Allah."

## Advice for Disputes
1. **Swear less**: "Do not make Allah an excuse in your oaths."
2. **Do not demand an oath** in every disagreement; rely on evidence and reconciliation.
3. **Do not force** someone to swear on the Mushaf if they accept to swear by Allah.
4. **Accept the oath of one who swears by Allah**: Ibn Majah reported, and al-Albani graded authentic: "Whoever is sworn to by Allah should be satisfied, and whoever is not satisfied with Allah is not of Allah."
5. **If you are truthful, do not fear swearing**; if you are lying, beware.

## Correct and Incorrect Oath Formulas
| Formula | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Wallahi, Billahi, Tallahi | Valid oath |
| By the Lord of the worlds, by the Most Merciful | Valid oath |
| By the might of Allah, by the power of Allah | Valid oath by an attribute |
| By the Mighty Quran, by Allah's speech | Valid according to the majority |
| By the Mushaf, intending the Quran | Valid oath |
| By the Prophet, by the Ka'bah | Swearing by other than Allah; prohibited |
| By my life, by my mother's life | Swearing by other than Allah; prohibited |
| By my honour, by my trust | Not allowed according to many scholars |
| "Divorce is binding on me" | Has special rulings; avoid using it |

## Oaths in Marriage
A common scene is one spouse asking the other to swear on the Mushaf to prove innocence or honesty, often in disputes driven by suspicion or jealousy. Advice:
- **Do not build a marriage on oaths**: frequent demands for oaths signal a crisis of trust that needs addressing.
- **Do not accuse without evidence**: "O you who believe, avoid much suspicion."
- **If your spouse swears by Allah**, accept it and do not demand the Mushaf.
- **Seek counsel** from scholars or family mediators if disputes keep recurring.
- **Beware of divorce oaths**: they have specific rulings, and many jurists consider that divorce may take effect, so they must not be used in everyday arguments.

## Oaths in Trade
Some traders swear constantly to promote their goods. Muslim reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Beware of swearing much in trade, for it promotes sales and then destroys blessing." And in al-Bukhari and Muslim: "Swearing promotes the goods but erases the blessing." In Sahih Muslim, among the three whom Allah will not speak to on the Day of Resurrection is "the one who sells his goods with a false oath."

## Swearing in Non-Muslim Courts
In some countries courts ask witnesses to swear on their religion's scripture. A Muslim swears by Allah; if a Mushaf is presented and he places his hand on it saying "I swear by Allah", his oath is valid. He should not swear on the scripture of another religion, and many legal systems allow swearing on the Quran or a formal affirmation without any book.

## Repentance from a False Oath
Whoever has sworn falsely, on the Mushaf or otherwise, should:
1. **Repent sincerely**: regret, stop, and resolve never to return.
2. **Return rights**: if he took property or rights through the oath, he must return them.
3. **Clear the accused**: if he accused an innocent person, he must clear him.
4. **Do many good deeds**: "Indeed, good deeds remove evil deeds."
5. **Give expiation** as a precaution, according to those who require it.

Allah accepts the repentance of His servants, however great the sin, when the repentance is sincere.

## Truthfulness Over Oaths
Oaths are a means of confirming speech when needed, but a true Muslim is truthful without them. In al-Bukhari and Muslim: "Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise, and a man keeps telling the truth until he is recorded with Allah as truthful." Whoever is known for truthfulness does not need to swear, and whoever is known for lying is not helped by oaths, however many.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Is swearing by the Quran a valid oath?
Yes, according to the majority, because the Quran is Allah's speech, which is one of His attributes.

### Is swearing by the Mushaf an oath?
Yes, if the person intends the Quran in it, which is usually the case.

### Must I place my hand on the Mushaf when swearing?
No. The oath is valid by saying "Wallahi" or "By the Quran"; placing the hand is not established in the Sunnah.

### What is the expiation for breaking an oath on the Quran?
One expiation: feeding or clothing ten poor people, or, if unable, fasting three days.

### What is a false oath?
Deliberately swearing falsely about the past; it is a major sin, remedied by repentance and returning rights.

### Can I swear by the Prophet ﷺ?
No. Swearing by other than Allah is prohibited.

### Is "by my life" a valid oath?
No. It is swearing by other than Allah, which is prohibited.

### What should I do if I swore falsely on the Quran?
Repent sincerely, return any rights you took, clear anyone you accused, and do many good deeds.

## Conclusion
Swearing by the Quran is a valid Islamic oath because it is Allah's speech; swearing by the Mushaf is an oath when the Quran is intended. Placing your hand on the Mushaf is neither required nor established in the Sunnah, though some jurists mentioned it as a way of emphasising oaths in court. Whoever breaks an oath owes one expiation, and whoever swears falsely has committed a major sin. Guard your tongue from oaths, be truthful when you swear, and accept it when someone swears to you by Allah.
