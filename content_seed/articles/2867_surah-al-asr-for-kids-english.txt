Just three verses. Fourteen Arabic words. A child can memorize it in a few minutes. Yet Imam ash-Shafi'i famously said something to the effect that if people truly reflected on this surah, it would be enough for them. How can such a short surah be enough? Because it answers the most important question in anyone's life: how do I succeed and not lose?

Surah Al-Asr begins with Allah swearing by al-'asr, time. Then it tells us that every human being is in loss, except those who combine four things: they believe, they do good deeds, they encourage each other to the truth, and they encourage each other to patience. It teaches children that time is the real "capital" of life: every minute that passes never comes back. In this guide, we explain Surah Al-Asr to children in simple English and teach the value of time with practical activities, plus a story and questions.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Asr

- **Position**: surah 103, in Juz 'Amma, the thirtieth juz.
- **Number of verses**: three.
- **Type**: Meccan according to most scholars.
- **Name**: al-'Asr has two meanings according to the commentators: time in general, or the time of Asr at the end of the day. Both remind us of time passing.

### The companions and this surah
At-Tabarani and others reported that when two of the Prophet's companions met, they would not part until one of them recited Surah Al-Asr to the other, then they greeted each other with salam. Al-Albani graded its chain authentic. The surah was like a motto of sincere advice between them.

## The Whole Surah

﴿وَٱلۡعَصۡرِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لَفِي خُسۡرٍ﴾ ﴿إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلۡحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلصَّبۡرِ﴾ (Quran 103:1-3)

## Verse 1: By Time

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah swears by time. Allah only swears by something important, so time must be very important! Every day you have twenty-four hours, exactly like everyone else: rich or poor, old or young. The difference between people is how each person uses their time."

### Activity: the sand timer
Bring a sand timer, or make one from two small bottles and some sand. Turn it over in front of your child and watch the sand fall. Say: "The sand that fell can't go back up. Time is the same: a minute that passes never comes back." Then ask: "What could you do before all the sand falls?"

### Activity: the ice cube
Put an ice cube on a plate and watch it melt over half an hour. During that time, read Surah Al-Asr together and memorize it. At the end, say: "The ice melted, but you memorized a surah! That's a great use of time." Some of the early scholars compared time to ice sold on a hot day: if you don't use it quickly, it melts away.

## Verse 2: Every Person Is in Loss

### Explaining it to your child
"Every human being is in loss! Why? Because our life gets shorter every day. Imagine a box with all the days of your life inside, and every day one day leaves the box. If you don't use that day for good, you lose it. But Allah didn't leave us like that. The next verse tells us how to escape the loss."

### Activity: the box of days
Write the days of the week on seven slips of paper and put them in a box. Every evening, your child takes out that day's slip and writes something good they did on it: "I read a page of Quran", "I helped Mum". If they wrote something, the day wasn't lost! At the end of the week, read all the slips together.

## Verse 3: The Four Who Are Saved

### Quality 1: those who believe
"The first thing that saves you from loss is iman: believing in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day and destiny. Faith is like the foundation of a house. Without it, nothing stands."

### Quality 2: and do good deeds
"Faith alone is not enough; good deeds must come with it: prayer, kindness to parents, honesty, helping people, studying with a good intention. Faith is in the heart, and good deeds show it with our hands and tongues."

### Quality 3: and encourage each other to the truth
"Tawasaw means advising each other. Al-haqq is what is right and good. A Muslim doesn't just try to be good alone; they kindly encourage others too: reminding a brother about prayer, or telling a friend that lying is wrong."

### Quality 4: and encourage each other to patience
"And they advise each other to be patient: patient in doing good, like waking up for Fajr; patient in avoiding wrong, like not cheating; and patient with hard things, like being ill. If you see a friend who is sad, tell them: Be patient, Allah is with you."

### Activity: the winning hand
Trace your child's hand on paper. On the thumb write: "I believe in Allah". On the index finger: "I do good deeds". On the middle finger: "I encourage truth". On the ring finger: "I encourage patience". Let them fill the little finger: "I'm one of the winners, in sha' Allah". Hang it in their room.

### Notice the beautiful order
"The first two are for yourself: faith and good deeds. The last two are for others: encouraging truth and patience. A Muslim fixes themselves first, then helps others to be good."

## Why "Mankind" and Not "People"?

The verse says al-insan, "the human being", meaning every single person, one by one. So the surah speaks to each of us personally: you, me, your father, your friend. Nobody can say: this verse is about someone else. And the word "except" opens the door of success to everyone who wants it.

### Activity: me and the verse
Ask your child to read the verse and quietly put their own name in place of "the human being": "Indeed Yusuf is in loss, except if he believes, does good, encourages truth and encourages patience." Then ask: "Which quality would you like to make stronger this week?"

## Al-Asr and the Asr Prayer

One meaning of al-'Asr is the time of the Asr prayer, which is a very important prayer. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever misses the Asr prayer, his deeds are lost" (al-Bukhari). Many scholars say it is the "middle prayer" that Allah specially told us to guard.

### Explaining it to your child
"Asr time often comes after school, when you're tired or busy playing, so it's easy to delay. But Asr is very important. Pray it as soon as you hear the adhan, then play with a peaceful heart."

### Activity: the Asr alarm
Set an alarm on your phone called "Asr time, champ!". Every time your child prays Asr on time for ten days in a row, they get a small reward they choose.

## How Do We Teach Children the Value of Time?

### 1. A flexible daily plan
Make a simple picture schedule with your child: prayer times, school, homework, play, Quran, and sleep. Play is part of the plan, so they learn that rest is also a good use of time when it's balanced.

### 2. The five-minute rule
Teach that small minutes matter: while waiting at the doctor's or in the car, they can revise a surah or say dhikr. Five minutes a day adds up to more than thirty hours a year!

### 3. Screen maths
Sit together and calculate: "If you spend two hours a day on screens, how many hours is that in a month?" Sixty! Then: "What if we used half an hour of that for memorizing Quran? How many surahs could you learn?" Let them decide for themselves.

### 4. The friendly timer
Use a timer for tasks: "Ten minutes to tidy your room", "Twenty minutes of homework". Children love racing the timer, and they learn that every task has its time.

### From the Sunnah
The Prophet ﷺ said: "There are two blessings that many people lose out on: health and free time" (al-Bukhari).

### Explaining it to your child
"Right now you are healthy and you have free time. Those are two big blessings. Use them well before you grow up and get busy."

## Examples of Encouraging Each Other in a Child's Life

- **At home**: your brother leaves the tap running, and you gently say: "Turn it off, water is a blessing."
- **At school**: a classmate wants to copy answers, and you say: "Cheating isn't allowed. Let's study together tomorrow."
- **In the street**: you see litter on the path, pick it up and tell your friend: "Removing harm from the road is charity."
- **With a sad friend**: "Be patient, Allah is with you, and tomorrow will be better."

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "Two Hours of Games"

Mazin loved phone games and played for two hours every day after school, sometimes more. His mother would say: "Mazin, time is slipping away," and he would answer: "There's plenty of time, Mum!"

One day, his Quran teacher announced a competition: whoever memorized Surah Al-Mulk within a month would win a prize. Mazin was excited, but after two weeks he had only memorized five verses, because he kept putting it off.

He sat with his grandfather, feeling sad. His grandfather took out an old sand timer, turned it over, and said: "Mazin, recite Surah Al-Asr." Mazin did. His grandfather said: "Allah swore by time because it's the most precious thing. Every day that passes without benefit is like this sand. It falls and never comes back."

Then they did the maths together: "Two hours of games a day, in two weeks, is twenty-eight hours! If you'd used just half an hour a day for memorizing, you'd have finished the whole surah."

Mazin decided to split his time: half an hour of memorizing first, then one hour of games. In the remaining two weeks, he memorized the whole surah! On competition day, he recited it without a mistake and won the prize. He told his grandfather: "I'm one of the winners now, not the losers!" His grandfather laughed: "And remember, encourage each other to the truth. Teach your little brother what you learned."

Ask your child: "Why didn't Mazin memorize in the first two weeks? What did he change?"

## What We Learn From Surah Al-Asr

- **Time is precious**: Allah swore by it.
- **Every minute** that passes never returns.
- **Everyone is in loss** except those with four qualities.
- **Faith** is the foundation.
- **Good deeds** show our faith.
- **We encourage truth** kindly.
- **We encourage patience**.
- **Health and free time** are blessings we shouldn't waste.

## One-Week Plan: Memorize and Practise

- **Day 1**: memorize "Wal-'asr" and do the sand-timer activity.
- **Day 2**: memorize verse 2 and start the box of days.
- **Day 3**: memorize verse 3.
- **Day 4**: draw the winning hand.
- **Day 5**: make the daily picture schedule.
- **Day 6**: practise encouraging: advise a sibling about something good.
- **Day 7**: recite the surah, use it in prayer, and celebrate!

## Questions to Ask Your Child

1. What does Allah swear by at the start of the surah?
2. Why is time so important?
3. Who is in loss in this surah?
4. What are the four qualities that save us from loss?
5. What does "encourage each other to the truth" mean?
6. Name two kinds of patience.
7. Which two blessings do many people lose out on?

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Is playing a waste of time?
No. Play is a real need for children and rest for their mind and body. The goal is balance: time to play, and time to learn and worship. Wasting time is when play takes over everything.

### How do I teach "encouraging each other" without my child becoming bossy?
Teach them to advise kindly, with a smile, once, without shaming or shouting, and to start with themselves first, as the order of the surah shows.

### Can my child recite Surah Al-Asr in prayer?
Yes. It is one of the best surahs for beginners because it is so short, and they can recite it after Al-Fatiha in any rak'ah.

### Is Surah Al-Asr suitable for a four-year-old?
Yes. At that age, it's enough to memorize it and know that al-'Asr means time. The four qualities can be explained as they grow.
