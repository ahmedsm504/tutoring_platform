When a loved one is in their final hours, families gather around the bed, and someone usually opens the Mushaf to Surah Yasin and begins to recite. Others ask quietly: Is this from the Sunnah? Should we keep reciting after the soul has left? Is there something more important we should be doing in these minutes?

These are among the hardest moments a person goes through, and they are the moments when knowing the established Sunnah matters most, because they never come back. This article examines reciting Surah Yasin for the dying in detail: the hadith, its grading and the scholars' views. It then sets out what is firmly established in the Sunnah at the time of death, and gives a practical guide for families.

## The Well-Known Hadith: "Recite Yasin over Your Dying"
Abu Dawud, Ibn Majah and Imam Ahmad reported from Ma'qil ibn Yasar that the Prophet ﷺ said: **"Recite Yasin over your dying (mawtakum)."**

### Its grading
Hadith scholars differed about this hadith:
- **Ibn Hibban** included it in his *Sahih*, and al-Hakim graded it authentic.
- **Most hadith critics weakened it**, including **ad-Daraqutni**, who said its chain is weak and its text unknown, and that nothing authentic is reported on this topic. **Ibn al-Qattan** weakened it, **an-Nawawi** in *al-Adhkar* noted that its chain contains unknown narrators, and **al-Albani** weakened it.

The weakness is because its chain contains **Abu 'Uthman and his father**, who are unknown narrators, along with inconsistency in the chain. So the stronger view among most hadith scholars is that the hadith is **weak**.

### What does "mawtakum" mean for those who act on it?
Those who acted on the hadith differed about the meaning of "your dead" (*mawtakum*):
- **View one**: it means **those who are dying**, those at the point of death who have not yet died, called "dead" because of what they are about to become, as in the hadith "Prompt your dying to say La ilaha illallah", which by agreement refers to the dying. This is the view of most who act on the hadith.
- **View two**: it means **the dead after the soul has left**, so it would be recited over the body or at the grave. This is the view of some later scholars.

The first is more apparent, because the reasons given for the recitation, easing the departure of the soul and reminding the dying person, only apply before death.

## The Hadith "Yasin Is the Heart of the Quran"
Another hadith is often quoted: "Everything has a heart, and the heart of the Quran is Yasin. Whoever recites Yasin, Allah will record for him the reward of reciting the Quran ten times." At-Tirmidhi reported it and said it is *gharib* (reported through a single unusual chain). **Hadith scholars weakened it**; Abu Hatim ar-Razi even judged it baseless, and al-Albani weakened it. So it should not be used as evidence.

This does not reduce the virtue of Surah Yasin itself. It is part of the great Quran, and it contains profound meanings about the oneness of Allah, the resurrection and the Hereafter that suit the state of a dying person. But the specific virtues mentioned in these hadiths are not established.

﴿إِنَّا نَحۡنُ نُحۡيِ ٱلۡمَوۡتَىٰ وَنَكۡتُبُ مَا قَدَّمُواْ وَءَاثَٰرَهُمۡۚ وَكُلَّ شَيۡءٍ أَحۡصَيۡنَٰهُ فِيٓ إِمَامٖ مُّبِينٖ﴾ (Quran 36:12)

## What the Jurists Say About Reciting Yasin for the Dying
Despite the weakness of the hadith, **many jurists** of the four schools, including the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis, held that **reciting Yasin for the dying is recommended**, acting on the hadith for the sake of virtuous deeds according to those who allow that, and on reports from some of the early generations. They mentioned that the surah contains the remembrance of the resurrection and the Hereafter, which suits the state of the dying.

It is reported that **Imam Ahmad** mentioned that the elders used to say that when Yasin is recited beside the dying person, it is made easier for them.

**Some scholars**, including Imam Malik, **disliked** singling out a particular surah for the dying as though it were a Sunnah, because no authentic hadith or widespread practice of the Companions is established about it.

### Summary of the fiqh
- **Reciting the Quran in general beside a dying person** is permitted without any problem; it is remembrance and a reminder.
- **Singling out Yasin** is a matter of difference: many jurists recommended it, while others disliked it because of the weakness of the hadith.
- **Do not believe** that reciting Yasin is an established, confirmed Sunnah, and do not condemn those who recite it for the dying.
- **What is firmly established** is what follows, and it takes priority.

## What Is Firmly Established in the Sunnah at the Time of Death
### 1. Prompting the testimony of faith (talqin)
In Sahih Muslim, Abu Sa'id al-Khudri reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: **"Prompt your dying to say La ilaha illallah."**

In Sunan Abi Dawud, with a chain graded authentic by al-Albani, Mu'adh ibn Jabal reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: **"Whoever's last words are La ilaha illallah will enter Paradise."**

**How to prompt**:
- Say the testimony **near them** gently, rather than commanding them to say it in a way that may weigh on them in their distress.
- **If they say it once, do not repeat it to them**, unless they speak about something else afterwards; then gently remind them again so that it is their last words.
- Do not press them insistently; scholars noted that insistence may distress a dying person in their hardship.

### 2. Thinking well of Allah
In Sahih Muslim, Jabir heard the Prophet ﷺ say three days before his death: **"None of you should die except while thinking well of Allah."**

So it is from the Sunnah for the family to remind the dying person of **Allah's mercy and the vastness of His forgiveness**, to mention their good deeds, and to give them hope, not to frighten them at this moment.

﴿۞قُلۡ يَٰعِبَادِيَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسۡرَفُواْ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ لَا تَقۡنَطُواْ مِن رَّحۡمَةِ ٱللَّهِۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغۡفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًاۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلۡغَفُورُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ﴾ (Quran 39:53)

### 3. Saying good things in the presence of the dying
In Sahih Muslim, Umm Salamah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: **"When you are with a sick or dying person, say good things, for the angels say Amin to what you say."**

Those present should make du'a for forgiveness, mercy and ease for the person, and avoid bad words, wailing or objecting to Allah's decree.

### 4. Closing the eyes and making du'a after death
In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ entered upon Abu Salamah after his eyes had become fixed open. He closed them and said: "When the soul is taken, the sight follows it." Then he prayed: **"O Allah, forgive Abu Salamah, raise his rank among those who are guided, be a successor to him among those he leaves behind, forgive us and him, O Lord of the worlds, make his grave spacious for him and give him light in it."**

So the Sunnah after the soul departs is **to close the eyes** and **pray for the person** with a du'a like this, changing the name.

### 5. Patience and saying "Inna lillah"
In Sahih Muslim, Umm Salamah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There is no Muslim who is struck by a calamity and says what Allah has commanded, 'We belong to Allah and to Him we will return. O Allah, reward me in my calamity and replace it for me with something better', except that Allah will replace it for him with something better."

﴿وَلَنَبۡلُوَنَّكُم بِشَيۡءٖ مِّنَ ٱلۡخَوۡفِ وَٱلۡجُوعِ وَنَقۡصٖ مِّنَ ٱلۡأَمۡوَٰلِ وَٱلۡأَنفُسِ وَٱلثَّمَرَٰتِۗ وَبَشِّرِ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِينَ إِذَآ أَصَٰبَتۡهُم مُّصِيبَةࣱ قَالُوٓاْ إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّآ إِلَيۡهِ رَٰجِعُونَ﴾ ﴿أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ صَلَوَٰتࣱ مِّن رَّبِّهِمۡ وَرَحۡمَةࣱۖ وَأُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُهۡتَدُونَ﴾ (Quran 2:155-157)

## What to Do Immediately After Death
1. **Close the eyes** and make du'a.
2. **Tie the jaw** gently with a cloth so the mouth does not remain open.
3. **Gently loosen the joints.**
4. **Cover the body** with a cloth.
5. **Hasten to prepare the body**, settle the deceased's debts and carry out their will.
6. **Inform relatives and friends** so that they can pray the funeral prayer.

## Reciting over the Body After Death
Reciting Yasin or other surahs over the **body** after death and before burial has no authentic hadith; some scholars disliked it, and others permitted it based on the second meaning of "your dead". It is better to be busy with what is established: preparing the body, praying for the deceased, praying the funeral prayer, and hastening the burial.

## Common Mistakes at the Deathbed
1. **Being so busy with reciting Yasin** that the family forgets talqin, du'a and comforting the dying person.
2. **Pressing the testimony insistently**, causing distress.
3. **Speaking of their sins or frightening them** instead of reminding them of Allah's mercy.
4. **Wailing, tearing clothes or slapping cheeks** after death, which the Prophet ﷺ forbade, as reported in al-Bukhari and Muslim: "He is not one of us who slaps his cheeks, tears his clothes, or calls out with the call of the days of ignorance."
5. **Delaying the burial** without need.
6. **Hiring reciters** to recite over the deceased for money, which many scholars forbade.
7. **Neglecting the deceased's debts** and their will.

## A Short Guide for Families
**While your loved one is dying**:
- Sit near them with calm and gentleness.
- Say "La ilaha illallah" softly near them.
- Remind them of Allah's mercy and their good deeds.
- Make du'a for them, and say only good.
- If you wish, recite the Quran, Yasin or any other surah, softly, without disturbing them, and without believing it is a confirmed Sunnah.

**When the soul leaves**:
- Close their eyes and pray for them as the Prophet ﷺ prayed for Abu Salamah.
- Say "Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un" and the du'a of calamity.
- Cover them and begin the preparations.

**Afterwards**:
- Pray the funeral prayer, hasten the burial, and make du'a at the grave.
- Give charity on their behalf, make du'a for them, and pay their debts.

## Grieving with Patience
The Prophet ﷺ wept when his son Ibrahim died and said, as reported in al-Bukhari and Muslim: "The eyes shed tears and the heart grieves, but we say only what pleases our Lord. Indeed we are saddened by your departure, O Ibrahim." Tears and sadness are not contrary to patience; what is forbidden is objecting to Allah's decree and wailing.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Is the hadith "Recite Yasin over your dying" authentic?
Ibn Hibban and al-Hakim graded it authentic, but most hadith scholars, including ad-Daraqutni, an-Nawawi and al-Albani, considered it weak.

### Is it permissible to recite Yasin for a dying person?
Yes. Many jurists recommended it, while others disliked singling it out; reciting the Quran in general beside the dying is permitted.

### Is "Yasin is the heart of the Quran" an authentic hadith?
Hadith scholars weakened it, and Abu Hatim judged it baseless.

### What is the most important thing to do for a dying person?
Gently prompt them to say "La ilaha illallah", remind them of Allah's mercy, and say only good things in their presence.

### Should we recite Yasin over the body after death?
There is no authentic hadith for it; it is better to prepare the body, pray for the deceased and hasten the burial.

### Is crying for the deceased permitted?
Yes. The Prophet ﷺ wept for his son Ibrahim; what is forbidden is wailing and objecting to Allah's decree.

## Conclusion
The hadith "Recite Yasin over your dying" is weak according to most hadith scholars, and many jurists still recommended reciting it for the dying, while others disliked singling it out. What is firmly established is gently prompting the testimony of faith, helping the dying person to think well of Allah, saying only good in their presence, closing their eyes and praying for them after death, and meeting the loss with patience. Give these established acts your first attention in those precious minutes, and if you recite Yasin or any part of the Quran, do so softly, as a reminder and a mercy, not as a ritual that replaces the Sunnah.
