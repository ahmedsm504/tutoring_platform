What do you do when you win? When you get top marks, score the winning goal or finish memorizing a surah? Many people jump, shout and say: "I'm the best!" Surah An-Nasr teaches us something different and beautiful. When Allah's help and victory came to the Prophet ﷺ, and people were entering Islam in crowds, Allah didn't say: Celebrate how great you are! He said: Glorify your Lord with praise, and ask Him for forgiveness.

Surah An-Nasr is only three verses, and it is among the last surahs revealed to the Prophet ﷺ. It talks about the greatest victory of his life: the conquest of Makkah, the city that had once forced him out. And instead of revenge, he forgave. In this guide for parents, we explain Surah An-Nasr to children in simple English, with the story of the conquest, lessons on humility in success, activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah An-Nasr

- **Position**: surah 110, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: three.
- **Type**: Madinan, revealed near the end of the Prophet's life.
- **Name**: an-Nasr means "the help" or "the victory".
- **Special note**: many scholars mention it as the last complete surah to be revealed.

## The Whole Surah

﴿إِذَا جَآءَ نَصۡرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡفَتۡحُ﴾ ﴿وَرَأَيۡتَ ٱلنَّاسَ يَدۡخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ ٱللَّهِ أَفۡوَاجٗا﴾ ﴿فَسَبِّحۡ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسۡتَغۡفِرۡهُۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابَۢا﴾ (Quran 110:1-3)

## The Story: The Conquest of Makkah

### Scene 1: Forced to leave
"For thirteen years in Makkah, the Prophet ﷺ called people to Allah, and many leaders of Quraysh hurt him and his companions. In the end, they planned to kill him, and he had to leave his beloved city and migrate to Madinah. As he left, he looked back at Makkah and said that it was the most beloved land to him, and that he would not have left if its people had not forced him out (at-Tirmidhi, who graded it authentic)."

### Scene 2: Coming back
"Eight years later, the Prophet ﷺ returned to Makkah with an army of ten thousand Muslims. But he didn't come for revenge. He entered Makkah with his head bowed so low out of humility before Allah that it almost touched his camel's back, as the books of seerah describe. He went to the Kaaba and removed the idols around it, reciting: The truth has come and falsehood has vanished."

### Scene 3: The great forgiveness
"The people of Makkah gathered, afraid. These were the same people who had hurt the Muslims for years. The Prophet ﷺ asked them: What do you think I will do with you? They said: Good, you are a noble brother and the son of a noble brother. And the books of seerah report that he said: Go, you are free. He forgave them all!"

### Scene 4: People enter Islam in crowds
"After the conquest of Makkah, whole tribes from all over Arabia came to Madinah to accept Islam. Before, people became Muslim one by one; now they came in groups, just as the surah describes: people entering the religion of Allah in crowds."

## Verse by Verse

### Verse 1: "When the help of Allah and the victory come"
"Nasr is Allah's help, and al-fath is the opening, the victory, meaning the conquest of Makkah. Notice: it's 'the help of Allah'. The Muslims didn't win because they were strong; they won because Allah helped them."

### Verse 2: "And you see people entering the religion of Allah in crowds"
"Afwaja means in big groups. After years of difficulty, the Prophet ﷺ saw his message spreading everywhere. All his patience was rewarded."

### Verse 3: "Then glorify your Lord with praise and ask His forgiveness; He is ever accepting of repentance"
"At the moment of the greatest success, Allah tells the Prophet ﷺ to say SubhanAllah, to praise Allah, and to say Astaghfirullah. Why seek forgiveness at a happy moment? Because no matter how well we do, we can never thank Allah enough, and we always have small mistakes. And Allah is at-Tawwab, always ready to accept those who turn back to Him."

## What the Prophet ﷺ Did After This Surah

'A'ishah said that after this surah was revealed, the Prophet ﷺ used to say often in his ruku' and sujud: "Subhanaka Allahumma Rabbana wa bihamdik, Allahummaghfir li", "Glory be to You, O Allah, our Lord, and praise be to You; O Allah, forgive me", acting on the Quran (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### Activity: the ruku' du'a
Teach your child this beautiful du'a to say in ruku' and sujud: "Subhanaka Allahumma Rabbana wa bihamdik, Allahummaghfir li." Explain that the Prophet ﷺ said it often after Surah An-Nasr was revealed.

## The Clever Understanding of Ibn 'Abbas

'Umar ibn al-Khattab once asked some senior companions what this surah meant. Some said: Allah is telling us to praise Him and seek forgiveness when victory comes. Then he asked young Ibn 'Abbas, who was a boy at the time. Ibn 'Abbas said: It is a sign that the Prophet's time on earth is ending. His mission is complete, so he should prepare to meet his Lord. 'Umar said: I know nothing of it except what you have said (al-Bukhari).

### Explaining it to your child
"Ibn 'Abbas was young, but he had studied the Quran deeply and understood something even some older companions didn't notice. When a job is finished, the worker goes home. The Prophet's job of spreading Islam was complete, and soon he would return to Allah. And that's what happened: he passed away not long after."

### Lesson for kids: young people can be wise
"Ibn 'Abbas shows that a child who loves the Quran and thinks about it can understand amazing things. The Prophet ﷺ had made du'a for him: O Allah, give him understanding of the religion (al-Bukhari)."

## Lessons for Kids

### Success comes from Allah
"When you do well, remember: Allah helped you. Your brain, your health, your teachers and your time are all gifts from Allah."

### Be humble when you win
"The Prophet ﷺ entered Makkah with his head bowed low. When you win, don't show off. Say Alhamdulillah, and be kind to those who didn't win."

### Forgive when you can
"The Prophet ﷺ could have taken revenge, but he forgave. When you have the chance to get back at someone who hurt you, choosing to forgive is the bigger, braver thing."

### Patience is rewarded
"The Prophet ﷺ waited more than twenty years to see people entering Islam in crowds. Good things often take time. Keep going!"

## Activities

### Activity: the winner's words
Make a card titled "When I win, I say..." with three phrases: "Alhamdulillah", "SubhanAllah wa bihamdih", and "Astaghfirullah". Keep it on the fridge, and whenever your child succeeds at something, they read it.

### Activity: good sport practice
Play a board game with your child. Whoever wins says "Alhamdulillah" and shakes hands with the others, saying "Well played!" Whoever loses says "Masha'Allah, well done!" Practising this at home makes it natural at school.

### Activity: forgiveness letter
Ask your child to think of someone who upset them. Together, write a short note: "I forgive you, and I hope we can be friends." They can give it, or keep it as a sign of forgiving in their heart.

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Spelling Bee"

Hamza practised for weeks for the school spelling bee. On the big day, he spelled every word correctly and won first place! The whole hall clapped, and he felt like jumping on the stage and shouting: "I'm the best!"

But then he remembered what his teacher had taught him about Surah An-Nasr. He quietly said: "Alhamdulillah," walked to the boy who came second, Leo, and shook his hand. "You were really good. That last word was so hard!" Leo smiled: "Thanks, you deserved it."

At home, Hamza's father asked: "Weren't you tempted to show off?" Hamza laughed: "A little bit! But I remembered how the Prophet ﷺ entered Makkah with his head bowed. If he was humble after the biggest victory ever, I can be humble after a spelling bee!"

His father hugged him. "And do you know what the Prophet ﷺ said after this surah came down?" He taught Hamza: "Subhanaka Allahumma Rabbana wa bihamdik, Allahummaghfir li." That night, Hamza said it in his sujud, thanking Allah for helping him win.

Ask your child: "What did Hamza feel like doing when he won? What did he do instead? Who did he learn it from?"

## What We Learn From Surah An-Nasr

- **Victory comes from Allah**, not from our own strength.
- **The conquest of Makkah** was peaceful and full of forgiveness.
- **Patience** is rewarded, even if it takes years.
- **When we succeed**, we glorify Allah and seek His forgiveness.
- **Humility** is the sign of a true winner.
- **Forgiving** is braver than revenge.
- **Young people** can understand the Quran deeply, like Ibn 'Abbas.

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### Why seek forgiveness at a time of success?
Scholars explain that success is a time when people can become proud and forget Allah, and that no one can fully thank Allah for His blessings. Seeking forgiveness keeps the heart humble and connected to Allah.

### Is it sad that the surah hints at the Prophet's death?
It can be explained gently: "The Prophet ﷺ finished the job Allah gave him perfectly, and then went back to Allah, who loves him. We will meet him, in sha' Allah, at his pool in Paradise."

### Is the forgiveness story at the conquest authentic?
The general forgiveness of the people of Makkah at the conquest is well known in the books of seerah. Some specific wordings are discussed by hadith scholars, but the Prophet's great mercy that day is widely reported.


### What age suits the story of the conquest of Makkah?
From about six, children can enjoy the story told simply: the Prophet ﷺ had to leave his home, came back years later, and forgave everyone. The detail can grow as they get older.


### Can my child recite An-Nasr in prayer?
Yes. It is short and easy, and children can recite it after Al-Fatiha in any rak'ah, alongside Al-Kawthar and Al-Ikhlas.
