Reading Surah Al-Kahf on Friday is a beloved weekly habit for many Muslims, and every week the same practical questions come up: "Can I read it on Thursday night after Maghrib?" "I didn't manage until 'Asr; is it still valid?" "Is listening in the car enough?" "Can I read it on my phone?" "I'm on my period; can I read it?"

This guide answers these questions after explaining the hadiths about the virtue of Surah Al-Kahf and their grading.

## The Hadiths
### The hadith of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri
Al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi reported from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever reads Surah Al-Kahf on Friday, a light will shine for him between the two Fridays." Al-Hakim graded it authentic, and al-Albani graded it authentic.

In another narration, reported by ad-Darimi and others from Abu Sa'id as his own statement: "Whoever reads Surah Al-Kahf on the night of Friday, a light will shine for him between himself and the Ancient House (the Ka'bah)." Although it is Abu Sa'id's statement, scholars say such a statement cannot be based on opinion, so it takes the ruling of a statement of the Prophet ﷺ, and al-Albani graded it authentic.

### The first ten verses
Muslim reported from Abu ad-Darda' that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever memorises ten verses from the beginning of Surah Al-Kahf will be protected from the Dajjal." In another narration in Muslim: "from the end of Al-Kahf."

﴿ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَنزَلَ عَلَىٰ عَبۡدِهِ ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ وَلَمۡ يَجۡعَل لَّهُۥ عِوَجَاۜ﴾ ﴿قَيِّمٗا لِّيُنذِرَ بَأۡسٗا شَدِيدٗا مِّن لَّدُنۡهُ وَيُبَشِّرَ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ ٱلَّذِينَ يَعۡمَلُونَ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ أَنَّ لَهُمۡ أَجۡرًا حَسَنٗا﴾ ﴿مَّـٰكِثِينَ فِيهِ أَبَدٗا﴾ (Quran 18:1-3)

## When Does the Time Begin and End?
Since the narrations mention both **Friday** and **the night of Friday**, scholars combined them:
- **The time begins** at **sunset on Thursday**, because in Islamic reckoning the night precedes the day, so the night of Friday begins at Thursday's sunset.
- **The time ends** at **sunset on Friday**.

So whoever reads it at any time between Maghrib on Thursday and Maghrib on Friday has attained the virtue, by Allah's permission. This is the view of many contemporary scholars, including the Permanent Committee for Fatwa.

### The best time
Some scholars mentioned that it is best to read it **on Friday itself**, especially **before the Friday prayer**, as Friday is a day of bathing, going early and remembrance. But the matter is broad; reading it on Thursday night or after 'Asr on Friday is fine.

## Practical Questions
### Can I split the surah?
Yes. It does not need to be read in one sitting. You can read half after Maghrib on Thursday and half on Friday morning, or divide it across the prayer times, as long as all of it is read within the time.

### Can I read it on my phone?
Yes. Reading from a phone or digital Mushaf is genuine reading, and the virtue is attained. Most contemporary scholars do not require wudu to touch a phone displaying the Quran, though wudu is better.

### Can I read it from memory?
Yes, reading from memory is genuine reading.

### Is listening enough?
The hadith uses the word "reads", so the stated virtue is tied to **reading**. Listening is an act of worship with its own reward, but it is better to read it yourself to attain the stated virtue. Someone unable to read, such as a sick person or someone who cannot read Arabic yet, can hope for reward through listening according to their intention.

### Do I have to read the whole surah?
The stated virtue is tied to reading the surah; whoever reads part of it is rewarded for what they read, and whoever completes it attains the virtue, by Allah's permission.

### Can I read it in prayer?
Yes; reading it in prayer, such as in night prayer or voluntary prayers, counts as reading it.

### What about women on their period?
There is a well-known difference of opinion among scholars about a menstruating woman reciting the Quran:
- **Those who prohibit it**: she listens to it, looks at it without reciting, or reflects on its meanings.
- **Those who permit it**, such as the Malikis and Ibn Taymiyyah: she may read it from memory or from a phone without touching a printed Mushaf.

## Why Al-Kahf on Friday?
Scholars mentioned meanings in Al-Kahf that fit Friday:
1. **The Hour and resurrection**: and the Hour will take place on a Friday, as in Sahih Muslim.
2. **The four trials**: of faith, wealth, knowledge and power.
3. **Protection from the Dajjal**, the greatest trial.
4. **A weekly light**: the believer's light is renewed every week.

﴿إِذۡ أَوَى ٱلۡفِتۡيَةُ إِلَى ٱلۡكَهۡفِ فَقَالُواْ رَبَّنَآ ءَاتِنَا مِن لَّدُنكَ رَحۡمَةٗ وَهَيِّئۡ لَنَا مِنۡ أَمۡرِنَا رَشَدٗا﴾ (Quran 18:10)

## The Four Trials in Surah Al-Kahf
### The trial of faith: the People of the Cave
Young men who believed in their Lord fled with their faith from a polytheistic people, and Allah protected them in the cave for years. Lesson: **righteous companionship** and **fleeing trials to protect one's faith**.

﴿نَّحۡنُ نَقُصُّ عَلَيۡكَ نَبَأَهُم بِٱلۡحَقِّۚ إِنَّهُمۡ فِتۡيَةٌ ءَامَنُواْ بِرَبِّهِمۡ وَزِدۡنَٰهُمۡ هُدٗى﴾ ﴿وَرَبَطۡنَا عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمۡ إِذۡ قَامُواْ فَقَالُواْ رَبُّنَا رَبُّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ لَن نَّدۡعُوَاْ مِن دُونِهِۦٓ إِلَٰهٗاۖ لَّقَدۡ قُلۡنَآ إِذٗا شَطَطًا﴾ (Quran 18:13-14)

### The trial of wealth: the owner of the two gardens
A man blessed with two gardens became arrogant about his wealth and denied the resurrection, until his fruit was destroyed. Lesson: **remember the Hereafter** and say "Ma sha' Allah, la quwwata illa billah."

﴿وَلَوۡلَآ إِذۡ دَخَلۡتَ جَنَّتَكَ قُلۡتَ مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِۚ إِن تَرَنِ أَنَا۠ أَقَلَّ مِنكَ مَالٗا وَوَلَدٗا﴾ (Quran 18:39)

### The trial of knowledge: Musa and al-Khidr
Musa, peace be upon him, one of the greatest prophets, travelled to learn from a servant to whom Allah had given knowledge. Lesson: **humility in seeking knowledge**, and that **Allah's wisdom** may be hidden from us. The damaged boat was saved from a tyrant king, the boy would have burdened his parents with transgression, and the wall hid a treasure for two orphans.

### The trial of power: Dhul-Qarnayn
A king whom Allah established in the land; he ruled justly, built the barrier, and attributed the achievement to Allah: "This is a mercy from my Lord." Lesson: **sincerity** and **attributing blessings to Allah**.

## Al-Kahf and the Dajjal
Scholars explain that Al-Kahf protects against the Dajjal because it contains the very trials he will bring:
- **Faith**: the Dajjal will claim divinity, as the People of the Cave faced a people worshipping others besides Allah.
- **Wealth**: he will command the sky to rain and the earth to produce, as in Sahih Muslim, a trial of wealth as in the story of the two gardens.
- **Knowledge**: he will bring wonders that the ignorant think are real; true knowledge exposes them, as in the story of Musa and al-Khidr.
- **Power**: he will dominate the earth for forty days, while Dhul-Qarnayn shows what just power looks like.

## The Conclusion of the Surah
﴿قُلۡ إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ بَشَرࣱ مِّثۡلُكُمۡ يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَيَّ أَنَّمَآ إِلَٰهُكُمۡ إِلَٰهࣱ وَٰحِدࣱۖ فَمَن كَانَ يَرۡجُواْ لِقَآءَ رَبِّهِۦ فَلۡيَعۡمَلۡ عَمَلٗا صَٰلِحٗا وَلَا يُشۡرِكۡ بِعِبَادَةِ رَبِّهِۦٓ أَحَدَۢا﴾ (Quran 18:110)

After the four trials comes the comprehensive cure: **righteous deeds and sincerity**. Whoever wants to be saved from trials should do righteous deeds and associate no one in the worship of his Lord.

## Making Al-Kahf a Weekly Habit
- **Set a fixed time**: after Fajr on Friday, or before Jumu'ah.
- **Set a reminder** on your phone.
- **Read it with your family**, each member reading a portion.
- **Memorise the first ten verses** for protection from the Dajjal, then gradually the rest.
- **Read its tafsir**: one story each week.
- **Reflect**: each Friday ask yourself which trial you most need protection from this week.

Surah Al-Kahf has 110 verses, about twelve pages in the Madinah Mushaf. At a moderate pace it takes roughly twenty to thirty minutes.

## Other Sunnahs of Friday
- **Bathing**: "Bathing on Friday is required of every adult" (al-Bukhari and Muslim); the majority consider it a confirmed Sunnah.
- **Wearing perfume and your best clothes**.
- **Going early** to Jumu'ah.
- **Listening silently** to the sermon: "If you say to your companion 'Be quiet' while the imam is speaking, you have engaged in idle talk" (al-Bukhari and Muslim).
- **Sending abundant blessings on the Prophet ﷺ**: Abu Dawud reported, and al-Albani graded authentic: "Among the best of your days is Friday... so send abundant blessings upon me on it."
- **Du'a in the hour of acceptance**: in al-Bukhari and Muslim, there is an hour on Friday in which no Muslim asks Allah for anything good while praying except that He gives it to him. The best-known views place it between the imam sitting on the pulpit and the end of the prayer, or in the last hour after 'Asr.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Thinking the time starts only at Fajr on Friday**: it begins at Maghrib on Thursday.
2. **Giving up because time is short**: read some of it, or read it after 'Asr before Maghrib.
3. **Always relying on listening** when able to read.
4. **Reading so fast that letters are dropped**.
5. **Reading without reflection** week after week.
6. **Sharing reminders with weak hadiths** or unestablished virtues.

## Al-Kahf for Children
Children love the stories of Surah Al-Kahf, so make Friday a day for telling one of them:
- **First Friday**: the People of the Cave and the dog that stayed with them.
- **Second Friday**: the owner of the two gardens.
- **Third Friday**: Musa, al-Khidr and the boat.
- **Fourth Friday**: Dhul-Qarnayn and the barrier.

Then memorise the first ten verses with them gradually, so they grow up loving this surah and reading it every week.

## Memorising the Whole Surah
If you are memorising the Quran, make Al-Kahf one of the first long surahs you memorise, because you will read it every week, turning its memorisation into a permanent weekly revision. Start with the first ten verses, then the last ten, then complete the middle story by story. Once you know it by heart, you can recite it in night prayer on Thursday or on the way to the mosque without needing a Mushaf.

## Sharing Reminders Responsibly
Reminding one another to read Al-Kahf on Friday is a good deed, but share only authentic hadiths, such as the light between two Fridays and the first ten verses protecting from the Dajjal. Avoid adding virtues that are not established, such as claims that reading it forgives all sins, or forwarding messages with exaggerated promises. A short, accurate reminder is better than a long one built on weak reports.

At sunset on Thursday.

At sunset on Friday.

Al-Hakim and al-Albani graded it authentic.

## Conclusion
Reading Surah Al-Kahf on Friday is an established Sunnah that brings light between two Fridays, according to the hadith of Abu Sa'id graded authentic by al-Hakim and al-Albani. Its time runs from sunset on Thursday to sunset on Friday; you may split it and read it from memory, a Mushaf, a phone or in prayer. The stated virtue is tied to reading rather than listening. Make it a weekly habit, memorise its first ten verses, and reflect on its four trials, so your light is renewed each Friday and you are protected from the greatest trial.
