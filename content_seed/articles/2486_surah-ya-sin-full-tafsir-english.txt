Surah Ya-Sin is one of the most widely recited surahs in the Muslim world. Many people know it by heart, recite it on Fridays, or read it beside the sick. But fewer people sit with its meanings. This guide walks through the whole surah in English, section by section, so that when you recite it you know what each passage is saying and why it is placed where it is.

Ya-Sin is a Meccan surah of 83 verses, the thirty-sixth surah in the mushaf. Like most Meccan surahs, it focuses on three foundations of faith: the truth of the Quran and the Messenger, the oneness of Allah shown through His signs, and the certainty of resurrection and judgement.

## Quick Facts About Surah Ya-Sin
- **Number of verses**: 83.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 36, in the twenty-second and twenty-third juz.
- **Name**: taken from the two separate letters that open it, *Ya* and *Sin*.
- **Central theme**: resurrection is real, and the signs of Allah in creation prove it.

## A Note on the Virtues of Surah Ya-Sin
You may have heard that Ya-Sin is "the heart of the Quran", or that reading it over the dying brings specific rewards. Scholars of hadith have examined these narrations carefully, and the well-known ones about the specific virtues of Ya-Sin have weak chains of narration according to many specialists. This does not reduce the surah at all: it is the speech of Allah, and every letter of the Quran carries reward. In a hadith of Ibn Mas'ud recorded by at-Tirmidhi, who graded it *hasan sahih*, the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever reads a letter of the Book of Allah receives a good deed, and a good deed is multiplied tenfold. So recite Ya-Sin often, but recite it for what it is, without attaching claims that are not established.

## Verses 1–12: The Wise Quran and the Messenger
﴿يسٓ﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡقُرۡءَانِ ٱلۡحَكِيمِ﴾ ﴿إِنَّكَ لَمِنَ ٱلۡمُرۡسَلِينَ﴾ ﴿عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٖ مُّسۡتَقِيمٖ﴾ ﴿تَنزِيلَ ٱلۡعَزِيزِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ﴾ ﴿لِتُنذِرَ قَوۡمٗا مَّآ أُنذِرَ ءَابَآؤُهُمۡ فَهُمۡ غَٰفِلُونَ﴾ (Quran 36:1-6)

**Meaning:** Ya-Sin. By the Quran full of wisdom, you are truly one of the messengers, on a straight path. It is a revelation from the Almighty, the Most Merciful, so that you may warn a people whose forefathers were not warned, so they are heedless.

The surah opens with an oath by the Quran itself, describing it as *al-hakim*, full of wisdom and precise in its rulings. The answer to the oath is a direct affirmation to the Prophet ﷺ: you are one of the messengers. In Mecca he was being called a poet, a magician and a madman, and Allah answers all of that with a calm, decisive statement.

﴿إِنَّمَا تُنذِرُ مَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلذِّكۡرَ وَخَشِيَ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنَ بِٱلۡغَيۡبِۖ فَبَشِّرۡهُ بِمَغۡفِرَةٖ وَأَجۡرٖ كَرِيمٍ﴾ ﴿إِنَّا نَحۡنُ نُحۡيِ ٱلۡمَوۡتَىٰ وَنَكۡتُبُ مَا قَدَّمُواْ وَءَاثَٰرَهُمۡۚ وَكُلَّ شَيۡءٍ أَحۡصَيۡنَٰهُ فِيٓ إِمَامٖ مُّبِينٖ﴾ (Quran 36:11-12)

**Meaning:** You can only truly warn the one who follows the Reminder and fears the Most Merciful unseen; give him glad tidings of forgiveness and a generous reward. It is We who give life to the dead, and We record what they sent ahead and the traces they left behind, and everything We have counted in a clear register.

Notice the phrase "the traces they left behind" (*atharahum*). Your record is not closed when you die. Knowledge you taught, a charity that keeps benefiting people, and a child you raised well continue to add to it. In Sahih Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reports that the Prophet ﷺ said: "When a person dies, his deeds come to an end except for three: an ongoing charity, knowledge that is benefited from, or a righteous child who prays for him."

## Verses 13–32: The People of the Town
This is the best-known story in the surah. Allah sets forth a parable of a town to which messengers were sent.

﴿وَٱضۡرِبۡ لَهُم مَّثَلًا أَصۡحَٰبَ ٱلۡقَرۡيَةِ إِذۡ جَآءَهَا ٱلۡمُرۡسَلُونَ﴾ ﴿إِذۡ أَرۡسَلۡنَآ إِلَيۡهِمُ ٱثۡنَيۡنِ فَكَذَّبُوهُمَا فَعَزَّزۡنَا بِثَالِثٖ فَقَالُوٓاْ إِنَّآ إِلَيۡكُم مُّرۡسَلُونَ﴾ (Quran 36:13-14)

**Meaning:** Set forth for them the example of the people of the town, when the messengers came to it. When We sent two to them and they denied them, We reinforced them with a third, and they said: We have been sent to you.

The Quran does not name the town or the messengers, and the lesson does not depend on their names. What matters is the pattern: the people rejected the messengers, accused them of being mere humans, called them a bad omen, and threatened to stone them. The messengers replied with patience: "Your omen is with yourselves."

### The Man Who Came Running
﴿وَجَآءَ مِنۡ أَقۡصَا ٱلۡمَدِينَةِ رَجُلࣱ يَسۡعَىٰ قَالَ يَٰقَوۡمِ ٱتَّبِعُواْ ٱلۡمُرۡسَلِينَ﴾ ﴿ٱتَّبِعُواْ مَن لَّا يَسۡـَٔلُكُمۡ أَجۡرٗا وَهُم مُّهۡتَدُونَ﴾ (Quran 36:20-21)

**Meaning:** Then a man came running from the farthest part of the city. He said: O my people, follow the messengers. Follow those who ask you for no payment and who are rightly guided.

This man is one of the most inspiring figures in the Quran. He was not one of the messengers. He came from the edge of the city, which suggests he was not a person of status, yet he ran to defend the truth. He gave his people two simple proofs: the messengers ask for no wage, and they are clearly guided. Then he declared his own faith openly.

﴿قِيلَ ٱدۡخُلِ ٱلۡجَنَّةَۖ قَالَ يَٰلَيۡتَ قَوۡمِي يَعۡلَمُونَ﴾ ﴿بِمَا غَفَرَ لِي رَبِّي وَجَعَلَنِي مِنَ ٱلۡمُكۡرَمِينَ﴾ (Quran 36:26-27)

**Meaning:** It was said: Enter Paradise. He said: If only my people knew how my Lord has forgiven me and placed me among the honoured.

The commentators explain that his people killed him for his words. Yet even after death, his first wish was for his people: if only they knew. He held no bitterness. He wanted them to be saved. This is the heart of a true caller to Allah.

**Lessons from the story:**
1. One sincere person can stand for the truth even when a whole town rejects it.
2. Sincerity shows in not asking people for anything in return.
3. The believer wishes good for others, even those who harm him.
4. The punishment of the town came with a single blast, showing how easy it is for Allah.

## Verses 33–44: The Signs in Creation
After the story, the surah turns to the evidence all around us.

﴿وَءَايَةࣱ لَّهُمُ ٱلۡأَرۡضُ ٱلۡمَيۡتَةُ أَحۡيَيۡنَٰهَا وَأَخۡرَجۡنَا مِنۡهَا حَبّٗا فَمِنۡهُ يَأۡكُلُونَ﴾ (Quran 36:33)

**Meaning:** A sign for them is the dead earth: We give it life and bring forth grain from it, and from it they eat.

The dead earth brought to life by rain is the Quran's favourite image for resurrection. If you have seen dry land turn green after rain, you have seen a small picture of the dead being raised.

﴿وَءَايَةࣱ لَّهُمُ ٱلَّيۡلُ نَسۡلَخُ مِنۡهُ ٱلنَّهَارَ فَإِذَا هُم مُّظۡلِمُونَ﴾ ﴿وَٱلشَّمۡسُ تَجۡرِي لِمُسۡتَقَرّٖ لَّهَاۚ ذَٰلِكَ تَقۡدِيرُ ٱلۡعَزِيزِ ٱلۡعَلِيمِ﴾ ﴿وَٱلۡقَمَرَ قَدَّرۡنَٰهُ مَنَازِلَ حَتَّىٰ عَادَ كَٱلۡعُرۡجُونِ ٱلۡقَدِيمِ﴾ ﴿لَا ٱلشَّمۡسُ يَنۢبَغِي لَهَآ أَن تُدۡرِكَ ٱلۡقَمَرَ وَلَا ٱلَّيۡلُ سَابِقُ ٱلنَّهَارِۚ وَكُلࣱّ فِي فَلَكٖ يَسۡبَحُونَ﴾ (Quran 36:37-40)

**Meaning:** A sign for them is the night: We strip the day from it, and they are in darkness. The sun runs to its appointed resting place; that is the decree of the Almighty, the All-Knowing. And the moon, We have set for it phases until it returns like an old, curved date stalk. It is not for the sun to overtake the moon, nor does the night outrun the day; each floats in an orbit.

These verses describe a cosmos of perfect order. The moon's phases, ending with the thin crescent that looks like a dried, curved palm stalk (*al-'urjun al-qadim*), are an image anyone can verify by looking at the sky. The phrase "each floats in an orbit" (*kullun fi falakin yasbahun*) presents the heavenly bodies as swimming in measured paths, each in its own track, never colliding.

The section ends with ships carrying people across the sea, another sign of Allah's mercy, because He could drown them if He willed.

## Verses 45–54: Heedlessness and the Trumpet
﴿وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمُ ٱتَّقُواْ مَا بَيۡنَ أَيۡدِيكُمۡ وَمَا خَلۡفَكُمۡ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تُرۡحَمُونَ﴾ ﴿وَمَا تَأۡتِيهِم مِّنۡ ءَايَةٖ مِّنۡ ءَايَٰتِ رَبِّهِمۡ إِلَّا كَانُواْ عَنۡهَا مُعۡرِضِينَ﴾ ﴿وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمۡ أَنفِقُواْ مِمَّا رَزَقَكُمُ ٱللَّهُ قَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓاْ أَنُطۡعِمُ مَن لَّوۡ يَشَآءُ ٱللَّهُ أَطۡعَمَهُۥٓ إِنۡ أَنتُمۡ إِلَّا فِي ضَلَٰلٖ مُّبِينٖ﴾ (Quran 36:45-47)

**Meaning:** When it is said to them: Beware of what is before you and what is behind you, so that you may receive mercy, they turn away. And when it is said to them: Spend from what Allah has provided you, those who disbelieve say to those who believe: Should we feed someone whom Allah would have fed if He wished?

This is a striking excuse. The deniers used the will of Allah as an argument against charity. The Quran exposes it as a misuse of belief in decree: Allah tests people by making some rich and some poor, and He commands the rich to give.

﴿وَنُفِخَ فِي ٱلصُّورِ فَإِذَا هُم مِّنَ ٱلۡأَجۡدَاثِ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِمۡ يَنسِلُونَ﴾ ﴿قَالُواْ يَٰوَيۡلَنَا مَنۢ بَعَثَنَا مِن مَّرۡقَدِنَاۜۗ هَٰذَا مَا وَعَدَ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنُ وَصَدَقَ ٱلۡمُرۡسَلُونَ﴾ ﴿إِن كَانَتۡ إِلَّا صَيۡحَةٗ وَٰحِدَةٗ فَإِذَا هُمۡ جَمِيعࣱ لَّدَيۡنَا مُحۡضَرُونَ﴾ (Quran 36:51-53)

**Meaning:** The trumpet will be blown, and suddenly they will be rushing from their graves to their Lord. They will say: Woe to us, who has raised us from our sleeping place? This is what the Most Merciful promised, and the messengers told the truth. It will be only one blast, and they will all be brought before Us.

## Verses 55–68: Paradise, Separation and Testimony
﴿إِنَّ أَصۡحَٰبَ ٱلۡجَنَّةِ ٱلۡيَوۡمَ فِي شُغُلٖ فَٰكِهُونَ﴾ ﴿هُمۡ وَأَزۡوَٰجُهُمۡ فِي ظِلَٰلٍ عَلَى ٱلۡأَرَآئِكِ مُتَّكِـُٔونَ﴾ ﴿لَهُمۡ فِيهَا فَٰكِهَةࣱ وَلَهُم مَّا يَدَّعُونَ﴾ ﴿سَلَٰمࣱ قَوۡلٗا مِّن رَّبّٖ رَّحِيمٖ﴾ (Quran 36:55-58)

**Meaning:** The people of Paradise on that Day will be busy in joy, they and their spouses, in shade, reclining on couches. They will have fruit there and whatever they ask for. "Peace", a word from a Merciful Lord.

The greatest gift in this passage is the last: a greeting of peace spoken directly by their Lord. Everything else is a blessing; this is an honour.

Then comes a frightening command: "Stand apart today, O criminals." Mixed together in this world, people will be separated on that Day.

﴿ٱلۡيَوۡمَ نَخۡتِمُ عَلَىٰٓ أَفۡوَٰهِهِمۡ وَتُكَلِّمُنَآ أَيۡدِيهِمۡ وَتَشۡهَدُ أَرۡجُلُهُم بِمَا كَانُواْ يَكۡسِبُونَ﴾ (Quran 36:65)

**Meaning:** Today We seal their mouths, and their hands will speak to Us, and their feet will testify to what they used to earn.

The tongue that lied in this life will be silenced, and the limbs that committed the deeds will speak. In Sahih Muslim there is a hadith of Anas in which the Prophet ﷺ described the servant arguing with his Lord on that Day, and then his mouth is sealed and his limbs speak about what he did.

## Verses 69–83: Not a Poet, and the Argument for Resurrection
﴿وَمَا عَلَّمۡنَٰهُ ٱلشِّعۡرَ وَمَا يَنۢبَغِي لَهُۥٓۚ إِنۡ هُوَ إِلَّا ذِكۡرࣱ وَقُرۡءَانࣱ مُّبِينࣱ﴾ ﴿لِّيُنذِرَ مَن كَانَ حَيّٗا وَيَحِقَّ ٱلۡقَوۡلُ عَلَى ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ﴾ (Quran 36:69-70)

**Meaning:** We did not teach him poetry, nor is it fitting for him. It is only a reminder and a clear Quran, to warn whoever is alive and so that the word may be proven against the disbelievers.

The Quran describes the one who benefits from it as "alive". A heart that receives guidance is a living heart.

### The Man with the Decayed Bone
﴿أَوَلَمۡ يَرَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ أَنَّا خَلَقۡنَٰهُ مِن نُّطۡفَةٖ فَإِذَا هُوَ خَصِيمࣱ مُّبِينࣱ﴾ ﴿وَضَرَبَ لَنَا مَثَلٗا وَنَسِيَ خَلۡقَهُۥۖ قَالَ مَن يُحۡيِ ٱلۡعِظَٰمَ وَهِيَ رَمِيمࣱ﴾ ﴿قُلۡ يُحۡيِيهَا ٱلَّذِيٓ أَنشَأَهَآ أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٖۖ وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ خَلۡقٍ عَلِيمٌ﴾ (Quran 36:77-79)

**Meaning:** Does man not see that We created him from a drop of fluid, yet he becomes an open opponent? He strikes an example for Us and forgets his own creation. He says: Who will give life to bones when they have decayed? Say: He will give them life who produced them the first time, and He has full knowledge of every creation.

The commentators report that a man from Quraysh came to the Prophet ﷺ holding a crumbling bone, crushed it in his hand and asked who could bring it back to life. The Quran's answer is simple and unbeatable: the One who made it the first time from nothing can remake it. Creating something a second time is, by human logic, easier than the first.

﴿إِنَّمَآ أَمۡرُهُۥٓ إِذَآ أَرَادَ شَيۡـًٔا أَن يَقُولَ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ﴾ ﴿فَسُبۡحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِي بِيَدِهِۦ مَلَكُوتُ كُلِّ شَيۡءٖ وَإِلَيۡهِ تُرۡجَعُونَ﴾ (Quran 36:82-83)

**Meaning:** His command, when He wills a thing, is only that He says to it: Be, and it is. So glory be to Him in whose hand is the dominion of everything, and to Him you will be returned.

The surah ends where it began: with certainty in Allah's power and in the return to Him.

## Key Vocabulary
- **Al-Hakim**: full of wisdom, precise.
- **Al-Qaryah**: the town in the parable.
- **Al-'Urjun al-qadim**: the old, curved, dried date stalk, an image of the thin crescent.
- **Falak**: orbit.
- **As-Sur**: the trumpet.
- **Al-Ajdath**: the graves.
- **Kun fa-yakun**: "Be, and it is."

## Tajweed Notes for Reciting Ya-Sin
- **Opening letters**: *Ya-Sin* is read *Yā Sīīīn*, with the *sin* stretched six counts (madd lazim), because the name of the letter ends in a long vowel followed by a sukun. In the narration of Hafs, the final *nun* of *Sin* is pronounced clearly (izhar) before the *waw* of *wal-Qur'an*, an exception to the usual merging rule.
- **Izhar and ikhfa**: compare *min aqsa* (verse 20), where the nun is pronounced clearly because it meets the throat letter hamza, with *li-tundhira* (verse 6), where the nun sakinah is hidden before *dhal* with a nasal sound.
- **Qalqalah**: at the end of verses ending in *dal* or *qaf* when stopping.
- **Madd 'arid**: most verses end in *-un*, *-in* or *-im*, giving the natural rhythm of the surah when you pause.

For a full guide to these rules, see [the rules of Noon Sakinah and Tanween](/blog/noon-sakinah-tanween-rules-english/).

## Practical Lessons from Surah Ya-Sin
1. **Leave good traces.** Your record continues after death; invest in ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge and righteous children.
2. **Be the man who came running.** You do not need a title to stand for the truth.
3. **Wish good for people who oppose you.** "If only my people knew."
4. **Read the signs.** Look at the dead land after rain, the phases of the moon and the order of the sky, and let them strengthen your faith in resurrection.
5. **Do not hide behind decree.** Allah's will is never an excuse for withholding charity.
6. **Guard your limbs.** They will testify.
7. **Trust "Be, and it is".** Nothing you ask of Allah is difficult for Him.

## How to Teach Surah Ya-Sin to Children
- Tell the story of the man who came running in simple words, and ask them what made him brave.
- Watch the moon over a month and connect its phases to verse 39.
- Explain "Be, and it is" when they ask how Allah can do something amazing.

## A Memorisation Plan
Ya-Sin is long enough that it helps to break it into the sections above:
1. Week 1: verses 1–12.
2. Week 2: verses 13–32, the story.
3. Week 3: verses 33–54, the signs and the trumpet.
4. Week 4: verses 55–83, the Hereafter and the closing argument.
Revise each section daily before adding the next.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does "Ya-Sin" mean?
Ya-Sin are two separate Arabic letters. Like the other opening letters of surahs, their exact meaning is known to Allah; scholars note they draw attention to the miracle of a Quran composed from the same letters the Arabs used.

### Is Surah Ya-Sin the heart of the Quran?
A narration says so, but its chain is weak according to many hadith scholars. The surah is still a great surah of the Quran.

### Who was the man who came running?
A believer from the town in the parable. The Quran does not name him; some reports give him a name, but the lesson is in his action, not his identity.

### What is the main theme of Ya-Sin?
Resurrection and the truth of the message, supported by the signs of Allah in creation.

### What does "kullun fi falakin yasbahun" mean?
Each of them, the sun and the moon, floats or swims in its own orbit.

### What does "kun fa-yakun" mean?
"Be, and it is": when Allah wills something, He only says to it "Be", and it comes into existence.

### How many verses are in Surah Ya-Sin?
83 verses.

## Conclusion
Surah Ya-Sin carries you from the certainty of revelation, through the courage of one believing man, across the signs of earth and sky, to the moment when the trumpet is blown and every deed is witnessed. Its final words leave no room for doubt: the One who created you the first time will bring you back, and to Him you will return. Recite it often, and let each recitation make your heart one of the "living" hearts the surah describes.
