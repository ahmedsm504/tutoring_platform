Many people enter Ramadan with a sincere intention: "This year I will finish the Quran and pray every night." Then the first week passes and they discover that twenty pages a day is heavy for someone who read very little during the year, that night prayer is tiring, and that their reading is slow and hesitant. They fall behind, feel guilty, and some give up.

The problem is rarely weak intention. It is the **sudden jump** from almost nothing to a full juz a day. The early Muslims understood this, and some of them called Sha'ban, the month before Ramadan, "the month of the reciters", because they increased their Quran reading in it to get ready.

This guide gives you a four-week plan for the month before Ramadan. It takes you gradually from where you are now to your Ramadan target, while correcting your recitation, reviewing what you have memorized, and preparing your heart and your home.

## Why Prepare at All?

The Prophet ﷺ fasted more in Sha'ban than in any month other than Ramadan. 'A'ishah said: "I never saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ complete a month of fasting except Ramadan, and I never saw him fast more in any month than in Sha'ban" (al-Bukhari and Muslim). Sha'ban was a practical warm-up: the body gets used to fasting and the heart to worship.

Preparing with the Quran brings clear benefits:
- **No shock in week one.** If you arrive in Ramadan already reading ten pages a day, moving to twenty is easy.
- **Faster, more accurate reading.** Regular reading improves your speed without sacrificing tajweed.
- **Memorized surahs ready for night prayer.** You can review what you know, or add a surah to pray with.
- **A realistic picture of your time.** You discover when you can actually read in your real day, not an imaginary ideal one.

Allah introduces Ramadan through the Quran:

﴿شَهۡرُ رَمَضَانَ ٱلَّذِيٓ أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانُ هُدٗى لِّلنَّاسِ وَبَيِّنَٰتٖ مِّنَ ٱلۡهُدَىٰ وَٱلۡفُرۡقَانِۚ فَمَن شَهِدَ مِنكُمُ ٱلشَّهۡرَ فَلۡيَصُمۡهُۖ وَمَن كَانَ مَرِيضًا أَوۡ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٖ فَعِدَّةࣱ مِّنۡ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَۗ يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ بِكُمُ ٱلۡيُسۡرَ وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ ٱلۡعُسۡرَ وَلِتُكۡمِلُواْ ٱلۡعِدَّةَ وَلِتُكَبِّرُواْ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَىٰكُمۡ وَلَعَلَّكُمۡ تَشۡكُرُونَ﴾ (Quran 2:185)

If the month is defined by the Quran, then the Quran should be at the centre of our preparation.

## Before You Start: Know Your Starting Point

Take five minutes and answer honestly:
1. How many pages do I read a day right now? (Zero is a fine answer; honesty is what matters.)
2. How long does one page take me?
3. Am I confident my recitation is correct, or do I stumble often?
4. What have I memorized, and can I pray with it without hesitating?
5. How much time do I truly have each day for the Quran?

Time yourself reading one page carefully. Most people take three to five minutes, so a juz takes an hour to an hour and a half. Once you know this, you know what Ramadan will demand and how to spread it. Our [khatam planner](/en/quran/khatam-planner/) can turn your available time into a schedule.

## Week One: Build a Fixed Daily Habit

**Goal**: make the Quran a fixed part of your day, however small.

- **Portion**: two pages a day if you do not read at all now, or double your current portion if you already have one.
- **Time**: attach it to an existing habit, such as straight after Fajr or after Maghrib. Linking a new habit to an old one is stronger than any alarm.
- **Place**: keep your mushaf in a fixed, visible spot, so you never have to search for it.
- **Tracking**: tick off each day on a sheet on the wall or in a notes app.

### Why start small?
Because consistency matters more than quantity. The Prophet ﷺ said: "The most beloved deeds to Allah are the most consistent, even if they are small" (al-Bukhari and Muslim). Two pages every day for a week builds a habit; twenty pages once and then nothing builds nothing.

## Week Two: Correct Your Recitation

**Goal**: read correctly, because you will read thousands of verses in Ramadan, and a repeated mistake becomes a fixed one.

- **Start with Al-Fatiha.** You will recite it hundreds of times in Ramadan. Check the letter ض in "ad-dallin", the doubled ي in "iyyaka", and the light س in "nasta'in".
- **Review noon sakinah, tanween and the long vowels.** These are the most frequent rules in the Quran, and mastering them alone transforms your recitation. See our guides to [noon sakinah and tanween](/blog/noon-sakinah-tanween-rules-english/) and [madd rules](/blog/madd-rules-tajweed-english/).
- **Listen, then read.** Choose a slow, precise reciter, listen to a page, then read it yourself and compare.
- **Read to a teacher.** Even one session a week. A teacher will spot in minutes mistakes you would not notice in years.

### How do I know my recitation needs work?
If you stumble on many words, are unsure when to lengthen a vowel, or do not know what ghunnah is, you need some correction. There is no shame in that. The Prophet ﷺ said that the one who reads the Quran and stumbles over it, finding it difficult, has a double reward (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

## Week Three: Increase Your Reading and Review Your Memorization

**Goal**: get close to your Ramadan portion and prepare what you will pray with.

- **Portion**: raise it to ten pages a day, half a juz, split into two sittings: five after Fajr and five in the evening.
- **Memorization**: review what you know, starting with the surahs you will recite in Tarawih or night prayer. If you know Juz Amma, review a quarter-hizb a day. Our [Quran revision system](/blog/quran-revision-system-english/) explains how to keep what you have memorized.
- **New memorization**: if you want to add a surah for night prayer, this is the week. Surah Al-Mulk or Al-Muzzammil are excellent choices.
- **Reflection**: choose one verse from your reading each day and read its short tafsir.

## Week Four: Rehearse a Ramadan Day

**Goal**: live one or two days on a Ramadan routine before Ramadan arrives, so you find and fix problems early.

- **Try a full juz.** On a day off, read a juz split across the five prayers: four pages after each.
- **Try fasting.** Fast a day or two in Sha'ban if you can, to see how you read while fasting and which times suit you.
- **Try night prayer.** Pray eight rak'ahs with what you have memorized and see how long it takes.
- **Write your final plan.** After the rehearsal, write down your Ramadan plan: how many khatams, when you will read, and what you will pray with. Our [guide to finishing the Quran in Ramadan](/blog/finish-quran-in-ramadan-english/) has ready-made schedules.

## The Four Weeks at a Glance

| Week | Daily reading | Focus | Sign of success |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 pages or double your current amount | A fixed daily habit | Seven days without a break |
| 2 | 5 pages | Correcting recitation | Reading Al-Fatiha to a teacher |
| 3 | 10 pages | Reviewing memorization | Reciting what you will pray with |
| 4 | One juz on rehearsal day | Simulating Ramadan | A written Ramadan plan |

## A Sample Rehearsal Day

Here is how one working person spent a rehearsal day to test whether a juz a day was realistic:

| Time | What he did | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| After Fajr | 4 pages slowly, then review of Surah Al-Mulk | 25 minutes |
| Commute | Listened to 4 pages from a precise reciter | 15 minutes |
| Lunch break | 4 pages on a mushaf app | 15 minutes |
| After Asr at home | 4 pages and the tafsir of one verse | 20 minutes |
| After Isha | 4 pages, then two rak'ahs with memorized surahs | 25 minutes |

He discovered two things: a juz was possible if he spread it out, and the lunch-break slot was the hardest because of work. So he moved more reading to Fajr. That is the value of a rehearsal: you learn about yourself before the month begins.

## If Your Circumstances Are Different

### Parents of young children
Your time comes in fragments, so make your portion fragmented too: two pages after each prayer gives you a khatam in two months, and four pages after each prayer gives you one in Ramadan. Listening while you cook counts as support for your reading, and children who see you reading will want to join you.

### Students
Ramadan may coincide with exams. Plan for it now: a small fixed portion you never drop on exam days, and more on lighter days.

### Older people and those who are unwell
Read what you can, and remember that listening is worship. The Prophet ﷺ taught that when a person who used to do a good deed falls ill or travels, the reward of what they used to do is still written for them (al-Bukhari). Do not grieve over what you cannot do; strive in what you can.

## Preparing Your Heart

The Quran does not benefit a distracted heart. Real preparation includes:
- **Repentance.** Start with the sins you know about; a heart weighed down by sins finds reading heavy.
- **Mending relationships.** Make peace with anyone you have fallen out with and keep ties of kinship; a clear heart reflects better.
- **Reducing distractions.** Decide now what you will cut back in Ramadan: apps, shows, late nights. Begin cutting back in Sha'ban so it is not a shock.
- **Du'a.** Ask Allah to let you reach Ramadan and to help you fast, pray and read in it.

Allah says:

﴿أَفَلَا يَتَدَبَّرُونَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَ أَمۡ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبٍ أَقۡفَالُهَآ﴾ (Quran 47:24)

A locked heart cannot reflect, and its key is repentance and turning back to Allah.

## Preparing Your Home and Family

- **A family khatam.** Agree on a khatam where each family member takes some juz, so the whole home finishes together.
- **A Quran corner.** Set up a space with mushafs, a stand and a prayer mat for reading and night prayer.
- **Children's goals.** Choose an age-appropriate target, such as the last ten surahs or our [Juz Amma 30-day challenge](/en/quran/juz-amma-challenge/) with its "Start on 1 Ramadan" option. Our [Ramadan plan for kids](/blog/ramadan-quran-plan-for-kids-english/) has ideas for every age.
- **The pre-iftar circle.** Agree on ten minutes before Maghrib for Quran and du'a together.

## Common Mistakes When Preparing

- **The perfect plan.** Writing a three-khatam plan when you currently read nothing. Start from reality.
- **"I'll start in Ramadan."** This is the most common way khatams fail. Start today, even with one page.
- **Skipping correction.** Lots of reading with repeated mistakes fixes those mistakes. One hour with a teacher before Ramadan is worth ten hours alone.
- **Ignoring your body.** Late nights and heavy meals weaken your ability to read. Fix your sleep in Sha'ban.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should I start preparing for Ramadan?
Ideally four weeks before, around the start of Sha'ban. Ramadan 1448 AH is expected to begin around 8 February 2027 according to astronomical calculations, so begin in early January. If you start late, compress the four weeks into two.

### I have missed fasts from last Ramadan. When should I make them up?
They should be made up before the next Ramadan if you are able. 'A'ishah said she would have fasts to make up from Ramadan and would only be able to do so in Sha'ban (al-Bukhari and Muslim). Make your make-up fasts part of your preparation, and read while fasting.

### Should I start a khatam before Ramadan?
If you can, it is excellent preparation, but the priority is building a daily habit. If you are close to finishing a khatam, complete it in Sha'ban and start fresh on the first night of Ramadan.

### I don't know tajweed. Should I read in Ramadan or learn first?
Do both. Do not stop reading because your tajweed is weak, and at the same time work on correcting it, starting with Al-Fatiha and the most frequent rules.
