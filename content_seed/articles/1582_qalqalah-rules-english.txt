**Qalqalah** means "shaking" or "echoing". It is a small bounce in the sound of certain letters when they carry a sukūn, so they do not sound swallowed.

## The five letters
They are gathered in the phrase **قُطۡبُ جَدٍّ** (*quṭbu jad*):
| Letter | Name |
|---|---|
| ق | Qaf |
| ط | Ṭaa |
| ب | Baa |
| ج | Jeem |
| د | Daal |

## Why do these letters bounce?
These letters are both **strong (shiddah)**, which blocks the sound, and **voiced (jahr)**, which blocks the breath. When they have a sukūn, nothing would be heard without a small release, so the qalqalah lets the letter be heard.

## Types of qalqalah
| Type | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Minor (ṣughrā) | Sukūn in the middle of a word or when joining | ﴿يَقۡطَعُونَ﴾ |
| Major (kubrā) | Stopping on the letter at the end of an ayah | ﴿ٱلۡفَلَقِ﴾, ﴿أَحَدٌ﴾ when stopping |
- Some scholars add a stronger level when the stopped letter is also doubled, as in ﴿وَتَبَّ﴾ when stopping.

## How to pronounce it
- **Release the letter** with a short, light bounce.
- **Do not add a vowel**: *al-falaq*, not *al-falaqa*.

## Common mistakes
1. **No bounce at all**, so ﴿أَحَدٌ﴾ sounds like *aḥat*.
2. **Too strong a bounce**, adding a full vowel.
3. **Qalqalah on other letters** like kaaf or taa (ت), which are whispered letters.
