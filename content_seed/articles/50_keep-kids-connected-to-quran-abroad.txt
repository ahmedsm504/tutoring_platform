Thousands of Muslim families live in the UK, the US, Canada, Australia and across Europe. One of their biggest concerns is the same: **how do our children grow up loving the Quran and Arabic when they spend all day in another language and culture?**

## The real challenges
- **Language:** children speak English (or another language) all day, so their Arabic weakens and some letters become hard to pronounce.
- **Time:** long school days, after-school activities and homework.
- **Environment:** the nearest mosque or Quran school may be far away.
- **Motivation:** they may not see friends memorizing around them.

## 1. Online classes: the practical solution
With online learning, your child studies with a qualified teacher from home:
- **Times in your own time zone**, after school or at weekends.
- **One-to-one sessions** adapted to your child's Arabic level.
- **No commuting, no waiting.**

## 2. Combine reading Arabic with memorization
A child who can't read Arabic well needs a foundation. The best approach combines:
- **Learning to read** with a step-by-step method such as our [Nour Al-Bayan course](/pricing/#nour-albayan).
- **Memorizing short surahs** by listening and repeating.

## 3. Give extra attention to "new" sounds
Children abroad usually struggle with ح ع ق خ غ ض ظ ص ط, and sometimes ث ذ. See [the hardest Arabic letters for non-Arabs](/blog/arabic-letters-pronunciation-for-non-arabs/).

## 4. Make home an Arabic, Quran-friendly place
- **Speak Arabic at home**, even in dialect: what matters is that they hear it daily.
- **Quran in the background:** in the car, in the morning before school.
- **Stories of the Prophets** at bedtime.
- **A small Quran corner:** their own Mushaf and a star chart.

## 5. Tie the Quran to identity, not just duty
Children abroad often ask, "Why am I different?" Make the Quran part of a **positive identity**:
- Celebrate their achievements in front of family.
- Connect them with other Muslim children who memorize.
- Link Ramadan and Eid to the Quran.

## 6. A realistic weekly routine
| When | What |
|---|---|
| Two weekdays | Online class with the teacher (30–40 min) |
| Other school days | 10 minutes of review and listening |
| Weekend | A longer session or a family Quran quiz |

## 7. Use technology on your side
- **Audio homework:** your child records their recitation when it suits them and the teacher replies.
- **Quran games** in our student portal make revision fun.
- **Monthly reports** keep you informed.

## Don't wait
The younger a child starts, the easier the Arabic sounds and the stronger the bond. And if your child is starting later, it's still not too late.
