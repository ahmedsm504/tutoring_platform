Children ask big questions. "Where is Allah?" "Who made Allah?" "Does Allah have a family?" These questions are not a problem; they are a sign of a curious, thinking mind. And Allah has given us a short, beautiful surah that answers them clearly: Surah Al-Ikhlas. Just four verses, and it tells us who Allah is in the simplest and most perfect way.

The Prophet ﷺ told us that this little surah is equal to a third of the Quran. Imagine: reciting it three times is like the reward of reading the Quran from cover to cover! In this guide for parents, we explain Surah Al-Ikhlas to children in simple English, verse by verse, with gentle answers to their questions about Allah, activities, a story and a memorization plan.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Ikhlas

- **Position**: surah 112, in Juz 'Amma.
- **Number of verses**: four.
- **Type**: Meccan according to many scholars.
- **Name**: al-Ikhlas means "sincerity" or "purity", because it purifies our belief in Allah from anything that does not suit Him. It is also called Surat at-Tawhid, the surah of Allah's Oneness.

## The Whole Surah

﴿قُلۡ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ﴾ ﴿ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ﴾ ﴿لَمۡ يَلِدۡ وَلَمۡ يُولَدۡ﴾ ﴿وَلَمۡ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدُۢ﴾ (Quran 112:1-4)

## Why Was It Revealed?

Scholars of tafsir mention that some people came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: "Describe your Lord to us. What is He made of? Who is His family?" So Allah revealed this surah as the answer. At-Tirmidhi reported a narration of this meaning, and scholars discuss its chain, but the meaning is agreed upon: the surah is Allah describing Himself.

### Explaining it to your child
"People wanted to know about Allah, so Allah Himself answered in this surah. This is the best way to learn about Allah: from what He told us about Himself."

## Verse 1: Say, He Is Allah, One

### Explaining it to your child
"Qul means 'say'. Allah is telling the Prophet ﷺ, and all of us, to say this out loud with confidence: He is Allah, Ahad, the One. There is only one God. Not two, not three. He has no partner, no helper He needs, and no one like Him."

### Lesson for kids
"When you say 'Allahu Ahad', you are saying the most important truth in the world. It's the first thing we teach babies, and the last thing we hope to say in life: La ilaha illallah."

### Activity: the one sun
Go outside on a sunny day and ask: "How many suns do we have?" One! "What if there were two suns fighting over the sky?" Everything would be chaos! Then say: "The whole universe works perfectly because there is only One Creator, Allah."

## Verse 2: Allah, As-Samad

### Explaining it to your child
"As-Samad is a special name. It means the One everyone turns to when they need something, and He doesn't need anyone. People need food, water, sleep and help. Allah needs nothing at all. Everybody, every animal and every plant depends on Allah, and Allah depends on no one."

### Activity: who needs what?
Make two columns on paper: "Things I need" and "Things Allah needs". In the first, your child writes or draws: food, water, sleep, parents, a home. In the second column... nothing! Then explain: "That's what As-Samad means. Everyone needs Allah, and Allah needs nothing."

### Lesson for kids: turn to Allah first
"When you need help, finding something you lost, passing a test, getting better when you're sick, turn to As-Samad first. Make du'a, then do your part."

## Verse 3: He Neither Begets Nor Is Born

### Explaining it to your child
"Allah has no children, and Allah has no parents. People are born and have children because we live and then die, and our children carry on after us. But Allah never began and will never end. He was always there, and He will always be there. He doesn't need a son or a daughter."

### Answering a common question: "Who made Allah?"
This is one of the most common questions children ask. A gentle answer: "Everything that is made needs a maker. Your toy was made by a factory, your house was built by builders. But Allah is not 'made'. He is the One who makes everything. He was always there, before anything else. That's exactly what this verse teaches: He was not born."

### Tip for parents
Don't panic or scold when your child asks this. Praise them for thinking, answer simply, and tell them that some things about Allah are too great for our minds, just as a small cup cannot hold the whole ocean.

## Verse 4: And There Is None Comparable to Him

### Explaining it to your child
"Nothing and no one is like Allah. Not a person, not an angel, not the sun, not anything we can imagine. Whatever picture comes into your mind, Allah is greater than that. That's why we don't draw pictures of Allah, and why we say 'Allahu Akbar', Allah is greater."

### Activity: "Allah is greater"
Play a game: your child names something huge, like a mountain, the ocean, the sun, or the whole galaxy, and after each one you both say: "Allahu Akbar! Allah is greater than that."

## Why Is It Equal to a Third of the Quran?

The Prophet ﷺ said: "By the One in whose hand is my soul, it is equal to a third of the Quran" (al-Bukhari). Scholars explain that the Quran talks about three big topics: who Allah is, the stories and laws, and the Hereafter. Surah Al-Ikhlas is completely about the first topic: who Allah is. So it covers a third of the Quran's meaning.

### Explaining it to your child
"The Quran is like a big tree with three main branches, and Surah Al-Ikhlas is one whole branch! That's why it's so special."

### Activity: three times
Teach your child to recite Al-Ikhlas three times, and tell them: "That's like the reward of reading the whole Quran!" Make it part of their bedtime routine.

## The Companion Who Loved This Surah

The Prophet ﷺ sent a man to lead a group, and in every prayer he recited Surah Al-Ikhlas after the other surah. When they asked him why, he said: "Because it describes the Most Merciful, and I love to recite it." The Prophet ﷺ said: "Tell him that Allah loves him" (al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### Explaining it to your child
"This companion loved Surah Al-Ikhlas because it talks about Allah. And because he loved it, Allah loved him! If you love this surah and recite it a lot, in sha' Allah, Allah will love you too."

## The Morning, Evening and Bedtime Surahs

The Prophet ﷺ used to recite Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas every night before sleeping: he would cup his hands, blow lightly into them, recite the three surahs, and wipe over as much of his body as he could, three times (al-Bukhari). He also taught that reciting them three times in the morning and evening is enough protection (Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi, who graded it authentic).

### Activity: the bedtime three
Make it a bedtime ritual: cup hands together, recite Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, blow gently and wipe over the head, face and body, three times. Children love this sunnah, and it gives them a sense of safety before sleep.

## Answering More of Your Child's Questions About Allah

### "Where is Allah?"
"Allah is above the heavens, over His Throne, as He told us in the Quran, and He sees and hears everything, and His knowledge is with us everywhere."

### "Can I see Allah?"
"Not in this life. Our eyes aren't strong enough. But the believers will see Allah in Paradise, and it will be the most wonderful thing ever."

### "Does Allah sleep?"
"Never. Ayat al-Kursi tells us that Allah never gets tired or sleepy. He looks after you all night while you sleep."

### "Does Allah love me?"
"Allah loves those who are kind, patient, truthful and who turn back to Him when they make mistakes. When you do good, you're doing what Allah loves."

## A Story to Tell Your Child: "The Big Question"

One evening, six-year-old Yahya was lying in bed looking at the ceiling. Suddenly he asked: "Mum, who made Allah?" His mother smiled and sat next to him.

"That's a really clever question," she said. "Let's think together. Who made your bed?" "The furniture shop," said Yahya. "And who made the furniture shop?" "Builders!" "And who made the builders?" Yahya thought. "Allah!" "Right. Everything that's made needs a maker. But Allah isn't 'made'. He's the One who makes everything. He was always there, before anything at all."

Yahya was quiet. "So Allah doesn't have a mum and dad?" "No," said his mother. "That's in a surah you know. Let's say it together." And they recited: "Qul huwallahu ahad, Allahus-Samad, lam yalid wa lam yulad, wa lam yakun lahu kufuwan ahad."

"Lam yulad means He wasn't born!" Yahya said. "Exactly," said his mother. "Allah told us the answer Himself."

Then they cupped their hands and recited Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas three times, blowing gently and wiping over Yahya's head and body. Yahya fell asleep smiling, whispering: "Allahu Ahad."

Ask your child: "What did Yahya ask? How did his mother answer?"

## What We Learn From Surah Al-Ikhlas

- **Allah is One**, with no partner.
- **Everyone needs Allah**, and Allah needs no one.
- **Allah has no children** and no parents.
- **Nothing is like Allah**.
- **Big questions are welcome**, and the Quran answers them.
- **Al-Ikhlas equals a third of the Quran**.
- **Loving this surah** brings Allah's love.
- **Recite it before sleep** with Al-Falaq and An-Nas.

## Frequently Asked Questions From Parents

### My child asked "Who made Allah?" Did I do something wrong?
Not at all. It's a natural question from a thinking child. Answer calmly and simply, as in the story above, and use verse 3. Children who feel safe asking questions at home grow up with stronger faith.

### At what age should a child memorize Al-Ikhlas?
Many children memorize it between ages two and four, because it's short and rhythmic. Understanding its meaning can grow with them over the years.

### Does reciting it three times replace reading the whole Quran?
It equals a third of the Quran in reward, as the hadith says, but it doesn't replace reading the Quran itself. We encourage children to love both.
