Surah Al-Masad is the only surah of the Quran that names one of the Prophet's personal enemies: his own uncle, Abu Lahab. It teaches a lesson that runs through the whole Quran: closeness in family to the Prophet ﷺ does not save anyone without faith, and wealth and children are worth nothing against the judgement of Allah. It also contains a striking prophecy: it declared that Abu Lahab would enter the Fire, meaning he would die a disbeliever. He lived for years afterwards and could have "disproved" the Quran simply by declaring Islam, even falsely. He never did.

This guide explains the surah verse by verse in English.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Masad
- **Number of verses**: 5.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 111, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Names**: Al-Masad (Palm Fibre), Tabbat, Al-Lahab, and the surah of Abu Lahab.

## The Full Text of Surah Al-Masad
﴿تَبَّتۡ يَدَآ أَبِي لَهَبٖ وَتَبَّ﴾ ﴿مَآ أَغۡنَىٰ عَنۡهُ مَالُهُۥ وَمَا كَسَبَ﴾ ﴿سَيَصۡلَىٰ نَارٗا ذَاتَ لَهَبٖ﴾ ﴿وَٱمۡرَأَتُهُۥ حَمَّالَةَ ٱلۡحَطَبِ﴾ ﴿فِي جِيدِهَا حَبۡلࣱ مِّن مَّسَدِۭ﴾ (Quran 111:1-5)

**Meaning:** Perish the hands of Abu Lahab, and perish he. His wealth did not avail him, nor what he earned. He will burn in a fire of blazing flame, and his wife, the carrier of firewood. Around her neck is a rope of twisted palm fibre.

## Who Was Abu Lahab?
Abu Lahab was 'Abd al-'Uzza ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, a paternal uncle of the Prophet ﷺ. He was given the nickname Abu Lahab, "father of flame", because of his radiant, reddish face. Despite his kinship, he was among the fiercest opponents of the Prophet ﷺ, following him through the markets and telling people not to believe him. His wife, Umm Jamil Arwa bint Harb, the sister of Abu Sufyan, joined him in harming the Prophet ﷺ.

**A striking contrast**: the Prophet ﷺ had several uncles. Some accepted Islam, like Hamzah and al-'Abbas. One protected him without accepting Islam, Abu Talib. And one became his bitter enemy, Abu Lahab. Family ties did not decide who was guided; faith and choice did.

## The Cause of Revelation
In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn 'Abbas reported that when the verse "And warn your closest relatives" (26:214) was revealed, the Prophet ﷺ climbed the hill of as-Safa and called out to the clans of Quraysh until they gathered. Those who could not come sent someone to see what was happening. Abu Lahab and Quraysh came. He said: "If I told you that there were horsemen in the valley about to attack you, would you believe me?" They said: "Yes, we have only ever known you to be truthful." He said: "Then I am a warner to you before a severe punishment." Abu Lahab said: "Perish you for the rest of the day! Is this why you gathered us?" Then Allah revealed: "Perish the hands of Abu Lahab, and perish he. His wealth did not avail him, nor what he earned."

**Lessons from this account:**
1. The Prophet ﷺ began his public call with his own relatives.
2. He appealed to his known truthfulness before presenting the message.
3. The first public rejection came from his own uncle.
4. Allah Himself answered on behalf of His Prophet ﷺ.

## Verse 1: Perish the Hands of Abu Lahab
﴿تَبَّتۡ يَدَآ أَبِي لَهَبٖ وَتَبَّ﴾ (Quran 111:1)

**"Tabbat"**: may they perish, be ruined. **"The hands of Abu Lahab"**: hands are mentioned because most actions are done with them; the meaning is that all his efforts are ruined. **"Wa tabb"**: and he himself has perished. The first phrase is like a prayer against him or a statement that his efforts are lost; the second confirms that his ruin is certain.

**Reflect**: Abu Lahab said to the Prophet ﷺ "tabban laka", "perish you", and Allah answered him with the same word: "tabbat yada Abi Lahab". The recompense matched the deed.

## Verse 2: His Wealth Did Not Avail Him
﴿مَآ أَغۡنَىٰ عَنۡهُ مَالُهُۥ وَمَا كَسَبَ﴾ (Quran 111:2)

His wealth did not protect him from Allah's punishment, nor did "what he earned". Commentators explain "what he earned" as his gains and status, or as his children, since a child is part of a man's earnings. Abu Lahab was wealthy and had sons, and none of it helped him.

## Verse 3: A Fire of Blazing Flame
﴿سَيَصۡلَىٰ نَارٗا ذَاتَ لَهَبٖ﴾ (Quran 111:3)

**"He will burn"**: the *sa-* prefix marks a certain future. **"A fire of blazing flame"** (*naran dhata lahab*): notice the link with his nickname. The man called "father of flame" will burn in a fire "of flame".

### The prophecy
This verse was revealed in Mecca while Abu Lahab was alive. It stated with certainty that he would die a disbeliever and enter the Fire. He lived for about ten more years, until shortly after the Battle of Badr, and never accepted Islam. Had he announced his Islam, even insincerely, he could have tried to discredit the Quran. He did not, and the prophecy was fulfilled. This is one of the signs of the truth of the Prophet ﷺ.

## Verses 4–5: His Wife, the Carrier of Firewood
﴿وَٱمۡرَأَتُهُۥ حَمَّالَةَ ٱلۡحَطَبِ﴾ ﴿فِي جِيدِهَا حَبۡلࣱ مِّن مَّسَدِۭ﴾ (Quran 111:4-5)

**"And his wife"**: Umm Jamil will burn with him. **"The carrier of firewood"** (*hammalat al-hatab*) has two well-known explanations:
1. **She spread malicious gossip.** The Arabs called a tale-bearer "a carrier of firewood", because gossip kindles the fire of enmity between people. She went around spreading slander about the Prophet ﷺ.
2. **She carried thorns.** It is said she placed thorns and brushwood in the Prophet's path to hurt him.
It is also said she will carry firewood in Hell to feed the fire of her husband.

**"Around her neck is a rope of palm fibre"** (*fi jidiha hablun min masad*): a coarse, tightly twisted rope. This may describe her state in Hell, or picture her humiliation: the neck that once wore necklaces now bears a rough rope.

## Word Meanings
- **Tabbat**: perished, were ruined.
- **Ma aghna 'anhu**: did not avail him.
- **Ma kasab**: what he earned, his gains or his children.
- **Sa-yasla**: he will burn.
- **Dhata lahab**: of blazing flame.
- **Hammalat al-hatab**: carrier of firewood.
- **Jid**: neck.
- **Masad**: twisted palm fibre.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **"Hands"** as a metaphor for effort and work.
2. **From the ruin of his deeds to the ruin of his self**: "tabbat yada" then "wa tabb".
3. **"Abu Lahab" and "dhata lahab"**: his nickname becomes his fate.
4. **"Hammalah"**: an intensive form, a habitual carrier.
5. **"Jid"**: a word Arabs used for the graceful neck of a beautiful woman, so the coarse rope in its place is a sharp irony.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Qalqalah kubra** when stopping on *lahab*, *tabb*, *kasab* and *al-hatab* (ba), and on *masad* (dal). It is strongest in *wa tabb* because of the doubled letter.
- **Madd munfasil** in *ma aghna*: alif at the end of *ma* followed by hamza, four or five counts in Hafs via ash-Shatibiyyah.
- **Ikhfa** in *naran dhata*: tanween hidden before *dhal*.
- **Idgham with ghunnah** in *hablun min* and *min masad*: nun and tanween merge into meem with a nasal sound.
- **Lam qamariyyah** in *al-hatab*.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before Al-Masad comes An-Nasr, the good news that Islam would triumph and people would enter it in crowds. Al-Masad then shows the loss of its enemies, even the closest relatives of the Prophet ﷺ. After it comes Al-Ikhlas, defining the pure tawhid that Abu Lahab fought against.

## Kinship Does Not Save Without Faith
The central lesson of the surah is that lineage and family ties do not replace faith. The Prophet ﷺ made this clear to his closest relatives. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that when "And warn your closest relatives" was revealed, the Prophet ﷺ said: "O people of Quraysh, save yourselves; I cannot avail you anything against Allah. O Banu 'Abd Manaf, I cannot avail you anything against Allah. O 'Abbas ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, I cannot avail you anything against Allah. O Safiyyah, aunt of the Messenger of Allah, I cannot avail you anything against Allah. O Fatimah daughter of Muhammad, ask me for whatever you wish of my wealth; I cannot avail you anything against Allah."

On the other side, Salman the Persian, Bilal the Abyssinian and Suhayb the Roman were raised by faith despite having no ties to Quraysh. "The most honoured of you with Allah is the most mindful of Him" (49:13).

## Gossip: The Firewood of Strife
If "carrier of firewood" refers to tale-bearing, the surah warns against one of the most destructive habits. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Hudhayfah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "A tale-bearer will not enter Paradise." And in al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn 'Abbas reported that the Prophet ﷺ passed by two graves and said: "They are being punished, and not for something major in their eyes: one did not protect himself from urine, and the other used to spread gossip."

**How to deal with a tale-bearer:**
1. Do not believe him straight away: "If a wrongdoer brings you news, verify it" (49:6).
2. Advise him to stop.
3. Do not spy on the person he spoke about.
4. Do not pass on what he said, or you become a carrier of firewood yourself.

## Spouses in Good and Evil
The surah shows a husband and wife united in evil and sharing the same fate. The Quran gives other examples: the wife of Pharaoh believed despite her husband and was saved; the wives of Nuh and Lut disbelieved despite their husbands and were lost. Spouses are partners on a road, and each should help the other towards good.

## Practical Lessons
1. Do not rely on your lineage; faith and deeds save.
2. Do not be deceived by wealth and children.
3. Beware of gossip.
4. Trust Allah's promises; this surah's prophecy came true.
5. Call your relatives to good, as the Prophet ﷺ did.
6. Choose a spouse who helps you towards good.

## Teaching Children
- Tell the story of as-Safa and how the Prophet ﷺ called his people.
- Teach that truthfulness is a treasure: even his opponents testified to it.
- Warn them gently against passing on hurtful words about others.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### Who was Abu Lahab?
'Abd al-'Uzza ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, an uncle of the Prophet ﷺ and one of his fiercest enemies.

### Why was Surah Al-Masad revealed?
Because Abu Lahab said to the Prophet ﷺ "Perish you for the rest of the day! Is this why you gathered us?" when he called Quraysh on as-Safa, as reported in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### Who is "the carrier of firewood"?
Umm Jamil, Abu Lahab's wife, described so either because she spread gossip or because she put thorns in the Prophet's path.

### What is the prophecy in the surah?
It declared that Abu Lahab would die a disbeliever and enter the Fire, and he lived for years without accepting Islam.

### Is Abu Lahab the only enemy named in the Quran?
Among the Prophet's contemporaries who opposed him, yes, he is the only one named.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Masad?
5 verses.

### Why is Abu Lahab mentioned by his nickname rather than his name?
His name was 'Abd al-'Uzza, "servant of al-'Uzza", an idol, so some scholars note the Quran avoided it; others note that the nickname fits his fate in "a fire of blazing flame".

### Can Surah Al-Masad be recited in prayer?
Yes, like any other surah of the Quran; it reminds the reciter that faith, not lineage, is what counts.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Masad tells you that no family tie, no fortune and no children can help you if you oppose the truth. Abu Lahab was the Prophet's uncle, and a surah was revealed about his loss that will be recited until the end of time. Make faith your lineage, righteous deeds your wealth, and keep away from gossip, for it is firewood that burns its carrier before anyone else.
