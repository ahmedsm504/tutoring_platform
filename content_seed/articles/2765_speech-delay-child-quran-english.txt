"My son is two and a half and only says a handful of words. People tell me to play the Quran all day and he will start talking. His doctor tells me to cut down screens. What should I do? And can I start teaching him the Quran when he isn't speaking properly yet?" This is one of the most common questions mothers send us, and it mixes up two different things: **the Quran itself**, and **the way the Quran reaches the child**.

The short answer, which we unpack below: the Quran can be a beautiful part of a late talker's life from day one, but in a **live, interactive way**: a parent's voice, face-to-face talqeen, play and affection. Leaving a child alone with a screen or speaker for hours is not what helps speech develop, even when the audio is the Quran.

This article is educational. It does not replace an assessment by your paediatrician or a speech and language therapist if there are signs that need one.

## Is My Child Actually Delayed?

Children vary in when they start talking, and some late talkers catch up quickly. But specialists commonly flag certain signs that deserve a professional opinion, including:
- Not responding consistently to their name or to sounds.
- Not pointing at things, or making little eye contact.
- No clear single words by around 18 months.
- No two-word phrases by around 2 years.
- Losing words or skills they used to have.
- Speech that the family cannot understand at all after age 3.

This is not a diagnostic checklist. It is a set of signals that say: "Ask a specialist." An early assessment doesn't mean something serious is wrong. Sometimes the cause is simple, such as temporary hearing loss; sometimes the child needs speech therapy, and starting early makes a big difference.

### Why check hearing first?
A child cannot copy speech they cannot hear clearly. Some hearing problems come and go, such as fluid behind the eardrum after colds, so the child hears well some days and poorly on others, and parents may think they "just aren't paying attention." If your child is late to talk, ask your doctor about a hearing test.

## Does Listening to the Quran Treat Speech Delay?

Here we need to be honest and precise. The Quran is the speech of Allah; it contains healing, blessing and tranquillity, and reciting and listening to it are acts of worship. But **speech delay** has causes, and the means Allah has made for addressing it are assessment, rich interaction and, when needed, speech therapy, alongside du'a, ruqyah and trust in Allah.

Specialists broadly agree that children learn to talk mainly through **live interaction**: hearing speech directed at them, being given a chance to respond, seeing the speaker's face and mouth, and being rewarded for trying. Sound coming from a device in the background, with no interaction, does very little for language learning, however beautiful it is.

So our advice to parents is: **don't make the Quran a background soundtrack that replaces your conversation with your child. Make it an interactive time when you sit with them face to face.** Then you combine all the good: the blessing of the Quran and the interaction speech needs.

## Screens and Speech

Many late talkers spend a long time in front of screens, sometimes with good intentions: "He's watching a Quran channel for kids." Common medical guidance recommends keeping screen time very limited in the early years, and when it happens, short and shared with a parent.

In practice:
- Replace "Quran on the screen" with "Quran with Mum or Dad."
- If you use audio, listen together, recite along out loud, and let your child see your mouth.
- Don't make the phone the first tool for calming your child, even with Quran on it.
- Keep screen-free times every day, full of talk and play.

## How a Late Talker Can Start With the Quran

### Stage 1: Familiarity and interactive listening
Before any memorization, we want the Quran to feel warm and safe. Sit your child on your lap and recite a short surah slowly and clearly, looking at them and smiling. Do it at the same time every day, after waking or before sleep.

Choose short surahs with a clear rhythm, such as Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, An-Nas and Al-Kawthar, and repeat the same surah for many days. Children love repetition and learn from it.

### Stage 2: Pause and wait
Once the surah is familiar, start the "pause" game: recite the verse but leave out the last word, and look at your child expectantly with a smile:
- "Qul huwa Allahu…" and wait.

At first they may not say anything. They may make a sound or a movement, and that is a success: they have understood there is a turn for them. Finish the word yourself happily and play again. Over the days, they may say "ahad", "had" or something close. Celebrate it enthusiastically.

### Stage 3: One word, then two
When your child starts completing the last word, extend gently: a word from the middle of the verse, then two words, then a whole short verse. Accept every attempt that is close to the word, and repeat the correct word clearly without saying "wrong."

For example, if the child says "fa'aq" instead of "falaq", say happily: "Yes! Al-falaq!" and repeat it slowly. The child hears the correct form without feeling they failed.

### Stage 4: Regular memorization
Once your child can repeat short phrases, regular memorization can begin with talqeen: a short verse, broken into parts, plenty of repetition and daily revision. This is when regular lessons with a teacher who specialises in young children can start.

## Interactive Talqeen, Step by Step

1. **Sit at your child's eye level**, face to face, not beside them.
2. **Recite slowly**, with clear lip movements, especially for the lip letters (ba', mim, fa', waw), which are easiest to copy.
3. **Split the verse** into the smallest part they can manage.
4. **Wait** at least five seconds before helping.
5. **Praise the attempt**, not only the result.
6. **Repeat at different times** of the day rather than one long session.
7. **Stop before boredom** sets in.

## Good First Surahs and Words

Some words are easier for a late talker because they rely on lip sounds or simple syllables. Start with short surahs and focus on completing the easier words:

| Surah | Easy words to complete |
|---|---|
| Al-Ikhlas | "ahad", "as-samad", "yulad" |
| Al-Kawthar | "al-kawthar", "wanhar", "al-abtar" |
| Al-Falaq | "al-falaq", "khalaq", "waqab" |
| An-Nas | "an-nas" (repeated, so it is easy to learn) |
| Al-Fatihah | "Ameen" after it, then "al-'alamin", "ar-rahim" |

Surah An-Nas is especially good because "an-nas" ends almost every verse, so the child can join in with one word each time:

﴿قُلۡ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿إِلَٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿مِن شَرِّ ٱلۡوَسۡوَاسِ ٱلۡخَنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿ٱلَّذِي يُوَسۡوِسُ فِي صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ﴾ ﴿مِنَ ٱلۡجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ﴾ (Quran 114:1-6)

## The Du'a of Musa (peace be upon him)

One of the most beautiful supplications for parents to make, and later to teach their children, is the du'a of Musa (peace be upon him) when he asked his Lord to free his tongue:

﴿قَالَ رَبِّ ٱشۡرَحۡ لِي صَدۡرِي﴾ ﴿وَيَسِّرۡ لِيٓ أَمۡرِي﴾ ﴿وَٱحۡلُلۡ عُقۡدَةٗ مِّن لِّسَانِي﴾ ﴿يَفۡقَهُواْ قَوۡلِي﴾ (Quran 20:25-28)

Meaning: "My Lord, expand for me my chest, ease for me my task, and untie the knot from my tongue, that they may understand my speech." Make this du'a for your child. Du'a is worship and reliance on Allah, and it goes hand in hand with taking the practical means, not instead of them.

## Working With the Speech Therapist

If your child is in speech therapy, that is one of the best things that can happen for them. Help the Quran teacher and therapist work together:
- Tell the therapist which surahs your child is learning; they may use some of the words in exercises.
- Ask which sounds the therapist is targeting now, and ask the teacher not to push sounds that aren't due yet.
- Ask how best to correct pronunciation at home so the teacher's approach and the therapist's don't conflict.

Some therapists notice that the steady rhythm of short surahs helps a child participate, because they can anticipate the next word. But the therapist who knows your child is the one to judge.

## Mistakes That Make Things Worse

1. **Forcing speech**: "Say it! Say it! You won't get the toy until you say it." Pressure makes children go quieter.
2. **Laughing at their pronunciation**, even innocently.
3. **Correcting every word**, until every attempt feels like a failure.
4. **Relying entirely on devices**: a Quran channel on all day instead of sitting together.
5. **Delaying assessment**: "He'll talk on his own; his father talked late." That may be true, but an assessment does no harm, and waiting can.
6. **Comparison**: "Your cousin is memorizing Juz' Amma and you can't say a sentence."
7. **Stopping the Quran because of the delay**: there is no need to stop; just change the method to something interactive and fun.

## A Simple Daily Plan

| Time | Activity | Length |
|---|---|---|
| After waking | A short surah on Mum's lap, with the pause game | 3–5 minutes |
| During play | Songs and talk directed at the child, no screens | Throughout |
| After lunch | "Finish the word" with this week's surah | 3 minutes |
| In the car | Listening together, with a parent reciting along out loud | 5 minutes |
| Before sleep | This week's surah in a parent's voice, and the du'a of Musa | 3–5 minutes |

The total is small, but it is frequent and interactive, which is what matters.

## Quran-Based Language Activities

The Quran is full of words and images that can become lovely language activities at home:
- **Animals**: the elephant in Surah Al-Fil, the birds, the horses in Surah Al-'Adiyat. Show a picture or toy, say the word clearly, wait for your child to copy or point, then recite the verse.
- **Nature words**: sun, moon, night, day, mountains, sky. Point at the sun and say "shams", then recite "Wash-shamsi wa duhaha." Linking the word, the real thing and the verse is a powerful way to build language.
- **Mini stories**: tell the story of a surah in one minute using very short, repeated sentences with sound and movement. "Big army… an elephant… Allah sent birds… little birds… little stones… the army was gone!"

## When to Start Lessons With a Teacher

There's no need to rush formal lessons. When your child can:
- sit and pay attention for a few minutes,
- repeat words or short phrases, and
- interact with someone other than their parents,

short lessons of 10 to 15 minutes with a teacher experienced with young children can begin, with a parent present. One-to-one online lessons can work well: the child is at home, the parent sits beside them, and the teacher brings her face close to the camera so the child can see her mouth clearly.

## A Word to Mothers and Fathers

You may feel worried. You may blame yourself. People around you may offer conflicting advice. Remember that children differ, that asking a specialist for help is strength, not weakness, and that sitting with your child and reciting the Quran to them with love is itself a great righteous deed with a lasting effect, whatever the pace of their speech.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "The one who is skilled in the Quran will be with the noble, righteous scribes, and the one who recites the Quran and stumbles over it, finding it difficult, will have two rewards" (al-Bukhari and Muslim). If your child grows up and recites with difficulty, they have a double reward, and you have the reward of the one who taught them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I start teaching my late-talking child the Quran?
Start with interactive listening and familiarity, then the "finish the last word" game. Regular memorization comes later, when they can repeat phrases. Nothing prevents starting, as long as the method suits them and there is no pressure.

### Will playing the Quran all day make my child talk sooner?
Background audio without interaction is not what helps speech. It is better for you to recite, or listen together and recite along, at set times, with lots of talk and play the rest of the day.

### Could hearing classical Arabic in the Quran confuse a child who speaks another language at home?
There is no reason to worry about hearing the Quran. Many children grow up hearing more than one language. What matters is plenty of everyday talk directed at them in the home language too.

### My child recites some verses but doesn't talk much in everyday life. Is that normal?
Some children repeat memorized verses, songs or rhymes more easily than they produce spontaneous speech. This is worth telling your speech therapist, as they will assess communication as a whole.

### Could speech delay be a sign of autism?
Speech delay has many possible causes, and autism is one possibility a specialist may consider if there are other signs in communication and social interaction. Don't diagnose your child yourself; ask for a specialist assessment.

### Is ruqyah helpful for speech delay?
Ruqyah with the Quran and authentic supplications is legitimate and beneficial by Allah's permission, and making du'a for your child is one of the greatest things you can do. It goes together with taking practical means such as assessment and therapy, not instead of them.
