**Sakt** is a very short pause of about two counts, **without taking a breath**, then continuing. In the Madinah Mushaf it is marked with a small **سـ (sīn)**.

## The four places
| # | Surah | Words | Read as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Al-Kahf 1–2 | ﴿عِوَجَاۜ﴾ then *qayyiman* | *ʿiwajā* (pause) *qayyiman* |
| 2 | Ya-Sin 52 | ﴿مِن مَّرۡقَدِنَاۜۗ هَٰذَا﴾ | *marqadinā* (pause) *hādhā* |
| 3 | Al-Qiyamah 27 | ﴿مَنۡۜ رَاقٖ﴾ | *man* (pause) *rāq* |
| 4 | Al-Mutaffifin 14 | ﴿بَلۡۜ رَانَ﴾ | *bal* (pause) *rāna* |

## Why sakt?
| Place | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Al-Kahf | So "qayyiman" (straight) is not heard as a description of "ʿiwajā" (crookedness) |
| Ya-Sin | Separates the words of the disbelievers from the reply that follows |
| Al-Qiyamah and Al-Mutaffifin | Keeps the noon and laam clear instead of merging them into the raa |

## How to do it
1. Stop the **sound** for about two counts.
2. **Don't breathe.**
3. Continue with the next word.

## Without sakt?
- In ﴿مَنۡۜ رَاقٖ﴾, without sakt the noon would merge into the raa (*marrāq*). Sakt keeps the noon heard.

## Optional sakt places
- Between Al-Anfal and At-Tawbah, and in ﴿مَالِيَهۡۜ﴾ in Al-Haqqah, Hafs has more than one option; learn them with a teacher.
