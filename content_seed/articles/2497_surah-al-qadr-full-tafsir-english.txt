Surah Al-Qadr is five verses about the greatest night of the year: the night the Quran was sent down, which Allah made better than a thousand months, more than eighty-three years. On that night angels descend in great numbers, and it is peace until dawn. The Prophet ﷺ said, in al-Bukhari and Muslim: "Whoever stands in prayer on Laylat al-Qadr out of faith and seeking reward will have his previous sins forgiven."

This guide explains the surah verse by verse in English and gathers what is authentic about Laylat al-Qadr: when it is, its sign, the supplication to make, and how to prepare.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Qadr
- **Number of verses**: 5.
- **Type**: Meccan as listed in most mushafs; scholars have discussed the point.
- **Position**: Surah 97, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: after *Laylat al-Qadr*, mentioned three times in it.

## The Full Text of Surah Al-Qadr
﴿إِنَّآ أَنزَلۡنَٰهُ فِي لَيۡلَةِ ٱلۡقَدۡرِ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا لَيۡلَةُ ٱلۡقَدۡرِ﴾ ﴿لَيۡلَةُ ٱلۡقَدۡرِ خَيۡرࣱ مِّنۡ أَلۡفِ شَهۡرٖ﴾ ﴿تَنَزَّلُ ٱلۡمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ وَٱلرُّوحُ فِيهَا بِإِذۡنِ رَبِّهِم مِّن كُلِّ أَمۡرٖ﴾ ﴿سَلَٰمٌ هِيَ حَتَّىٰ مَطۡلَعِ ٱلۡفَجۡرِ﴾ (Quran 97:1-5)

**Meaning:** Indeed, We sent it down on the Night of Decree. And what will make you know what the Night of Decree is? The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months. The angels and the Spirit descend in it by the permission of their Lord with every matter. Peace it is, until the rise of dawn.

## Verse 1: We Sent It Down on the Night of Decree
﴿إِنَّآ أَنزَلۡنَٰهُ فِي لَيۡلَةِ ٱلۡقَدۡرِ﴾ (Quran 97:1)

**"We sent it down"**: "it" is the Quran, not named because its greatness makes it obvious. The plural of majesty shows the greatness of both the Sender and what was sent.

**"On the Night of Decree"**: Surah Al-Baqarah tells us this was in Ramadan ("the month of Ramadan in which the Quran was sent down"), and Surah Ad-Dukhan calls it "a blessed night".

**How was the Quran sent down in one night when it was revealed over twenty-three years?** Ibn Kathir and others report from Ibn 'Abbas that the Quran was sent down all at once from the Preserved Tablet to the House of Honour in the lowest heaven on Laylat al-Qadr, then revealed to the Prophet ﷺ in parts over twenty-three years according to events. It is also said that its revelation began on that night.

**Why is it called Laylat al-Qadr?** Scholars give three meanings:
1. **The Night of Honour**: from *qadr*, rank and status.
2. **The Night of Decree**: from *taqdir*, because Allah decrees in it the affairs of the coming year, as Surah Ad-Dukhan says: "In it every wise matter is made distinct."
3. **The Night of Constriction**: because the earth becomes crowded with the angels who descend.

## Verse 2: What Will Make You Know?
﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا لَيۡلَةُ ٱلۡقَدۡرِ﴾ (Quran 97:2)

A question of magnification: what will make you understand what this night is? It is greater than your imagination can grasp, except through what Allah tells you. The Quran uses this style to emphasise great matters, as in "And what will make you know what the Day of Judgement is?"

## Verse 3: Better Than a Thousand Months
﴿لَيۡلَةُ ٱلۡقَدۡرِ خَيۡرࣱ مِّنۡ أَلۡفِ شَهۡرٖ﴾ (Quran 97:3)

Worship on this night, prayer, remembrance, recitation and supplication, is better than worship in a thousand months that do not contain it. A thousand months is about eighty-three years and four months, longer than many people's lives.

**Reflect**: one night can be worth a lifetime. Whoever catches it for ten years is like someone who worshipped Allah for over eight hundred years. This is Allah's generosity to an Ummah with shorter lifespans than earlier nations: He gave it seasons in which rewards are multiplied. Notice also that it says "better than", not "equal to": the reward may exceed even that.

## Verse 4: The Angels and the Spirit Descend
﴿تَنَزَّلُ ٱلۡمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ وَٱلرُّوحُ فِيهَا بِإِذۡنِ رَبِّهِم مِّن كُلِّ أَمۡرٖ﴾ (Quran 97:4)

**"The angels descend"**: in great numbers, bringing blessing and mercy. The verb *tanazzalu* is in the present tense, showing that this descent recurs every year.

**"And the Spirit"**: Jibril, peace be upon him, mentioned separately to honour him.

**"By the permission of their Lord"**: the angels, despite their greatness, move only by Allah's command.

**"With every matter"**: with every affair Allah has decreed for that year.

## Verse 5: Peace Until the Rise of Dawn
﴿سَلَٰمٌ هِيَ حَتَّىٰ مَطۡلَعِ ٱلۡفَجۡرِ﴾ (Quran 97:5)

**"Peace it is"**: the whole night is peace and safety, free of evil, full of tranquillity for the believers. Placing "peace" first gives it emphasis, as if the night is nothing but peace.

**"Until the rise of dawn"**: its blessing lasts from sunset until dawn, so whoever spends any part of it in worship gains from its blessing.

## The Virtues of Laylat al-Qadr in Authentic Hadith
1. **Forgiveness of sins.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported: "Whoever stands in prayer on Laylat al-Qadr out of faith and seeking reward will have his previous sins forgiven."
2. **Missing it is a true loss.** An-Nasa'i recorded with an authentic chain from Abu Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ said about Ramadan: "In it is a night better than a thousand months; whoever is deprived of its good has truly been deprived."
3. **The Prophet's effort in the last ten nights.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah said: "When the last ten nights began, the Prophet ﷺ would tighten his waist-wrapper, stay up the night and wake his family." In Sahih Muslim she said: "He used to strive in the last ten nights more than at any other time."

## When Is Laylat al-Qadr?
- **In the last ten nights of Ramadan.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Seek Laylat al-Qadr in the odd nights of the last ten of Ramadan."
- **Especially the last seven.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn 'Umar reported that some companions were shown in dreams that it was in the last seven nights, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "I see that your dreams agree on the last seven, so whoever seeks it, let him seek it in the last seven."
- **The twenty-seventh night.** In Sahih Muslim, Ubayy ibn Ka'b used to swear that it was the twenty-seventh night. Many scholars consider it the most likely night, though it cannot be stated with certainty.

**Why is its date hidden?** So that Muslims strive in all of the last ten nights. In Sahih al-Bukhari, 'Ubadah ibn as-Samit reported that the Prophet ﷺ came out to tell them its date, but two men were quarrelling, and he said: "I came out to tell you about Laylat al-Qadr, but so-and-so and so-and-so were quarrelling, and it was taken up; perhaps that is better for you."

## The Sign of Laylat al-Qadr
The established sign is in Sahih Muslim from Ubayy ibn Ka'b: "The sun rises on its morning without rays." Some scholars mention other signs, such as a calm, moderate night, but a believer should not make his worship depend on signs. The clearest sign appears after the night is over, and the reward is for whoever stood in prayer, whether he noticed a sign or not.

## What to Do on Laylat al-Qadr
1. **Night prayer**: taraweeh and tahajjud, with long recitation and prostration.
2. **Supplication**, especially the one the Prophet ﷺ taught 'A'ishah. At-Tirmidhi recorded, grading it *hasan sahih*, that she asked what she should say if she knew which night it was. He said: "Say: *Allahumma innaka 'afuwwun tuhibbul-'afwa fa'fu 'anni*. O Allah, You are Pardoning and You love to pardon, so pardon me."
3. **Reciting the Quran**, on the night it was sent down.
4. **Remembrance and seeking forgiveness.**
5. **Charity**, which carries greater reward in Ramadan.
6. **I'tikaf in the last ten nights.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, 'A'ishah said that the Prophet ﷺ observed i'tikaf in the last ten nights of Ramadan until Allah took him, and then his wives did so after him.

## Laylat al-Qadr for a Woman on Her Period
A woman who is menstruating is not deprived of this night. She can make du'a, especially the du'a of pardon; remember Allah through tasbih, tahlil, takbir and istighfar; give charity and feed fasting people; and listen to the Quran. Allah knows the sincerity of her intention when a valid excuse prevents her from praying.

## Word Meanings
- **Anzalnahu**: We sent it (the Quran) down.
- **Al-Qadr**: honour, decree.
- **Adraka**: made you know.
- **Tanazzalu**: descend, again and again.
- **Ar-Ruh**: the Spirit, Jibril.
- **Min kulli amr**: with every matter.
- **Salam**: peace.
- **Matla' al-fajr**: the rise of dawn.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **The Quran is not named** in "We sent it down", because it needs no naming.
2. **"Laylat al-Qadr" repeated three times** to magnify it and build longing.
3. **"Better than" a thousand months**, not "equal to".
4. **The present tense *tanazzalu***: the descent recurs every year.
5. **"Peace" placed before "it"**: the night is nothing but peace.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Ghunnah** on the doubled nun in *inna*.
- **Lam qamariyyah** in *al-qadr*, *al-mala'ikah* and *al-fajr*; **lam shamsiyyah** in *ar-ruh*.
- **Qalqalah kubra** when stopping on *al-qadr* (dal).
- **Izhar** in *min alfi* and in *salamun hiya*, before hamza and ha.
- **Idgham with ghunnah** in *khayrun min*: tanween merges into meem with a nasal sound.
- **Madd muttasil** in *al-mala'ikah*.

## How to Prepare for Laylat al-Qadr
- **Before Ramadan**: renew repentance, organise your time, and reduce commitments for the last ten nights.
- **During the last ten**: rest a little after 'Isha if you need to, then rise in the last part of the night with a set portion of Quran and du'a.
- **Wake your family**, as the Prophet ﷺ did.
- **Write a list of your supplications** so you do not forget them.
- **Avoid quarrels**: the knowledge of its date was lifted because of a quarrel, so keep your heart clean.

## Practical Lessons
1. Honour the Quran; Allah honoured the night it came down.
2. Seize the seasons of worship; life is short and they multiply reward.
3. Strive in all ten nights, not one.
4. Repeat the du'a of pardon.
5. Stand in prayer with faith and hope of reward; intention is the condition of forgiveness.
6. Keep your heart free of disputes.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What is Laylat al-Qadr?
The night the Quran was sent down, better than a thousand months, in the last ten nights of Ramadan.

### When is Laylat al-Qadr?
In the last ten nights of Ramadan, most likely the odd nights, with the twenty-seventh considered most likely by many scholars, without certainty.

### What is its sign?
The sun rises the next morning without rays, as reported in Sahih Muslim.

### What du'a should I make on Laylat al-Qadr?
"Allahumma innaka 'afuwwun tuhibbul-'afwa fa'fu 'anni," recorded by at-Tirmidhi, who graded it *hasan sahih*.

### Who is "the Spirit" in Surah Al-Qadr?
Jibril, peace be upon him.

### Do I get the reward if I did not know it was Laylat al-Qadr?
Yes. The reward is for whoever stands in prayer with faith and hope of reward; striving in all ten nights ensures you catch it, by Allah's will.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Qadr?
5 verses.

### Is Laylat al-Qadr on the same night every year?
Many scholars hold that it moves between the last ten nights from year to year, which is why striving in all ten is the safest way.

### What does "with every matter" mean?
The angels descend with every affair Allah has decreed for that year, of provision, life, death and other matters.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Qadr tells you that somewhere in your year there is one night that can outweigh your whole life. Do not let it pass in heedlessness. Prepare for it, strive in all of the last ten nights, fill them with prayer, Quran and supplication, and say with a humble heart: O Allah, You are Pardoning and You love to pardon, so pardon me.
