Every Arabic letter has a **makhraj**: the exact place in the mouth or throat where it is produced. Getting the makhraj right is the first step of tajweed.

## How to find a letter's makhraj
- Put a **hamza with a fatḥah** before the letter and give the letter a **sukūn**: *ab*, *at*, *aq*.
- Where the sound stops is the makhraj.

## The five main areas
| Area | Letters |
|---|---|
| Al-Jawf (the empty space in the mouth and throat) | The three madd letters: ا و ي when they are madd |
| Al-Ḥalq (the throat) | ء هـ / ع ح / غ خ |
| Al-Lisān (the tongue) | 18 letters |
| Ash-Shafatān (the lips) | ف ب م و |
| Al-Khayshūm (the nasal passage) | The ghunnah of noon and meem |
- Imam Ibn al-Jazarī counted **17 detailed makharij** within these five areas.

## The throat in three levels
| Level | Letters |
|---|---|
| Deepest | ء هـ |
| Middle | ع ح |
| Closest to the mouth | غ خ |

## Hardest letters for English speakers
| Letter | Common mistake | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| ع | Said like a plain vowel | Tighten the middle of the throat |
| ح | Said like h | A tight, breathy sound from the middle of the throat |
| ق | Said like k | Further back, at the very back of the tongue |
| ض | Said like d | Side of the tongue against the upper molars |
| ظ / ذ | Mixed together | ظ is heavy, ذ is light |

## Practice
- **Use a mirror** to check your lips and tongue.
- **Listen** to a careful reciter and copy one letter at a time.
