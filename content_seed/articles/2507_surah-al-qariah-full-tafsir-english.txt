Surah Al-Qari'ah is eleven verses that strike the heart. It begins with a name of the Day of Judgement that evokes a terrifying, pounding blow, then pictures people like moths scattered without direction and mountains like fluffed-up wool. Then the scales are set up: those whose scales are heavy will live a life of contentment, and those whose scales are light will have the Abyss as their refuge, a blazing fire.

This guide explains the surah verse by verse in English and gathers from the authentic Sunnah what makes the scale heavy.

## Quick Facts About Surah Al-Qari'ah
- **Number of verses**: 11.
- **Type**: Meccan.
- **Position**: Surah 101, in the thirtieth juz.
- **Name**: from *al-qari'ah*, the Striking Calamity, a name of the Day of Judgement.

## The Full Text of Surah Al-Qari'ah
﴿ٱلۡقَارِعَةُ﴾ ﴿مَا ٱلۡقَارِعَةُ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلۡقَارِعَةُ﴾ ﴿يَوۡمَ يَكُونُ ٱلنَّاسُ كَٱلۡفَرَاشِ ٱلۡمَبۡثُوثِ﴾ ﴿وَتَكُونُ ٱلۡجِبَالُ كَٱلۡعِهۡنِ ٱلۡمَنفُوشِ﴾ ﴿فَأَمَّا مَن ثَقُلَتۡ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ﴾ ﴿فَهُوَ فِي عِيشَةٖ رَّاضِيَةٖ﴾ ﴿وَأَمَّا مَنۡ خَفَّتۡ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ﴾ ﴿فَأُمُّهُۥ هَاوِيَةࣱ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا هِيَهۡ﴾ ﴿نَارٌ حَامِيَةُۢ﴾ (Quran 101:1-11)

**Meaning:** The Striking Calamity. What is the Striking Calamity? And what will make you know what the Striking Calamity is? The Day people will be like scattered moths, and the mountains will be like carded wool. Then as for the one whose scales are heavy, he will be in a pleasing life. But as for the one whose scales are light, his refuge will be the Abyss. And what will make you know what it is? A blazing fire.

## Verses 1–3: The Striking Calamity
﴿ٱلۡقَارِعَةُ﴾ ﴿مَا ٱلۡقَارِعَةُ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلۡقَارِعَةُ﴾ (Quran 101:1-3)

**Al-Qari'ah** is a name of the Day of Judgement. It is called this because it strikes hearts with terror, the way someone pounds on a door. **"What is the Striking Calamity?"**: a question to magnify it. **"And what will make you know?"**: its reality is beyond anything you can imagine.

**Notice the repetition**: the name is mentioned three times in three short verses, like repeated knocks on the door of a heedless heart. Other names of the Day in the same style include *al-haqqah*, *at-tammah*, *as-sakhkhah*, *al-ghashiyah* and *al-waqi'ah*, each pointing to its force.

## Verse 4: Like Scattered Moths
﴿يَوۡمَ يَكُونُ ٱلنَّاسُ كَٱلۡفَرَاشِ ٱلۡمَبۡثُوثِ﴾ (Quran 101:4)

**Al-farash** are small flying insects that flutter around fire and light, like moths. **Al-mabthuth** means scattered and spread out. People on that Day will be like moths in:
- **Number**: countless.
- **Weakness**: no one will have any strength.
- **Confusion**: surging into each other, not knowing where to go.
- **Flight**: driven by terror.
Surah Al-Qamar gives another image: "as if they were scattered locusts". Both show vast numbers in confusion.

## Verse 5: Mountains Like Carded Wool
﴿وَتَكُونُ ٱلۡجِبَالُ كَٱلۡعِهۡنِ ٱلۡمَنفُوشِ﴾ (Quran 101:5)

**Al-'ihn** is wool, and some say dyed wool. **Al-manfush** is wool that has been fluffed and separated until it is light and drifting. The solid, towering mountains will be like fluffed-up wool. If mountains become like that, what of the weak human being?

**A subtle point**: some commentators note that coloured wool matches the varied colours of mountains in Surah Fatir: "and among the mountains are streaks, white and red of varying colours, and intensely black."

## Verses 6–7: Heavy Scales
﴿فَأَمَّا مَن ثَقُلَتۡ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ﴾ ﴿فَهُوَ فِي عِيشَةٖ رَّاضِيَةٖ﴾ (Quran 101:6-7)

**"As for the one whose scales are heavy"**: whose good deeds outweigh his bad deeds. **"He will be in a pleasing life"** (*'ishatin radiyah*): a life in Paradise that he is pleased with, lacking nothing.

**Why "scales" in the plural?** Some say each person has a scale; others say the plural reflects the many deeds weighed. According to Ahl as-Sunnah, the Scale is real, with two pans, and deeds are weighed on it, as Surah Al-Anbiya' says:

﴿وَنَضَعُ ٱلۡمَوَٰزِينَ ٱلۡقِسۡطَ لِيَوۡمِ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِ فَلَا تُظۡلَمُ نَفۡسࣱ شَيۡـٔٗاۖ وَإِن كَانَ مِثۡقَالَ حَبَّةٖ مِّنۡ خَرۡدَلٍ أَتَيۡنَا بِهَاۗ وَكَفَىٰ بِنَا حَٰسِبِينَ﴾ (Quran 21:47)

**Meaning:** And We will set up the just scales on the Day of Resurrection, so no soul will be wronged at all. Even if it is the weight of a mustard seed, We will bring it forth, and sufficient are We as reckoners.

## Verses 8–11: Light Scales
﴿وَأَمَّا مَنۡ خَفَّتۡ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ﴾ ﴿فَأُمُّهُۥ هَاوِيَةࣱ﴾ ﴿وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا هِيَهۡ﴾ ﴿نَارٌ حَامِيَةُۢ﴾ (Quran 101:8-11)

**"As for the one whose scales are light"**: whose bad deeds outweigh his good, or who has no good deeds at all, like the disbeliever. **"His refuge will be the Abyss"** (*fa-ummuhu hawiyah*). Two explanations:
1. **His refuge and dwelling is al-Hawiyah**, a name of Hell. It is called his "mother" because he returns to it as a child returns to his mother: a powerful, ironic image.
2. **The "mother" of his head plunges into it**: he falls into the Fire headfirst.

**"And what will make you know what it is?"** The *ha* at the end of *mahiyah* is a pausal *ha* that lengthens the sound and increases the awe. **"A blazing fire"** (*narun hamiyah*): intensely hot. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "This fire of yours that the son of Adam kindles is one part of seventy parts of the heat of Hell."

## What Makes the Scale Heavy
1. **Glorification.** In al-Bukhari and Muslim, Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Two words are light on the tongue, heavy on the scale, and beloved to the Most Merciful: *Subhan Allahi wa bi-hamdih, Subhan Allahil-'Azim*."
2. **Praise.** In Sahih Muslim, Abu Malik al-Ash'ari reported: "Purity is half of faith, and *al-hamdu lillah* fills the scale."
3. **Good character.** Abu Dawud and at-Tirmidhi recorded, with at-Tirmidhi grading it *hasan sahih*, that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Nothing is heavier in the scale of the believer on the Day of Resurrection than good character."
4. **Keeping a horse for Allah's cause.** In Sahih al-Bukhari, its feed, drink and droppings will be in its owner's scale.
5. **Patience in hardship**: "the patient will be given their reward without measure."

**Reflect**: some of the heaviest things on the scale look light: a few words of remembrance and good manners. Allah's scale does not measure things by human standards.

## What Makes the Scale Light
- **Shirk and disbelief**: they nullify all deeds.
- **Showing off**: a deed done for other than Allah has no weight.
- **Wronging people.** In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ described the bankrupt person who comes on the Day of Resurrection with prayer, fasting and zakat, but having insulted this one, slandered that one and taken another's wealth; his good deeds are given to those he wronged, and if they run out, their sins are thrown on him and he is thrown into the Fire.
- **Bad character**: the opposite of what makes the scale heavy.

## Word Meanings
- **Al-Qari'ah**: the Striking Calamity, the Day of Judgement.
- **Al-Farash**: moths, small flying insects.
- **Al-Mabthuth**: scattered.
- **Al-'Ihn**: wool.
- **Al-Manfush**: carded, fluffed.
- **Thaqulat mawazinuhu**: his scales are heavy.
- **'Ishatin radiyah**: a pleasing life.
- **Ummuhu**: his refuge, literally his mother.
- **Hawiyah**: the Abyss, a name of Hell.
- **Hamiyah**: intensely hot.

## Rhetorical Beauty
1. **A one-word opening verse**, *al-qari'ah*, that jolts the listener.
2. **A double simile**: people like moths, mountains like wool.
3. **Contrasts**: heavy and light, a pleasing life and a blazing fire.
4. **"His mother is the Abyss"**: an ironic metaphor; a mother is a child's refuge, and his refuge is the Fire.
5. **The pausal *ha* in *mahiyah***, lengthening the sound for effect.

## Tajweed Notes
- **Lam qamariyyah** in *al-qari'ah*, *al-farash*, *al-mabthuth*, *al-jibal*, *al-'ihn* and *al-manfush*; **lam shamsiyyah** in *an-nas*.
- **Qalqalah sughra** in *al-mabthuth*: *ba* with sukun.
- **Ikhfa** in *al-manfush* (nun before *fa*) and *man thaqulat* (nun before *tha*).
- **Idgham without ghunnah** in *'ishatin radiyah*: tanween merges into ra.
- **Ghunnah** on the doubled meem in *fa-ummuhu*.
- **Izhar** in *narun hamiyah*: tanween clear before *ha*.
- **Pausal ha** in *mahiyah*, pronounced with sukun in Hafs.

## Connection with the Surrounding Surahs
Before Al-Qari'ah comes Al-'Adiyat, ending with "when what is in the graves is scattered and what is in the chests is brought out". Al-Qari'ah describes what follows the Resurrection: people like moths, then the scales. After it comes At-Takathur, which shows what distracts people from filling their scales: competing for more until they visit the graves.

## Fill Your Scale in One Day
A simple programme from the authentic Sunnah:
- **After Fajr**: the morning adhkar, and *Subhan Allahi wa bi-hamdih* a hundred times. In al-Bukhari and Muslim: "Whoever says *Subhan Allahi wa bi-hamdih* a hundred times in a day, his sins are wiped away even if they were like the foam of the sea."
- **On the road**: *Subhan Allahi wa bi-hamdih, Subhan Allahil-'Azim*.
- **With family and colleagues**: a smile, a good word and good manners.
- **With your money**: a charity, however small.
- **Before sleep**: self-examination and seeking forgiveness.

## Practical Lessons
1. Prepare for the Day of the Striking Calamity; it is coming.
2. Fill your scale with remembrance, light on the tongue and heavy on the scale.
3. Improve your character; nothing is heavier in the scale.
4. Beware of people's rights, lest you be bankrupt.
5. Do not be deceived by strength; mountains will be like wool.
6. Review yourself every day.

## Teaching Children
- Use a small kitchen scale to show how good deeds make one side heavier.
- Hold a dhikr challenge: who can say *Subhan Allahi wa bi-hamdih* most.
- Connect kind words with the scale: every kind word makes it heavier.

## Names of the Day of Judgement in Juz 'Amma
The last juz of the Quran is full of names for the Day of Judgement, each showing one of its aspects:
- **Al-Qari'ah**: the Striking Calamity, because it strikes hearts with terror.
- **At-Tammah al-Kubra**: the Great Overwhelming Event (Surah An-Nazi'at), because it overwhelms every other disaster.
- **As-Sakhkhah**: the Deafening Blast (Surah 'Abasa), because its sound pierces the ears.
- **Al-Ghashiyah**: the Overwhelming (Surah Al-Ghashiyah), because it covers people with its terrors.
- **Yawm ad-Din**: the Day of Recompense (Surah Al-Infitar).
- **Yawm al-Fasl**: the Day of Sorting (Surah An-Naba').
The Arabs gave many names to something of great importance, so the many names show how great this Day is. When a reader meets these names, he should recall the meaning each carries, so his awe and preparation grow.

## What Is Weighed on the Day of Judgement?
Scholars mention, based on the texts, that the Scale weighs: **the deeds themselves**, as in "two words... heavy on the scale"; **the records of deeds**; and **the doer himself**. In al-Bukhari and Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: "A huge, fat man will come on the Day of Resurrection and he will not weigh, with Allah, the wing of a mosquito," then he recited: "and We will not assign them any weight on the Day of Resurrection" (18:105). A person's true value with Allah is his faith and deeds, not his body, wealth or status.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What does "al-qari'ah" mean?
A name of the Day of Judgement, because it strikes hearts with its terror.

### What does "like scattered moths" mean?
Like small flying insects scattered in their great numbers, weakness and confusion.

### What does "like carded wool" mean?
Like fluffed-up wool, light and drifting.

### What does "his refuge is the Abyss" mean?
His dwelling is al-Hawiyah, a name of Hell; or he will fall into it headfirst.

### What makes the scale heavy?
Remembrance such as *Subhan Allahi wa bi-hamdih, Subhan Allahil-'Azim*, *al-hamdu lillah*, and good character, as reported in the authentic Sunnah.

### Is the Scale real?
Yes. According to Ahl as-Sunnah it is a real scale on which deeds are weighed, as the Quran and Sunnah show.

### How many verses are in Surah Al-Qari'ah?
11 verses.

## Conclusion
Surah Al-Qari'ah asks you one question: how will your scale be? Mountains will fly like wool, people will be like moths, and nothing will help you then except what you placed in the pan of your good deeds. Begin now: *Subhan Allahi wa bi-hamdih, Subhan Allahil-'Azim*, a kind word and good character, for they are heavy on the scale.
